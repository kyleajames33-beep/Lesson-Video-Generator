## Models, movement and scientific limits

- Expansion increases separations of a local comoving grid and stretches one wave by the same scale factor. The grid centre is a chosen viewpoint, not a cosmic centre. The Hubble line is an illustrative nearby-universe relation with H0 = 70 km/s/Mpc, not a cosmological fit or high-redshift conversion.
- Stellar branches are schematic sequences, with no calibrated time or hard remnant-mass cutoffs. Core contraction and an expanding ring locate collapse and expelled material; the ring is not a simulated shock mechanism. No claim that every core collapse yields an observable supernova.
- HR axes use log10 temperature and luminosity, hotter to the left. A synthetic guide uses L/Lsun = (T/5780 K)^7 over this displayed range to place hot stars high, cool stars low and the Sun at one solar luminosity. It is an approximate population guide, not an empirical fit, universal stellar law or radius calculation. The displayed post-main-sequence path is qualitative. The star does not move along the main sequence as it ages.
- Spectral lines use illustrative rest wavelengths 450, 550 and 650 nm on a 400 to 700 nm mapping, then multiply wavelength by (1+z), with z up to 0.04. Line identity is retained. The animation compares radial recession states, not a star accelerating during a measurement. Background colour blocks are an approximate wavelength key, not radiometric spectra. Cosmological redshift has a different cause.
- Binding-energy values are a rounded sample. The maximum is described as the iron-group region; Ni-62 has slightly greater binding energy per nucleon than Fe-56. Individual reactions must still satisfy conservation laws and have a positive Q-value to release energy. Fusion beyond the iron group cannot generally power ordinary stellar burning. Neutron capture and subsequent beta decay can build heavier nuclei. Neutron-star mergers are an established r-process site; not all supernovae are asserted to be major r-process sites.
- Rutherford paths are qualitative repulsive paths around one greatly enlarged positive nucleus. They are neither numerical Coulomb solutions nor measured scattering proportions. The alpha source and foil are schematic.
- Hydrogen energy lines use -13.6/n^2 eV and a proportional vertical energy scale. The transition arrow is an energy difference, not an electron flight path or measured transition duration. A separate 1s density slice uses opacity proportional to exp(-r/55); it illustrates probability per volume, not radial probability or repeated measured positions. No planetary electron trajectory is drawn.
- The decay population is an explicitly deterministic, stratified inverse-CDF sample of exponential lifetimes. Real individual lifetimes are random. Grey marks indicate products; particles do not disappear. The curve is the expectation, not a claim every finite population exactly halves.
- Electron and positron meet and are replaced at one event by two photon markers. The markers move apart at a constant illustrative speed. Fixed, equal arrow lengths encode their opposite momenta. Initial total momentum is zero. Valence quark charges are schematic; no literal separated quark trajectories or classical mediator pellets are implied. Carrier names do not explain a force mechanism. Gravity is outside the Standard Model.
- A ring marker follows a fixed-radius path with an inward magnetic-force arrow. A cavity is located but the kit does not animate its RF field or magnetic ramp. Detector curves are qualitative examples, not a field solution or enough evidence to identify a particle unambiguously.

## Official and primary references

- [NASA universe overview](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/overview/) supports hot early expansion, nucleosynthesis and recombination. [NASA Hubble Big Bang](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/science/science-behind-the-discoveries/hubble-big-bang/) explains expansion evidence and neutral-atom formation.
- [ESA Gaia HR diagram](https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2018/04/Gaia_s_Hertzsprung-Russell_diagram) supports population regions and the dependence on physical stellar properties.
- [DOE nucleosynthesis](https://www.energy.gov/science/doe-explainsnucleosynthesis) supports distinct element-producing environments. [Kasen et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.05455) reports lanthanide-rich kilonova evidence for an r-process contribution from neutron-star mergers.
- [NIST hydrogen atomic data](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/Handbook/Tables/hydrogentable1.htm) supports the rounded 13.6 eV ground-state binding and visible Balmer lines. This kit omits fine structure.
- [NRC radioactive decay](https://www.nrc.gov/education-regulatory-research/glossary/radioactive-decay) supports the ensemble half-life interpretation.
- [CERN Standard Model](https://home.web.cern.ch/science/physics/standard-model/) supports the three included interactions and gravity's exclusion. [CERN fundamental interactions](https://wwwcompass.cern.ch/compass/wwwsmc/target/fundamentalparticles.html) distinguishes residual nuclear binding from quark colour interactions.
- [CERN accelerator principles](https://home.cern/how-accelerator-works/) supports electric acceleration, magnetic steering and repeated cavity passes.
