# Animation planning before production

## Accepted direction

User feedback on 2026-10-02 favours the hand-drawn and painted prototypes and
permits a mixture of styles. Preserve the existing catalogue. Editorial
layouts remain useful for precise calculations and comparisons, without
being a mandated default for every scene.

Choose by teaching task, not a quota of styles. Keep shared colour tokens,
typography, caption space and diagram conventions across treatments. Change
treatment at a natural scene boundary, not halfway through one explanation.

| Teaching task | First choice to inspect | Useful movement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calculation or comparison | Existing worked example or diorama | Reveal the operation, transform the relevant quantity, hold the result |
| Mechanism or process | Existing process diagram or hand-drawn clip | Show causality, direction and order, with stable reference labels |
| Apparatus or physical context | Existing lab visual or painted plate | Highlight the named part or action; code exact connections and labels |
| Misconception | Existing misconception layout | Contrast the mistaken prediction with the observed result |
| Recall question or recap | Existing quiz or summary | Reveal the prompt, allow a thinking hold, then disclose the answer |

Painted artwork supplies context. Scientific structures, connections, units
and labels must remain correct and reviewable. Avoid an atmospheric image
that suggests the wrong experiment. A moving diagram is useful only if the
movement accurately represents the process or is clearly an illustration.

## One compact plan per pilot

Plan one 60 to 90 second pilot first. Reuse the chosen lesson's scenes; do not
invent extra scenes solely to exhibit a style. Copy this table and give each
teaching beat a row:

| Scene and beat | What the learner should understand | Existing component or asset | Treatment | Narration cue | Reveal or transformation | Hold and next beat | Review status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Example: limiting reagent | Compare moles divided by coefficients | Existing worked example and bars | Hand-drawn marks with stable coded values | Divide each amount by its coefficient | Na capacity bar halves; retain the original reference | Hold both capacities for comparison, then highlight sodium | Needs narrated preview |

Use estimated cues while drafting. Set final reveal times from recorded
narration and alignment. Include what stays visible, the misconception to
avoid, and any intentional pause. A missing asset or teaching capability is
a specific gap to fix, not a reason to rebuild a working design.

## Review sequence

1. Audit the selected lesson's existing scenes and assets. Keep what already
   explains the idea. Record changes as reuse, adjust or genuinely missing.
2. Check science and scene logic before generating narration. Check all
   selected lesson copy for U+2014; rewrite with normal punctuation. Preserve
   mathematical minus signs and ranges. If narration text changes, regenerate
   the affected audio and alignment, then rebuild timed captions.
3. Render short previews of the difficult teaching beats. Still images check
   layout, but cannot verify motion, timing or causality. Review at phone size.
4. Make the narrated pilot. Check pronunciation, cue alignment, readable holds,
   caption collisions, transition continuity and audio balance.
5. Fix specific failures, complete one full lesson and watch the exported MP4.
   Run media preflight before release. Batch production follows that review.

The general punctuation check is currently an editorial requirement, not an
automatic release-preflight check. The molar mass pilot renderer now rejects
U+2014 in its selected scenes. Search other selected source and exported copy
for U+2014 before production.

## Acceptance questions

- Is the scientific meaning correct throughout the movement, not just at its end?
- Can a learner explain the intended relationship after watching once?
- Does each major animation explain a change, direct attention or locate context?
- Does the visual arrive when its narration refers to it?
- Are essential labels, units and equations legible at phone size and stable
  after revealing? Does the learner have time to read or think?
- Can the same point be explained more clearly with less movement?

Record failures and uncertainty. A polished preview is not evidence of improved
learning, and a subjective preference is not a reason to replace good assets.
