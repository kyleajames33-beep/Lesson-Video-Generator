# HSC science video pilot protocol

Proposed 2 October 2026. No learners have been recruited, no experiment has run and no new media has been generated. Read with the [production proposal](hsc-video-production-standard-2026-10-02.md) and [script proposals](script-proposals.md).

## Decisions and scope

The first decision is whether a corrected causal explanation helps students choose and justify a molar-mass operation more effectively than a corrected descriptive explanation. It is not a comparison of accurate and inaccurate teaching. Hold voice, model, board, facts, question, response opportunity and feedback constant. Then test delivery separately. Test a mechanism and an investigation task before treating calculation results as a library-wide standard.

Recruitment access remains unresolved. Plan initially for independent volunteers studying relevant Year 11 Chemistry foundations, with teacher review of materials. A school partnership could improve access and curriculum matching, but introduces institutional approval and scheduling requirements. Do not describe a convenience sample as representative of Australian HSC students.

## Stage 0: expert and device validation

Resources: one science teacher, one editor/developer, existing board and Simon voice selection, two scripts and test forms. Allow 4 to 8 hours preparation plus 1 to 2 hours subject review, within the roadmap allowances rather than additional automatic charges.

1. Confirm target cohort and syllabus edition, objective, prerequisite and the supplied atomic values. A teacher independently solves every test item and checks ambiguous wording, scientific claims and scoring rules.
2. Lock scripts below before recording. Both variants need fresh audio, alignment and captions. Preserve original assets. No generation is authorised by this research document.
3. Produce matched isolated exports only in a later authorised production step. Use the same model/settings, overall delivery direction, board, reveals, loudness target and export configuration. Log take selection. One producer selects both against a common pronunciation rubric rather than favouring the preferred hypothesis.
4. Insert a measured six-second answer-free gap after the prompt in each variant. Include the same invitation to pause. Ensure no answer or helpful arithmetic appears through a fade before the gap ends. Exact silence must come from timeline assembly, not an unverified speech-model pause.
5. Review the entire short export with and without captions on desktop and actual phone, including start/end and hold boundaries. Check label legibility, sound, captions and scientific meaning during movement. Essential accessibility accommodations stay available in both groups; record them.

Pass only with correct science and speech, readable essential information, faithful captions and a verified response interval. Any critical error stops learner testing. A mismatch in duration, answer visibility or delivery that could explain the comparison requires correction before recruitment. The 60 to 90 second target is a planning hypothesis. If natural, readable speech exceeds it, adjust both fairly instead of speeding one variant to force the range.

## Matched script comparison

Both variants teach the same scientific facts. Only the explanatory framing differs. These are newly proposed passages, not replacements for any recorded narration. The common introduction, question and feedback are identical. The two middle passages have approximately matched word counts; final audio durations must also be checked. Full speech, including the common sections and gap, may sit near the upper end of the target range.

**Common introduction:**

> Molar mass is mass per mole. For carbon, use twelve point zero one grams per mole. We will find the mass of two point zero zero moles of carbon, then find an amount in moles from a mass.

**A: descriptive middle, 66 words:**

> To find mass, use m equals n times M. Substitute two point zero zero for n and twelve point zero one for M. Multiply the values to get twenty-four point zero two grams. Report twenty-four point zero grams to three significant figures. To find an amount in moles from a mass, use n equals m divided by M. Substitute the values, divide, then report your result.

**B: causal middle, 65 words:**

> Each mole contributes twelve point zero one grams. Two moles contribute twice that mass, so m equals n times M. Multiplying gives twenty-four point zero two grams. Report twenty-four point zero grams to three significant figures. To find an amount in moles from a mass, ask how many one-mole masses fit into the sample. That is a division, so n equals m divided by M.

**Common prompt:**

> A sample contains thirty-six point zero three grams of carbon. Would you multiply or divide by twelve point zero one to find the amount in moles? Explain why. Pause if you need longer.

**Common timeline action:** six seconds without the answer, arithmetic or narration. Keep only the prompt and supplied values visible.

**Common feedback:**

> Divide. You are finding how many one-mole masses fit into the sample. Thirty-six point zero three divided by twelve point zero one gives three point zero zero moles. Multiplication finds a mass from an amount; division finds an amount from a mass.

The shared feedback deliberately gives both groups the causal explanation. The comparison therefore tests the added value of causal framing during the worked example, after equal feedback, rather than denying one group reasoning support. This may reduce the measured difference. Record that limitation in the results. Do not claim it tests every feature of the much broader V3 script.

Board control: both show the same carbon value, given amount, formula, substitution and result, followed by the same transfer prompt and feedback. Synchronise reveals to the equivalent spoken ideas. Do not add a grouping animation only in B. Any difference in time on an equation belongs in the confound log.

## Stage 1: small formative sessions

Recruit 6 to 8 learners with a spread of prerequisite knowledge and confidence. Allocate only one version of this lesson to each student, balanced approximately across versions. Do not show A then B to the same learner and mistake repeated teaching for a treatment benefit. This stage diagnoses problems, not comparative effectiveness.

Have learners watch normally, including pause/replay controls. Collect the immediate test before asking about preference or explanations of the design. A retrospective interview can ask what a pause meant, which label was hard to read, what an expression meant and why they chose an operation. Think-aloud viewing, if useful in a separate usability session, changes normal learning behaviour and should not supply the main outcome data.

Use the items and rubric below, then interview for five minutes. Allow about 20 to 25 minutes per learner. Log device, captions, prior topic exposure, pauses, replay and assistance. Staff allowance: 8 to 14 hours including recruitment administration, sessions and analysis; recruitment lead time is additional.

Local progress criterion: at least 6 of 8, or 5 of 6, independently read the essential board and correctly choose the operation on the new mass-to-amount item. This is a usability signal chosen for this project, not a published benchmark. Revise if two or more learners express the same design-induced misconception, cannot read essential notation or say the hold ended before they could start reasoning. One critical science or speech defect stops testing. A low score caused by absent prerequisite knowledge suggests a prerequisite cue or different entry route, not automatically a failed art style.

Decision: repair the script, visual or hold problem, or proceed to the controlled comparison. Report individual counts and examples of reasoning. With this sample, do not rank A and B by a one-person difference.

## Stage 2: controlled feasibility comparison

If feasible, recruit 24 to 36 learners, roughly 12 to 18 per version. Randomise within broad prior-knowledge bands using an allocation list prepared before sessions. Avoid assigning one whole class to A and another to B without acknowledging class/teacher confounding. Each learner sees only one variant. Keep facilitator instructions identical and do not tutor during assessment.

Before viewing: a brief prerequisite check on units and proportional reasoning, topic exposure and confidence. Avoid pretesting the exact worked example or teaching the target method through feedback. Record rather than exclude low prior knowledge after seeing results. Agree any exclusion rules, such as a playback failure, in advance; report all allocations and reasons for missing data.

After viewing: administer the immediate assessment independently. Use no notes for the first attempt. Record first answers before discussion. At seven days, with a practical window of plus or minus two days, use a parallel form without replaying the lesson. Record intervening study rather than pretending students had no other instruction. Provide the lesson and corrected explanations to everyone after delayed testing, or sooner if educational needs require it, documenting the resulting limitation.

Primary outcome: new mass-to-amount problem plus operation explanation, scored 0 to 4 as below. Secondary learning outcomes: opposite-direction problem, misconception item and delayed score. Engagement, confidence and preference remain secondary. Decide this hierarchy before collecting results, not after choosing the favourable measure.

Report group sizes, baseline distributions, individual scores, averages and score spread, difference with uncertainty, errors and delayed attrition. Use blinded scoring with variant labels removed. A second reviewer scores at least a quarter of responses and all ambiguous ones; resolve disagreement using the predefined rubric. A small convenience pilot has weak power for modest effects. A null difference does not prove equivalence; an apparent gain may be unstable. Do not generalise to all subjects or model choices.

Provisional decision rule, explicitly a local hypothesis: prefer causal framing for this task if its primary mean is at least 0.5 points higher on the four-point scale, errors do not increase, delayed outcomes do not suggest a material reversal, and sessions reveal no access or timing penalty. Consider uncertainty alongside the point estimate. An imprecise result justifies another targeted comparison or a professional choice with unresolved efficacy, not a claim of superiority. Stop/change if a repeated misconception increases, a critical science error emerges or dropouts make the groups uninterpretable. Allow 16 to 30 staff hours including allocation, sessions, follow-up and analysis, excluding approval/recruitment lead time.

## Assessment forms and scoring

Supply values to prevent periodic-table lookup differences. A calculator is available for both groups. Explain that answers are ungraded and that reasoning matters. Teacher checks parallel difficulty before use; randomise immediate forms across variants so form difficulty is not confounded with script.

| Purpose | Form A | Parallel form B | Scoring |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Primary transfer, 0 to 4 | Carbon mass 18.015 g; M = 12.01 g mol⁻¹. Find amount and explain multiply/divide | Carbon mass 30.025 g; same M. Find amount and explain multiply/divide | 1 correct division setup; 1 correct amount with unit (1.500 or 2.500 mol under four significant figures); 2 explanation connects mass per mole to how many one-mole masses fit, 1 merely repeats a rule, 0 absent/incorrect. Total max 4 |
| Opposite direction, 0 to 2 | 0.500 mol carbon; same M. Find mass | 0.250 mol carbon; same M. Find mass | 1 multiplication setup; 1 correct mass/unit, 6.01 or 3.00 g to three significant figures. Accept correct guard-digit values while recording rounding separately |
| Misconception, 0 to 2 | A student multiplies 18.015 g by 12.01 g mol⁻¹ and reports moles. Identify and repair the error | Same claim using 30.025 g | 1 identifies wrong operation/quantity or invalid resulting units; 1 repairs using division and explains what is being counted. Passing a unit check alone is not proof of correctness |
| Delayed recall | State the meaning and unit of molar mass, then take the unused primary and opposite-direction forms | Counterbalance forms | Meaning/unit 0 to 2; transfer rubric retained. Interpret with intervening study and follow-up attendance |

Do not deduct the primary numerical point solely for rounding when conceptual understanding is the tested objective and the value/unit are correct. Record rounding as a separate diagnostic. Exact worked and prompted numbers do not appear in the primary transfer test. These are close transfer items, not broad evidence of scientific reasoning. Later testing should vary substances and include bracketed formula counting.

After assessment, ask confidence in solving a new problem from 0 to 100, interest and mental effort on clearly anchored five-point scales, and one free response about confusing or useful moments. Compare confidence with accuracy to identify overconfidence. Do not interpret confidence or enjoyment as evidence of learning. Record viewing behaviour only with consent; a simple facilitator log may be adequate.

## Stage 3: separate voice comparison

Use the scientifically approved script, one board and fixed assembled response hold. Subject to actual account access and later generation authorisation, compare Simon in Eleven v4 and Multilingual v2 on three short passages: a plain causal explanation, dense units/numbers and a bracketed compound. Produce two takes of each model/passage, at most twelve short clips. Record model-specific supported settings; equal numeric settings do not mean equal delivery across models.

Teacher/editor screening first: listen to every number, scientific term, pronunciation, instability and unintended sound. Stop any failed take from becoming learner material. Log failed takes rather than reporting only the best sample. A small consenting listener panel can compare anonymised A/B clips in counterbalanced order. Repeated exposure is acceptable for pronunciation and preference, but does not establish learning advantage.

Score number/term accuracy, intelligibility, naturalness, warmth, useful emphasis and consistency separately. Required gate: zero critical pronunciation/number errors in selected clips, exact holds supplied by assembly, complete captions/alignment and acceptable full-scene playback. Investigate repeated errors across both takes. Prefer a model only after weighing accepted-take consistency, listening burden, controllability and actual cost. Keep Simon as selected voice. This protocol does not presume v4 wins. If neither model is reliable, repair spelling/dictionary/chunking within supported controls and repeat only the failed passage.

## Stage 4: task transfer and first complete release

Reuse the native DNA assets for a short template/daughter-strand prediction and explanation. Ask learners to trace one original strand into a daughter molecule and explain why the model is semi-conservative. Separately use the enzyme practical/graph material to ask for an observation, a justified inference, one controlled variable and one limitation. A teacher scores mechanistic and evidential reasoning. A calculation pilot cannot validate those tasks.

Start with 6 to 8 formative learners for each task, sharing participants only where prerequisites permit and topics do not contaminate the comparison. Fix scientifically incomplete models first. Use the selected delivery and one script per task; this is a transfer/usability check, not another multi-factor effectiveness experiment. Require most learners to perform the target reasoning and investigate repeated failure patterns. Report exact counts instead of treating “most” as a universal standard.

Then review one complete molar-mass lesson and the short Biology checks through all production gates. Watch full exports on desktop and phone; listen to every critical spoken value; verify caption coverage including any narrated intro. No short-clip result replaces complete-lesson review. Proceed to a monitored batch of three to five task-diverse lessons only after those gates pass and results justify the chosen approach. Measure accepted-minute cost and defect/rework rates. Expand the batch only after inspecting those records.

## Consent, data and reporting

Participation should be voluntary, age-appropriate and unrelated to grades. Obtain learner assent and parent/guardian permission where required for minors, plus institutional approval where applicable. Offer withdrawal, accessibility accommodations and an alternative learning resource. Collect the minimum data, use participant codes, keep consent records separate, restrict access and agree retention/deletion periods. Do not collect faces, names, school identifiers or screen recordings merely because software can. Disclose any recording and its purpose separately.

For NSW public schools, check the Department's [research-in-schools process](https://education.nsw.gov.au/about-us/education-data-and-research/research-with-us/researching-in-our-schools), including whether SERAP approval applies, before recruitment. A teacher's informal agreement should not be assumed to satisfy that process. This proposal does not determine institutional ethics requirements for a particular organiser.

Retain an experiment manifest: objective, cohort, allocation method, recruitment route, approval/consent basis, scripts/hashes, voice/model/settings/takes, boards/cue times, exact hold boundaries, captions/export hashes, device controls, assessment forms/rubric, outcome hierarchy, decision rules, exclusions, deviations and analysis. Report negative, contradictory and null findings as well as favourable ones. Keep identifying learner data out of the repository.

The smallest next action is teacher review of the two short scripts and assessment forms, followed by a matched formative molar-mass pilot when new media generation and recruitment are authorised. It can reveal whether students understand the operation and where the explanation fails. It cannot prove library-wide effectiveness or establish the best art style.
