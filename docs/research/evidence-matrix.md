# Evidence matrix for HSC science video decisions

Companion to the [production proposal](hsc-video-production-standard-2026-10-02.md). Sources accessed 2 October 2026. This is a focused synthesis, not a preregistered systematic review. “Moderate” and “strong” are practical judgements about support for the bounded claim, not formal GRADE ratings. Numerical study results are not predicted gains for our videos. Mixed-age samples are not automatically Australian secondary samples. Where the accessible source does not break down populations, that uncertainty is retained.

## Learning and instructional design

| ID and specific claim | Supporting source and date | Population and context | Outcomes | Strength and limitations | HSC relevance and repository implication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E1. Multimedia design affects learning; spatial/temporal contiguity and selective signalling are useful priorities | [Noetel et al., 2022, overview of reviews](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211052329), online 2021; publisher abstract and review description accessed | 29 reviews, 1,189 studies, 78,177 participants; heterogeneous multimedia environments, not a single secondary-video population | Learning and cognitive load | Strong synthesis-level support, but constituent reviews overlap and include differing tasks, media and outcome tests. It cannot prescribe one layout or exact cue delay | Moderate directness. Put labels near their referents, synchronise explanation and graphic, reduce search. Keep caption accessibility rather than interpret redundancy as a blanket ban on text |
| E2. Meaningful segmentation can support retention and transfer, often taking more study time | [Rey et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-9456-4); publisher abstract accessed | 56 investigations, 88 comparisons; multimedia tasks across participant and pacing conditions. Detailed age breakdown not extracted | Retention, transfer, cognitive load and learning time | Moderate to strong, small to medium average learning effects. Both learner- and system-paced segmentation are supported. More time and chunking can contribute; reported prior-knowledge moderation is not “only novices benefit” | Moderate directness. Use meaningful beats and chapters, hold connected calculations/mechanisms together. Do not infer a universal short-video duration or exact pause length |
| E3. Selective signals can help learning, but effects vary | [Alpizar, Adesope and Wong, 2020](https://rex.libraries.wsu.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/A-meta-analysis-of-signaling-principle-in/99900601052901842); authors' institutional abstract | 29 experiments, 2,726 participants, 44 effect sizes; multimedia presentations, multiple education levels | Learning outcomes, moderated by participant, presentation and methods | Moderate; overall d = 0.38 with variation. Quality, pretesting and prior-knowledge controls matter. Cueing every object is not tested as a universal benefit | Moderate directness. Existing leader lines, underlines and active-variable highlights should direct attention to the current inference, not decorate every label |
| E4. Representational animation can help, particularly where change or a procedure is the learning target | [Höffler and Leutner, 2007](https://fis.leuphana.de/en/publications/instructional-animation-versus-static-pictures-a-meta-analysis/); institutional abstract | 26 primary studies, 76 dynamic/static comparisons; declarative, problem-solving and procedural/motor contexts. Age distribution not available in abstract | Learning, with knowledge-type and visualisation moderators | Moderate; overall d = 0.37. Old technologies, heterogeneous materials and stronger procedural-motor effects limit extrapolation. Not evidence for perpetual ambient motion | Moderate directness. Retain a static equation during reasoning. Use DNA fork movement or an apparatus action when its sequence explains something; review transitory information and preserve reference states |
| E5. Interesting irrelevant details can impair learning; impact depends on design and context | [Sundararajan and Adesope, 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-020-09522-4); publisher abstract | Experimental learning materials including irrelevant details across formats and subjects. Full population breakdown not extracted | Learning with design/method moderators | Moderate synthesis support. Mixed degrees of harm and moderator variation do not establish that all artwork is distracting | Moderate directness. Keep an atmospheric plate if it locates the task and avoids misleading science. Remove context that competes with the relationship being taught |
| E6. Newer synthesis finds a small average negative effect of seductive details, not a universal large penalty | [Cheng, Wu, Wang and Wang, 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-025-10099-z), 5 March; publisher abstract | 50 studies, 177 effect sizes, multiple learning environments; complete age distribution not extracted | Recall, comprehension, transfer and reported cognitive-load mediation | Moderate: overall g = -0.16; comprehension -0.19, recall -0.17, transfer -0.12. Moderator and meta-analytic mediation findings do not prove a causal mechanism in our films. Full risk-of-bias analysis not inspected | Moderate directness. Prioritise reducing irrelevant information. A small pooled effect does not require a wholesale minimalist restyle. Measure whether the painted context distracts in our task |
| E7. Retrieval practice often improves later learning in real classrooms | [Agarwal, Nunes and Blunt, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09595-9); publisher abstract and methods summary | 50 classroom experiments, n = 5,374, varied education levels and content; only 6% outside WEIRD countries | Assessed learning across delays and retrieval/final-test formats | Strong broad support: 57% of effects medium or large, leaving substantial smaller or null outcomes. A video invitation does not establish that retrieval occurred. Task and feedback design remain important | Relatively relevant, but not directly an HSC AI-video trial. Use a visible held prompt, collect a response in pilots, revisit through delayed practice; do not equate “quickCheck scene present” with learning |
| E8. Self-explanation prompts can improve learning when students generate meaningful relations | [Bisra et al., 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-018-9434-x); publisher abstract | Worked problems, problem solving and text study across subjects/education levels; 64 reports, 69 effect sizes | Learning, with task and prompt moderators | Moderate to strong; pooled g = 0.55. Broad heterogeneous evidence, not an optimal count of video prompts. Prompting may add time and depends on what learners actually produce | Moderate directness. Ask “why divide by the coefficient?” or trace synthesis direction. Give a bounded prompt and sufficient silence, then compare a reasoned answer |
| E9. Worked-example support should change with acquired skill; fading can aid transition to solving | [Renkl and Atkinson, 2003](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_3), author-posted paper; [Renkl, Atkinson and Grosse, 2004](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ732331), author abstract via ERIC | Cognitive-skill acquisition and computer-based example learning; full individual-study samples not extracted | Problem solving, learning processes, efficiency and think-aloud evidence | Moderate. 2003 includes theoretical analysis rather than a meta-analysis. 2004 did not find an increase in productive self-explanation events, but found fewer unproductive events. Do not claim one established mechanism or prescribe the same fading for experts | Moderate directness for calculations. Complete carbon example, completion problem using the same relation, independent molecular conversion later. Do not retrofit Known/Find/Formula labels onto every biological explanation |
| E10. Feedback is more useful when it carries information about task/reasoning; effects are heterogeneous | [Wisniewski, Zierer and Hattie, 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03087/full); full article available, abstract/methods/discussion inspected | 435 studies, 994 effects, over 61,000 participants; multiple education and skill contexts | Learning, cognitive/motor skills, motivational/behavioural outcomes | Moderate to strong broad support; d = 0.48 overall, substantial heterogeneity and information-content moderation. The mean is not a guaranteed effect of praise or canned video feedback | Moderate directness. Explain the reason and next repair, for example “Cl₂ needs two atomic contributions”. Distinguish response-specific feedback from a generic answer reveal |
| E11. Shorter videos can correlate with more watching, but this is not an optimal-learning-duration experiment | [Guo, Kim and Rubin, 2014](https://juhokim.com/files/LAS2014-Engagement.pdf); author-hosted conference paper | 6.9 million viewing sessions across four edX MOOCs; self-selected online learners, observational production differences | Time watching and attempts at post-video problems | Strong descriptive engagement dataset, weak causal support for production rules. No direct delayed-learning/transfer test establishing a six-minute ceiling. Tutorial behaviour differs from lecture behaviour | Low directness. Use retention to locate possible friction; preserve calculation pauses and chapters. Do not accelerate Simon or cut a coherent mechanism solely to emulate MOOC watch-time patterns |
| E12. Video can support learning alongside other instruction | [Noetel et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654321990713); publisher abstract | 105 randomised higher-education trials, 7,776 college students | Assessed learning; substitution and supplementary-video comparisons | Moderate to strong within higher education: g = 0.28 for replacement, 0.80 for added video. Extra time/material, experimental and publication biases matter. Not evidence of replacement of practical learning | Explicit university-to-HSC extrapolation. Put videos in a loop with practice, feedback and real investigations rather than selling watching as course completion |
| E13. A perceived easy or enjoyable lesson need not produce the most learning | [Deslauriers et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1821936116); original classroom study | Introductory university Physics; active learning versus passive lectures with controlled instruction | Actual test performance and reported feeling of learning | Moderate for the distinction. Human classroom activity and whole instruction differ from short narrated videos; do not infer an identical effect size here | Explicit extrapolation. Measure new-problem performance and delayed recall separately from confidence, preference and watch time |
| E14. Conversational style is a reasonable starting point, with topic-specific boundaries | [Noetel et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211052329); synthesis includes personalisation. Counterexample: [Almeida, Münzer and Kühl, 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jcal.13026), full open paper | Synthesis has mixed multimedia populations. Counterexample: 139 university students learning neutral/aversive diabetes material with personalised or impersonal language | Retention, transfer, anxiety, load, motivation and learning time | Moderate general support; counterexample found better transfer with non-personalised material. Direct medical self-reference can be inappropriate, and pleasant conversational voice is not identical to the pronoun manipulation | Moderate/low directness. “Choose the quantity” is useful task address. Do not say “your diabetes” or assume a health condition. Warmth, humour and AI prosody remain local tests |
| E15. Explicitly refuting and replacing a misconception can support scientific understanding | [Schroeder and Kucera, 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-021-09656-z); publisher abstract | 44 independent comparisons, n = 3,869; scientific misconception texts across contexts, age breakdown not extracted | Conceptual/scientific learning | Moderate; g = 0.41. Text interventions are not automatically equivalent to an animated mistake card. Need an explicit correction and explanation, not merely repeat the wrong idea | Moderate directness. Contrast saturation and damage, or raw moles and reaction capacity. Show why the initial prediction fails and test an altered case |
| E16. Background music has inconsistent cognitive effects across tasks, music and people | [Cheah et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1177/20592043221134392); publisher/author paper accessible, review summary consulted | Cognitive tasks across memory, language, reasoning, attention and other domains; not exclusively adolescent instructional videos | Cognitive task performance, with task/music/population factors | Limited to moderate for any particular video decision. Broad inconclusive findings do not justify universal benefit or universal harm | Low directness. Default to clean narration and exact quiet response holds. Test music only if a concrete pedagogical or context purpose warrants it |

| H1. Mixed visual treatments will be usable and acceptable when chosen by task | User feedback, existing source/stills, professional judgement; no direct comparative HSC study identified | Our artwork and students; proposed readability, distraction, understanding and preference outcomes | Hypothesis. ## Conclusions the evidence does not support

No source inspected establishes that our best videos are always six minutes, that the screen must change every few seconds, that Australian accent alone improves learning, that Simon/v4 is the best model for every science task, that painted artwork is inherently harmful, or that a polished render with high confidence ratings demonstrates mastery.

Positive pooled averages coexist with null or adverse individual outcomes. Signalling depends on the cue and task. Segmentation can consume more time. Fading need not work by increasing self-explanation events. Personalisation can backfire in health information. Irrelevant detail has a small recent pooled penalty with moderators. Treat principles as reasons to construct and test a clear lesson, not as a formula that guarantees effectiveness.
