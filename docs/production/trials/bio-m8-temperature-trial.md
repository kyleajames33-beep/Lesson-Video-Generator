# Biology Module 8 temperature feedback trial

Status: isolated design trial, 16 October 2026. Composition: `Explainer-bio-m8-temperature-trial`. No recording, full export or public release approval is claimed.

## Learner question and boundary

How does sweating help reverse a rise in body temperature? A student starting Year 12 needs only the idea that working muscles produce heat. This trial introduces homeostasis, receptor, control centre, effector and negative feedback in one human example. The next lesson should construct and interpret a blood glucose loop. This temperature trial alone does not complete B8-IQ1-01, which also names glucose.

## Teaching and visual decisions

| Scene | Narration draft | Visual action | Reuse or adjustment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | Working muscles release heat during a run. Your body warms. How does it bring its temperature back towards its usual range? Homeostasis means keeping internal conditions within a workable range. | Painted person arrives first; a warm halo and leader line mark the change. The definition enters after the question. | Reuse the Module 5 painted person and cream paper. Do not label the whole person as a receptor. |
| Signal | Thermoreceptors detect the temperature rise. Some in the skin sense skin temperature; others in the hypothalamus sense the temperature of the blood flowing through it. The hypothalamus is the control centre here. It signals sweat glands, the effectors in the skin, to release sweat. | Three cards arrive in spoken order. Connectors draw only after the relevant relationship is explained. Drops appear beside the effector. | Reuse Caveat labels, Inter Tight body and blue and amber accents. Diagram is native SVG. |
| Cooling | The sweat has to evaporate. As liquid sweat becomes water vapour, heat leaves the skin. This helps body temperature fall back towards its usual range. As the rise is reduced, the sweating response eases. That is negative feedback: the response reduces the original change. | Droplets rise from a drawn skin line and heat arrows move away. A return arrow and reduction label complete the loop. | Native drawing avoids an unrelated stock visual. |
| Practice | Draw the loop for a runner: receptor, control centre, effector and response. Why is it negative feedback? Answer: thermoreceptors detect the rise; the hypothalamus signals sweat glands; evaporation removes heat from skin; body temperature falls towards its usual range. The original rise is reduced. A common mistake is to say sweat cools before it evaporates. | Question remains visible during a ten second countdown. Answer and one crossed-out mistake appear after the hold. | Standard practice hold and brief feedback. |
| Key notes | Write the short loop and the meaning of negative feedback. | Brief optional copy hold with three essentials. | Module 5 note treatment. |

## Review and gates

Independent source script review by the scope reviewer found the focused teaching sequence sound. It requested a cause for the initial rise, precise receptor wording, and a completed return step. The visual reviewer found that the first diagram relied too heavily on text cards and that droplets seemed to rise as liquid. The revised diagram points to the painted body's skin and head, carries the signal through the three roles, and shrinks drops at the skin while heat arrows leave. The return label was moved above the caption area. The code uses timing placeholders, so the exact voiced preview gate remains pending. It must be checked at playback speed, including caption fit and the ten second hold, before full export. Paid narration, final review and public release remain separate decisions.
