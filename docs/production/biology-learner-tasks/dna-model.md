# Student sheet: operate and assess a DNA replication model

Use after the DNA draft. Allow about 25 to 35 minutes for modelling plus a
written evaluation. Total: 20 formative points. Use paper strips, removable
base labels and role cards. This is a school model, not a molecular simulation
or a test of actual replication accuracy. Entry check: pair A with T and C
with G, and distinguish bases from the sugar-phosphate backbone.

## Build and operate

Begin with two original strands. At the pictured instant, the fork moves to
the right. The already exposed template region lies to its left; parental
double-stranded DNA ahead of the fork lies to its right. Label the exposed
upper template left-to-right 3′ → 5′ and the lower template 5′ → 3′.
Use this short paired section as an orientation check:

```
upper original:  3′-T A C G G T-5′
lower original:  5′-A T G C C A-3′
```

1. Separate paired bases near the advancing fork while retaining both original
   backbones. Place a helicase role card. Add RNA-primer labels providing an
   extendable 3′ end, with a primase card. (3 points)
2. Extend the upper new strand continuously to the right as the fork advances.
   Show that its new DNA grows 5′ → 3′ by adding at the existing 3′ end.
   Give its complementary sequence for the six pictured template bases.
   (3 points)
3. On the lower template, add repeated primers as new template is exposed
   near the fork, then extend each new fragment to the left, also 5′ → 3′.
   Explain why fragments are needed and identify leading/lagging synthesis.
   Both strands grow in the same chemical direction despite opposite page
   directions. (3 points)
4. Use separate model actions for removing/replacing RNA primers with DNA
   and then sealing remaining backbone nicks. Include a processing card and
   a ligase card. Do not make ligase remove RNA or fill missing nucleotides.
   A drawn gap can represent a discontinuity but is not an empty molecular
   space accurately drawn to scale. (3 points)
5. Assemble two completed double-strand pairs and identify one original plus
   one newly synthesised strand in each. Explain why this semi-conservative
   composition does not guarantee no copying errors. Name additional fidelity
   processes. (3 points)

Keep a drawing or photo record of the labelled stages and a short observation
log. Record who built, operated and checked the model. Teacher review should
inspect your actual actions and directions, rather than accepting a list of
enzyme names alone. The displayed six-base example checks orientation; the
fragment activity can extend the paper template without claiming real fragment
lengths or counting each school label as a molecular measurement.

## Assess the particular model (5 points)

State the question your model helps answer. Give two visible features that
support that purpose and two relevant omissions or distortions. Link one
omission to a conclusion you cannot draw. Give a qualified judgement and one
useful improvement. For example, adding direction or processing labels can
improve represented causality; adding decoration need not do so.

Compare it with a model that only shows one old plus one new strand in each
daughter molecule. Which questions can that simpler model answer? Which fork,
enzyme or fidelity questions need additional representation or evidence?
Do not claim that handling a model itself proves better understanding or memory.
