# Student sheet: observations and enzyme mechanisms

Use after the graph-analysis draft. Allow about 25 minutes. Total: 20 formative
points. Use paper or a spreadsheet to plot each dataset with labelled axes;
do not identify factors from anonymous shapes. Entry check: distinguish a
measured outcome from a proposed explanation.

All numbers are synthetic relative initial-rate data from defined exercises,
not measurements or universal enzyme curves. Within each series, the same
active-enzyme preparation and other stated assay conditions are held constant.
The relative rates share one arbitrary scale within that series only. Do not
compare their magnitudes between different series.

1. Plot all three datasets, retaining labels, units and the relative-rate
   qualification. Describe the main trend in each without first claiming a
   molecular cause. Locate the highest measured temperature and pH points.
   Do they fix the precise optimum between tested values? (6 points)
2. Give a plausible explanation for each trend. Scope the substrate explanation
   to a simple fixed-enzyme saturation model. Separate each explanation from
   what was directly observed. (4 points)
3. Assess these claims: “The temperature fall proves permanent denaturation”;
   “the pH fall proves the whole enzyme unfolded”; and “every active site is
   occupied at every instant at the end of series C”. Correct each one.
   (3 points)
4. Design a recovery comparison for the temperature or pH case. State how
   treatment history differs, which conditions match during the new assay,
   and what recovery or continued low rate would support. Include a control
   for a changed dilution or transfer procedure. (4 points)
5. Choose one explanation other than simple substrate saturation that could
   flatten a real assay. Propose a measurement or control that helps distinguish
   it. Predict the effect of doubling the same active enzyme only under the
   explicit simple model and suitable substrate/assay conditions. (3 points)

An apparent plateau in real data could involve substrate availability, product
effects, assay readout limits or loss of active enzyme. Choose one and explain
how your proposed check addresses it. A generic extra repeat is not a test
that distinguishes every explanation.
