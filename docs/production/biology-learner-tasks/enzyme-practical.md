# Student sheet: define the enzyme assay

Use after the enzyme-practical draft. Allow about 20 minutes for synthetic-data
analysis and a separate supervised planning session. Total: 20 formative points.
Keep the teacher key closed while answering. Entry check: identify an independent
variable, dependent variable and controlled variable in a stated experiment.

## Supplied conditions

These are synthetic exercise data. Each run uses the same standardised catalase
preparation, peroxide concentration and volume, pH, mixing and collection method.
Reactants are equilibrated separately before mixing. The stated gas volumes
have been compared at common recorded gas temperature and pressure. Each run
collects oxygen for exactly 60 s. No actual laboratory work is established by
this description. Catalase is the catalyst for 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂.

| Assay temperature (°C) | Trial 1 O₂ (mL) | Trial 2 O₂ (mL) | Trial 3 O₂ (mL) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 15 | 8.4 | 9.0 | 8.7 |
| 30 | 16.8 | 17.4 | 17.1 |
| 45 | 21.0 | 20.4 | 20.7 |
| 60 | 1.8 | 2.1 | 1.5 |

1. Identify the independent variable and the measured outcome. Calculate the
   mean collected volume and repeat range (maximum minus minimum) for each
   temperature. Then calculate the interval-average collected-gas rate,
   reporting to three decimal places in mL s⁻¹. Show one complete calculation.
   (6 points)
2. Which tested temperature gives the highest mean rate? Explain why this
   does not prove an exact universal optimum or directly measure an initial
   rate. What further time data would help with an initial-rate question?
   (4 points)
3. A leak removes the same proportion of gas in every repeat. Explain why
   consistent repeats cannot by themselves detect or remove this error.
   Name a useful apparatus check. (3 points)

## A separate matched-control comparison

In another synthetic exercise, all assays and collected gas are at the same
recorded conditions. Equal preparation volumes are tested for 60 s at 25 °C.
The heated preparation had a specified prior heat treatment, then was cooled
to 25 °C before testing. Reagent concentrations, pH, dilution and mixing match.
The blank replaces the enzyme preparation with the same volume of vehicle.

| Preparation | Trial 1 O₂ (mL) | Trial 2 O₂ (mL) | Trial 3 O₂ (mL) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Untreated | 19.8 | 20.4 | 20.1 |
| Heated, then cooled | 3.0 | 3.6 | 3.3 |
| No-enzyme blank | 1.2 | 1.2 | 1.5 |

4. Give a bounded conclusion about the heat treatment. Explain why matching
   assay temperature matters, what the blank checks, and why these gas data
   alone do not uniquely prove permanent unfolding. Do not automatically
   subtract this blank from the first temperature series. (5 points)
5. Replace a confounded plan that changes temperature and tissue mass together
   with a fairer one. Explain why equal tissue mass from different sources
   does not guarantee equal active enzyme. (2 points)

## Teacher-reviewed practical follow-up

Plan separate temperature, pH and substrate-concentration tests using the same
selected enzyme. For each, specify the varied range, measurement interval,
preparation controls, appropriate blank/control, repeated measurements and
uncertainty checks. Do not assume one factor's controls automatically fit the
others; for example, changing pH can also change the assay solution.

Use a teacher-approved procedure and risk assessment before handling reagents
or heated materials. Record actual enzyme source/preparation, volumes and
concentrations, assay and gas conditions, time-resolved measurements, repeats,
apparatus checks and departures from the plan. Submit raw records and a qualified
evaluation. These are planning requirements, not an unsupervised laboratory
method or a claim that the named experiments have been conducted.
