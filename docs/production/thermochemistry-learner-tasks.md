# Thermochemistry transfer tasks: student sheet

Original formative draft, 3 October 2026. Use after the relevant lesson, or
select tasks individually. Allow about 35 to 45 minutes for all six, then
review reasoning. Timing is a planning choice. These are supplied exercises,
not actual experimental observations or official NESA questions. Record your
working and explanation before opening feedback. The teacher has a separate key.

Entry check: distinguish a system from its surroundings, balance an equation,
calculate an amount in moles and convert joules to kilojoules. If those steps
are uncertain, resolve them before interpreting a molar energy.

## 1. State the energy basis (5 points)

For the hypothetical gas-phase equation 2A(g) + B(g) → 2C(g), the enthalpy
change is −120 kJ for the written stoichiometric quantities at the specified
conditions. A mixture contains 1.50 mol A and 1.50 mol B. Assume the limiting
reagent reacts completely and only pressure-volume work occurs at constant
pressure.

1. Identify the limiting reagent and the amount of C formed.
2. Calculate the system enthalpy change and quote the heat released as a
   positive magnitude.
3. Write the reverse equation and give its enthalpy both for the written
   quantities and per mole of C consumed. Explain the difference in basis.

## 2. Use a cycle without inventing a pathway (5 points)

Estimate ΔH for CH₄(g) + Cl₂(g) → CH₃Cl(g) + HCl(g). Use the following
supplied mean gas-phase bond enthalpies: C−H 413, Cl−Cl 243, C−Cl 338 and
H−Cl 432, all in kJ mol⁻¹ of bonds. Treat these rounded values as the exercise
data and report the integer estimate per mole of methane reacted.

1. Show which bond terms remain after cancelling unchanged bonds.
2. Calculate the estimate and classify its sign.
3. Explain why a cycle drawn through separated atoms does not prove that
   the reaction actually follows that route or identify its activation barrier.
   State why the numerical estimate is approximate.

## 3. Match reference states and coefficients (5 points)

Use these supplied standard formation enthalpies at 298.15 K and standard
pressure 1 bar: C₂H₅OH(l) −277.7, CO₂(g) −393.5 and H₂O(l) −285.8,
all in kJ mol⁻¹. O₂(g) is the elemental reference form here.

1. Calculate ΔH for C₂H₅OH(l) + 3O₂(g) → 2CO₂(g) + 3H₂O(l).
   Report to one decimal place per mole of ethanol reacted.
2. State the enthalpy for the exactly reversed written quantities.
3. Assess: “Every allotrope of an element has zero standard formation
   enthalpy, and standard means the temperature must be 25 °C.” Correct
   both parts, specifying what the table actually states.

## 4. Separate overall bookkeeping from biology (5 points)

For the stated reference conditions and phases, the supplied hypothetical
enthalpy for C₆H₁₂O₆(s) + 6O₂(g) → 6CO₂(g) + 6H₂O(l) is −2810 kJ
for the written quantities. Reverse exactly this overall equation.

1. Give the reversed equation and enthalpy.
2. Explain why this reversal does not establish that photosynthesis runs
   every respiratory biochemical step backwards.
3. Assess whether the number alone determines the sunlight energy required,
   usable ATP yield or whether the reverse process can occur. Name another
   kind of evidence needed for one of those questions.

## 5. Calculate heat using the balanced capacity (5 points)

Synthetic data: a mixture contains 0.0300 mol H₂SO₄ and 0.0500 mol NaOH.
Assume full acid neutralisation capacity, sufficient equilibration for the
supplied model, H₂SO₄ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O, and no heat contributions
other than the modelled reaction. These assumptions do not claim both protons
were entirely free before mixing. The total solution mass is 125 g, specific
heat capacity 4.18 J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹ and temperature rise 5.2 °C. Assume negligible
vessel heat and external exchange.

1. Identify the limiting neutralisation capacity and amount of water formed.
2. Calculate solution heat gained and the modelled reaction enthalpy per mole
   of water. Retain guard digits; report the final molar estimate to 2 s.f.
3. Explain why taking the smaller number of acid/base moles without the
   balanced coefficients would give the wrong denominator here.

## 6. Bound a heat comparison (5 points)

In three synthetic complete monoprotic neutralisations at the same initial
temperature, each run forms 0.0500 mol water. Each solution has mass 100.0 g
and c = 4.18 J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹. Attribute the measured heat to the reaction within
the exercise model, neglecting vessel heat and external exchange. The supplied
temperature rises are X: 7.1 °C, Y: 6.3 °C and Z: 5.8 °C.

1. Calculate the three modelled enthalpies per mole of water to 2 s.f.
   Rank their heat-release magnitudes.
2. Independent equilibrium measurements in the same solvent at the same
   temperature give Ka(X) = 1.0 × 10⁻⁵, Ka(Y) = 1.0 × 10⁻³ and
   Ka(Z) = 1.0 × 10⁻⁴. Rank the acid strengths and compare the two orders.
3. A reviewer rejects X solely because its modelled enthalpy is more negative
   than a supplied dilute comparison of −57.3 kJ mol⁻¹. Is that single
   comparison sufficient to reject the data? Give one useful check before
   drawing a conclusion from a real run.

For a supervised practical follow-up, propose how a teacher-approved procedure
would record initial temperatures, both reagent amounts, solution mass,
temperature versus time, apparatus heat and mixing/dilution controls. Explain
which source of uncertainty each measurement addresses. Completing this
sheet does not count as conducting the published neutralisation practical.
