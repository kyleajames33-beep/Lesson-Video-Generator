Entry check: If water warms during combustion, what is the sign of its heat
change? What further assumptions connect this to reaction heat?

________________________________________________________________________

Synthetic open-calorimeter data: 200.0 g of water warms by 12.5 °C while the fuel
container loses 0.500 g. For calculation, assume the fuel is ethanol, all mass
loss is ethanol combusted, c(water) = 4.18 J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹, and neglect vessel heat
capacity and heat exchange with the room. Use M(ethanol) = 46.07 g mol⁻¹. Report
the molar estimate to three significant figures.

1. Calculate heat gained by the water, ethanol amount and inferred reaction
   enthalpy per mole under the supplied assumptions. Explain the sign.
2. Compare its magnitude with the NIST WebBook standard combustion value for
   liquid ethanol, −1367.6 ± 0.3 kJ mol⁻¹ (Chao and Rossini, 1965). Calculate
   100 × |your estimate| ÷ 1367.6 using guard digits, then report to three
   significant figures. This is a magnitude comparison, not a demonstrated
   energy-capture efficiency. Explain why the open experiment and standard
   complete-combustion reference may differ in heat capture, reaction completeness
   and product states.
3. Predict separately how omitted vessel heating and evaporation counted as
   fuel consumption affect the estimated magnitude. Which measurement would
   help distinguish these explanations?
4. Explain what repeat runs help estimate and why repeating the same setup
   cannot by itself remove a systematic heat loss.
