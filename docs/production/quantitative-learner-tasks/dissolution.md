Synthetic data: 5.00 g of KCl dissolves completely in 100.0 g of water. The
solution temperature falls by 2.4 °C. For this calculation, assume
c(solution) = 4.18 J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹, negligible vessel heat capacity and heat
exchange, and a common initial temperature for salt and water. Use
M(KCl) = 74.55 g mol⁻¹. Report the final enthalpy estimate to two significant
figures because the temperature change has two significant figures.

1. State the mass represented by the solution heat model. Calculate
   q(solution), q(dissolution), salt amount and enthalpy per mole of dissolved KCl.
2. Explain the signs and classify the dissolution as endothermic or exothermic.
3. Recalculate using only water mass. Compare the unrounded results. Does this
   change the sign? Explain why rounding the answers first can obscure a
   comparison of model choices.
4. A reference value concerns dissolution at infinite dilution and a specified
   temperature. Is it automatically an exact prediction for this finite
   concentration experiment? Name the conditions that need comparison.
5. If some solid remains, can you divide by the full added salt amount and
   claim an enthalpy per mole actually dissolved? Explain the missing evidence.

procedure. Record salt identity, masses, complete/incomplete dissolution,
initial temperatures, temperature versus time, apparatus and measurement
resolution. Justify the heat-capacity model and choose a reliable reference
with appropriate salt form, concentration and temperature. Evaluate heat flow
