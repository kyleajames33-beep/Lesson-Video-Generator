Synthetic data: 75.0 mL of 0.200 mol L⁻¹ HCl is mixed with 50.0 mL of
0.500 mol L⁻¹ NaOH. Both solutions start at the same temperature; the mixture
warms by 1.60 °C. Assume complete neutralisation, density 1.00 g mL⁻¹,
c(solution) = 4.18 J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹, negligible vessel heat capacity and no heat
exchange with the room. Assume the observed heat change is attributable to
neutralisation within this exercise model. Report to three significant figures.

1. Write the molecular and net ionic equations. Determine the limiting
   reactant, water amount and remaining excess hydroxide amount.
2. Calculate heat gained by the solution and reaction enthalpy per mole of water.
   Explain why using the larger reactant amount as the denominator is wrong.
3. A second synthetic run uses 50.0 mL of fully dissolved 0.0100 mol L⁻¹
   Ca(OH)₂ and 40.0 mL of 0.0200 mol L⁻¹ HCl. Its temperature rise is 0.12 °C.
   Use the same heat-model assumptions and report to two significant figures.
   Write a balanced equation and calculate water amount before inferring
   enthalpy per mole of water. Do not assume one mole of base always gives
   one mole of water.
4. Name two observations or controls needed before applying this model to real
   measurements. Could a temperature reading rounded to the nearest 1 °C
   resolve the second run's change meaningfully? Explain.
