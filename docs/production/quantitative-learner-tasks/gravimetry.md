Synthetic practice data: a 250.0 mL sample gives 0.3842 g of BaSO₄. For the first
calculation, assume pure, dry BaSO₄ and complete selective recovery of sulfate.
Use Ba = 137.33, S = 32.06 and O = 15.999 g mol⁻¹. Report the concentration to
four significant figures.

1. Calculate the weighing-form molar mass, sulfate amount and sulfate
   concentration in mol L⁻¹. Show the ratio and volume conversion.
2. Subsequent net masses after repeated preparation are 0.3842, 0.3841 and
   0.3842 g. The supplied method accepts variation within 0.0002 g. Do these
   masses meet that criterion? What do they fail to prove?
3. In a different run, some precipitate passes through the filter and some
   unrelated material remains in the collected solid. Explain the separate
   effect of each error. Can you determine the net concentration bias from
   these statements alone?
4. A student proposes heating every precipitate until it glows, then using the
   original precipitate formula in the calculation. Identify the missing
   evidence needed before accepting this plan.
