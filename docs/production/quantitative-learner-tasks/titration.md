## A. Residual acid and aliquots

Synthetic data: a 1.000 g tablet is assumed to contain CaCO₃ and inert material
only. It reacts completely with 50.00 mL of 0.5000 mol L⁻¹ HCl. The remaining
acid is quantitatively transferred and diluted to 250.0 mL. A 25.00 mL aliquot
requires 8.20 mL of 0.1000 mol L⁻¹ NaOH. Assume a validated preparation means
dissolved CO₂ and other components do not consume titrant, and acid is not lost
in preparation. Use M(CaCO₃) = 100.09 g mol⁻¹. Use three significant figures
as the exercise's reporting convention for mass and percentage; retain guard
digits first.

1. Write the carbonate/acid and residual-acid/base equations.
2. Calculate initial HCl, HCl remaining in the aliquot, HCl remaining in the
   entire prepared solution, HCl consumed by the tablet and CaCO₃ amount.
3. Calculate CaCO₃ mass and percentage by mass. Justify the dilution/aliquot
   multiplier and distinguish it from reaction stoichiometry.
4. Evaluate these proposed shortcuts: subtract aliquot HCl directly from all
   initial HCl; equate consumed HCl moles to carbonate moles; compare the
   calculated tablet composition to a product label that has not been supplied.

## B. Conductivity evidence

A separate synthetic HCl/NaOH titration starts with 25.00 mL of unknown HCl
and uses 0.1000 mol L⁻¹ NaOH. Temperature is held constant. The supplied signal
has already been corrected for dilution to a common volume basis. It is a
schematic signal in arbitrary units, not an absolute conductivity measurement.

| Added NaOH (mL) | Volume-corrected signal (arbitrary units) |
| --- | --- |
| 0.00 | 425.9 |
| 5.00 | 366.0 |
| 10.00 | 306.1 |
| 15.00 | 246.2 |
| 20.00 | 186.3 |
| 25.00 | 126.4 |
| 30.00 | 176.0 |

1. Describe the trend and estimate the equivalence volume from the intersection
   of the pre- and post-equivalence linear trends. Calculate acid concentration,
   but explain why the sparse synthetic table does not justify a precise endpoint
   uncertainty or four-significant-figure concentration in a real experiment.
2. Explain the falling and rising trends in terms of which ions replace or
   accumulate. Why is the signal nonzero near equivalence?
3. Would this exact V-shaped strong-acid/strong-base explanation automatically
   describe a weak-acid titration? Identify a limitation.
