# Quantitative practice: separate teacher key

Draft marking guide. Check science and curriculum scope before using these
original formative tasks. Do not give this file to students during their first
response. The suggested scores describe evidence in this package only and are
not official NESA marking criteria or validated mastery thresholds. Accept
equivalent reasoning, clearly labelled units and alternative valid methods.
Apply follow-through for a numerical slip where the later reasoning remains sound.

## Empirical and molecular formulas: 10 points

Entry: empirical subscripts give the simplest whole-number ratio; molecular
subscripts give atom counts in one molecule.

On a 100 g basis, n(N) = 30.45/14.01 and n(O) = 69.55/16.00. Their ratio is
approximately 1:2, supporting NO₂ under the supplied approximate-data assumption.
M(NO₂) = 46.01 g mol⁻¹; 92.02/46.01 = 2. The inferred molecular formula is N₂O₄.
The proposed formula gives nitrogen percentage 100 × 14.01/46.01 ≈ 30.4499%,
consistent with the rounded composition. Formula inference assumes a single
molecular substance and suitable data; it does not prove sample purity or
establish identity/structure uniquely from composition alone.

For the second ratio, multiply both terms by two: 1:1.5 becomes 2:3, giving N₂O₃.
Rounding one term alone alters the supported ratio.

| Evidence | Points |
| --- | --- |
| States a valid basis and converts both masses to mole amounts | 2 |
| Shows unrounded ratio and justified NO₂ inference | 2 |
| Computes empirical mass, multiplier and N₂O₄ | 2 |
| Bounds identity/purity claim and states the molecular-substance assumption | 2 |
| Justifies N₂O₃ and checks the nitrogen percentage/precision | 2 |

Diagnostic follow-up: If a student obtains a formula by rounding each mole
amount, change the sample basis and ask whether the inferred ratio should change.
If identity is overclaimed, ask which additional evidence would distinguish
possible substances or a mixture. No practical hours are established here.

## Gravimetry: 10 points

Entry: Ba²⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) → BaSO₄(s), with a 1:1 solid-to-sulfate mole ratio.

Retain M(BaSO₄) = 233.386 g mol⁻¹ for calculation. Sulfate amount is
0.3842/233.386 ≈ 0.001646199858 mol. Divide by 0.2500 L to obtain
0.006584799431 mol L⁻¹, reported as 6.585 × 10⁻³ mol L⁻¹ (4 s.f.).

The successive masses span 0.0001 g and meet the supplied 0.0002 g criterion.
They do not prove purity, selective recovery, complete recovery or chemical
identity. Losing target precipitate lowers its measured mass and inferred
concentration; retained unrelated material raises the apparent mass. Their net
bias cannot be determined without magnitudes and chemical evidence. A heating
plan needs a validated preparation and known stable weighing-form composition;
some solids change composition or decompose when heated.

| Evidence | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Correct equation, ratio and weighing-form molar mass | 2 |
| Correct amount, volume conversion and reported concentration | 3 |
| Applies constant-mass criterion and bounds its meaning | 2 |
| Separates error directions and leaves unknown net bias unresolved | 2 |
| Requires a validated preparation/actual weighing form | 1 |

Diagnostic follow-up: Ask whether a constant mass of contaminated solid is
sufficient. Review the learner's proposed raw-data table separately. This is a
Year 11 extension, not a named Module 2 gravimetry obligation. The Year 12 Module
8 point allows investigations and/or data processing; this sheet alone does not
establish a full Year 12 teaching sequence or course practical completion.

## Combustion: 10 points

Entry: q(water) is positive; equating q(reaction) to −q(water) needs the stated
heat model and reaction/amount assumptions.

q(water) = 200.0 × 4.18 × 12.5 = 10450 J (guard digits).
n(ethanol) = 0.500/46.07 ≈ 0.010853049707 mol.
ΔH estimate = −10.450/n = −962.863 kJ mol⁻¹, reported as
−963 kJ mol⁻¹ (3 s.f.). The magnitude ratio is
100 × 962.863/1367.6 ≈ 70.4053%, reported as 70.4%.

The [NIST ethanol compilation](https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/cbook.cgi?ID=C64175&Mask=2)
lists the selected liquid standard-combustion value, −1367.6 ± 0.3 kJ mol⁻¹,
from Chao and Rossini (1965). This is a comparison reference, not a claim that
the open experiment measures standard enthalpy. Check the complete balanced
combustion reaction, product phases and standard/reference temperature when
using a value with real data. Warm water measures captured heat; other energy
can heat apparatus or escape. Incomplete combustion, product states and fuel
evaporation can also matter. The ratio is not a measured capture efficiency
because fuel consumption and reaction completeness were assumed.

Omitted vessel heating generally makes the water-only magnitude too small.
Evaporated fuel counted as reacted makes inferred moles too large and magnitude
per mole too small, under the stated separate-error assumptions. Useful evidence
includes a calorimeter heat-capacity measurement, temperature-versus-time loss
assessment and an appropriate evaporation control. Repeats estimate spread;
consistent systematic heat loss can persist.

| Evidence | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Heat, fuel amount and signed per-mole estimate with units | 3 |
| Magnitude comparison using guard digits | 1 |
| Explains reference/experimental differences and bounds efficiency claim | 2 |
| Separate error directions and discriminating measurements | 3 |
| Distinguishes repeats from systematic-error removal | 1 |

Diagnostic follow-up: Hold water heat fixed and increase the inferred fuel mass.
Ask how the per-mole magnitude changes. Require actual supervised results and
source/condition comparison before claiming investigation completion.

## Neutralisation: 10 points

Entry: compare stoichiometrically available H⁺ and OH⁻, then find water from the
limiting amount. Use the balanced reaction, not a universal cV shortcut.

HCl(aq) + NaOH(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l).
H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l).
n(HCl) = 0.0750 × 0.200 = 0.0150 mol;
n(NaOH) = 0.0500 × 0.500 = 0.0250 mol.
HCl limits; n(water) = 0.0150 mol and excess OH⁻ = 0.0100 mol.
Model solution mass = 125 g; q(solution) = 125 × 4.18 × 1.60 = 836.0 J.
ΔH per mole water = −0.8360/0.0150 ≈ −55.7333 kJ mol⁻¹, reported as
−55.7 kJ mol⁻¹ (3 s.f.). Dividing by excess base amount counts material that
did not form water and understates the reaction magnitude per mole water.

Ca(OH)₂(aq) + 2HCl(aq) → CaCl₂(aq) + 2H₂O(l).
n(Ca(OH)₂) = 0.000500 mol, providing 0.00100 mol OH⁻;
n(HCl) = 0.000800 mol, so HCl limits and water amount is 0.000800 mol.
Model mass = 90.0 g; q(solution) = 45.144 J.
ΔH estimate = −0.045144/0.000800 = −56.43 kJ mol⁻¹,
reported as −56 kJ mol⁻¹ (2 s.f.). One mole of this base can form two moles of
water when sufficient acid is available; here acid availability sets the amount.

Useful checks include matching initial temperatures, known concentrations,
mixing/dilution contribution, calibrated temperature response, vessel heat
capacity and exchange with the room. A thermometer rounded to the nearest
1 °C cannot meaningfully resolve a 0.12 °C rise. Neither precision displayed in
the exercise nor repetition removes that limitation.

| Evidence | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Equations, limiting reagent, water and excess hydroxide | 3 |
| Heat model and first signed molar estimate | 2 |
| Calcium reaction, stoichiometric capacities and second estimate | 3 |
| Real-model checks and measurement-resolution limitation | 2 |

Diagnostic follow-up: Change only base volume and ask when water amount stops
increasing. Do not infer acid strength from these calorimetry numbers alone.
An actual investigation and reliable matched reference comparison remain pending.

## Dissolution: 10 points

Entry: cooling gives negative q(solution); dissolution heat is its opposite in
the specified heat model.

Solution model mass is 105.0 g, using added salt plus water.
q(solution) = 105.0 × 4.18 × (−2.4) = −1053.36 J.
q(dissolution) = +1053.36 J; n(KCl) = 5.00/74.55 ≈ 0.067069081154 mol.
ΔH estimate = +1.05336/n ≈ +15.7055976 kJ mol⁻¹, reported as
+16 kJ mol⁻¹ (2 s.f.), endothermic under the model.

Water-only heat gives +14.957712 kJ mol⁻¹ (reported +15 kJ mol⁻¹).
Its unrounded estimate is lower by about 4.76% relative to the solution-mass
model. The sign is unchanged. Compare unrounded values before interpreting
rounded differences. Both approaches still depend on the supplied approximation
that the relevant heat capacity is 4.18 J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹.

A reference needs matching salt identity/form, temperature, concentration and
enthalpy convention. Infinite-dilution enthalpy is not automatically the exact
finite-concentration value. With incomplete dissolution, full added amount is
not established as actual dissolved amount; reaction attribution and heat-model
mass may also need revisiting. Do not promote the exercise assumption to a
validated experiment result.

| Evidence | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Correct model mass, heat magnitudes and opposite signs | 3 |
| Mole amount and reported endothermic molar estimate | 2 |
| Water-only calculation and bounded comparison | 2 |
| Matched reference conditions and incomplete-dissolution limitation | 3 |

Diagnostic follow-up: Reverse the temperature-change sign while keeping the
mass and amount fixed. Ask which heat signs change and why. Actual measurements
and appropriate reference evidence are required for practical follow-up.

## Back and conductometric titration: 12 points

Entry: the titre measures residual reagent in the specified aliquot, not the
sample directly.

CaCO₃(s) + 2HCl(aq) → CaCl₂(aq) + CO₂(g) + H₂O(l).
HCl(aq) + NaOH(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l).
Initial HCl = 0.05000 × 0.5000 = 0.02500 mol.
Aliquot residual HCl = 0.00820 × 0.1000 = 0.000820 mol.
Entire residual HCl = 0.000820 × (250.0/25.00) = 0.00820 mol.
Consumed HCl = 0.02500 − 0.00820 = 0.01680 mol.
n(CaCO₃) = 0.01680/2 = 0.008400 mol.
Mass = 0.840756 g and mass percentage = 84.0756%, reported as
0.841 g and 84.1% under the stated 3 s.f. exercise convention. Subtraction
precision and uncertainty propagation should not be replaced by blindly taking
the smallest significant-figure count from all inputs.

The factor ten scales representative aliquot to the full homogeneous solution.
The factor two comes from reaction stoichiometry; they perform different jobs.
Subtracting only aliquot acid overstates consumed acid. Equating consumed acid
and carbonate amounts ignores the coefficient and doubles inferred carbonate.
No conclusion about a missing product label is supported. Real measurements
also need completion, residual-acid specificity and preparation-loss validation.

The supplied pre-equivalence line is approximately S = 425.9 − 11.98V;
the post-equivalence line is approximately S = 126.4 + 9.92(V − 25), with
V in mL and S in arbitrary units. Their intersection is about 25 mL.
The central concentration estimate is (0.1000 × 0.025)/0.02500 ≈
0.100 mol L⁻¹. Give credit for the estimate with an appropriate uncertainty
qualification, not invented four-significant-figure endpoint accuracy.
Only two points constrain the rising branch here, one at the apparent minimum;
the table is insufficient for a credible real endpoint-uncertainty analysis.

Before equivalence, high-contribution H⁺ is consumed and replaced by Na⁺;
after equivalence excess OH⁻ accumulates together with added Na⁺. Near equivalence,
Na⁺ and Cl⁻ remain, so signal is nonzero. Weak-acid dissociation and neutralisation
alter the species/contributions; this exact strong-acid V-shape explanation is
not a universal conductivity model. The supplied signal is already dilution
corrected and schematic; raw readings need separate treatment.

| Evidence | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Equations and initial/aliquot acid amounts | 2 |
| Aliquot scaling, consumed acid and carbonate stoichiometry | 3 |
| Mass/percentage and unsupported-label qualification | 2 |
| Data-derived endpoint, concentration and uncertainty limitation | 2 |
| Ion explanation, nonzero signal and weak-acid limitation | 3 |

Diagnostic follow-up: Halve aliquot volume while keeping the prepared mixture
unchanged. Ask which measured amount changes and which full-sample result should
stay the same. Review actual titration data and learner procedures separately
before claiming investigation outcomes or practical completion.
