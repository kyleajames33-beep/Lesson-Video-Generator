# Enzyme models: script and visual revision, 9 October 2026

The user reported that the unrelated atom was small and blurry, and that
the narration sounded like directions without an engaging explanation.

## Revised teaching approach

The opening starts with lactose in milk and lactase in the small intestine.
The lesson asks how a protein can form a selective partnership, then uses
the two models to explain binding and shape change. It defines active site
and substrate, connects induced fit to positioning catalytic groups and
transition-state stabilisation, and explains why binding alone does not
guarantee catalysis. The comparison question asks learners to interpret a
diagram. The conclusion returns to the milk example and the limits of models.

All nine narration segments were recorded afresh with Simon v4, including
the prompt and answer. Provider alignment was rebuilt before cue placement.
The script contains approximately 560 words. The revised timeline lasts
233.53 seconds, compared with 281.6 seconds previously. There is a two-second
practice gap and a half-second visual tail after speech. No speech speed-up
is applied.

The milk example and products are supported by
[NIH MedlinePlus: LCT gene](https://medlineplus.gov/genetics/gene/lct/).
Substrate-induced conformational change and selective catalysis are supported
by [primary enzyme-specificity research](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21548586/).
The existing curriculum mapping is retained. This is a model explanation,
with practical investigations remaining separate.
