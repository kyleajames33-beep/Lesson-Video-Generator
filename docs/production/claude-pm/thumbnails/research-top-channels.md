# Thumbnail research: what ten successful education channels do

Written 14 October 2026 for HSCScience (NSW HSC Year 12 Chemistry and Biology, 8 to 10 minute painted-plate explainers). Purpose: replace the rejected generated concepts in `concepts/` with designs grounded in what works, without looking like a generic AI channel.

Method and honesty note. Channel descriptions below are pattern summaries from browsing each channel's uploads over the years, not a measured audit; check current uploads before copying a detail. Claims about why things work are sourced where a source exists (linked). Several widely repeated "statistics" (for example "faces lift CTR by 20 to 30 percent") could not be traced to an official source and are not used.

## What YouTube itself says

- Custom thumbnails: 1280 x 720, 16:9, minimum width 640, JPG/PNG/GIF, under 2 MB; they must follow Community Guidelines ([YouTube Help: video thumbnails](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72431)).
- Test and compare: up to three thumbnails (or titles) per video; the winner is the option with the highest **watch time share**, not the highest click-through rate, because a click that leads to a quick exit is not a win. Tests take a few days to two weeks. Make variants meaningfully different, test older videos first, and upload at 720p or higher, or every variant is downscaled to 480p ([YouTube Help: Test and compare](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714)).

The watch-time rule matters for us: a thumbnail that over-promises loses even if it is clicked. Accurate curiosity wins.

## Industry practice

- **MrBeast production document** (leaked 2024; authenticity unconfirmed). Treats CTR, average view duration and average view percentage as the three key numbers, decides title and thumbnail before filming, and says the first minute must meet the thumbnail's promise ([Creator Handbook summary](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/leaked-document-allegedly-reveals-mrbeasts-secrets-to-youtube-success-the-key-takeaways/), [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20240917-mrbeast-youtube-production)).
- **Paddy Galloway** (strategist for MrBeast and others). Packaging is where large gains are; a clear curiosity gap and "visual story" beat a plain description; a video should feel familiar within its niche with a twist; keep a branding format that works even when copied; do not judge on metrics alone ([Colin and Samir notes](https://www.colinandsamir.com/resources/the-new-rules-of-youtube-from-paddy-galloway)). Reported checklist: obvious, clean, passes a glance test, about four or five words at most ([Content Packaging 101](https://rpn.beehiiv.com/p/content-packaging-101)).
- **Veritasium, "Clickbait is Unreasonably Effective"** (2021). Derek Muller separates fair attention-getting packaging from misleading packaging, shows his own re-packaging raising views, and argues good packaging is part of good teaching because nobody learns from a video they never open ([Veritasium](https://www.veritasium.com/videos/2021/8/17/we-need-to-talk-about-clickbait), [Gigazine summary](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20210830-clickbait-effective)).

## The ten channels

Chosen as five "curiosity" science leaders whose packaging is studied across the industry, two curriculum-series channels (closest to our playlist structure) and three student-revision channels (closest to our audience's search behaviour).

### 1. Kurzgesagt
Look: one large illustrated subject, flat and saturated on a dark or single-colour field; zero to three words; the art style itself is the brand (recognised before the name is read). Very consistent across a decade ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurzgesagt)).
Why it works: instant style recognition in a crowded feed, and one object reads at any size.
**Lesson:** our painted plates are a distinctive style. Make the plate the hero, large and alone, rather than a small picture next to a headline.

### 2. Veritasium
Look: a real object or experiment, often with Derek, high contrast, zero to three words that create a question the image cannot answer alone.
Why it works: concrete object plus open question; he openly tests and re-packages (above).
**Lesson:** pair one real object with a short question that only the video resolves, and keep the promise true.

### 3. 3Blue1Brown
Look: black background, one striking visual lifted from the actual animation, sometimes a few plain sans words; series use chapter numbers ("Chapter 3") ([3Blue1Brown about](https://www.3blue1brown.com/about)).
Why it works: the thumbnail is an honest preview of the video's visual explanation; dark ground makes the drawing glow.
**Lesson:** show the actual teaching visual (a coded helix, a colour strip) rather than decoration. A dark ground is a valid contrast option for one series.

### 4. Vsauce
Look: Michael's face beside one object, with a short question; titles are questions.
Why it works: the question is the hook; the object grounds it.
**Lesson:** a question in two to four words ("Which way?") is a hook a student can answer only by watching.

### 5. Mark Rober
Look: big physical object, before/after or comparison, bright colours, one arrow or circle pointing at the payoff.
Why it works: the change is visible; the arrow tells the eye where to look in a fraction of a second.
**Lesson:** show a visible change (pink to blue, one cell to two) and, if needed, one pointer, never several.

### 6. CrashCourse
Look: a fixed series frame per course (course name, episode number, host or illustration, a course colour). Every Biology episode is recognisably Biology ([CrashCourse](https://thecrashcourse.com/)).
Why it works: viewers working through a course can find "the next one"; the frame signals a trustworthy curriculum.
**Lesson:** a consistent frame with subject colour and episode marker. Most relevant precedent for a syllabus playlist.

### 7. TED-Ed
Look: an illustrated still from the animation; the artist changes per lesson, but the red brand mark and calm composition stay; often a question title.
Why it works: illustration stands out from photo-heavy feeds, and the brand anchor makes varied art read as one channel.
**Lesson:** our plates already vary by lesson; one fixed brand anchor (a corner mark and subject colour) can unify them.

### 8. Amoeba Sisters
Look: their own hand-drawn cartoon characters with the topic word set large ("Mitosis", "DNA Replication"), bright flat colour.
Why it works: students search by topic; the topic word in the thumbnail confirms "this is the one my teacher meant", and the drawn characters are unmistakably theirs.
**Lesson:** for revision students, the syllabus word itself can be the text, in our own hand.

### 9. Tyler DeWitt
Look: plain backgrounds, a large topic or question in bold type, sometimes a simple drawn element; friendly and unglossy.
Why it works: matches high-school chemistry search intent; nothing over-promises.
**Lesson:** a calm teacher brand can still be bold if the type is big and the subject is named.

### 10. The Organic Chemistry Tutor
Look: very plain, text-led frames naming the exact topic; packaging is utilitarian.
Why it works: enormous catalogue matched precisely to search terms; viewers arriving from search care about the topic match more than spectacle.
**Lesson:** our ceiling is higher than plain text, but the floor is clear: the topic must be readable at sidebar size.

## What is different for exam-revision students

- Most arrive by **search** ("le chatelier temperature", "binary fission") or from a **playlist**, not from a browse feed. The title carries the exact search term; the thumbnail must confirm the topic at a glance and add one reason to choose us.
- **Syllabus recognition** builds trust: a subject colour, "Module 5" and an episode number tell a Year 12 student this belongs to their course.
- **Clarity beats shock.** Fake shock faces and red arrows signal entertainment, not study, and would fail the watch-time test if the video is calm.
- **Sidebar and mobile sizes** (roughly 168 px wide in the suggested column) decide whether the topic is read at all. One object, very high contrast, at most four words.

## Implications carried into the concepts

1. One focal painted object, large; the plate is the brand (Kurzgesagt, TED-Ed).
2. A real visible change or contrast from the lesson (Mark Rober, Veritasium).
3. Zero to four words: a true question or the syllabus term, never both crowded together (Vsauce, Amoeba Sisters).
4. Show the actual teaching visual, not a decorative scene (3Blue1Brown).
5. A series frame: subject colour, module and episode marker in a fixed place (CrashCourse).
6. Readability test at 320 px and 168 px; anything unreadable there is removed.
7. The promise must be kept in the first minute, because Test and compare rewards watch time share (YouTube Help; MrBeast document).
8. Our own hand: hand-lettering and warm paper, used boldly, are the anti-AI signal; no gradients, stock 3D or fake faces.
