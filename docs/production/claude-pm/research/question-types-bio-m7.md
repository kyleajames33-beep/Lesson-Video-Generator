## 1. Pathogen, adaptation and transmission

**Given and asked:** organism, symptoms, spread data, map or a possible pathogen; identify the agent and explain how an adaptation moves it to a new host. **Verbs:** identify, explain, analyse, compare.

**Method:** Name a specific pathogen and classify it accurately. Separate an adaptation for entry or survival inside a host from one that helps transmission between hosts. State the physical route, such as water, droplets, vector or contaminated tools, then connect the adaptation to that route and to the observed pattern. Use the supplied map or table, including exceptions, before drawing a conclusion.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Naming a broad group instead of a specific pathogen, or giving an entry adaptation without explaining the entry process ([2019 Q31](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2019)); treating an in-host survival feature as a transmission feature ([2025 Q22(b)](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2025)); and using a map of vector presence as if it alone established where disease cases occur ([2022 Q30](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2022)).

## 2. Immune defence and vaccination

**Given and asked:** barrier diagram, rapid test, immune-cell flowchart or antibody-response graph; explain a defence or compare primary and secondary responses. **Verbs:** identify, explain, describe, analyse, evaluate.

**Method:** Begin at the correct defence level: physical/chemical barriers, then innate responses, then antigen-specific adaptive responses. In a cell flowchart, follow antigen presentation, lymphocyte activation and antibody production using the labelled cells. For secondary exposure, link memory cells formed after first exposure to the faster, larger response to the same antigen. For a vaccination claim, use actual graph values and the stated schedule before judging it.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Repeating the barrier name without explaining how it stops entry ([2025 Q21](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2025)); narrating the graph without explaining memory-cell formation and action ([2025 Q28](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2025)); and presenting innate and adaptive defences as unrelated lists instead of an interacting response ([2024 Q32](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2024)).

## 3. Prevention and outbreak control

**Given and asked:** an outbreak setting, surveillance data or control proposal; choose and justify measures that break transmission. **Verbs:** outline, justify, evaluate, propose.

**Method:** Identify the infectious agent's route and vulnerable contacts. Match each action to a named link in the chain of transmission, then say how it reduces movement of the agent to the next host. Include the limits that the stimulus exposes, such as sanitation, case reporting, vectors or contact patterns. Give a conclusion supported by the data rather than a generic list of health advice.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Naming a cholera control without connecting it to contaminated water and the route of spread ([2020 Q21](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2020)); repeating a suggested procedure without showing how it protects the rest of the population ([2025 Q29(b)](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2025)); and judging a vaccination program without drawing on both its stated information and graph data ([2023 Q30](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2023)).

## 4. Microbiology investigation design

**Given and asked:** a proposed antimicrobial test or microbe-handling practical; improve the method or assess reliability. **Verbs:** design, justify, evaluate.

**Method:** Name the independent variable, measurable outcome and controlled variables. Use a comparison control, replicate trials, safe handling and a specified way to measure the inhibition or growth. Explain why each control improves validity, why repeats address reliability and how the safety measure blocks the exposure route.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Saying only that repeats improve reliability without linking repeats to consistency or outlier detection ([2024 Q22](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2024)); and giving a method without controls, repeats or a safe, measurable outcome ([2022 Q26](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2022)).
