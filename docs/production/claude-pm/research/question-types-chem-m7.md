# Chemistry Module 7: HSC question types and classic mistakes

Research scope: NESA 2019 to 2025 Chemistry exam papers, guidelines and question-level marking feedback for the 2017 syllabus. The references in the ranking are confirmed Section II questions, so counts are lower bounds and omit multiple-choice items. Ranking weighs repeat appearances and the marker feedback; NESA does not publish a difficulty rank. ## 1. Structures, names and isomers

**Given and asked:** names, molecular formulas, structural diagrams or a reaction clue; name an organic compound, draw its structure or distinguish isomers. **Verbs:** identify, draw, name, distinguish, justify.

**Method:** Identify the longest parent chain containing the main functional group, number it to give that group and multiple bonds the required locants, then place branches and name the class. Check every carbon has four bonds. To classify isomers, compare molecular formula first, then the connectivity and functional group. When a chemical test is given, use the observed reaction to justify the group rather than naming a structure by appearance alone.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Students missed functional isomers such as an aldehyde and ketone pair ([2019 Q21](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2019), [2023 Q21](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2023)); drew condensed rather than full structural formulas when all bonds were required ([2021 Q24](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2021)); or overlooked a substituent or functional group in a skeletal structure ([2025 Q24](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2025)).

## 2. Organic reaction pathways

**Given and asked:** a starting compound, several products, reagents or a pathway; infer missing structures, choose conditions and justify a synthesis. **Verbs:** predict, construct, outline, design, justify.

**Method:** Mark the changing functional group at every arrow. Identify whether each step is addition, substitution, oxidation, dehydration, esterification or polymerisation. Write the reagent and condition beside the specific change, draw the product with all atoms and bonds accounted for, then test whether the next arrow is possible. For a school-laboratory proposal, match apparatus and hazard controls to the actual reagent and process.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Listing reagents without conditions or tests for the product ([2019 Q34](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2019)); failing to work backward from an ester to the acid and alcohol ([2021 Q26](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2021)); and confusing addition with substitution when a C=C bond reacts ([2025 Q24(b)](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2025)).

## 3. Molecular forces and physical properties

**Given and asked:** homologous-series structures, boiling-point or solubility data, a polymer comparison; explain a trend or rank properties. **Verbs:** explain, compare, analyse.

**Method:** Identify relevant intermolecular forces for each molecule. Separate changes within one series, such as increasing dispersion forces with chain length, from differences between functional groups, such as hydrogen bonding. Connect the force to the energy needed to separate molecules or to solvent compatibility. When the question gives a graph, quote its direction and relevant values.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Students said only that molecules were polar without naming the actual forces ([2020 Q32](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2020)); described a trend without the corresponding cause ([2019 Q32](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2019), [2022 Q24](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2022)); or treated polymer softening as breaking covalent chains instead of separating chains ([2025 Q28(b)](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2025)).

## 4. Alcohol fuels and reaction evidence

**Given and asked:** combustion or fermentation data, temperature change or fuel comparison; calculate enthalpy or explain a reaction outcome. **Verbs:** calculate, compare, explain, evaluate.

**Method:** Balance the chemical equation, identify limiting quantities and use the measured water or solution mass in q = mcΔT. For molar enthalpy, divide by moles of fuel consumed with the correct sign and units. For incomplete combustion, connect soot to inadequate oxygen for the fuel amount. When comparing fossil fuel and biofuel, use the stated data and distinguish energy yield from environmental effects.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Using the mass of ethanol instead of the water heated ([2019 Q23](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2019)); attributing soot to the fuel name without considering oxygen supply ([2020 Q24](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2020)); or giving a fermentation equation with a nutrient as a reactant when it was not consumed ([2021 Q25](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2021)).

## 5. Polymer structure and properties

**Given and asked:** a monomer, polymer section or copolymer; draw a repeat unit, infer a monomer or explain material properties. **Verbs:** draw, identify, explain, compare.

**Method:** For addition polymerisation, open the monomer double bond, keep substituents on their original carbon atoms and show continuation at both ends. For a copolymer, track all atoms in the repeat section to recover each monomer. For property questions, connect chain structure and intermolecular attraction to chain movement.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Leaving a C=C in an addition-polymer repeat unit, omitting continuation bonds or giving carbon the wrong number of bonds ([2024 Q22](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2024)); and omitting atoms when reconstructing a copolymer monomer ([2025 Q28(a)](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2025)).
