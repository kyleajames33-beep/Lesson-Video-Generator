## 1. Titration and titration curves

**Given and asked:** titres, an analyte and standard, a reaction or curve; find concentration, decide which titre to use, identify equivalence or half equivalence, or explain conductivity. **Verbs:** calculate, determine, explain, analyse, justify.

**Method:** Write a balanced reaction first. Reject an anomalous or rough titre where warranted, average concordant results, convert volume to litres, calculate moles in the standard aliquot and apply the reaction ratio. For a curve, read the equivalence volume from the graph and identify what the half equivalence point means for a weak acid. For a conductivity curve, account for the particular ions removed and added on either side of equivalence.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Students used NaOH as though it were a primary standard or chose an unsuitable indicator ([2020 Q28](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2020)); included an outlier in the average or lost the aliquot dilution ([2021 Q35](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2021), [2022 Q32](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2022)); or named the low-conductivity point without explaining the ions ([2019 Q24](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2019), [2024 Q34](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2024)).

## 2. pH, Ka, pKa and excess reactant

**Given and asked:** concentration, pH, Ka, Kb, pKa, acid strength or two mixed solutions; find a new pH or compare acids. **Verbs:** calculate, compare, determine, explain.

**Method:** Identify strong or weak species before choosing an equation. For strong acid/base mixing, calculate each reactant's moles, subtract using the balanced ratio, divide the excess by total volume, then use pH or pOH. For a weak acid/base, write its equilibrium with water and a Ka/Kb expression, then use an ICE table or the supplied pH. State and check any approximation. Use pKa = -log Ka and remember that smaller pKa means stronger acid.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Students omitted the acid/base equilibrium equation, lost the connection between Ka and Kb, or used an unshown approximation ([2019 Q27](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2019)). They did not carry through an excess-reactant mole calculation before finding pH ([2024 Q29](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2024)). In comparing weak acids, they failed to use the given pH to calculate hydrogen-ion concentration or relate Ka and pKa correctly ([2024 Q28](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2024), [2025 Q26(b)](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2025)).

## 3. Acid/base model and proton-transfer equations

**Given and asked:** species or reaction, sometimes an unfamiliar conjugate acid/base; classify it, show proton transfer or predict a salt's effect on pH. **Verbs:** identify, explain, write, justify.

**Method:** Mark the proton donor and acceptor, write formulas and charges before balancing, and show the conjugate pair after one proton has moved. For a weak acid or base use a reversible arrow. A salt may act through ion hydrolysis, so identify the ion that reacts with water and write that reaction.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Treating an acidic salt as an Arrhenius acid without showing its reaction in water ([2023 Q22](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2023)); missing ionic charges or the correct water species ([2023 Q24](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2023)); and identifying a basic salt without the methanoate hydrolysis equation ([2021 Q23](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2021)).

## 4. Buffer and mixed-solution reasoning

**Given and asked:** a conjugate pair or pH response after an acid/base addition; explain resistance to pH change or calculate an equilibrium value. **Verbs:** explain, calculate, analyse.

**Method:** Identify both buffer components. Show the conjugate base consuming added H3O+ and the weak acid consuming added OH-. Account for any initial neutralisation in moles before calculating the final ratio or equilibrium. If a graph supplies pH and equivalence data, read it before using a formula.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Ignoring that OH- remains approximately stable in the stated buffered system, or putting water into the equilibrium expression ([2022 Q34](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2022)); treating pH change as neutralisation alone when dilution also contributes ([2025 Q34(b)](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2025)).

## 5. Neutralisation calorimetry

**Given and asked:** masses or volumes, initial and final temperatures, specific heat capacity; find molar enthalpy or assess a procedure. **Verbs:** calculate, determine, evaluate.

**Method:** Find total solution mass and the temperature rise, calculate q = mcΔT, calculate moles of the limiting reacting species, then ΔH = -q/n in kJ mol-1 for the stated reaction. Say which heat exchanges bias the measured value when asked.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Using only one solution's mass, not the combined mass, or using the wrong initial temperature ([2022 Q26](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2022)); and mixing the signs or units while sequencing n, q and ΔH ([2020 Q36](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2020), [2024 Q36](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2024)).
