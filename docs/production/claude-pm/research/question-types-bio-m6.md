## 1. Evaluate a biotechnology in its stated context

**Given and asked:** a biotechnology, crop, animal or medical case with data; judge benefits, risks, biodiversity or social effects. **Verbs:** evaluate, assess, analyse, justify.

**Method:** Name exactly what the technology changes and how. Use the supplied case evidence to explain a benefit and a cost or limitation, including any effect on genetic diversity, population or ecosystem. Make a clear judgement that matches the question and its evidence. Distinguish effects on one organism from effects on the gene pool or biodiversity.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Giving generic claims about biotechnology without explaining its actual effect on biodiversity ([2019 Q24](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2019)); describing Bt cotton's higher yield without linking the expressed trait to insect mortality and crop survival ([2022 Q29](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2022)); and making a judgement about society without identifying specific technologies and their effects ([2024 Q34](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2024), [2025 Q30(b)](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2025)).

## 2. Mutation type, inheritance and protein effect

**Given and asked:** DNA sequence, chromosome image or exposure; classify a mutation and trace its consequence. **Verbs:** identify, explain, compare, distinguish.

**Method:** Name point or chromosomal change from the scale of the evidence, then specify substitution, deletion, duplication or non-disjunction where supported. Trace any altered base through codon, amino acid, protein shape and function. State whether the mutation is somatic or in a germ-line cell before claiming inheritance. Use a karyotype to identify the actual chromosome difference.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Confusing somatic and germ-line changes ([2019 Q22](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2019), [2024 Q23](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2024)); naming a chromosome condition without showing the missing sex chromosome and non-disjunction process ([2019 Q25](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2019)); and jumping straight from DNA change to phenotype without explaining the altered protein ([2023 Q35](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2023)).

## 3. Gene-pool change and population size

**Given and asked:** allele-frequency data, maps or a population event; explain genetic drift, gene flow, mutation or selection. **Verbs:** explain, compare, analyse, evaluate.

**Method:** Define the population and its gene pool first. Identify the event: incoming alleles for gene flow, chance changes for drift, or differential survival and reproduction under selection. Compare allele counts with frequencies, and explain why the same number of immigrant alleles can have a greater percentage effect in a small population. Trace consequences for variation and later generations.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Using gene flow and drift interchangeably ([2022 Q32](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2022)); naming a bottleneck without linking the bushfire and survivors to the changed gene pool ([2023 Q32](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2023)); and saying the gene pool causes gene flow, or failing to compare the effect of the same four incoming alleles on different-sized populations ([2025 Q34](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2025)).

## 4. Recombinant DNA and cloning process

**Given and asked:** target gene, recipient organism or desired product; describe the sequence of a genetic technology and its outcome. **Verbs:** describe, outline, explain, assess.

**Method:** Identify and isolate the gene, cut a vector and insert the gene with ligase, introduce the recombinant DNA into cells, amplify or select cells and show where the product or heritable trait appears. For a transgenic animal, explain why germ-line modification is needed if offspring should inherit the trait. For cloning, distinguish copying a gene from copying a whole organism and state which genome supplies the clone's nuclear DNA.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Naming recombinant DNA without the ordered molecular steps or the means of transferring it into the recipient ([2023 Q31](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2023), [2025 Q30(a)](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2025)); and claiming a clone has the changed donor phenotype when the donor genome was not altered ([2024 Q25](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2024)).
