## 1. Homeostasis and feedback

**Given and asked:** feedback loop, temperature or glucose graph, response of an organism; explain the adjustment and outcome. **Verbs:** explain, interpret, analyse, compare.

**Method:** Name the internal variable and its normal range or set point, the receptor detecting a change, the control centre and the effector. State what the effector does and how that action reverses the initial deviation. For glucose, distinguish insulin and glucagon and follow their effects on target tissues. For temperature, link vasodilation, sweating, shivering or altered posture to heat gain or loss. Use the graph's axes, key and units when the case depends on data.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Treating a change in the external environment as the monitored internal variable ([2019 Q29](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2019)); naming thermoregulatory features without the causal path to body-temperature change ([2021 Q29](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2021), [2025 Q24](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2025)); and confusing the hormone and tissue responses on a glucose graph ([2020 Q31](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2020)).

## 2. Epidemiology and disease-pattern evidence

**Given and asked:** case data, a disease-rate graph or a study design; interpret a pattern or judge validity and reliability. **Verbs:** analyse, evaluate, justify, calculate.

**Method:** Read the population, time period and measurement before interpreting. Incidence counts new cases over a period; prevalence counts existing cases at a point or period. For a graph, plot and label the axes carefully, cite data values and distinguish interpolation from extrapolation. For a study, check sampling, controls, confounding variables and repeatability, then explain precisely how each affects validity or reliability. Support the final judgement with the stimulus.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Repeating general definitions of validity and reliability without judging the study's actual sample and design ([2021 Q31](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2021), [2023 Q27](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2023)); failing to use the supplied case details to justify design changes ([2025 Q32](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2025)); and drawing a trend line or extrapolating without maintaining the graph's scale ([2024 Q29](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2024)).

## 3. Non-infectious disease and prevention

**Given and asked:** a cause, exposure or health campaign with evidence; explain a disease pathway or assess prevention. **Verbs:** explain, assess, evaluate, propose.

**Method:** Identify the environmental, genetic, nutritional or lifestyle factor given, then trace it to a biological change and the disorder. For a campaign, name the target exposure or behaviour, explain how the action reduces risk, and use outcome data to judge effectiveness. A claim about a consumer health product needs a definite evidence-based judgement against the graph and the claim's wording.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Naming a program without explaining why it should change disease incidence ([2019 Q23](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2019)); proposing a general campaign that was not specific to the named exposure and disorder ([2024 Q24](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2024)); and making an indefinite judgement without using the plotted data and product statement ([2025 Q25](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2025)).

## 4. Assistive technology mechanism

**Given and asked:** visual or hearing disorder, technology and outcome data; explain how a device restores function or compare treatments. **Verbs:** explain, compare, evaluate.

**Method:** Start with the damaged structure and the function lost. Name where the technology acts and how its physical action restores a usable signal to the sensory pathway. For comparisons, give a relevant similarity and difference in mechanism or outcome, then use the stated patient data to judge effectiveness.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Saying only that a cochlear implant improves hearing without explaining its direct stimulation of the auditory nerve or using data for all age groups ([2024 Q35](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2024)); and comparing LASIK with another technology only by cost or recovery while omitting the treatment's effect on refraction and the named visual disorder ([2025 Q26](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2025)).
