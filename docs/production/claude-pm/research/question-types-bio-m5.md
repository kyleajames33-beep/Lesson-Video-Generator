## 1. Pedigree and inheritance reasoning

**Given and asked:** pedigree, phenotypes, allele data or an offspring cross; infer a mode of inheritance or calculate a probability. **Verbs:** determine, explain, justify, predict, compare.

**Method:** First identify whether the pattern is dominant, recessive, sex-linked, codominant or incomplete dominance from the named individuals and given constraints. Assign clear uppercase and lowercase allele symbols, write parental genotypes, show gametes and a Punnett square, then turn the genotypic ratio into the requested phenotype or probability. Cite particular pedigree individuals when justifying a mode.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Naming recessive inheritance without showing why the given parents and offspring require it ([2020 Q26](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2020)); failing to use pedigree individuals as evidence ([2021 Q24](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2021), [2025 Q31(a)](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2025)); and giving a genotype ratio when the question asked for phenotype probability ([2022 Q22](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2022)).

## 2. Reproduction and fertilisation comparisons

**Given and asked:** named organisms, flower structure, life cycle or fertilisation data; compare sexual and asexual reproduction, or internal and external fertilisation. **Verbs:** compare, distinguish, explain, identify.

**Method:** Choose one comparison axis at a time. Sexual reproduction involves gamete formation and fusion, while asexual reproduction does not. Internal and external describe where gametes fuse, not whether reproduction is sexual. For a flower, identify the shown structures precisely. Use a specific organism and explain how its process produces offspring or variation.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Treating self-pollination as asexual because it can involve one plant, or treating external fertilisation as asexual ([2024 Q21](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2024)); substituting parental care for fertilisation location ([2025 Q27](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2025)); and saying budding occurs without connecting the bud's separation to a genetically identical organism ([2025 Q22](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2025)).

**Technology variant:** A comparison of artificial pollination and artificial insemination requires a clear similarity and difference along the same comparison axis. Marker feedback warns against writing two separate descriptions or substituting IVF for insemination ([2025 Q23](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2025)). The site covers this in L05.

## 3. Meiosis and variation

**Given and asked:** chromosome drawings, allele locations or two division diagrams; explain the resulting gametes and genetic variation. **Verbs:** model, compare, explain, analyse.

**Method:** Pair homologous chromosomes by size and gene loci, with one chromosome from each parent. Place alleles at matching loci, then show separation of homologues in meiosis I and sister chromatids in meiosis II. Explain how crossing over and independent assortment change allele combinations. If comparing mitosis and meiosis, name process events as well as final cell number and chromosome number.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Drawing a homologous pair as duplicate chromosomes from the same parent ([2020 Q28](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2020)); mentioning crossing over without its link to genetic diversity and population survival ([2024 Q33](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2024)); and comparing only the outcomes of cell division when the question asks for the processes ([2025 Q33(a)](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2025)).

## 4. From DNA to protein

**Given and asked:** a DNA template, codon chart, mutation or transcript; complete an mRNA or amino-acid sequence, then explain a protein effect. **Verbs:** transcribe, determine, explain, compare.

**Method:** Establish which DNA strand is the template and its direction. Write complementary mRNA using U instead of T, group into codons, then use the mRNA codon chart to find amino acids. For a mutation, locate the changed codon and explain any change in protein sequence, shape or function. Distinguish DNA replication from transcription when comparing processes.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Using DNA bases directly in the codon chart ([2025 Q31(b)](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2025)); omitting the roles of mRNA and tRNA in polypeptide synthesis ([2024 Q30](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2024)); and completing mRNA without applying the complement rules ([2023 Q21](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2023)).
