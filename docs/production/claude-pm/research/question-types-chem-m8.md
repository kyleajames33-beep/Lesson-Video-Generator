## 1. Ion analysis, Ksp and sequential tests

**Given and asked:** a mixture, possible ions, solubility data, precipitate amount or observations; choose tests, identify ions or calculate Ksp. **Verbs:** identify, calculate, outline, analyse, justify.

**Method:** Write balanced ionic equations with states. Use solubility rules and the provided Ksp values to predict each precipitate. To distinguish ions, plan tests in an order that removes an interfering species, and give both positive and negative observations. For quantitative Ksp, subtract any ions removed in the solid before finding equilibrium concentrations. Convert moles to mol L-1 and raise each concentration to its stoichiometric power. For precipitation from mixed solutions, account for dilution before comparing Q with Ksp.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Students listed a test without an observation or did not make the sequence distinguish the candidate ions ([2021 Q30](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2021), [2023 Q30](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2023)). In Ksp work, they used moles where concentrations were required or failed to subtract the ion that had precipitated ([2023 Q34](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2023), [2024 Q32](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2024)).

## 2. Combined spectroscopy

**Given and asked:** mass, IR and NMR spectra plus possible structures or a formula; determine and justify a structure. **Verbs:** identify, propose, deduce, justify, analyse.

**Method:** Establish the molecular mass and formula first. Use the mass-spectrum parent ion and fragments as constraints, IR to identify or exclude functional groups, carbon-13 NMR for distinct carbon environments and proton NMR for hydrogen environments, relative areas and splitting if supplied. Draw candidate structures and check every piece of evidence against each candidate. State which signal supports each structural feature rather than naming a compound alone.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Calling the isotope peak the parent ion ([2020 Q21](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2020)); recognising an IR peak but not linking it to the relevant part of a candidate molecule ([2021 Q29](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2021)); and proposing a structure without reconciling all supplied spectra ([2023 Q36](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2023), [2024 Q38](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2024)).

## 3. Calibration and quantitative analysis

**Given and asked:** absorbance or concentration graph, titres, raw measurements; find an unknown amount, concentration or percentage. **Verbs:** plot, determine, calculate, evaluate.

**Method:** Put the independent variable on the horizontal axis, label units, use a scale that spreads the data, plot and draw a best-fit line. Read an unknown concentration by interpolation, then account for all dilution and sample-volume steps. In titration, use the average concordant titre and the balanced reaction ratio. In gravimetric work, connect precipitate mass to analyte moles through the formula, then calculate the original sample percentage.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Poor scale or failure to show the reading from a calibration line ([2023 Q31](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2023), [2024 Q25](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2024)); including an outlier or losing the unit conversion in a titre calculation ([2023 Q32](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2023)); and proposing a procedural change without saying how it reduces a specific error ([2025 Q23](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2025)).

## 4. Chemical synthesis and design

**Given and asked:** alternative processes, hazards, yields or materials; select a process or design a pathway and evaluate it. **Verbs:** assess, evaluate, justify, design.

**Method:** Identify the target product and compare each process using the supplied evidence: yield, waste, hazard, conditions, energy use and availability of inputs. Support a definite judgement with the specific data in the stimulus. For a synthesis route, show the transformation at each step and the reagent, condition and safety control that makes it feasible.

**Marker-reported mistakes:** Listing general green-chemistry slogans without processing the actual alternatives ([2024 Q31](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2024)); choosing controls that do not address the stated chemical hazard ([2025 Q30(a)](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2025)); and describing a route without identifying the reaction type or connecting each stimulus clue to the proposed substance ([2025 Q37](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2025)).
