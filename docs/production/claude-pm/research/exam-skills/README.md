# HSC exam skills research pack

Research for the [exam skills series plan](../../../programme/exam-skills-series-plan.md), checked 19 October 2026. The pack uses NESA's glossary, the 2017 Biology and Chemistry syllabuses, recent HSC papers, marking guidelines and marking feedback. The file on verb frequency states its exact paper range and counting method.

## Top 10 takeaways for video design

1. **Teach students to read the whole demand.** Underline the command, circle the marks and mark every content word and constraint. NESA feedback repeatedly identifies answers to a nearby question, or answers that miss one requested part. See [common faults](common-faults.md).
2. **Teach verbs through a real question.** NESA says the glossary is a guide and that the specific question matters. A rigid answer template can miss a required diagram, equation, context or judgement. See [glossary](glossary.md).
3. **Make an explanation show the link.** Show the cause, what it changes and the resulting effect. Marker feedback repeatedly distinguishes this from naming or describing a process. See [common faults](common-faults.md) and [marking criteria](what-markers-reward.md).
4. **Align a comparison around the same feature.** A side-by-side pair makes the similarity or difference clear. Separate lists for A and B are a recurring weak pattern. See [common faults](common-faults.md).
5. **Build judgements from evidence.** In assess, evaluate and justify tasks, show the decision, the relevant criteria and reasons from the case. The top band often gives a fuller, better supported judgement than the next band. See [marking criteria](what-markers-reward.md).
6. **Use the stimulus to do work.** A good response reads the relevant table, graph, diagram or procedure, then explains what its details mean for the question. Copying it without interpretation is a recurring fault. See [common faults](common-faults.md).
7. **Show calculation steps students can trace.** Put the equation, substitution, intermediate result and final answer in order; retain units and the requested precision. NESA guidelines award partial credit for relevant working in several questions. See [marking criteria](what-markers-reward.md).
8. **Do not promise an automatic consequential mark.** The audited public guidelines specify question-level criteria and sometimes reward intermediate steps, but do not state a universal carried-forward-error rule. See [marking criteria](what-markers-reward.md).
9. **Make graph choices visible.** Teach scale, labelled axes and units, plotted points, a suitable line of best fit, and reading or extending that line for the required inference. See [marking criteria](what-markers-reward.md) and [common faults](common-faults.md).
10. **Diagnose the practical problem before proposing a fix.** Reliability concerns the consistency of repeat observations; validity concerns whether the method and data address the intended target; accuracy concerns closeness to the true value. Use NESA's exact current definitions and distinguish the 2017 wording where needed. See [science definitions](vra-definitions.md) and [marking criteria](what-markers-reward.md).
