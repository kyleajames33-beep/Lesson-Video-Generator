# NESA key words in HSC Chemistry and Biology papers, 2021 to 2025

## Scope and counting method

- Source set: the official NESA Section II written-response papers for Chemistry and Biology in each year from 2021 through 2025, 10 papers in all. The links below go directly to the PDFs. Section I multiple-choice items are excluded because their stem verbs do not consistently name a written-answer skill.
- Unit of count: one numbered question or lettered/roman subpart with its own mark allocation. An unlettered question uses its question total. A verb used twice in one unit is counted once. A unit with two distinct NESA key words appears in both verb rows, each against the full unit mark allocation. These counts cannot be added as if they were independent marks.
- The count uses the headwords on the [NESA glossary of key words](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/hsc/student-guide/glossary). Inflected forms count when they direct the student to perform that action. For example, “What can be deduced” is filed under *deduce*, and “How do ... account for” under *account for*. An infinitive counts when it names the required answer action, such as “complete the table to compare”.
- Only words in the student-facing answer demand count. A word in background, a data caption or a method described in the stimulus does not count. Formatting or source-use instructions, such as “use the data”, “include an equation” and “show working”, are excluded. So are non-glossary commands such as *draw*, *plot*, *state*, *determine*, *design*, *name* and *complete*. The table does not infer a glossary verb from “how”, “why”, “what” or “to what extent”.
- Marks are copied from the printed question or subpart allocation in the paper, including units whose task continues on the next page. Question references below contain numbers and part labels only; no question wording is reproduced.

## Coverage limits

- The following glossary headwords had no explicit written-response command in this paper set: *apply*, *appreciate*, *clarify*, *classify*, *contrast*, *critically analyse/critically evaluate*, *define*, *demonstrate*, *distinguish*, *examine*, *extract*, *extrapolate*, *interpret*, *investigate*, *propose*, *recall*, *recommend*, *recount*, *summarise* and *synthesise*. This is a zero within these 10 Section II papers, not a claim about all NESA exams.
- The five-year range covers the 2017 syllabus era. It does not cover 2019–2020 or earlier papers. There is no trend estimate or weighting by the number of students who attempted each question.
- Some explicit commands are outside the NESA glossary and therefore outside the frequency table. For example, *determine* appears in these papers. An answer-writing video should still teach students to read those commands in context.
