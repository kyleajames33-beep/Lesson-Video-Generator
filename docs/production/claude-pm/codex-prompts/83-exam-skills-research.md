# Brief 83: research pack for the HSC exam skills video series

Read `00-standing-rules.md` and `docs/production/programme/exam-skills-series-plan.md` first. The owner wants videos that teach students how to read, understand and answer HSC science questions. Many students know the content but lose marks on how they write the answer. Build the research Claude will design from.

1. **NESA glossary of key words.** Find the official NESA glossary (the "Glossary of key words" used across HSC syllabuses). Quote every verb and its definition word for word, with the source link and its date. Mark which verbs appear in the 2017 Chemistry and Biology syllabuses.
2. **How often each verb is used.** Using the public NESA HSC Chemistry and Biology exam papers from the last 5 to 8 years, count which verbs appear and at what mark values. For example: "explain" at 3 marks, N times; "evaluate" at 7 marks, N times. Record question numbers only; don't copy the questions.
3. **What markers reward.** From the public NESA marking guidelines for those years, summarise the patterns behind each verb's mark bands:
   - what separates the top band from the next for "explain", "assess", "evaluate", "justify", "compare" and "analyse";
   - how calculation marks are split (working, units, significant figures, consequential marks);
   - how graph and practical questions are marked.
   Paraphrase in your own words and cite each paper and question. Short quotes only, under 15 words.
4. **Common faults.** From NESA "HSC marking feedback" (Notes from the Marking Centre) for Chemistry and Biology, list the most common answer-writing faults. Group them by skill, give a short paraphrase and source for each, and say how often each recurs across years.
5. **Validity, reliability and accuracy.** Give NESA's current definitions as used in the science syllabuses, word for word, with a source. Teachers and websites often disagree on these, so record NESA's version exactly.

**Output:** `docs/production/claude-pm/research/exam-skills/`, containing:
- `glossary.md`
- `verb-frequency.md`, with a table
- `what-markers-reward.md`
- `common-faults.md`
- `vra-definitions.md`
- a `README.md` with the top 10 takeaways for video design

Australian spelling, no em dashes. Hand in to `status.md`.
