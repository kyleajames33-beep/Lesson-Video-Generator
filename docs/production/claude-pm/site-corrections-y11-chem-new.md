| FA1 L08 | "technetium-99m (half-life about 6 hours) clears a patient within a day." | Physical half-life alone does not establish biological clearance. | FA1 L11 | "The p-block contains Groups 13–18" and "s-block contains Groups 1 and 2." | Helium sits in Group 18 but has 1s2 configuration and belongs to the s-block; L13 later gives the exception. | Add the helium exception where the blocks are first defined. | [RSC helium data](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/2/helium) |
| FA1 L15 | "So you can rank any two atoms by size from their grid positions." | Period and group directions compete for diagonal pairs. | Use the grid trend within a period or group; use measured radii or more detailed reasoning for diagonal comparisons. | [RSC periodic-trend teaching](https://edu.rsc.org/cpd/how-to-teach-chemistry-through-electrostatics-at-post-16/4018759.article) |

Compare lattice calculations or a broader dataset while naming the confounding variables. | Page's own table. |
| FA1 L24 | "metals conduct in every state, and covalent substances conduct in none." | The claim ignores conducting covalent networks, including graphite and graphene; ordinary aqueous metal is not a preserved metallic-bonding state. | Metals conduct as solids and melts; many covalent substances do not, while graphite and graphene are exceptions. | [RSC carbon bonding](https://edu.rsc.org/cpd/how-to-teach-structure-and-bonding-of-carbon-at-14-16/4020312.article) |
| FA1 L26 | "diamond ... very high melting point (around 3550 °C)" and a graphite "melting point" at normal conditions. | Carbon sublimes at ordinary pressure; melting requires high pressure. | Describe sublimation under standard pressure, and give pressure if quoting a melting temperature. | [RSC carbon data](https://periodic-table.rsc.org/element/6/C) |
| FA2 L01 | QB q06: "Why must a balanced equation use the smallest whole-number coefficients rather than fractions or decimals?" | FA2 L16 | L16:284,292: "Under the NESA 2025 definition, STP is ... 100 kPa ... 273.15 K." | The syllabus says STP but does not define those numerical conditions. | Attribute 100 kPa and 273.15 K to the chosen IUPAC STP convention and state it explicitly in each calculation. | [IUPAC STP](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/S06036); [NESA FA2](https://curriculum.nsw.edu.au/learning-areas/science/chemistry-11-12-2025/content/year-11/faf0876f2f) |
| FA3 L03 | L03:276: "the thiaminase was never decomposed; it continued to destroy their thiamine, and they grew weaker and died despite eating." | The historical account does not establish this single molecular cause for Burke and Wills' deaths. | Present starvation and possible thiamine deficiency from poorly prepared nardoo without assigning a proven sole cause. | [State Library Victoria](https://ergo.slv.vic.gov.au/explore-history/land-exploration/exploration/burke-wills-aboriginal-guides) |
| FA3 L10 | L10:271: ionisation energy, atomic radius and electronegativity "all three describe the same loosely held outer electron." | Ionisation energy removes an electron from a gas-phase atom; electronegativity describes attraction of a bonding pair. | Explain the separate definitions, then relate their periodic trends to metal reactivity. | [IUPAC teaching material](https://old.iupac.org/didac/Didac%20Eng/Didac04/Content/CB03.htm) |
| FA3 L13 | L13:221: concentration and surface area both "pack more reacting particles into the space where collisions can happen." | Powder exposes more solid reaction sites; it does not increase the particle concentration of the solution. | More concentrated solution has more particles per volume. More finely divided solid presents more surface for collisions. | [OpenStax reaction rates](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry/pages/12-2-factors-affecting-reaction-rates) |
| FA3 L15 | L15:253,350: "An endothermic reaction absorbs heat ... Examples: ... dissolving NH4NO3." | Dissolution is a physical solution process with an enthalpy change, not automatically a chemical reaction. | Call it endothermic dissolution and distinguish it from an endothermic chemical reaction. | [OpenStax energy basics](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/5-1-energy-basics) |
| FA3 L18 | QB q02 keyed answer: the energy-profile peak is "The activation energy / activated complex (transition state)." | The peak denotes a transition state; activation energy is the vertical gap from reactants to the peak. | Key the peak as the transition state and label the vertical gap as activation energy. | [OpenStax collision theory](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry/pages/12-5-collision-theory) |
