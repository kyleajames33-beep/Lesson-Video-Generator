## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**L09 order:** Connect: predict what the hydroxyl group changes; Explain: hydroxyl polarity and hydrogen bonding; Represent: classify primary, secondary and tertiary alcohols; Respond: explain boiling-point trends; Explain: balance polar and non-polar regions; Feedback: compare structures using a complete IMF chain; Apply: classify, rank and explain alcohol properties.

**L09 key ideas:** 1. The -OH group is the defining feature of alcohols, it is small, polar, and capable of hydrogen bonding, and those three characteristics together explain almost every physical property that separates alcohols from the hydrocarbons they are built on.
2. The classification of an alcohol as primary, secondary, or tertiary is not just a naming convention, it determines every reaction the alcohol can undergo, from what oxidation products it gives to whether it undergoes elimination or substitution, and how quickly.
3. The boiling points of alcohols are dramatically higher than those of comparable hydrocarbons, and the reason is hydrogen bonding, but the variation in boiling point within the alcohol series, and between primary, secondary, and tertiary isomers, reveals additional layers of IMF subtlety.
4. Within a Primary Alcohol Series, Increasing Chain Length.
5. Comparing Primary, Secondary, Tertiary Isomers (same formula).
6. Short-chain alcohols dissolve in water because the -OH group H-bonds with water, but chain length is a limit, and understanding where solubility ends and why gives you the framework to predict solubility for every functional group in Module 7.
7. Problem: Classify each alcohol and arrange in order of decreasing boiling point: (a) 2-methylbutan-2-ol, (b) pentan-1-ol, (c) pentan-2-ol, (d) 2-methylbutan-1-ol.
8. For each of the following alcohols: (i) classify as 1°, 2°, or 3°; (ii) identify the C-OH carbon and count its carbon neighbours.

## 3. Number and science check

The four C5H12O examples classify as follows:2-methylbutan-2-ol tertiary; pentan-1-ol primary; pentan-2-ol secondary;2-methylbutan-1-ol primary. For this set decreasing boiling order is pentan-1-ol >2-methylbutan-1-ol > pentan-2-ol >2-methylbutan-2-ol. Classification counts carbon neighbours of the OH-bearing carbon. The primary/secondary/tertiary label alone is not a universal boiling-point rule. No numerical worked calculation.
