## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

Order: known precipitate as analyte measure; precipitation/digestion; filtration and washing; drying to constant mass; mole calculation; error direction.

Worked example:0.500 g sample gives 0.350 g BaSO₄; M(BaSO₄)233.39 and M(SO₄²⁻)96.06 g mol⁻¹. Page gives 0.00150 mol,0.144 g sulfate and 28.8%. Practice:0.500 g samples give 0.348,0.351,0.392 g precipitate; third was damp. Valid mean 0.3495 g gives 28.8%. Questions ask which reagent precipitates chloride, why damp solid reads high, why lost solid reads low, and why Ba²⁺ works for sulfate while Na⁺ does not. Aid: precipitate mass → precipitate moles → analyte moles → mass/percentage.

Full MC and short-answer sets remain in the linked pages. The read-only module8/question-bank-data.js supplements them.

## 3. Number and science check

0.350/233.39=0.00149964 mol; ×96.06=0.144055 g; /0.500×100=28.8110%. Valid mean gives 28.7699%, also 28.8% to three significant figures. Damp trial gives 32.2683%. Formula masses agree with Ba137.33, S32.06, O16.00, consistent with [CIAAW atomic weights](https://ciaaw.org/atomic-weights.htm). No numerical correction found.
