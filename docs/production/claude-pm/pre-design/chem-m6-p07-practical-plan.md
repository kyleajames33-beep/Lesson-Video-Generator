# Chemistry Module 6 P07 practical plan (draft for Claude's review)

**Aim and route.** Measure the titratable acidity of plain white vinegar and distinguish it from its pH. `C6-IQ3-06`: “conduct a chemical analysis of a common household substance for its acidity or basicity (ACSCH080)   , for example:” with the syllabus examples soft drink, wine, juice and medicine. This reproduces the [official 2017 syllabus](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/noindex/2025-03/chemistry-stage6-syllabus-word.docx) and the local ledger, including their unusual spacing before the comma. Site L19. Prerequisites: chem-m6-c16 and chem-m6-p06.

**School method.** Measure a 10.00 mL white-vinegar aliquot with a volumetric pipette into a conical flask. Add about 40 mL distilled water and two drops phenolphthalein. Titrate with a previously standardised 0.5000 mol/L NaOH solution to the first faint pink that lasts 30 s. Take one rough and three accurate titres. Separately calibrate a pH probe with pH 4.00 and 7.00 buffers and measure fresh undiluted vinegar. Record product label and density or state an assumed density. The measured titration result is total titratable acid expressed as ethanoic-acid equivalent, because other acids could contribute.

**Variables and results.** Independent: NaOH volume during titration. Dependent: persistent pink endpoint. Control vinegar aliquot, NaOH concentration, indicator dose and endpoint rule. Sample titres 16.55, 16.60 and 16.55 mL; mean = 16.5667 mL. A plausible undiluted pH is about 2.4 to 3.0, depending on vinegar. Do not equate pH directly with percentage acid.

**Analysis and working.** CH3COOH + NaOH → CH3COONa + H2O. n(NaOH) = 0.5000 × 0.0165667 = 0.00828335 mol. The 10.00 mL aliquot contains the same moles of titratable acid, so c = 0.00828335/0.01000 = 0.8283 mol/L as ethanoic acid. With M = 60.05 g/mol, mass in the aliquot = 0.00828335 × 60.05 = 0.4974 g. If measured density is 1.005 g/mL, vinegar mass = 10.00 × 1.005 = 10.05 g, giving 0.4974/10.05 × 100 = 4.95% w/w. State density source or measurement before reporting mass percentage.

**VRA.** Validity: plain, uncoloured vinegar permits a visible endpoint; standard base and a consistent endpoint measure total titratable acidity. Reliability: repeat concordant titres. Accuracy: standardise NaOH because it absorbs CO2, use volumetric glassware, eye-level burette readings and a measured density if reporting mass percentage. A pH probe measures current hydrogen-ion activity, not total acid capacity.

**Errors and fixes.** Pink overshoot raises the titre and reported acidity: add NaOH dropwise near endpoint. Unstandardised NaOH may be weaker than the label: standardise before use. Coloured vinegar masks the endpoint: choose plain vinegar or a pH/conductivity endpoint.

**HSC-style question idea.** A vinegar sample gives three concordant titres. Calculate its mass percentage as ethanoic acid equivalent, then explain why its probe pH does not directly give that percentage.
