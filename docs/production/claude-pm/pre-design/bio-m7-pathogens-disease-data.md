## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

Distinguish infectious disease from other disease; compare bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoans, helminths, viroids and prions. Site cases include TB, TMV, wheat rust, malaria, root-knot nematodes, PSTVd and BSE. Entry adaptations and transmission adaptations are separate comparisons. Incidence counts new cases over time; prevalence counts existing cases at a time. Practice includes classification error spotting and incidence calculation.

**L01 page sequence:** Define and classify pathogens.; Pathogen Classification Diagram.; Analysing COVID-19 Transmission Data.; Interpret disease data.; Studying the whole course, not just this lesson?

**L02 page sequence:** See why classification decides treatment.; Classify the seven pathogen types.; Error Spotting, Pathogen Classification Report.; Get inside the host.; Reach the next host.; Lock in the classification.

## 3. Number and science check

Worked malaria province: population 500,000; 4,000 new symptomatic diagnoses/year; 15,000 current carriers. 4,000/500,000 × 1,000 = 8 per 1,000/year; 15,000/500,000 × 100 = 3%. Town practice: 120/40,000 × 1,000 = 3 per 1,000/year. Both correct. Error: L01 attributes 11 million cases in 188 countries to WHO on 11 March 2020. WHO reported over 118,000 cases in 114 countries then. Source: https://www.who.int/europe/health-topics/coronavirus . L02 puts Pythium (an oomycete) under Protozoan, contradicting its own fungus-like description. Do not use it as a protozoan example. Genome sequencing within 10 days of the first case is not verified.

Bank calculations: 60/20,000 ×1,000=3 per1,000/year;450/20,000 ×100=2.25%; Region A200/500,000 ×1,000=0.4 versus Region B40/50,000 ×1,000=0.8. Correct. 

Historical amounts and teaching datasets are kept distinct from verified arithmetic. No chemistry constants are used. Unverified claims above are questions for sourcing, not approved factual statements.
