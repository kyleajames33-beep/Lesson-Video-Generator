**Teaching points in order:** 1. Two loops. One variable. 2. After a meal, insulin helps reduce the rise in blood glucose. 3. During fasting or exercise, glucagon helps increase blood glucose by signalling the liver to release glucose from stored glycogen. 4. Diabetes mellitus is the collective name for conditions in which blood glucose homeostasis fails.

## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes

Insulin follows rising blood glucose; glucagon follows falling blood glucose. The pancreas senses and secretes, while liver and other tissues change the glucose level. NESA feedback: 2020 Q31 reports confusion between hormone and target-tissue responses on a glucose graph. See [exam research](../research/question-types-bio-m8.md) for the official paper links and marker context.
