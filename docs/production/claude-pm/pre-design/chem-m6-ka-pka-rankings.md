# Module 6 pre-design: Ka, pKa and Acid Strength Rankings. How do Ka and pKa compare acid strength, conjugate bases and successive ionisations?

Sources: [L12 extract](../site-extracts/chem-m6-l12.md) and [site page](https://hscscience.com.au/subjects/chemistry/year12/module6/lesson12.sa). These are site teaching notes for pre-design.

## 1. Exact 2017 syllabus content

> calculate and apply the dissociation constant (Ka) and pKa (pKa = -log10 (Ka)) to determine the difference between strong and weak acids (ACSCH098)

Source: [NESA Chemistry Stage 6 Syllabus (2017)](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/science/chemistry-stage-6-2017), Module 6.

## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**Teaching order.**

**L12:**

1. Ka measures an acid's extent of ionisation at a given temperature; solution pH also depends on concentration.
2. Larger Ka means stronger acid; smaller pKa means stronger acid.
3. For a conjugate pair, Ka(HA) × Kb(A⁻) = Kw, so acid and conjugate-base strengths rank in opposite directions.
4. Polyprotic acids ionise in separate steps with Ka₁ much greater than later Ka values; the page relates this to removing H⁺ from increasingly negative ions.
5. The page applies Ka comparisons to cola and sparkling water, then to food, pharmaceutical and environmental analysis.

**Worked examples and numbers from the extracts.**

**L12:**

- For acids P, Q, R and S with Ka 1.3 × 10⁻², 8.4 × 10⁻⁵, 2.1 × 10⁻⁸ and 6.7 × 10⁻¹², the page ranks P > Q > R > S. Their pKa values are 1.89, 4.08, 7.68 and 11.17; conjugate-base Kb values are 7.7 × 10⁻¹³, 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁰, 4.8 × 10⁻⁷ and 1.5 × 10⁻³.
- For 0.0150 mol/L H₂CO₃ with Ka₁ 4.3 × 10⁻⁷ and Ka₂ 4.7 × 10⁻¹¹, the page gives pH 4.10, Ka₁/Ka₂ about 10⁴, Kb(CO₃²⁻) 2.1 × 10⁻⁴, and predicts basic Na₂CO₃ solution.
- A sports-drink example gives citric acid at 0.0200 mol/L (Ka₁ 7.4 × 10⁻⁴) and malic acid at 0.0150 mol/L (Ka 3.5 × 10⁻⁴), asking for separate pH contributions, a pKa ranking and dental-health assessment.

**Practice.**

**L12:** Multiple choice, acid ranking, conjugate-base calculations, polyprotic equations, environmental data analysis and error spotting. Representative question: “Phosphoric acid (H₃PO₄) is a triprotic acid. Ka1 = 7.5 × 10⁻³; Ka2 = 6.2 × 10⁻⁸; Ka3 = 4.8 × 10⁻¹³. (a) Write the three ionisation equations with correct arrow notation. (b) Calculate the pH of a 0.0800 mol/L H₃PO₄ solution, showing all steps including an assumption check.”

## 3. Number and science check

−log₁₀(1.3 × 10⁻²) = 1.89; −log₁₀(6.7 × 10⁻¹²) = 11.17. For a conjugate pair at 25 °C, Kb = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴/Ka. The listed rankings and carbonic-acid pH about 4.10 check from the stated values. Treat each acid separately in the sports-drink prompt; their shared solution is not two independent pH values. Source for flagged corrections: [Module 6 site corrections](../site-corrections-m6.md).

## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes

**L12:**

- Larger pKa means stronger acid; the page reverses this ranking.
- Ka × Kb = Kw for unrelated acids and bases; it applies to a conjugate pair.
- One Ka expression removes all protons from a polyprotic acid; each ionisation has its own equilibrium.
- Equal carbonic acid content means cola and sparkling water affect enamel equally; the page points to cola's additional acids.

These traps come from the linked site lessons. They are not attributed to NESA markers without a specific exam report.

## Site corrections for Brief 76

L12 mislabels a 46% child tooth-decay finding as enamel erosion and attributes it to cola. Ka values compare acid strength, but they do not alone establish the damage caused by two drinks. See [the exact Module 6 site fix draft](../site-fixes-draft-m6.md).

L12 exam worksheet 04 also miscalculates the model carbonic-acid pH: √(4.3 × 10⁻⁷ × 0.001) is 2.07 × 10⁻⁵ mol/L, giving pH 4.68, not 5.2. Do not turn its assumed carbonation concentration into a measured product claim.

L12 embedded Q8 applies clean-rain pH 5.6 as a universal natural-water threshold and attributes a water sample at pH 5.1 to acid deposition. Check alkalinity, anions and local baseline first; use the corrected model answer in the draft.
## Further site corrections for Brief 76

L12 worked carbonic-acid example compares Ka₁/Ka₂ as if it were the ratio of H⁺ supplied by the two stages. Recalculation gives a far smaller second-stage contribution. Another worked line mistakes Ka₁/c for percentage ionisation. See [the exact draft pairs](../site-fixes-draft-m6.md).
