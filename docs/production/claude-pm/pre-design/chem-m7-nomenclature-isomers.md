## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**L01 order:** Connect: decode the name; Explain: carbon and structural formulae; Represent: families and functional groups; Respond: name alkanes systematically; Feedback: multiple bonds and molecular shape; Represent: predict molecular shape from bond type; Apply: build and check complete names.

**L02 order:** Connect: retrieve the naming language; Explain: structural signatures; Respond: name and classify alcohols; Represent: the carbonyl decision tree; Feedback: other functional-group families; Apply: distinguish isomers.

**L01 key ideas:** 1. Carbon's ability to form four stable covalent bonds, with itself and with H, O, N, and halogens, creates a structural diversity no other element comes close to matching.
2. A functional group is the part of a molecule that reacts; a homologous series is a family of molecules that share a functional group and differ only in chain length, once you know the family rules, you know how any member will behave.
3. IUPAC names are built from a small set of rules applied in a fixed order, learn the rules, and you can name or decode any straight-chain or branched alkane you encounter.
4. Alkenes and alkynes follow the same chain-length and branch-naming rules as alkanes, with two additions: the suffix changes and a locant is required to show where the double or triple bond begins.
5. The number of other atoms a carbon is bonded to determines the shape around it, and you can predict bond angles directly from the type of bond present.
6. Many organic substances are flammable, volatile and/or toxic, so they must be handled and disposed of using procedures that control those specific hazards.

**L02 key ideas:** 1. structural signatures.
2. Alcohols all contain the -OH group, but WHERE on the carbon skeleton it sits, and how many other carbons surround it, determines both the name and the entire chemistry of that alcohol.
3. Carbonyl group position · -al / -one / -oic acid · terminal vs internal.
4. Esters smell like fruit, amines smell like fish, amides form the backbone of every protein in your body, and haloalkanes are the building blocks for most organic synthesis reactions, each has a structural signature you can read instantly once you know the pattern.
5. Structural isomers prove that molecular formula alone tells you almost nothing useful about a compound, the arrangement of atoms changes everything about physical properties, chemical reactivity, and biological activity.
6. Activity A, Functional Group Identification and IUPAC Naming.
7. Activity B, Classify the Isomer Type and Explain the Structural Difference.

## 3. Number and science check

Three acyclic carbonyl isomers of C4H8O: butanal, 2-methylpropanal and butan-2-one. CH3CH2CH(CH3)CH2CH3 is 3-methylpentane. The 2,2-dimethylpentane example has C7H16, not C6H14. Propan-1-ol/propan-2-ol are position isomers. Carbon counts and valences checked directly.
