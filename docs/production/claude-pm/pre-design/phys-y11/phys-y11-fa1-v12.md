### Exact site example `we-1`

WORKED EXAMPLE · RESOLVING A PULL A rope pulls a sledge with $150\ \text{N}$ at $20°$ above the horizontal. Find the horizontal and vertical components of this force. 1 GIVEN: $F = 150\ \text{N}$, $\theta = 20°$ to the horizontal. FIND: $F_x$ and $F_y$. The angle is from the horizontal $x$-axis, so $x$ uses cosine. 2 METHOD: resolve along each axis. $$F_x = F\cos\theta, \qquad F_y = F\sin\theta$$ $F_x$ pulls the sledge forward; $F_y$ lifts it slightly, reducing $N$. 3 ANSWER: $$F_x = 150\cos 20° = 141\ \text{N}$$ $$F_y = 150\sin 20° = 51.3\ \text{N}$$ Most of the $150\ \text{N}$ acts horizontally because $20°$ is a shallow angle. ✓ 1 of 3 Reveal next step →

## 4. Misconceptions and causal teaching

Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: A free-body diagram should include the forces the object exerts on other things. ✓ Right: It shows only the forces acting on the chosen object, not the forces it exerts on its surroundings. ✗ Wrong: The net force is found by adding the sizes of all the forces. ✓ Right: Net force is a vector sum, opposing forces subtract, and angled forces must be resolved into components first. 

Explain the definition or physical cause before relying on the formula. A direction convention belongs on the task, not only in the answer. A graph is a relation between labelled quantities, not a picture of the road.

## 7. Candidate practice and questions for Claude

### Candidate 1
Horizontal forces on a box are 8.00 N east and 6.00 N north. Find the net horizontal force.

Worked answer: Magnitude=sqrt(8.00²+6.00²)=10.0 N; angle=atan(6/8)=36.9 degrees north of east. Weight and normal balance separately under zero vertical acceleration.

### Candidate 2
A 2.00 kg box rests on a horizontal surface, g=9.80 m/s². State its free-body forces and net force.

Worked answer: Weight=mg=19.6 N down; normal=19.6 N up. Net=0 N. The force the box exerts on the surface acts on another body and is excluded.
