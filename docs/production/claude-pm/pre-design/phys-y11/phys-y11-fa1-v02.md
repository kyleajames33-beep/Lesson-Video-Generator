### Exact site example `we-1`

WORKED EXAMPLE · ADDING PERPENDICULAR VECTORS A boat heads straight across a river at $3.0\ \text{m s}^{-1}$. The current flows downstream at $4.0\ \text{m s}^{-1}$, perpendicular to the boat's heading. Find the boat's resultant velocity (magnitude and direction relative to its heading). 1 GIVEN: across $= 3.0\ \text{m s}^{-1}$, downstream $= 4.0\ \text{m s}^{-1}$, perpendicular. FIND: resultant magnitude $R$ and angle $\theta$. Two perpendicular vectors form a right-angled triangle when drawn head-to-tail. 2 METHOD: resultant magnitude by Pythagoras. $$R = \sqrt{3.0^2 + 4.0^2}$$ The across and downstream velocities are the two legs of the right triangle. 3 ANSWER: $$R = \sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5.0\ \text{m s}^{-1}$$ $$\theta = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{4.0}{3.0}\right) = 53° \text{ downstream of heading}$$ The classic 3-4-5 triangle: the boat moves at $5.0\ \text{m s}^{-1}$, angled $53°$ off its intended line. ✓ 1 of 3 Reveal next step →

## 4. Misconceptions and causal teaching

Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: To add two vectors you just add their magnitudes. ✓ Right: You add magnitudes only when the vectors point the same way. In general you add them head-to-tail; for perpendicular vectors the resultant comes from Pythagoras. ✗ Wrong: Subtracting a vector means making the answer smaller. ✓ Right: $\vec{A} - \vec{B}$ means add the reverse of $\vec{B}$. The result can be larger or smaller than $\vec{A}$ depending on directions. 

Explain the definition or physical cause before relying on the formula. A direction convention belongs on the task, not only in the answer. A graph is a relation between labelled quantities, not a picture of the road.

### Candidate 1
A=(6.0 east,0 north) m and B=(0 east,8.0 north) m. Find A-B.

Worked answer: Reverse B: (6.0,-8.0) m. Magnitude =10 m (1.0e1 m, two figures), direction =53 degrees south of east.

### Candidate 2
Two 5.0 N forces act in opposite directions. Find their resultant.

Worked answer: Choose east positive: +5.0-5.0=0 N. Equal opposite vectors cancel; their magnitudes must not simply be added.
