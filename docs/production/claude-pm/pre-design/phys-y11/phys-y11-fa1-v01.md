### Exact site example `we-1`

WORKED EXAMPLE · DISTANCE AND DISPLACEMENT A student walks $4\ \text{m}$ east, then $3\ \text{m}$ north. Find (a) the total distance walked and (b) the magnitude of the displacement. 1 GIVEN: leg 1 $= 4\ \text{m}$ east, leg 2 $= 3\ \text{m}$ north. FIND: total distance and displacement magnitude. Distance just adds path lengths; displacement is the straight line from start to end. 2 METHOD (distance): add the path lengths. $$d = 4 + 3 = 7\ \text{m}$$ Distance is a scalar, direction is ignored, the legs simply sum. 3 ANSWER (displacement): the legs are perpendicular, so use Pythagoras. $$s = \sqrt{4^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5\ \text{m}$$ Displacement $= 5\ \text{m}$ (north-east of start) is less than the $7\ \text{m}$ distance, the path was not straight. ✓ 1 of 3 Reveal next step →

## 3. Recalculation, units and precision

4 m east then 3 m north: distance = 4+3 = 7 m; displacement magnitude = sqrt(4²+3²) = 5 m, angle atan(3/4) = 36.87 degrees north of east. The site asks only magnitude, so its loose north-east description is acceptable there; use 37 degrees for a full vector. Whole-number teaching values have unstated measurement precision.

## 4. Misconceptions and causal teaching

Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: Distance and displacement are just two words for the same thing. ✓ Right: Distance is the total path length (scalar); displacement is the straight-line change in position with direction (vector). They are equal only for straight-line motion that never reverses. ✗ Wrong: Speed and velocity mean the same thing, so the words are interchangeable. ✓ Right: Speed is a scalar (how fast); velocity is a vector (how fast and in which direction). A car at constant speed around a bend has changing velocity. 

Explain the definition or physical cause before relying on the formula. A direction convention belongs on the task, not only in the answer. A graph is a relation between labelled quantities, not a picture of the road.

## 7. Candidate practice and questions for Claude

### Candidate 1
A student walks 6.0 m east then 8.0 m north. Give distance and displacement.

Worked answer: Distance = 6.0+8.0 = 14 m. Displacement magnitude = sqrt(36+64)=10 m, at atan(8/6)=53 degrees north of east. Use 1.0e1 m to make two figures explicit.

### Candidate 2
A runner returns to the start after a 400 m lap. Is the displacement a scalar?

Worked answer: No. It remains a vector quantity, with zero magnitude for this journey. Distance is 400 m; no direction is needed for the zero displacement.
