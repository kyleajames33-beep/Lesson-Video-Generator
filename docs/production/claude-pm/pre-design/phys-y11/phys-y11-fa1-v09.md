### Exact site example `we-1`

WORKED EXAMPLE · STOPPING DISTANCE A car travelling at $30\ \text{m s}^{-1}$ brakes uniformly and stops in $5.0\ \text{s}$. Find the braking distance. 1 GIVEN: $u = 30\ \text{m s}^{-1}$, $v = 0$, $t = 5.0\ \text{s}$. FIND: displacement $s$. We have $u$, $v$ and $t$, and want $s$, the missing one here is $a$. 2 METHOD: use the equation with $u$, $v$, $t$ and $s$ (no $a$): the average-velocity form. $$s = \left(\frac{u + v}{2}\right)t = \left(\frac{30 + 0}{2}\right)(5.0)$$ For constant acceleration, displacement is the average velocity times time, equivalent to the area under the v–t graph. 3 ANSWER: $$s = (15)(5.0) = 75\ \text{m}$$ The car needs $75\ \text{m}$ to stop. Check via $a = \dfrac{v-u}{t} = -6\ \text{m s}^{-2}$, then $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$ gives the same $s$. ✓ 1 of 3 Reveal next step →

## 4. Misconceptions and causal teaching

Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: The equations of motion work for any kind of motion. ✓ Right: They only apply when the acceleration is constant (uniform). They do not work while acceleration is changing. ✗ Wrong: Deceleration uses different equations from acceleration. ✓ Right: The same equations handle deceleration, you just use a negative value for $a$ (opposite to the motion).

### Candidate 1
A trolley starts from rest and accelerates uniformly at 2.00 m/s² for 3.00 s. Find v and s.

Worked answer: v=u+at=6.00 m/s. s=ut+0.5at²=9.00 m, both three significant figures.
