### Exact site example `we-1`

WORKED EXAMPLE · ACCELERATION FROM A v–t GRAPH A velocity–time graph is a straight line rising from $4\ \text{m s}^{-1}$ at $t = 0$ to $20\ \text{m s}^{-1}$ at $t = 8\ \text{s}$. Find the acceleration. 1 GIVEN: $u = 4\ \text{m s}^{-1}$, $v = 20\ \text{m s}^{-1}$, $t = 8\ \text{s}$. FIND: acceleration $a$. Acceleration is the gradient of the v–t line. 2 METHOD: gradient $= \dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}$. $$a = \frac{v - u}{t} = \frac{20 - 4}{8}$$ Rise is the change in velocity; run is the time interval. 3 ANSWER: $$a = \frac{16}{8} = 2.0\ \text{m s}^{-2}$$ The velocity gains $2.0\ \text{m s}^{-1}$ each second, constant acceleration. ✓ 1 of 3 Reveal next step →

## 4. Misconceptions and causal teaching

Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: The area under a displacement–time graph gives the distance travelled. ✓ Right: It is the area under a velocity –time graph that gives displacement. The gradient of a d–t graph gives velocity, not its area. ✗ Wrong: To read velocity off a curved d–t graph you use the slope of the straight line from the origin. ✓ Right: For the instantaneous velocity on a curve you draw a tangent at that point and take its gradient. 

Explain the definition or physical cause before relying on the formula. A direction convention belongs on the task, not only in the answer. A graph is a relation between labelled quantities, not a picture of the road.

## 7. Candidate practice and questions for Claude

### Candidate 1
A straight v-t line runs from -2.0 m/s to +6.0 m/s over 4.0 s. Find acceleration and displacement.

Worked answer: a=(6.0-(-2.0))/4.0=2.0 m/s². Signed area=(-2.0+6.0)4.0/2=8.0 m. Negative area must subtract.

### Candidate 2
A displacement-time tangent rises 8.0 m over 2.0 s. Find instantaneous velocity.

Worked answer: Tangent gradient=8.0/2.0=4.0 m/s in the positive direction. Do not use the secant from the origin.
