### Exact site example `we-1`

WORKED EXAMPLE · STRAIGHT-LINE DISPLACEMENT A cyclist rides $12\ \text{km}$ east, then turns around and rides $5\ \text{km}$ west. Find the total distance and the net displacement. 1 GIVEN: $12\ \text{km}$ east, then $5\ \text{km}$ west. Let east $= +$. FIND: total distance and net displacement. A sign convention turns directions into $+$ and $-$ numbers. 2 METHOD (distance): add path lengths. $$d = 12 + 5 = 17\ \text{km}$$ Distance ignores direction, both legs add as positives. 3 ANSWER (displacement): add signed legs. $$s = (+12) + (-5) = +7\ \text{km} = 7\ \text{km east}$$ The net displacement of $7\ \text{km}$ east is less than the $17\ \text{km}$ distance because the trip reversed. ✓ 1 of 3 Reveal next step →

## 4. Misconceptions and causal teaching

Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: To find displacement you always just add the leg lengths together. ✓ Right: Displacement combines legs with direction , opposite legs subtract, and perpendicular legs combine by Pythagoras. ✗ Wrong: Displacement is always smaller than distance. ✓ Right: The magnitude of displacement is at most equal to the distance, and they are equal only for straight-line motion that never reverses direction. 

Explain the definition or physical cause before relying on the formula. A direction convention belongs on the task, not only in the answer. A graph is a relation between labelled quantities, not a picture of the road.

### Candidate 2
Walk 5.0 m west then 12 m north. Find displacement.

Worked answer: Components (-5.0,12) m, magnitude sqrt(25+144)=13 m; atan(5/12)=23 degrees west of north (two significant figures). Distance=17 m.
