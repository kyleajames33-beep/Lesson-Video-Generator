### Exact site example `we-1`

WORKED EXAMPLE · RAIN FROM A MOVING CAR Rain falls straight down at $80\ \text{km h}^{-1}$ (object A). A car drives north at $80\ \text{km h}^{-1}$ (object B). Find the velocity of the rain relative to the car. 1 GIVEN: $\vec{v}_A = 80\ \text{km h}^{-1}$ down, $\vec{v}_B = 80\ \text{km h}^{-1}$ north. FIND: $\vec{v}_{AB} = \vec{v}_A - \vec{v}_B$. Subtract the car's velocity: add the reverse ($80\ \text{km h}^{-1}$ south) to the rain's downward velocity. 2 METHOD: the two perpendicular parts are $80$ down and $80$ south. $$|\vec{v}_{AB}| = \sqrt{80^2 + 80^2}$$ Down and south are perpendicular, forming a right triangle. 3 ANSWER: $$|\vec{v}_{AB}| = \sqrt{12800} \approx 113\ \text{km h}^{-1}$$ $$\theta = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{80}{80}\right) = 45° \text{ from vertical}$$ The rain appears to come in at $45°$ toward the car, exactly the slant you see. ✓ 1 of 3 Reveal next step →

## 3. Recalculation, units and precision

Rain 80 km/h down, car 80 km/h north: rain relative to car = (80 down,80 south) km/h. Magnitude sqrt(80²+80²)=113.137085 km/h, angle 45 degrees south of downward vertical. Site 113 km/h is the unrounded-to-three-figures calculation, but original 80 has ambiguous precision. With two significant figures report 1.1e2 km/h; with exact teaching values 113 is acceptable as an approximate intermediate. Distinguish velocity arrow (down and south) from the direction rain appears to come from (up and north).

## 4. Misconceptions and causal teaching

Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: An object has one true velocity that everyone measures the same. ✓ Right: Velocity depends on the observer's frame of reference. A passing car has different velocities relative to the ground and relative to you. ✗ Wrong: To find a relative velocity you always add the two velocities. ✓ Right: You subtract: $\vec{v}_{AB} = \vec{v}_A - \vec{v}_B$. Same-direction motion subtracts to a small relative velocity; opposite-direction motion gives a large one. 

Explain the definition or physical cause before relying on the formula. A direction convention belongs on the task, not only in the answer. A graph is a relation between labelled quantities, not a picture of the road.

## 7. Candidate practice and questions for Claude

### Candidate 1
A moves north at 12.0 m/s; B moves east at 5.00 m/s. Find A relative to B.

Worked answer: vAB=(-5.00 east,+12.0 north) m/s. Magnitude=13.0 m/s; direction=atan(5/12)=22.6 degrees west of north.

### Candidate 2
A and B both move east at 15.0 m/s. What is vAB?

Worked answer: vAB=15.0-15.0=0 m/s. Both can move relative to the ground while their separation stays fixed.
