### Exact site example `we-1`

WORKED EXAMPLE · g FROM A v–t GRADIENT A falling mass passes light gates giving velocities of $2.0\ \text{m s}^{-1}$ at $t = 0.20\ \text{s}$ and $6.9\ \text{m s}^{-1}$ at $t = 0.70\ \text{s}$. Find $g$ from the v–t gradient. 1 GIVEN: $(0.20\ \text{s}, 2.0\ \text{m s}^{-1})$ and $(0.70\ \text{s}, 6.9\ \text{m s}^{-1})$. FIND: $g$ as the gradient. For a freely falling object, the v–t gradient equals $g$. 2 METHOD: gradient $= \dfrac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$. $$g = \frac{6.9 - 2.0}{0.70 - 0.20}$$ Rise is the change in velocity; run is the change in time. 3 ANSWER: $$g = \frac{4.9}{0.50} = 9.8\ \text{m s}^{-2}$$ Matches the accepted value of $9.8\ \text{m s}^{-2}$, a good result. ✓ 1 of 3 Reveal next step →

## 3. Recalculation, units and precision

Light-gate readings (0.20 s,2.0 m/s) and (0.70 s,6.9 m/s): g=(6.9-2.0)/(0.70-0.20)=4.9/0.50=9.8 m/s² downward, two significant figures. Correct. One two-point slope matching the accepted value establishes neither reliability nor the uncertainty of the experiment. Compare repeated best-fit slopes and residuals. The other method uses s vs t² with gradient g/2 only for release from rest and consistent zeroes.

## 4. Misconceptions and causal teaching

Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: Accuracy and reliability mean the same thing. ✓ Right: Accuracy is closeness to the true value; reliability is consistency between repeats. Results can be reliable (consistent) but inaccurate if a systematic error is present. ✗ Wrong: Taking more repeat readings fixes a systematic error. ✓ Right: Repeats reduce random error and improve reliability, but a systematic error (e.g. air resistance, a slow timer) biases every reading and is not removed by averaging. 

Explain the definition or physical cause before relying on the formula. A direction convention belongs on the task, not only in the answer. A graph is a relation between labelled quantities, not a picture of the road.

## 7. Candidate practice and questions for Claude

### Candidate 1
A fall-distance vs time-squared best-fit gradient is 4.85 m/s². Find g and percentage error against 9.80 m/s².

Worked answer: g=2(4.85)=9.70 m/s². Percentage error=abs(9.70-9.80)/9.80 x100=1.02%. This alone does not measure reliability.

### Candidate 2
Three repeat g estimates are 9.6,9.8,10.0 m/s². Give mean, range and one improvement.

Worked answer: Mean=(9.6+9.8+10.0)/3=9.8 m/s²; range=10.0-9.6=0.4 m/s². Increase independent repeats and assess timing/calibration before averaging; repeats do not remove a common bias.
