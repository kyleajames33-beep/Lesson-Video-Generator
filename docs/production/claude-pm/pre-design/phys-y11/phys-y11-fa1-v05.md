### Exact site example `we-1`

WORKED EXAMPLE · AVERAGE SPEED AND VELOCITY A cyclist rides $300\ \text{m}$ east, then $100\ \text{m}$ west, taking $40\ \text{s}$ in total. Find the average speed and the average velocity. 1 GIVEN: $300\ \text{m}$ east then $100\ \text{m}$ west, $\Delta t = 40\ \text{s}$. FIND: average speed and average velocity. Distance $= 300 + 100$; displacement $= 300 - 100$ (east positive). 2 METHOD: $$\text{avg speed} = \frac{\text{distance}}{\Delta t} = \frac{400}{40}, \qquad v_{av} = \frac{\Delta s}{\Delta t} = \frac{200}{40}$$ Distance is $400\ \text{m}$; displacement is $200\ \text{m}$ east. 3 ANSWER: $$\text{avg speed} = 10\ \text{m s}^{-1}, \qquad v_{av} = 5.0\ \text{m s}^{-1}\ \text{east}$$ The velocity is smaller because the westward leg cancelled part of the displacement. ✓ 1 of 3 Reveal next step →

## 3. Recalculation, units and precision

300 m east then 100 m west in 40 s: distance = 400 m, displacement = +200 m; average speed = 400/40 = 10 m/s; average velocity = 200/40 = 5.0 m/s east. Correct arithmetic. Trailing-zero givens do not state precision; name an intended two-figure precision rather than infer it.

## 4. Misconceptions and causal teaching

Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: Average speed and average velocity are always the same number. ✓ Right: Average speed uses distance; average velocity uses displacement. They are equal only when the motion is in a straight line without reversing. ✗ Wrong: A speedometer shows your average speed for the journey. ✓ Right: A speedometer shows instantaneous speed, your speed at that single moment, which constantly changes. 

Explain the definition or physical cause before relying on the formula. A direction convention belongs on the task, not only in the answer. A graph is a relation between labelled quantities, not a picture of the road.

## 7. Candidate practice and questions for Claude

### Candidate 1
Run 60.0 m east and 20.0 m west in 20.0 s. Find average speed and average velocity.

Worked answer: Speed=(60.0+20.0)/20.0=4.00 m/s. Velocity=(60.0-20.0)/20.0=2.00 m/s east.

### Candidate 2
A speedometer reads 15 m/s now. Must the journey average be 15 m/s?

Worked answer: No. The reading is instantaneous speed. Journey average speed requires the total path length divided by the whole elapsed time.
