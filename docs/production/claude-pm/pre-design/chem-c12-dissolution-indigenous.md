## Exact syllabus words

> investigate the use of solubility equilibria by Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples when removing toxicity from foods, for example:

## Site order and material

Get oriented/Think First (does a saturated solution stop changing?); Know/Understand/Can Do, formula and misconception cards; lattice separation and hydration; exothermic/endothermic salts; saturated solution as dynamic equilibrium; cycad seed leaching; reading the knowledge in cultural context; two worked examples; spotter; practice and an eight-mark challenge; retrieve. The ionic section uses a Hess cycle MX(s) → gaseous ions → hydrated ions, ΔHsolution = positive lattice separation plus negative hydration. Saturated solid and solution exchange ions at equal rates. A labelled KBr crystal in saturated KBr solution is described as tracer evidence. The page distinguishes unsaturated, saturated and supersaturated.

Numbers: NaOH −44, CaCl₂ −81, NH₄NO₃ +25.7, KNO₃ +35 and NaCl +3.9 kJ mol⁻¹. Example 1: MgCl₂(s) ⇌ Mg²⁺ + 2Cl⁻ warms solution; hydration releases more energy than lattice separation needs; adding water to a saturated mixture allows more solid to dissolve. Example 2: crushed cycad seeds in a woven bag in running water for two weeks; answer says water-soluble cycasin leaves the seed, moving water removes the dissolved material and crushing exposes more tissue. The page also mentions repeated still-water changes and roasting with soaking. It asserts a 65,000-year history for the specific processing account without a source.

Practice: explain CaCl₂ warming using both energy processes (4 marks); explain constant concentration and mass of a saturated KBr solution with solid present, tracer evidence and a change that allows more KBr to dissolve (3 marks). Spotter asks which of CaCO₃ with solid, NaCl with solid, completely dissolved salt or sugar crystals has dissolution equilibrium. Eight-mark challenge asks toxin/property, running versus still water, and why the knowledge is systematic scientific inquiry. Memory aids: two energy contributions, equal opposing rates, and the site's four-point cycad answer (toxin, solubility, continuing removal, knowledge system). ## Number and science check

- [University enthalpy table](https://chem.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/General_Chemistry/Book%3A_General_Chemistry%3A_Principles_Patterns_and_Applications_%28Averill%29/05%3A_Energy_Changes_in_Chemical_Reactions/5.05%3A_Enthalpies_of_Solution) gives CaCl₂ −81.3, NH₄NO₃ +25.7, KNO₃ +34.9, NaCl +3.9 and NaOH −44.5 kJ mol⁻¹ at 25 °C. Site rounding is consistent. The 2-week duration is a scenario premise, not a universal practice or experimentally checked constant. Neither worked example requires numerical arithmetic.
- Site correction, **“Leaching cycad seeds” and Boss Challenge model answer**: the latter writes `cycasin(s) ⇌ cycasin(aq)` and says “continuous removal of products shifts the equilibrium right continuously, the maximum dissolution rate is maintained.” Cycasin is in seed tissue; extraction also involves diffusion and hydrolysis. The [Australian Curriculum teacher background](https://v9.australiancurriculum.edu.au/support-resources/background-information/science_teacher_background_information_AC9S10U07_E6) says water hydrolyses cycasin and helps remove water-soluble methylazoxymethanol. Correct mechanism: opening/crushing exposes tissue, extended water contact permits hydrolysis and movement of soluble material into water, and flowing or changed water carries it away. Avoid an ionic Ksp equation or a constant maximum-rate claim. [Nungalinya College's account](https://www.nungalinya.edu.au/cycad) supports cracked kernels in woven bags and fresh running water for the practice it describes; it is not a universal Aboriginal method.
- Site correction: the page's “65,000+ years” describes Aboriginal presence broadly, not a dated record for this particular cycad method. Remove the specific-duration assertion until evidenced. The page also invents observational details such as community members testing small quantities for safety and “below detectable” toxin after soaking. Do not repeat these without a direct source. The site names Macrozamia, Cycas and Bowenia together, while local techniques and plant species differ.
- `Na¹(aq) + Cl¹(aq)` in the page's reveal should be Na⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq). The historical attribution to van 't Hoff for a NaCl enthalpy measurement is not supported by a source on the page.

## Exam traps, objects and overlap

[NESA 2023 Q33 feedback](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2023) asks for equality of forward and reverse rates, not equality of amounts. The site's traps are assuming all dissolution absorbs heat, using lattice energy alone to predict solubility, describing saturation as static, and describing leaching as boiling or complete chemical destruction. [Nungalinya College](https://www.nungalinya.edu.au/cycad) documents cracked kernels in woven bags and fresh running water. [Australian Curriculum](https://v9.australiancurriculum.edu.au/support-resources/background-information/science_teacher_background_information_AC9S10U07_E6) describes North Queensland rainforest Aboriginal practice and the hydrolysis issue. Seek community-specific attribution before naming a nation or presenting a generic recipe.
