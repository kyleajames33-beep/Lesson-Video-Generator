## Official 2017 syllabus

> derive equilibrium expressions for saturated solutions in terms of Ksp and calculate the solubility of an ionic substance from its Ksp value

NSW Education Standards Authority, *Chemistry Stage 6 Syllabus* (2017), Module 5, Solution Equilibria, p. 51, [official NSW Education Standards Authority syllabus page](https://c.educationstandards.nsw.edu.au/wps/portal/nesa/11-12/stage-6-learning-areas/stage-6-science/chemistry-2017).

## Site order and teaching material

Get oriented (Nernst silver-salt comparison; Ca(OH)₂ fill-in) → Writing a Ksp expression → Molar solubility from Ksp → Ksp from molar solubility → When comparing Ksp values is valid → fluoride and tooth enamel extension → worked examples → activities → practice questions → retrieve and reflect. The pure solid is omitted from Ksp; coefficients become powers. For a pure-water 1:1 salt, Ksp = s²; for 1:2 or 2:1, Ksp = 4s³; for 1:3, Ksp = 27s⁴. The page's useful aid is to write the dissolution equation, derive each ion concentration in terms of s, then substitute. Compare Ksp directly only for the same formula type.

**All displayed examples and numerical checks:** AgCl Ksp 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁰ gives s = 1.342 × 10⁻⁵ mol/L. AgI Ksp 8.5 × 10⁻¹⁷ gives s = 9.220 × 10⁻⁹. CaF₂ Ksp 3.9 × 10⁻¹¹ gives s = 2.137 × 10⁻⁴, greater than AgCl despite lower Ksp. Ag₂CrO₄ Ksp 1.12 × 10⁻¹² gives s = 6.542 × 10⁻⁵. Mg(OH)₂ with s = 1.54 × 10⁻⁴ gives [OH⁻] = 3.08 × 10⁻⁴ and Ksp = 1.461 × 10⁻¹¹. PbCl₂ with s = 3.9 × 10⁻² gives [Cl⁻] = 7.8 × 10⁻² and Ksp = 2.373 × 10⁻⁴. Worked example 1 on this raw page: Ag₂SO₄ s = 7.60 × 10⁻³, [Ag⁺] = 1.520 × 10⁻², [SO₄²⁻] = 7.60 × 10⁻³, Ksp = 1.756 × 10⁻⁶. Worked example 2: PbSO₄ Ksp 1.6 × 10⁻⁸ gives s = 1.265 × 10⁻⁴; AgBr Ksp 5.0 × 10⁻¹³ gives s = 7.071 × 10⁻⁷. Both are 1:1, so AgBr is less soluble.

**Practice questions and aids:** True/false on CaF₂ expression and valid Ksp comparison; interactive AgCl versus Ag₂CrO₄; three application questions on silver-salt comparisons, 1:1 versus 1:2 calculations, and using s in place of 2s. Short answers ask for PbF₂ s = 2.1 × 10⁻³ (Ksp = 3.704 × 10⁻⁸ and reverse check), AgCl versus Ag₂CrO₄ Ksp 1.1 × 10⁻¹² (s = 1.342 × 10⁻⁵ versus 6.503 × 10⁻⁵), cave CaCO₃ Ksp 3.4 × 10⁻⁹ versus BaSO₄ Ksp 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁰ (s = 5.831 × 10⁻⁵ versus 1.049 × 10⁻⁵), and Bi₂S₃ s = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁵ (Ksp = 1.08 × 10⁻⁷³). Calculation practice: CaF₂ s = 2.1 × 10⁻⁴ gives Ksp = 3.704 × 10⁻¹¹; Ag₂CO₃ Ksp 8.5 × 10⁻¹² gives s = 1.286 × 10⁻⁴ mol/L and 0.0355 g/L using 275.8 g/mol. Extended response compares SrSO₄ Ksp 3.4 × 10⁻⁷ (s = 5.831 × 10⁻⁴), Ag₂SO₄ Ksp 1.2 × 10⁻⁵ (s = 1.442 × 10⁻²), and Al(OH)₃ Ksp 3.0 × 10⁻³⁴ (s = 1.826 × 10⁻⁹), then asks about endothermic warming. The Ca(OH)₂ fill-in currently shows 4.68 × 10⁻⁵ with answer 0.023; exact cube-root result is 0.0227.

**Named misconceptions:** including a solid in the denominator; replacing the CaF₂ ion concentration 2s by s (makes calculated s 4^(1/3) = 1.587 times too high); directly ranking unlike formula types by Ksp; assuming water dissolves every ionic solid. The enamel extension uses hydroxyapatite Ca₁₀(PO₄)₆(OH)₂, fluorapatite Ca₁₀(PO₄)₆F₂ and acid removal of OH⁻/phosphate. Its numerical constants need correction below.

## Number and site corrections

Arithmetic above independently recomputed. The site's PbSO₄ Ksp 1.6 × 10⁻⁸ at 25 °C is defensible: [MIT OpenCourseWare](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/5-111sc-principles-of-chemical-science-fall-2014/7e17795c825ede37725ff7cbfd2de2cc_MIT5_111F14_Lec20Soln.pdf) derives 1.96 × 10⁻⁸ from measured solubility, [CSU Dominguez Hills](https://www2.csudh.edu/oliver/chemdata/data-ksp.htm) lists 1.6 × 10⁻⁸, and [University of Texas at Austin](https://ch302.cm.utexas.edu/worksheets/old%20versions/RICE-table-wkst-KEY1.pdf) uses 2.53 × 10⁻⁸. A [different college table](https://www.cgc.edu/sites/default/files/inline-files/solubility-rules-and-activity-series.pdf) gives 6.3 × 10⁻⁷; choose and cite one table for a video, since mixing tables could change a borderline conclusion. `site-corrections.md` C5 and C14 found the Ca(OH)₂ and Ag₂SO₄ inconsistencies. The corrections worktree changes them to Ksp 5.0 × 10⁻⁶, s about 0.011 and Ag₂SO₄ s = 1.44 × 10⁻², Ksp = 1.2 × 10⁻⁵. The requested raw site path still has the old values. The [NIST hydroxyapatite study](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/77A/jresv77An1p149_A1b.pdf) gives Ksp 6.3 ± 2.1 × 10⁻⁵⁹ for **Ca₅(PO₄)₃OH**, whereas the site attaches 2.3 × 10⁻⁵⁹ to the doubled Ca₁₀ formula. The formula bases cannot be compared without conversion. [NHMRC fluoride guidance](https://guidelines.nhmrc.gov.au/australian-drinking-water-guidelines/part-5/physical-chemical-characteristics/fluoride) gives a 0.7 to 1.0 mg/L fluoridation target and 1.5 mg/L health limit; the site's 0.6 to 1.1 and sharp >2 mg/L fluorosis threshold misstate these.
