## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**Teaching order.**

**L16:**

1. Compare starting pH, buffer region, equivalence-point pH and jump size to identify a curve.
2. Strong acid/strong base has the largest jump at pH 7; weak acid/strong base has a buffer region and an equivalence point above 7; strong acid/weak base has an equivalence point below 7.
3. Weak acid/weak base has no sharp pH jump suitable for a visual indicator.
4. In a weak acid/strong base titration, start, buffer, half-equivalence, equivalence and post-equivalence regions require different calculations.
5. Read equivalence volume at the midpoint of the steepest section, and read pKa at half that volume.

**Worked examples and numbers from the extracts.**

**L16:**

- A curve starting at pH 2.87 has pH 4.74 at 12.50 mL and pH 8.72 at 25.00 mL. The page identifies a weak acid, equivalence at 25.00 mL, pKa 4.74 and phenolphthalein.
- 25.00 mL of 0.200 mol/L lactic acid (Ka 1.4 × 10⁻⁴) titrated with 0.200 mol/L NaOH gives pH 2.28 initially, 3.85 at 12.50 mL, 4.33 at 18.75 mL, 8.43 at 25.00 mL and 12.26 at 30.00 mL.

## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes

**L16:**

- Every equivalence point is pH 7; weak conjugate ions hydrolyse.
- The highest point or pH 7 crossing is the equivalence point; find the jump's midpoint.
- Half-equivalence occurs at pH 7; its pH equals pKa.
- A larger pH jump means a faster acid-base reaction; the page attributes it to pH scale and buffering differences.

These traps come from the linked site lessons. They are not attributed to NESA markers without a specific exam report.
