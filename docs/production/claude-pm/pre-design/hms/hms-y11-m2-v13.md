## 3. Number and science check

Figures the page states (to be independently checked in the design; current statistics must come from a dated report):
- Estimate maximum heart rate as 220 minus age, then take the required percentage.
- For a 16 year old, maximum is estimated at 204, so a 70 to 85 per cent aerobic zone is roughly 143 to 173 beats per minute .
- The formula 220 minus age is a population average with a standard deviation of about 10 to 12 beats per minute, so a real 16 year old's maximum may be anywhere from roughly 190 to 215.
- A program prescribing maximal intensity for 40 continuous minutes has described something physiologically impossible, and that is a substantive error rather than an ambitious one.
- A 40 minute session containing eight minutes of work is a different physiological event from a 40 minute continuous run, and writing only "40 minutes" conceals which one you meant.
- Every change is a single change, and week 5 is deliberately not an increase.
- A widely used starting guideline is to increase total weekly load by no more than about 10 per cent per week.

## 6. Course coherence

- **Entry knowledge:** hms-y11-m2-v12: Aerobic versus anaerobic training.
- **Opens on:** the work-to-rest dial from v12 splits into four labelled dials: F, I, T, T (the final frame of hms-y11-m2-v12).
- **Start:** Start from a vague program ('run four times a week, go hard') and make each FITT decision.
- **Stop:** Stop when students can set each FITT variable with a number, set intensity by heart rate (with the 220 minus age caveat), perceived exertion or pace, explain why the variables constrain each other, build progression, and say what FITT does not cover.
