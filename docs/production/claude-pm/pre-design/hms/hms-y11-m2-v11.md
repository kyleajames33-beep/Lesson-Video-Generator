## 3. Number and science check

Figures the page states (to be independently checked in the design; current statistics must come from a dated report):
- A 2025 review of adult endurance studies found a small average energy contribution, with protein oxidation rising with exercise intensity.
- At around 65 per cent of maximum the two are roughly equal.
- Above about 85 per cent, carbohydrate supplies almost all of it, because fat cannot be broken down fast enough and needs more oxygen per unit of energy released.
- The 1500 metre runner works for long periods above the crossover point, so almost all of their energy comes from a store that holds about ninety minutes' worth.
- Intake beyond roughly 2 grams per kilogram is not stored as extra muscle; the surplus is used as energy or stored as fat, and the amino group is excreted.
- A fluid loss of about 2 per cent of body mass, 1.4 kilograms in a 70 kilogram athlete, which is an ordinary summer training session, is enough to produce a measurable fall in endurance performance and in the quality of decision-making.

## 6. Course coherence

- **Entry knowledge:** hms-y11-m2-v06: Digestion and what macronutrients become.; hms-y11-m2-v10: Three systems and fuel mix by intensity.
- **Opens on:** the three-system stack from v10 with a fuel gauge under each system (the final frame of hms-y11-m2-v10).
- **Start:** Start from nutrition as the supply chain for the three systems' fuels.
- **Stop:** Stop when students can explain the job of each macronutrient for each system, explain why intensity decides the fuel mix (and reject the fat-burning-zone myth), explain per-kilogram requirements, explain the role of key micronutrients (iron, calcium, B vitamins) and water.
