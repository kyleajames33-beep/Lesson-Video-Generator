## 2. Site order, ideas, examples and practice

- **Resilience is a process, and it needs something to work with:** Resilience is the process of adapting well in the face of adversity and recovering function afterwards. Three words in that definition are doing work, and each rules out a common misuse. Process, not trait. It is something that happens over time and can be supported or obstructed. Saying a person “is resilient” describes an outcome and explains nothing. Adversity. Resilience is only meaningful against a real threat. ...
- **Coping strategies, and why the bad ones work:** A coping strategy is whatever a person does to manage a stressor or the feelings it produces. They divide into adaptive and maladaptive, and the division is about timescale rather than about morality. The same stressor, two families of response. ## 6. Course coherence

- **Entry knowledge:** hms-y11-m1-v10: Four skills and the conditions they need.
- **Opens on:** the v10 board's 'skill needs a condition' row carries over to five more skills (the final frame of hms-y11-m1-v10).
- **Start:** Start from resilience as a process that needs resources, not a personality trait.
- **Stop:** Stop when students can explain resilience as a process, adaptive versus maladaptive coping by timescale (and why bad coping persists), the mechanism of a sense of purpose, ethical behaviour as a health skill, and connectedness as the strongest protective factor, each linked to the researched issue.
- **Next handoff:** hms-y11-m1-v12: opens on the nine skills from v10 and v11 turn inward: an audit sheet with your own name space.
- **Later homes:** Applying the skills to your own health: hms-y11-m1-v12.
- **Recurring models:** reuse:skill needs a condition, intro:coping timescale (relief now, cost later)
- **Cross-year overlap:** None.
