## 2. Site order, ideas, examples and practice

- **What is digital health, and what is it for?:** Digital health is not a technology; it is a response to a specific failure. In a system where funding, delivery and regulation are split across governments and sectors, a person's health information ends up in pieces held by organisations that cannot see each other's pieces : the general practice, the specialist, the pathology provider, the pharmacy, two hospitals and an aged care service. - **What services exist in Australia:** My Health Record. The national shared summary. It holds discharge summaries, medicines information, pathology and imaging reports, immunisation history and shared health summaries. It operates on an opt-out basis, so most Australians have one, and individuals control access settings and can see who has viewed their record. Crucially, it is a summary that providers choose to contribute to, not a complete clinical reco...
- **To what extent has it succeeded in connecting health information?:** The graded judgement: digital health has succeeded where the task was narrow and the custodian single, and has succeeded only partially where the task was to connect information across organisations that were never designed to share it. The failure is not technical, it is that the fragmentation digital health was meant to overcome is the same fragmentation that makes contribution voluntary and inconsistent. That sent...
- **Challenges and opportunities, for individuals and for organisations:** One structural observation worth a sentence in an evaluation: the costs of digital health fall on providers and the benefits fall on the system and the patient. Where costs and benefits land on different parties, adoption is slow no matter how good the idea, and that, rather than technology, is what has determined the pace here.
- **What would actually make it work:** Five conditions. None of them is a new technology, which is the point. Contribution has to be the default, not a choice. A shared record is only as good as what is in it, and voluntary contribution guarantees an incomplete record. It has to be faster than not using it. Clinicians work in short consultations. A system that costs thirty seconds will be used; one that costs three minutes will not, whatever its contents....
