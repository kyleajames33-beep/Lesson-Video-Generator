## 3. Number and science check

Figures the page states (to be independently checked in the design; current statistics must come from a dated report):
- Maximal speed is unsustainable because its power source has a ten second tank, which is also why the world record for 100 metres has always been run with a decelerating final phase and always will be.
- A 400 metre runner finishes in a far worse state than a 100 metre runner precisely because the race is slower.
- At 100 metre pace the event is over before glycolysis has contributed much; at 400 metre pace the athlete runs near-maximally for 45 to 60 seconds, which is exactly the window in which the glycolytic system supplies most of the energy.
- The 100 metre runner simply ran out of fuel and stopped; the 400 metre runner ran the system into the ground.
- This is why hydrogen ions begin accumulating well before the ten second mark in a maximal effort, and why even a 100 metre sprint produces a measurable rise in blood lactate.
- Lactate is cleared within roughly an hour of exercise finishing, while delayed onset muscle soreness peaks 24 to 48 hours later, long after the lactate has gone.
- ATP-PCr: fuel is phosphocreatine in the fibre, one ATP per molecule, maximal intensity, 10 to 12 seconds, recovery 2 to 3 minutes, fatigue by depletion.
- Glycolytic: fuel is glucose and glycogen, two ATP per glucose anaerobically, high submaximal intensity, predominant from about 10 seconds to 2 minutes, recovery 30 to 60 minutes, fatigue by hydrogen ion accumulation lowering pH.

## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes (from the page)

- Why "the body switches from one system to the next" is wrong, and what to say instead All three systems operate at all times.
