## 2. Site order, ideas, examples and practice

- **Measuring: technology decides what counts as health:** Before anything can be managed it has to be measured, and what can be measured shapes what gets attention. That is a deeper relationship than it sounds. At the individual level. Blood pressure, blood glucose, imaging, genetic testing, and now consumer devices measuring heart rate, sleep, activity and rhythm. - **Monitoring: care moves out of the building:** Monitoring is measurement repeated, and repetition changes what care can be. This is the technology with the clearest fit to the problem this module keeps returning to: chronic conditions managed over years by a system built for episodes. What it enables. Continuous glucose monitoring for diabetes; blood pressure and weight monitoring at home for heart failure; implanted devices reporting rhythm; and remote monitorin...
- **Early diagnosis, and the case against assuming it is always good:** Earlier detection is one of the largest genuine gains of the past fifty years. It is also the area where a technology's benefit is most often assumed rather than demonstrated, and being able to explain why is a top-band distinction. The genuine gains. Improved imaging finds disease at a treatable stage; more sensitive tests detect infection and cancer earlier; screening programs find bowel, breast and cervical cancer...
- **Precision surgery, and where its benefits land:** Surgical technology has changed what an operation costs a person in recovery, and that change has consequences well beyond the operating theatre. What changed.
