## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes (from the page)

- The trap in this dot point A question about determinants invites a list of five paragraphs, one per group. That answer describes the determinants and never discusses them, and it caps out around half marks. Every strong answer names at least one interaction, a chain, a cluster or a feedback loop, and carries one population group through it.
- No NESA marking-trend claim is made.

## 6. Course coherence

- **Entry knowledge:** hms-y11-m1-v02: Circumstances change health directly and change the range of choices.; hms-y11-m1-v04: Equity and access.
- **Opens on:** the six access gates from v04 sit at the end of a longer chain: what comes before the gate? (the final frame of hms-y11-m1-v04).
- **Start:** Start from the five syllabus groups of determinants as an ordered sequence (broad features of society, then environment, socioeconomic, behaviours, biomedical).
- **Stop:** Stop when students can order the five groups, explain three kinds of interaction (chains, clustering, feedback), explain the sociological causes of risky behaviour, name where inequities exist in Australia, and predict why behaviour-only campaigns can widen gaps.
- **Next handoff:** hms-y11-m1-v06: opens on the determinants chain from v05 is re-pointed at one age group: young people.
- **Later homes:** Young people's health status: hms-y11-m1-v08. Year 12 groups experiencing inequities: hms-y12-m3-v02 and v03 reuse the determinants chain.
- **Recurring models:** intro:determinants chain (five ordered groups), intro:behaviour-only campaigns widen gaps
- **Cross-year overlap:** Year 12 recalls the five groups in one sentence and reuses the chain.
