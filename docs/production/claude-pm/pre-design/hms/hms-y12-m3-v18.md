## 2. Site order, ideas, examples and practice

- **How big data is being used:** Five uses, from the routine to the ambitious. The first is the one students overlook, and it is the largest. Knowing what is happening at all. - **How it reduces healthcare spending, and where that claim is weakest:** The syllabus asks how big data is reducing healthcare spending. Answer it honestly, because the mechanisms are real and the savings are frequently overstated. Where savings are genuine. Avoiding duplication. Knowing a test has already been done avoids repeating it, direct, immediate and measurable. Preventing avoidable admissions. - **How it is being used to cure and manage diseases:** Finding causes. Linking exposure, genetic, environmental and outcome data across very large populations reveals risk and protective factors that no single study is large enough to detect. Rare effects and small effects only appear at scale. Precision medicine. - **What measures are needed for privacy and confidentiality:** The syllabus asks what measures need to be taken, so the answer is a set of protections with reasons, not a general statement that privacy is important. Collect the minimum, and keep it only as long as it is needed. The strongest protection for data is not holding it. - **How big data is shaping the health of Australians:** The dot point says shaping, which is a stronger word than helping . Four ways it shapes rather than merely assists. It decides what is visible. A problem that is measured becomes a policy problem; one that is not stays invisible.
