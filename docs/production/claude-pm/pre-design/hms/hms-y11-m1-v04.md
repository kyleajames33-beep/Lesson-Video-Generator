## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes (from the page)

- The exam trap in this card A question asking you to evaluate access almost always supplies a service that is free. The free-ness is bait: it is there so that students who equate access with cost will declare the service accessible and stop. Name at least three kinds of barrier every time, and say which ones the case actually addresses.
- No NESA marking-trend claim is made.

## 6. Course coherence

- **Entry knowledge:** hms-y11-m1-v03: Population measures show averages that hide differences between groups.
- **Opens on:** the v03 table splits by group: the averages hide rows that are much worse (the final frame of hms-y11-m1-v03).
- **Start:** Start with the equality versus equity contrast on one concrete case (the same free service, very different journeys to reach it).
- **Stop:** Stop when students can define equity, access, participation and rights in plain words, distinguish equity from equality, test access against six kinds of barrier (not only cost), explain how participation changes whether a program works, and explain the mechanism by which the four principles together promote health status, including spotting the 'it's free' trap.
- **Next handoff:** hms-y11-m1-v05: opens on the six access gates from v04 sit at the end of a longer chain: what comes before the gate?.
- **Later homes:** Who experiences inequity and why (determinants): hms-y11-m1-v05. Equity of access to the healthcare system: hms-y12-m3-v08 reuses the access gates.
- **Recurring models:** intro:equality vs equity, intro:access gates (barriers beyond cost)
- **Cross-year overlap:** Year 12 hms-y12-m3-v08 reuses the access gates for the healthcare system.
