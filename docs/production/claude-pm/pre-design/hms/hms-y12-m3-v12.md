## 2. Site order, ideas, examples and practice

- **The products and services available:** The category is enormous and internally inconsistent, and the first analytical move is to stop treating it as one thing. - **As a preventative measure:** This is where complementary approaches are used most and where the case for them is strongest, because prevention is mostly about sustained behaviour and complementary practice is often good at sustaining behaviour. What people are trying to do. Maintain wellbeing rather than treat a diagnosis; manage stress; support sleep, digestion, immunity or energy; and stay in control of their own health. - **Treating the health issue, and where the real risk is:** Using a complementary approach to treat a condition is the use with the strongest evidence for some things and the greatest potential for harm in others. The determining factor is not the therapy; it is whether it is used instead of, or alongside, effective treatment. Where it works. - **As a supplement to other medical treatments:** This is how complementary approaches are most often actually used, and it is where the healthcare system has the clearest job to do. What it looks like. Someone having cancer treatment also using meditation for anxiety and acupuncture for nausea. Someone with chronic back pain using medication, physiotherapy and yoga together. - **Explaining this without dismissing or endorsing:** The command word in this dot point is “explain”, and the strongest explanations do four things. Separate the category. Judge specific approaches for specific uses, not “complementary medicine” as a whole. Evidence exists for parts of it and not for others. Take the reasons for use seriously.
