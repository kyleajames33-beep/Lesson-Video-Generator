# Chemistry Module 6 P06 practical plan (draft for Claude's review)

**Aim and route.** Find the concentration of unknown hydrochloric acid by titrating it with a standard sodium carbonate solution. `C6-IQ3-01`: “conduct practical investigations to analyse the concentration of an unknown acid or base by titration”. Site L14 and L18. Prerequisite: chem-m6-c12. Analysis of a pH or conductivity curve can be a later extension, not a replacement for collecting a titre.

**School method.** Dry anhydrous sodium carbonate as directed by the school method, cool in a desiccator, then weigh 1.3250 g. Dissolve and make to 250.0 mL in a volumetric flask. Mix thoroughly. Rinse a 25.00 mL volumetric pipette with carbonate solution and transfer an aliquot to a conical flask. Add methyl orange. Rinse a burette with the unknown HCl, fill it, remove tip bubbles and record the initial meniscus at eye level. Titrate while swirling to a persistent endpoint; take one rough run, then at least three concordant accurate runs. A few mL distilled water can rinse flask walls without changing carbonate moles. Use a pipette filler, never mouth pipette.

**Variables and results.** Independent: volume of HCl delivered. Dependent: indicator endpoint. Control carbonate aliquot, standard concentration, indicator amount and endpoint rule. Realistic accurate titres: 23.60, 23.65 and 23.60 mL; rough titre 24.1 mL excluded. Mean accurate titre = (23.60 + 23.65 + 23.60)/3 = 23.6167 mL, recorded as 23.62 mL. Concordance criterion here is a range of 0.05 mL, within the site's 0.10 mL convention.

**Analysis and working.** M(Na2CO3) = 106.0 g/mol. Standard concentration = (1.3250/106.0)/0.2500 = 0.05000 mol/L. Na2CO3 + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H2O + CO2. Carbonate in aliquot = 0.05000 × 0.02500 = 0.001250 mol. HCl at endpoint = 2 × 0.001250 = 0.002500 mol. Unknown concentration = 0.002500/0.0236167 = 0.10586 mol/L, report 0.1059 mol/L. The arithmetic uses the unrounded mean.

**VRA.** Validity: known primary-standard concentration, balanced 1:2 ratio and a suitable endpoint. Reliability: concordant independent titres, excluding only the declared rough run. Accuracy: meniscus at eye level, conditioned burette/pipette, removed tip bubble and accurate volumetric glassware. A biased endpoint can still yield concordant but inaccurate titres.

| Hazard and classification | Control |
| --- | --- |
| Sodium carbonate powder: eye irritation possible; avoid airborne powder | Safety glasses, small weighed mass, clean spills. [RSC titration teaching resource](https://edu.rsc.org/resources/titration/2258.article) covers carbonate standardisation. |
| Unknown dilute HCl, nominal about 0.106 mol/L: near a supplier's non-hazardous 0.1 M classification, but eye splashes matter | Goggles, burette below eye level while filling, wash spills. [Southern Biological 0.1 M HCl SDS](https://www.southernbiological.com/content/MSDS/HydrochloricAcid0.1M_SDS_2025.pdf). Verify actual unknown bottle before use. |
| Indicator solution may be alcohol-based and flammable | Use a few drops, cap bottle, keep flames away; check chosen product label. [RSC school titration guidance](https://edu.rsc.org/experiments/microscale-titration-11-16-years/4020614.article) flags alcohol-based indicator flammability. |

**Errors and fixes.** A water-rinsed burette dilutes HCl and raises the apparent titre: condition with HCl. A bubble leaving the tip makes delivered-volume arithmetic too large: expel before initial reading. Rinsing the conical flask with carbonate adds unmeasured moles: rinse it only with water.

**HSC-style question idea.** Students obtain 23.60, 23.65, 23.60 and 24.10 mL, with the last declared a rough run. Calculate c(HCl), then explain the direction of error if the burette was rinsed only with water.
