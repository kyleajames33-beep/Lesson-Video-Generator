## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**Teaching order.**

**L10:**

1. Strong acid plus strong base is compared with the net reaction H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O and ΔHn near −57 kJ/mol.
2. The page attributes a less negative value with weak reactants to heat absorbed during their ionisation.
3. It uses particle-level proton transfer to explain the comparison.
4. A fair foam cup test keeps volumes and concentrations the same, records temperature rise and calculates −q/n(H₂O). 5. pH measurement offers a second way to distinguish the tested acids.

**Worked examples and numbers from the extracts.**

**L10:**

- Two 50.0 mL + 50.0 mL, 1.00 mol/L acid/NaOH trials start at 21.0°C. HCl reaches 27.8°C: ΔHn = −56.8 kJ/mol. CH₃COOH reaches 27.2°C: ΔHn = −51.8 kJ/mol; the page attributes a 5.0 kJ/mol difference to ionisation.
- Unknowns X, Y, Z start at 20.0°C and reach 26.6, 26.8 and 25.9°C: ΔHn = −55.2, −56.8 and −49.3 kJ/mol. The page classifies Y strong, X and Z weak; ionisation differences from Y are +1.6 and +7.5 kJ/mol.
- Three 1.00 mol/L acids: HCl pH 0.00, HNO₂ (Ka = 4.5 × 10⁻⁴) pH 1.67, CH₃COOH (Ka = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵) pH 2.37. The page gives HNO₂ ΔHn about −54 to −55 and CH₃COOH about −55 to −56 kJ/mol.

**Practice.**

**L10:** Design a fair calorimetry comparison, calculate ΔHn, classify unknown acids and compare pH as complementary data. Representative: “Two experiments use identical 50.0 mL + 50.0 mL, 1.00 mol/L acid + 1.00 mol/L NaOH setups. Experiment A (HCl + NaOH): T_initial = 21.0°C, T_max = 27.8°C. Experiment B (CH₃COOH + NaOH): T_initial = 21.0°C, T_max = 27.2°C.”

The extracts record no named mnemonic unless shown above. Keep any useful contrast or question from the page; do not manufacture a memory aid.

## 3. Number and science check

100 g × 4.18 J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹ × 6.8 °C ÷ 0.0500 mol = 56.8 kJ/mol, with a negative reaction sign. A 6.2 °C rise gives −51.8 kJ/mol. Arithmetic checks. Ka alone cannot rank neutralisation enthalpy, and the page’s HNO₂/acetic-acid ranking contradicts its own claim. See corrections register. Relevant site-correction register entries: L10. Use the corrected statements in that register. Source for flagged corrections: [Module 6 site corrections](../site-corrections-m6.md).

**Site correction:** Site L10 ranks neutralisation enthalpy from Ka alone, then gives a numerical example in the opposite order. Ka does not determine ionisation enthalpy by itself. See [Module 6 site corrections](../site-corrections-m6.md) for the source and replacement statement.

## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes

**L10:**

- A smaller ΔT for a weak acid means neutralisation is incomplete: the page says ionisation absorbs heat while neutralisation completes.
- The −57 kJ/mol value changes with the named strong acid: the page uses the same net ionic equation.
- Weak acid is fully ionised before mixing: OH⁻ drives further ionisation as it reacts.

These traps come from the linked site lessons. They are not attributed to NESA markers without a specific exam report.
