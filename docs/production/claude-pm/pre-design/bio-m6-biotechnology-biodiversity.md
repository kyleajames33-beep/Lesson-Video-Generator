## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**Teaching order.**

**L11:**

1. Judge biotechnology at genetic, species and ecosystem levels separately.
2. Reliance on a few successful crop varieties can narrow genetic variation and increase vulnerability to disease or environmental change.
3. Conservation genetics can identify at-risk populations, guide breeding and support threatened species.
4. A sound judgement names the level affected and weighs benefits against risks and context.
**L12:**

1. A biotechnology evaluation identifies its benefit, risk, stakeholders and biodiversity effect before reaching a conditional conclusion.
2. Medical, agricultural and animal uses have different benefits and concerns, so one verdict does not fit them all.
3. A biodiversity question needs genetic, species or ecosystem effects, not productivity alone.
4. A synthesis response compares cases, weighs trade-offs and states when its conclusion holds.

**Worked examples and numbers from the extracts.**

**L11:**

- The seven-mark evaluation compares crop uniformity, herbicide-tolerant and insect-resistant crops, land sparing and conservation gene banks, then makes a judgement that depends on the farming baseline.
- The six-mark analysis traces two routes by which a modified crop could reduce genetic diversity in a compatible wild relative: introgression with selection at linked loci, and repeated crossing that swamps a small wild population with crop alleles.
**L12:**

- The eight-mark evaluation weighs agricultural benefits of Bt, herbicide-tolerant and drought-resistant crops against genetic uniformity, field-margin effects and resistance evolution. It considers reversibility and the farming baseline.
- The six-mark synthesis traces one DNA base change through a protein, organism fitness and ecosystem biodiversity.

**Practice.**

**L11:** Misconception challenge, multiple choice, a three-level classification activity, a mixed-outcome paragraph, two guided extended answers and three short answers. Representative question: “Evaluate the claim: If a biotechnology helps one species survive, then it must improve biodiversity overall.”
**L12:** Benefit-risk paragraph, comparison of medical and agricultural cases, multiple choice, true or false, two guided extended responses and three short answers. Representative question: “Evaluate the use of genetic technologies in agriculture, weighing benefits against effects on biodiversity.”

The extracts record no named mnemonic unless shown above. Keep any useful contrast or question from the page; do not manufacture a memory aid.

## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes

**L11:**

- Biodiversity means only species count. The page uses genetic, species and ecosystem levels.
- Improved crop productivity must increase biodiversity. Production and diversity can move in different directions.
- Biotechnology always helps or always harms biodiversity. Effects depend on the technique and context.
**L12:**

- A strong answer lists all relevant facts. The page requires connected evidence and a judgement.
- Higher yield answers a biodiversity question. The page requires a named biodiversity level and effect.
- A gene drive always spreads completely and irreversibly. The page identifies resistance, fitness, design and population structure as limits.

These traps come from the linked site lessons. They are not attributed to NESA markers without a specific exam report.
