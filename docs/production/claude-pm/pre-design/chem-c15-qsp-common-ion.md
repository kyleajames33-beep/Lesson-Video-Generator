## Site order and full numerical material

Get oriented (kidney-stone prediction) → Qsp and the decision rule → The dilution step everyone forgets → The common ion effect → Temperature and Ksp → kidney-stone context → worked examples → activities → practice questions → retrieve and reflect. Qsp uses the Ksp expression with current ion concentrations. Qsp > Ksp means supersaturation and precipitation; equality means saturation; Qsp < Ksp means unsaturated, no precipitate. The page's aid is: **dilute each ion first, then calculate Qsp, then compare with Ksp**. It also uses “too many ions → solid forms.” A common ion lowers solubility while Ksp stays fixed at one temperature.

**Embedded calculations:** Mixing 50.0 mL of 2.0 × 10⁻³ mol/L AgNO₃ with 50.0 mL of 2.0 × 10⁻³ mol/L NaCl gives total 100.0 mL, [Ag⁺] = [Cl⁻] = 1.0 × 10⁻³ mol/L, Qsp = 1.0 × 10⁻⁶ versus AgCl Ksp = 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁰. Without dilution Qsp is wrongly 4.0 × 10⁻⁶. AgCl in pure water: s = 1.342 × 10⁻⁵; in 0.10 mol/L NaCl, s ≈ 1.8 × 10⁻⁹, a 7,454-fold reduction (site rounds 7,500). The site's immediate Qsp after adding Cl⁻ to initially saturated AgCl is 1.342 × 10⁻⁶, greater than Ksp. Interactive check asks for Qsp when both mixed AgCl solutions start at 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ mol/L; equal-volume mixing gives 2.5 × 10⁻⁹.

**Worked example 1:** 25.0 mL 4.0 × 10⁻³ mol/L Pb(NO₃)₂ plus 75.0 mL 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ mol/L Na₂SO₄; total 100.0 mL. [Pb²⁺] = 1.0 × 10⁻³, [SO₄²⁻] = 1.5 × 10⁻⁴ mol/L. Qsp = 1.5 × 10⁻⁷ > stated PbSO₄ Ksp 1.6 × 10⁻⁸, so precipitate. **Worked example 2:** CaF₂ Ksp 3.9 × 10⁻¹¹: pure-water s = 2.137 × 10⁻⁴ mol/L; in 0.10 mol/L NaF, s ≈ 3.9 × 10⁻⁹ (2s = 7.8 × 10⁻⁹, so approximation holds), about 54,800 times less. **Worked example 3:** 100.0 mL 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ mol/L CaCl₂ plus 100.0 mL 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ mol/L Na₂SO₄: after mixing [Ca²⁺] = [SO₄²⁻] = 5.0 × 10⁻⁶; Qsp = 2.5 × 10⁻¹¹ < stated CaSO₄ Ksp 4.9 × 10⁻⁵; no precipitate. All arithmetic checks.

**Practice questions:** predict/reveal AgCl in water versus 0.10 mol/L NaCl; true/false on Qsp < Ksp, dilution, unchanged Ksp and endothermic warming; interactive Qsp predictor; three application questions on water treatment, comparing the three Qsp cases, and a borderline mixing error. Short answers: (1) 30.0 mL 5.0 × 10⁻³ mol/L Ba(NO₃)₂ plus 70.0 mL 3.0 × 10⁻³ mol/L Na₂SO₄ gives [Ba²⁺] 1.5 × 10⁻³, [SO₄²⁻] 2.1 × 10⁻³, Qsp 3.15 × 10⁻⁶ > Ksp 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁰; (2) AgCl in 0.050 mol/L Cl⁻ gives [Ag⁺] 3.6 × 10⁻⁹; (3) 0.050 mol/L Cl⁻ and 5.0 × 10⁻⁴ mol/L CrO₄²⁻: AgCl starts at [Ag⁺] 3.6 × 10⁻⁹, Ag₂CrO₄ at 4.690 × 10⁻⁵ using Ksp 1.1 × 10⁻¹²; (4) Na₂SO₄ lowers CaSO₄ solubility. Calculation practice: equal volumes of 2.0 × 10⁻³ mol/L Ba(NO₃)₂ and Na₂SO₄ give Qsp 1.0 × 10⁻⁶ > 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁰; PbI₂ Ksp 7.1 × 10⁻⁹ in 0.010 mol/L KI gives s ≈ 7.10 × 10⁻⁵ versus pure-water s = 1.211 × 10⁻³. Extended response: groundwater [Ca²⁺] 2.0 × 10⁻³, [F⁻] 1.5 × 10⁻⁴, [CO₃²⁻] 8.0 × 10⁻⁴ mol/L gives Qsp(CaF₂) 4.5 × 10⁻¹¹ > Ksp 3.9 × 10⁻¹¹ and Qsp(CaCO₃) 1.6 × 10⁻⁶ > Ksp 3.4 × 10⁻⁹. It then raises [F⁻] to 0.020 mol/L and asks for new CaF₂ solubility.

**Named misconceptions:** reversing the Qsp inequality, omitting dilution, thinking a common ion changes Ksp, assuming adding a common ion always causes precipitation, and changing formula subscripts to balance equations. Temperature section says endothermic dissolution generally raises Ksp when warmed and cites KNO₃/NaCl; exothermic dissolution lowers it and cites Ce₂(SO₄)₃. Context examples include silver chloride, lead sulfate, calcium fluoride, hot-water CaCO₃ scale and calcium oxalate kidney stones.

## Number check and corrections

The calculations above were independently recomputed. AgCl 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁰ and CaF₂ 3.9 × 10⁻¹¹ agree with this [25 °C college reference table](https://www.cgc.edu/sites/default/files/inline-files/solubility-rules-and-activity-series.pdf). The site's PbSO₄ Ksp 1.6 × 10⁻⁸ at 25 °C is defensible: [MIT OpenCourseWare](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/5-111sc-principles-of-chemical-science-fall-2014/7e17795c825ede37725ff7cbfd2de2cc_MIT5_111F14_Lec20Soln.pdf) derives 1.96 × 10⁻⁸ from measured solubility, [CSU Dominguez Hills](https://www2.csudh.edu/oliver/chemdata/data-ksp.htm) lists 1.6 × 10⁻⁸, and [University of Texas at Austin](https://ch302.cm.utexas.edu/worksheets/old%20versions/RICE-table-wkst-KEY1.pdf) uses 2.53 × 10⁻⁸. The college table above gives 6.3 × 10⁻⁷, an outlying value. Worked example 1 correctly predicts precipitation with its stated Ksp. Choose and cite one reference table; mixing tables can change a borderline conclusion. The extended-response request for “new molar solubility ... in this water” ignores the existing 2.0 × 10⁻³ mol/L Ca²⁺: after adding F⁻ to 0.020 mol/L, Qsp is 8.0 × 10⁻⁷, well above Ksp, so CaF₂ precipitates. Simply calculating Ksp/(0.020)² = 9.75 × 10⁻⁸ mol/L gives final equilibrium [Ca²⁺] only if enough solid can form; it is not an amount of new CaF₂ dissolved. The raw page's 2.3 × 10⁻⁹ CaC₂O₄ Ksp “at 37 °C” has no source in the page; `site-corrections.md` marks it unclear. The site's AgCl superscripts render correctly in raw HTML; the extract's broken entities were an extraction artefact, as `site-fixes-applied.md` confirms.
