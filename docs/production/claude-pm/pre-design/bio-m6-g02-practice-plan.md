# Draft for Claude's review: bio-m6-g02 practice plan

Main question: How do we evaluate a biotechnology claim with a mechanism, a comparison and a justified judgement?

Route: after B19. Entry knowledge: B09 to B13 explain biotechnology, stakeholders and biodiversity at genetic, species and ecosystem levels; B15, B16 and B18 explain reproductive and genetic methods; B19 compares agricultural, medical and industrial benefits. Site lessons: Biology Module 6 L08 to L12 and L14 to L17. These are three original HSC-style extended-response questions. “Evaluate” requires a judgement supported by criteria and evidence; “assess” requires a reasoned judgement of value or effect. Neither verb is answered by a benefit-risk list alone. Keep the entire question and its demands visible during each attempt countdown. Reveal each causal link while explaining it and finish with a brief model answer, then cross one classic mistake.

## Q1. Assess insect-resistant cotton as an agricultural biotechnology (6 marks)

**Question.** A cotton farm replaces one conventional variety with an insect-resistant variety containing a gene that makes a protein toxic to a target caterpillar pest. The farm previously sprayed an insecticide against that pest. Assess the likely benefits and biodiversity effects of this change. Refer to the farming baseline and at least two levels of biodiversity.

**Worked response.** The inserted gene causes the cotton to produce a protein that can kill susceptible caterpillars when they feed, so the farm may lose less crop to that pest and may need fewer sprays targeting it. Fewer applications could reduce exposure of non-target organisms, although that outcome depends on which insecticide was previously used and how the crop is managed. If the farm plants only one high-performing cotton variety across a wide area, variation **among cotton plants** may fall, making the crop population more vulnerable to a new disease or environmental change. Repeated exposure can select resistant pest populations, weakening the initial benefit and potentially changing later pesticide use. Effects on **species diversity** in the field and margins depend on the insects present, spray changes and habitat management. On this baseline, the technology has a credible pest-control benefit, but its biodiversity value cannot be inferred from yield alone. A fair decision would compare pest losses, spray records and biodiversity measures against the previous farm system over several seasons, while maintaining crop variation and resistance management.

**Why this earns the verb.** It traces gene to organism to farm outcome, names genetic and species-level effects, compares the prior system and makes a conditional judgement. Cross out “higher yield proves greater biodiversity”.

## Q2. Evaluate animal cloning for conservation (7 marks)

**Question.** A conservation programme has preserved living cells from a rare mammal. It proposes using somatic cell nuclear transfer and a related species as egg donor and surrogate to produce offspring. Evaluate this plan as a way to protect the rare species. Include the method, genetic diversity, animal welfare and one alternative.

**Worked response.** In somatic cell nuclear transfer, a nucleus from a preserved body cell is placed in an egg whose nucleus has been removed. If the embryo develops and a surrogate carries it, the offspring's nuclear DNA largely matches the cell donor. This could recover an animal from stored cells and add individuals to a very small population. It does **not** automatically add new nuclear genetic variation: repeated clones of one donor reproduce largely the same nuclear genotype. Using cells from several genetically different donors would better support diversity, but the programme must still establish whether the offspring survive, reproduce and can enter a suitable habitat. Egg collection, embryo loss and surrogate pregnancies also impose welfare costs, so the expected conservation benefit must justify those costs. Protected habitat and breeding among existing genetically different animals should be compared with cloning, because more animals alone will not remove the original threat. I would support a carefully monitored trial only if donor diversity, welfare safeguards and a viable habitat plan are in place; cloning by itself is not a complete conservation strategy.

**Why this earns the verb.** It follows the actual steps of nuclear transfer, distinguishes headcount from genetic variation, weighs welfare and compares an alternative before judging. Cross out “every clone increases genetic diversity”.

**Source videos:** bio-m6-b10, bio-m6-b12, bio-m6-b13, bio-m6-b16, bio-m6-b19.

## Q3. Evaluate benefits across medicine and industry (7 marks)

**Question.** A company uses genetically modified microorganisms to manufacture a human therapeutic protein and an enzyme for food processing. Evaluate the benefits of this biotechnology to society compared with obtaining each product from animals. Include how the microorganisms make the products, access and ethical costs, and a final judgement.

**Worked response.** Researchers place an appropriate protein-coding DNA sequence into a vector and introduce it into a microbial host. Cells that express the sequence make the desired protein, which is then recovered and purified. For a human therapeutic protein, the method can provide a consistent supply without extracting the protein from large numbers of animals. For a food-processing enzyme, microbial manufacture can also supply a consistent product at scale, and a non-animal source may suit users who avoid animal-derived ingredients. These benefits depend on the engineered product meeting quality and safety standards; producing a protein does not itself prove clinical effectiveness or food suitability. A proprietary process may keep prices high, so reliable production is not the same as equitable access. It may reduce use of animals as a protein source, but it still uses energy, materials and controlled waste handling. Overall, the technology offers strong potential benefit in both sectors when the purified products work as intended and are affordable; access and manufacturing impacts should be assessed alongside output. The medical benefit matters most where a patient needs the therapeutic protein, while the industrial benefit is usually judged against cost, performance and the previous animal source.

**Why this earns the verb.** It links DNA to protein production, compares two applications with their previous sources, considers beneficiaries and access, and ends with a reasoned judgement. Cross out “the new method is better because it is modern”.

**Source videos:** bio-m6-b09, bio-m6-b10, bio-m6-b18, bio-m6-b19.

## Design handoff

Use one recurring evaluation board with four plain prompts: what the method does, who gains, what could be harmed, and what the judgement depends on. Each question uses a relevant painted organism or apparatus plate, with no people or hands. During the response, reveal one causal chain and only the current comparison. The final answer may briefly gather the evidence into a judgement. This is a teaching plan, not approved narration or a scene design.
