## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**Teaching order.**

**L10:**

1. Emerging biotechnology trends toward more targeted intervention through gene editing, synthetic biology, precision breeding and disease screening.
2. Potential benefits include resilient crops, earlier disease detection, targeted treatment and specialised industrial production.
3. Current clinical capability must be separated from a claim of universal safety, effectiveness or access.
4. The page assesses future benefit against need, feasibility and fairness, including regulation, cost and scale.

**Worked examples and numbers from the extracts.**

**L10:** An ex vivo sickle-cell treatment extracts blood-forming stem cells, edits BCL11A regulation to reactivate foetal haemoglobin and reinfuses the cells. The page says early participants had major reductions in severe events; the sickle mutation itself was not corrected. The matching activity pairs gene editing with targeted change, screening with earlier detection, precision breeding with desired traits, and synthetic biology with specialised production.

**Practice.**

**L10:** Future-direction matching, overclaim rewrite, multiple choice and three short answers. Representative: “Outline two future directions of biotechnology.” “Evaluate why CRISPR or another gene-editing technology is a useful example of future biotechnology, but should not be described as a guaranteed solution to major social problems.”

The extracts record no named mnemonic unless shown above. Keep any useful contrast or question from the page; do not manufacture a memory aid.

## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes

**L10:**

- An advanced technique guarantees social benefit. Safety, cost, access and reproducibility matter.
- CRISPR has no off-target effects. The page says guide RNAs may bind unintended sequences.
- Every emerging technology is already a universal solution. Current evidence and future prediction are distinguished.

These traps come from the linked site lessons. They are not attributed to NESA markers without a specific exam report.
