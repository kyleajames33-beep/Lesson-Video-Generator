## Exact syllabus words

> conduct an investigation to determine solubility rules, and predict and analyse the composition of substances when two ionic solutions are mixed, for example:
> - potassium chloride and silver nitrate
> - potassium iodide and lead nitrate
> - sodium sulfate and barium nitrate (ACSCH065)

## Site order and material

Get oriented and a four-step card; NAGSAG rules; four-ion prediction; three syllabus example reactions; 5 × 5 well-plate investigation; molecular/full/net ionic equations; water treatment; two worked examples; consolidation, practice and extended response. NAGSAG means nitrates, ammonium, Group 1, sulfates, acetates, Group 17 halides, then other ions. It treats nitrate, ammonium and Group 1 compounds as soluble. Sulfate exceptions include BaSO₄ and PbSO₄; CaSO₄ and Ag₂SO₄ are sparingly soluble. Halide exceptions include Ag⁺ and Pb²⁺. Carbonates and most hydroxides are generally insoluble except with Group 1/NH₄⁺, with hydroxide exceptions. Four-ion method: list ions, make two crossed cation-anion pairs, assess both, then write equations. Three reactions: KCl + AgNO₃ → AgCl(s) white + KNO₃; 2KI + Pb(NO₃)₂ → PbI₂(s) bright yellow + 2KNO₃; Na₂SO₄ + Ba(NO₃)₂ → BaSO₄(s) white + 2NaNO₃.

Investigation uses 0.1 mol L⁻¹ dropper solutions, a 24/96 well plate, five cations (Na⁺, Ca²⁺, Ba²⁺, Pb²⁺, Ag⁺) and five anions (Cl⁻, I⁻, SO₄²⁻, CO₃²⁻, OH⁻). Predict 25 cells, add three drops of each, record precipitate and colour, write equations, derive only rules evidenced by the matrix. The page asks why PbCl₂/PbI₂ respond differently to warming. This is a conduct-an-investigation requirement; a video can prepare or analyse it, not replace students' practical work.

Worked 1: Na₂CO₃ + CaCl₂ forms white CaCO₃; KNO₃ + NaCl gives no precipitate; FeCl₃ + 3NaOH gives rusty brown Fe(OH)₃. Net equations are Ca²⁺ + CO₃²⁻ → CaCO₃(s) and Fe³⁺ + 3OH⁻ → Fe(OH)₃(s). Worked 2: water has Pb²⁺ 5.0 × 10⁻³ mol L⁻¹ plus Na⁺, Ca²⁺, Cl⁻ and NO₃⁻. It compares sulfate versus carbonate and declares Na₂CO₃ preferred, with Pb²⁺ + CO₃²⁻ → PbCO₃(s). The stated Pb concentration is not used in a calculation.

Practice: FeCl₃ + NaOH (3 marks); evaluate Na₂SO₄ versus Na₂CO₃ when Pb²⁺, Na⁺, Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, Cl⁻, NO₃⁻ coexist (4); 50 mL 0.10 mol L⁻¹ MgCl₂ + 50 mL 0.10 mol L⁻¹ Na₂CO₃, make white MgCO₃ and all equation forms (4); explain source-independent AgCl net equation (3). Extended wastewater case has Pb²⁺ 8.0 × 10⁻³ and Fe³⁺ 5.0 × 10⁻³, adds NaOH then Na₂SO₄. Memory aids: NAGSAG priority, four-ion method, three example colours, five-step equation procedure (identify solid, molecular, split aqueous, cancel spectators, check charge).

## Number and science check

- All worked molecular/net equations balance atoms and charge. For the 50 mL + 50 mL practice case, each initial solute has 0.0050 mol; after mixing [Mg²⁺] and [CO₃²⁻] are each 0.050 mol L⁻¹ before reaction. The one-to-one stoichiometry allows up to 0.0050 mol MgCO₃. The page states no product mass.
- Site correction, **“Worked examples”, Worked Example 2 and Practice SA2 in the raw site lesson**: its own analysis says “Na₂CO₃ would co-precipitate Ca²⁺ as well,” yet the summary says “Na₂CO₃ preferred for Pb²⁺ removal; Na₂SO₄ risks CaSO₄ co-precipitation.” The preference cannot be decided from Pb²⁺ = 5.0 × 10⁻³ alone: Ca²⁺ concentration, added reagent amount and target selectivity are missing, and carbonate sacrifices Ca²⁺ more readily. The same issue grows with Mg²⁺ in SA2. [University Ksp table at 25 °C](https://chem.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Introductory_Chemistry/Book%3A_Introductory_Chemistry_%28CK-12%29/19%3A_Equilibrium/19.13%3A_Conversion_of_%28K_textsp%29_to_Solubility) gives CaCO₃ 4.5 × 10⁻⁹, CaSO₄ 2.4 × 10⁻⁵, PbCO₃ 7.4 × 10⁻¹⁴ and PbSO₄ 6.3 × 10⁻⁷. Correct answer: both can precipitate Pb²⁺ and both may co-precipitate other ions; choose only after concentrations and treatment goal are stated. Do not call carbonate selective.
- Site rule calls PbCl₂/PbBr₂/PbI₂ “all insoluble”, but its own well-plate analysis says PbCl₂ and PbI₂ differ in warm water. The [25 °C Ksp table](https://chem.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Introductory_Chemistry/Book%3A_Introductory_Chemistry_%28CK-12%29/19%3A_Equilibrium/19.13%3A_Conversion_of_%28K_textsp%29_to_Solubility) lists PbCl₂ 1.7 × 10⁻⁵ versus PbI₂ 7.1 × 10⁻⁹. Use “sparingly soluble” for PbCl₂ and consider concentrations; simple classification is a first pass.
- Historical hook: the page credits Fox Talbot's “1856 photographic fixing process” based on AgCl precipitation without a source. Exclude pending a primary historical source; fixing dissolves residual silver halide with thiosulfate, distinct from forming a precipitate.
