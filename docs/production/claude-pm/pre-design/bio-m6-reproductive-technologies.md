# Module 6 pre-design: Reproductive Technologies, Artificial Insemination and Artificial Pollination. How do the two methods control fertilisation and inherited traits?

Sources: [L14 extract](../site-extracts/bio-m6-l14.md) and [site page](https://hscscience.com.au/subjects/biology/year12/module6/lesson14.sa). These are site teaching notes for pre-design.

## 1. Exact 2017 syllabus content

> compare the processes and outcomes of reproductive technologies, including but not limited to:
> artificial insemination
> artificial pollination

Source: [NESA Biology Stage 6 Syllabus (2017)](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/science/biology-stage-6-2017), Module 6.

## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**Teaching order.**

**L14:**

1. Both methods choose which parents contribute gametes; neither directly edits a gene sequence.
2. Artificial insemination collects, prepares and introduces selected semen into a female at the appropriate time.
3. Artificial pollination transfers selected pollen to a chosen stigma while excluding unwanted pollen.
4. Both make desired trait combinations more likely, but meiosis and fertilisation still produce variation.

**Worked examples and numbers from the extracts.**

**L14:**

- A six-mark comparison contrasts semen collection, storage and timed introduction with pollen collection, stigma transfer, anther removal and bagging. It compares parental reach and diversity effects.
- A six-mark analysis considers 10,000 cows bred to five bulls to explain how heavy use of few sires can reduce effective population size and diversity.

**Practice.**

**L14:** Two three-step flowcharts, a farmer-claim response, multiple choice, true or false, two guided extended answers and three short answers. Representative question: “Compare the processes and outcomes of artificial insemination and artificial pollination as reproductive technologies.”

The extracts record no named mnemonic unless shown above. Keep any useful contrast or question from the page; do not manufacture a memory aid.

## 3. Number and science check

The 10,000-cows/five-bulls case is an effective-population-size warning, not a claim that five bulls alone determine every allele frequency. Source for flagged corrections: [Module 6 site corrections](../site-corrections-m6.md).

## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes

**L14:**

- “Artificial” means DNA was edited. Both methods change the reproductive event.
- Selected parents guarantee the desired offspring. The methods increase probability, not certainty.
- Selective breeding produces only desired traits. The page notes increased homozygosity and harmful recessive traits.

These traps come from the linked site lessons. They are not attributed to NESA markers without a specific exam report.
