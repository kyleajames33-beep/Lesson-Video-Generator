## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**Teaching order.**

**L02:**

1. A mutagen increases the chance of DNA damage or copying error; a lasting mutation results when an unrepaired sequence change is copied.
2. UV radiation can form abnormal bonds between neighbouring bases, while ionising radiation can break DNA strands.
3. Chemicals can modify bases, mimic bases or interfere with DNA replication.
4. Natural sources include background radiation and some viruses that insert genetic material into host DNA.
5. The page plans a UV exposure investigation with a zero-second control, repeated trials and colony counts; it says fewer colonies alone cannot distinguish mutation from cell death.

**Worked examples and numbers from the extracts.**

**L02:** The UV and baker’s yeast investigation varies exposure time at 0, 15, 30, 60 and 120 seconds at fixed lamp distance. Mean colonies per plate across three trials are 142, 98, 61, 24 and 5 respectively. The page also offers radish or mung bean germination as an alternative. It identifies exposure time as independent, viable colonies or germination as dependent, and inoculum, medium, lamp and incubation conditions as controls.

**Practice.**

**L02:** Mutagen matcher, checks, source-to-mechanism matching, risk explanation, multiple choice and three short answers. Representative: “Explain the difference between a mutagen and a mutation.” “Compare UV radiation, ionising radiation and chemical mutagens in terms of how they increase mutation risk.”

The extracts record no named mnemonic unless shown above. Keep any useful contrast or question from the page; do not manufacture a memory aid.

## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes

**L02:**

- A mutagen is the same as a mutation. The first is an agent; the second is a DNA sequence change.
- Exposure guarantees mutation. Repair can prevent a lasting change.
- Natural mutagens are harmless. Background radiation and some viruses can damage DNA.
- Only germ-line cells are affected. Somatic cells can also acquire mutations.

These traps come from the linked site lessons. They are not attributed to NESA markers without a specific exam report.
