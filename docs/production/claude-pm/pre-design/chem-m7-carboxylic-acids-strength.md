## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**L14 order:** Connect: from wine to vinegar; Explain: carboxyl structure, naming and dimers; Represent: explain boiling point and solubility trends; Respond: write weak-acid equations and observations; Feedback: discriminate acids using structure and tests; Apply: identify an unknown and justify each inference.

**L18 order:** Connect: diagnose three unknown solutions; Explain: interpret Ka and pKa; Represent: stabilise the conjugate base; Respond: predict diagnostic observations; Feedback: compare amines and amides; Apply: identify and rank unknowns.

**L14 key ideas:** 1. The carboxyl group (-COOH) is not simply a carbonyl plus a hydroxyl side by side, the two parts interact electronically in ways that give carboxylic acids properties that neither aldehydes, ketones, nor alcohols possess individually.
2. Carboxylic acids consistently have the highest boiling points among organic compounds of comparable chain length, higher than even alcohols, and the explanation is dimerisation, not simply "strong H-bonds.".
3. Carboxylic acids are weak acids, they partially ionise in water rather than fully dissociating, and this equilibrium behaviour, learned in Module 6, now applies directly to predicting which reagents they react with and at what rate.
4. The acid strength ranking of organic compounds directly reflects the structural stability of their conjugate bases, the more stable the conjugate base, the stronger the acid, and this can be predicted from structure without memorising a list.
5. Given: Propanoic acid (CH₃CH₂COOH).
6. Identifying an Unknown from Test Results.

**L18 key ideas:** 1. pKa is not just a number, it is a ranking of how willing a molecule is to give up a proton.
2. The pKa difference between carboxylic acid, phenol, and alcohol is not arbitrary, it is a direct consequence of how stable the conjugate base is after the proton is lost.
3. The reactions of NaOH, Na₂CO₃, and NaHCO₃ with organic acid classes are thermodynamic probes, each tells you whether the organic acid is stronger than the reference acid produced in the reaction.
4. The pKb scale ranks organic bases by the same principle as pKa ranks acids, the more available the lone pair for proton acceptance, the stronger the base.
5. (a) Ethanoic acid + Na₂CO₃ (b) Phenol + NaHCO₃ (c) Propan-1-amine + HCl (d) Ethanamide + HCl.
