# Module 6 pre-design: Cloning, Whole-Organism and Gene Cloning. What does each method copy, and how effective is it for its purpose?

Sources: [L15 extract](../site-extracts/bio-m6-l15.md) and [site page](https://hscscience.com.au/subjects/biology/year12/module6/lesson15.sa). These are site teaching notes for pre-design.

## 1. Exact 2017 syllabus content

> investigate and assess the effectiveness of cloning, including but not limited to:
> whole organism cloning
> gene cloning

Source: [NESA Biology Stage 6 Syllabus (2017)](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/science/biology-stage-6-2017), Module 6.

## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**Teaching order.**

**L15:**

1. Whole-organism cloning and gene cloning copy genetic material at different scales.
2. Somatic-cell nuclear transfer places a donor body-cell nucleus into an enucleated egg, then stimulates development and may implant an embryo.
3. Gene cloning inserts a selected sequence into a vector and host cell to make many copies.
4. Assess whole-organism cloning by live-birth success, genetic identity and health; assess gene cloning by its narrower aim of copying a sequence.

**Worked examples and numbers from the extracts.**

**L15:**

- Dolly the sheep was produced through nuclear transfer from an adult udder cell after 277 attempts. The seven-mark assessment uses the success rate, recipient egg mitochondria and developmental health to judge effectiveness.
- A five-mark distinction compares an entire organism's nuclear genome with multiple copies of one selected DNA sequence, using plasmid hosts or PCR.

**Practice.**

**L15:** Two-cloning-type comparison, company-claim evaluation, multiple choice, two guided extended answers and three short answers. Representative question: “Evaluate the statement: ‘Cloning is highly effective because it makes perfect copies.’”

The extracts record no named mnemonic unless shown above. Keep any useful contrast or question from the page; do not manufacture a memory aid.

## 3. Number and science check

Dolly followed 277 attempts in the cited example. That denominator describes the reported process, not a general success probability for all cloning methods. Source for flagged corrections: [Module 6 site corrections](../site-corrections-m6.md).

## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes

**L15:**

- All cloning makes a whole organism. Gene cloning copies a target sequence.
- A clone is identical in every way. Mitochondrial DNA, epigenetic state, development and environment may differ.
- Making one copy proves a method is highly effective. The page also considers success rate and health.

These traps come from the linked site lessons. They are not attributed to NESA markers without a specific exam report.
