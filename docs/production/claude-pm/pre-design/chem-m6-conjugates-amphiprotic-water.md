## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**Teaching order.**

**L07:**

1. Conjugate pairs differ by one proton and their acid/base strengths are inversely related.
2. An amphiprotic species can donate or accept H⁺ depending on its reaction partner.
3. HCO₃⁻ in NaHCO₃ and H₂PO₄⁻ in KH₂PO₄ are the named salt examples; HPO₄²⁻ is also shown.
4. The phosphate sequence is H₃PO₄ ⇌ H₂PO₄⁻ ⇌ HPO₄²⁻ ⇌ PO₄³⁻.
5. Water self-ionises: 2H₂O ⇌ H₃O⁺ + OH⁻, with Kw = [H₃O⁺][OH⁻] = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ at 25°C.
6. Neutrality means equal [H₃O⁺] and [OH⁻]; neutral pH changes with temperature.

**Worked examples and numbers from the extracts.**

**L07:**

- H₂PO₄⁻ + OH⁻ → HPO₄²⁻ + H₂O: acid H₂PO₄⁻, base OH⁻; pairs H₂PO₄⁻/HPO₄²⁻ and H₂O/OH⁻. H₂PO₄⁻ also accepts H⁺ to form H₃PO₄.
- At 50°C, Kw = 5.5 × 10⁻¹⁴: pure water has neutral pH 6.63, so pH 6.8 is basic; one calculation gives [OH⁻] = 2.2 × 10⁻¹¹ mol/L for an acidic solution.
- At pH 7.4 and 25°C, [OH⁻] = 2.51 × 10⁻⁷ mol/L. HPO₄²⁻ is amphiprotic; the page compares Ka3 = 4.8 × 10⁻¹³ with Kb = 1.6 × 10⁻⁷.

**Practice.**

**L07:** Write both proton-transfer equations for amphiprotic ions, identify conjugate pairs, sort species and calculate with Kw at different temperatures. Representative: “At 50°C, Kw = 5.5 × 10⁻¹⁴. (a) Calculate the pH of pure water at 50°C. (b) A solution at 50°C has pH 6.8. Is it acidic, basic, or neutral?”

The extracts record no named mnemonic unless shown above. Keep any useful contrast or question from the page; do not manufacture a memory aid.

## 3. Number and science check

At 50 °C, √(5.5 × 10⁻¹⁴) = 2.35 × 10⁻⁷ mol/L and neutral pH = 6.63. At 25 °C and pH 7.4, [OH⁻] = 10⁻⁶·⁶ = 2.51 × 10⁻⁷ mol/L. Arithmetic checks. Kw must be stated at the relevant temperature. Source for flagged corrections: [Module 6 site corrections](../site-corrections-m6.md).

## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes

**L07:**

- H₂SO₄/SO₄²⁻ is one conjugate pair: HSO₄⁻ is between them.
- Amphiprotic and amphoteric have identical scope: the page says amphoteric is broader.
- Neutral pH is always 7: at 37°C the page gives neutral pH about 6.81.
- Negative HPO₄²⁻ cannot donate H⁺: it retains an O–H bond.

These traps come from the linked site lessons. They are not attributed to NESA markers without a specific exam report.
