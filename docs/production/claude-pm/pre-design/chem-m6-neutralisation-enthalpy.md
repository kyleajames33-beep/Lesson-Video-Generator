## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**Teaching order.**

**L03:**

1. Strong acid plus strong base has the net ionic reaction H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O and a reference ΔHn near −57 kJ/mol water.
2. The page says ionisation of a weak acid consumes some heat during neutralisation, making the measured ΔHn less negative.
3. A foam cup experiment measures initial and maximum temperatures after mixing known concentrations and volumes.
4. Calculate solution heat with q = mcΔT, find the limiting reagent and moles of water, then ΔHn = −q/n(H₂O).
5. Heat loss lowers the measured maximum temperature; a lid, insulation and temperature logging improve the method.

**Worked examples and numbers from the extracts.**

**L03:**

- 50.0 mL 1.00 mol/L HCl + 50.0 mL 1.00 mol/L NaOH; 21.5 to 28.0°C, density 1.00 g/mL, c = 4.18 J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹: q = 2717 J, n(H₂O) = 0.0500 mol, ΔHn = −54.3 kJ/mol.
- Replace HCl with 50.0 mL 1.00 mol/L CH₃COOH; 21.5 to 26.8°C: q = 2215.4 J, ΔHn = −44.3 kJ/mol.
- 30.0 mL 2.00 mol/L HNO₃ + 50.0 mL 1.00 mol/L NaOH; 20.0 to 26.4°C: NaOH limits at 0.0500 mol; q = 2140 J; ΔHn = −42.8 kJ/mol.

**Practice.**

**L03:** Predict measured versus theoretical ΔHn, calculate limiting reagent, evaluate error and improve a calorimetry method. Representative: “30.0 mL of 2.00 mol/L HNO₃ is mixed with 50.0 mL of 1.00 mol/L NaOH. T i = 20.0°C; T max = 26.4°C. (a) Identify the limiting reagent. (b) Calculate q.”

The extracts record no named mnemonic unless shown above. Keep any useful contrast or question from the page; do not manufacture a memory aid.

## 3. Number and science check

50.0 mL + 50.0 mL at 1.00 g/mL gives 100 g. The 6.5 °C rise gives 2.717 kJ; divided by 0.0500 mol gives −54.3 kJ/mol. The 5.3 °C trial gives 2.215 kJ and −44.3 kJ/mol. The limiting NaOH trial gives 80 g × 4.18 × 6.4 = 2140 J, or −42.8 kJ/mol. Arithmetic checks. The blanket claim about all systematic errors is false; see the corrections register. Relevant site-correction register entries: L03. Use the corrected statements in that register. Source for flagged corrections: [Module 6 site corrections](../site-corrections-m6.md).

**Site correction:** Site L03 says every systematic error makes measured ΔH less negative. Heat loss tends to do that, but the direction of other systematic errors depends on their effect on q and reacting amount. ## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes

**L03:**

- All neutralisations have ΔHn = −57 kJ/mol: the page restricts this to strong acid plus strong base.
- Use only the acid mass in q = mcΔT: use the combined solution mass.
- Calculate ΔT as initial minus final: use final minus initial, then apply the negative sign in ΔHn.
- Say “human error”: name heat loss and a specific improvement.

These traps come from the linked site lessons. They are not attributed to NESA markers without a specific exam report.
