# Chemistry Module 6 P05 practical plan (draft for Claude's review)

**Aim and route.** Prepare an acetate buffer and show how it resists small additions of acid and base. `C6-IQ3-07`: “conduct a practical investigation to prepare a buffer and demonstrate its properties (ACSCH080)”. Site L13. Prerequisite: chem-m6-c11.

**School method.** Mix 50.0 mL 0.100 mol/L ethanoic acid with 50.0 mL 0.100 mol/L sodium ethanoate. This gives 100.0 mL buffer containing 0.00500 mol of each form. Dispense three 20.0 mL buffer aliquots and three 20.0 mL distilled-water controls into labelled beakers. Calibrate a pH probe, then add 1.00 mL 0.100 mol/L HCl to one buffer and one water beaker; add 1.00 mL 0.100 mol/L NaOH to a second pair. Leave the third pair untouched. Mix, wait for stable readings, rinse the probe between samples. The acid and base trials must use separate aliquots.

**Variables and results.** Independent: medium (buffer/water) and additive (acid/base), considered as two matched tests. Dependent: change in pH. Control starting volume, added moles, temperature and meter procedure. With Ka = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵, pKa = 4.745, buffer pH begins about 4.74. Each 20.0 mL aliquot has 0.00100 mol HA and 0.00100 mol A−. Adding 0.000100 mol HCl gives HA 0.00110 and A− 0.000900; pH = 4.745 + log(0.000900/0.00110) = 4.66. Adding NaOH swaps these amounts and gives pH 4.83. Water plus acid: [H+] ≈ 0.000100/0.0210 = 0.00476 mol/L, pH 2.32. Water plus base: pOH 2.32, pH 11.68 at 25 °C. The water-only initial pH is variable with dissolved CO2, so compare changes cautiously and report measured values.

**VRA.** Validity: same acid/base addition to equal volumes of buffer and water isolates buffering. Reliability: three independently prepared aliquot sets. Accuracy: calibrate probe across pH 4, 7 and 10, make solutions volumetrically and use pipettes for the 1.00 mL additions.

| 0.100 mol/L HCl: supplier SDS identifies it as non-hazardous at this dilution, but splashes matter | Safety glasses and small volumes. [Southern Biological 0.1 M HCl SDS](https://www.southernbiological.com/content/MSDS/HydrochloricAcid0.1M_SDS_2025.pdf). |

**Errors and fixes.** Using the same aliquot for sequential acid then base hides separate responses: split samples first. Adding unequal doses invalidates the comparison: use a 1.00 mL pipette. Calling the buffer constant-pH overstates the data: report the small measured changes and the finite capacity.

**HSC-style question idea.** Given initial and final pH for buffer and water after equal HCl additions, explain which solution is the buffer and calculate its predicted final pH from the acetate mole ratio.
