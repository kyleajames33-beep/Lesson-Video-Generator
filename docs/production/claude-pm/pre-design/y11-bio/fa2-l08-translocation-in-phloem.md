## 2. Site lesson material

Source: `C:/Users/kygs/hscscience/subjects/biology/year11/fa2-cells-to-systems/lesson08.sa.html` and `docs/production/claude-pm/site-extracts/bio-y11-fa2-l08.md`. Site section order: 1. Sources, Sinks and Translocation; 2. The Pressure-Flow Mechanism; 3. Why the Direction Can Reverse; 4. Why aphids tap into phloem, and how scientists use them. Relevant ideas:

- Sources, Sinks and Translocation: Phloem carries sugar from wherever it is made or stored to wherever it is being used, and these "from" and "to" points can change with the season.
- The Pressure-Flow Mechanism: When sugar is actively pumped into the phloem at the source, water follows by osmosis, raising the pressure there and forcing the sap to flow toward the lower-pressure sink.
- Why the Direction Can Reverse: Because translocation is driven by wherever sugar is loaded and unloaded, the direction of flow simply follows the changing positions of source and sink.
- Why aphids tap into phloem, and how scientists use them: Aphids feed by pushing a needle-like mouthpart straight into a single phloem sieve tube, and the sap is under such high pressure that it flows into the insect on its own, the aphid barely has to suck.

## 4. Mistakes and traps

The site names this misconception: Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: Sugar always travels downwards in phloem, from leaves to roots, because of gravity. ✓ Right: Translocation moves sugar from source to sink in whichever direction the sink lies, upward to growing shoots and fruit, or downward to roots. This is a page-backed teaching trap, not a claim about NESA marking trends.
