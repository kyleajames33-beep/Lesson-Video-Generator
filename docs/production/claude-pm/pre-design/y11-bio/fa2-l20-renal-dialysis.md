## 2. Site lesson material

Source: `C:/Users/kygs/hscscience/subjects/biology/year11/fa2-cells-to-systems/lesson20.sa.html` and `docs/production/claude-pm/site-extracts/bio-y11-fa2-l20.md`. Site section order: 1. How Dialysis Cleans the Blood; 2. Dialysis vs the Kidney; 3. Evaluating Your Sources; 4. Living on dialysis in Australia. Relevant ideas:

- How Dialysis Cleans the Blood: Dialysis is, at heart, controlled diffusion: blood flows past a membrane with a carefully designed fluid on the other side, so only the wastes leave.
- Dialysis vs the Kidney: Dialysis copies the kidney's outcome (clean blood) but uses a simpler mechanism, which means it matches some functions and misses others.
- Evaluating Your Sources: A secondary-source investigation is only as good as its sources, so judging their reliability is part of the science, not an optional extra.
- Living on dialysis in Australia: Thousands of Australians rely on dialysis, typically spending around four hours, three times a week, hooked to a machine, a major life impact that a transplant can remove.

Practice demand: Site review sample: What is the dialysate in haemodialysis?

Site short-answer questions (3): 1. Compare the process of renal dialysis with the function of the kidney, identifying two similarities and two differences. 2. Explain why the dialysate used in haemodialysis contains glucose and salts at concentrations similar to healthy blood, but no urea. 3. For a secondary-source investigation comparing dialysis with the kidney, a student uses three sources: a national kidney foundation webpage, a leaflet published by a dialysis equipment manufacturer, and a peer-reviewed review article. Evaluate the reliability of each source, and explain why a conclusion drawn from all three is stronger than one drawn from any single source.
