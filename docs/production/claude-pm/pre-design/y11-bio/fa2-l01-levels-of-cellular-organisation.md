## 2. Site lesson material

Source: `C:/Users/kygs/hscscience/subjects/biology/year11/fa2-cells-to-systems/lesson01.sa.html` and `docs/production/claude-pm/site-extracts/bio-y11-fa2-l01.md`. Site section order: 1. One Cell That Does Everything; 2. Colonial Life, Together but Independent; 3. Multicellular, Specialised and Dependent; 4. Why this matters for growing tissue in the lab. Relevant ideas:

- One Cell That Does Everything: A unicellular organism must carry out every function of life inside one cell, so that cell tends to be a "generalist" packed with all the structures needed for independent living.
- Colonial Life, Together but Independent: A colony is a group of similar cells living together for a shared benefit, but with little specialisation, each cell remains structurally a generalist that could, in principle, survive alone.
- Multicellular, Specialised and Dependent: Once cells specialise to do one job extremely well, they lose the ability to do the others, so they must depend on other specialised cells, and the organism becomes a single interdependent whole.
- Why this matters for growing tissue in the lab: When scientists grow human cells in a dish to test drugs or build "lab-grown" skin, they relearn this lesson every day.

## 4. Mistakes and traps

The site names this misconception: Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: A colony of cells is the same thing as a multicellular organism, they are both "many cells stuck together". ✓ Right: In a colony the cells are largely identical and each can survive alone; in a multicellular organism the cells are specialised and interdependent, so a single cell cannot survive on its own. This is a page-backed teaching trap, not a claim about NESA marking trends.
