## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

- **Simple Diffusion and Osmosis:** Two passive processes driven by gradients Particles in liquids and gases are always moving randomly, and this random motion alone spreads them out evenly over time. Simple diffusion is the net movement of particles from a region of high concentration to a region of low concentration, down the concentration gradient, until evenly spread . Small non-polar molecules such as O₂ and CO₂ diffuse straight through the bilayer. Osmosis is a special case for water: the net movement of water molecules across a partially permeable membrane from a region of higher water concentration (dilute solution) to lower water concentration (concentrated solution) . - **Demonstrating Diffusion and Osmosis:** The bench evidence for both processes Both diffusion and osmosis can be shown directly, and the observed change is the evidence that the process occurred. Diffusion demo: a crystal of potassium permanganate dropped into still water spreads colour outward over time with no stirring, visible evidence of particles moving down a concentration gradient . Osmosis demo: Visking (dialysis) tubing filled with concentrated sugar solution and placed in water gains mass as water moves in by osmosis; potato cylinders in concentrated sugar solution lose mass . Measuring mass change before and after is the quantitative evidence for osmosis. Controls (e.g. tubing in water vs water in water) and repeats…

**Page activity:** Apply Band 4 Design the Practical Pattern B, Plan an investigation Outline a method to demonstrate diffusion (e.g. potassium permanganate crystal in water, or agar cubes with dye) AND osmosis (e.g. Visking/dialysis tubing filled with concentrated sugar solution placed in water, or potato cylinders in different sugar concentrations). For each, state what you would measure and the expected result.

**Practice:** Explain the processes of simple diffusion, osmosis and facilitated diffusion. Then outline how you could demonstrate osmosis using Visking tubing, stating what you would measure.

**Page misconception:** ✗ Wrong: Facilitated diffusion needs energy because it uses a protein. ✓ Right: Facilitated diffusion is still passive , it moves substances DOWN their concentration gradient, so no ATP is required. The protein just provides a route through the hydrophobic core; it does not pump. Active transport, which goes against the gradient, is the one that costs energy.
