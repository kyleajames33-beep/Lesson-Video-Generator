## 2. Site lesson material

Source: `C:/Users/kygs/hscscience/subjects/biology/year11/fa2-cells-to-systems/lesson06.sa.html` and `docs/production/claude-pm/site-extracts/bio-y11-fa2-l06.md`. Site section order: 1. Measuring the Rate with Oxygen Bubbles; 2. Planning a Fair Test; 3. Predicting the Outcome from the Model; 4. How crop scientists optimise glasshouses. Relevant ideas:

- Measuring the Rate with Oxygen Bubbles: Because oxygen is a product of photosynthesis, the faster oxygen is released, the faster photosynthesis must be occurring, so counting oxygen gives a direct measure of the rate.
- Planning a Fair Test: A fair test changes one condition at a time and holds all others constant, so any change in oxygen output can confidently be attributed to that one condition.
- Predicting the Outcome from the Model: A good practical does not just observe, it predicts, so the data can confirm or challenge the underlying model.
- How crop scientists optimise glasshouses: The same predict-change-measure cycle you practise with pondweed is exactly how agricultural scientists fine-tune commercial glasshouses.

Practice demand: Design a pondweed test with one independent variable, a measured rate, three controls, five levels and repeats.

Site short-answer questions (3): 1. Outline a practical investigation to predict how changing light intensity affects the rate of photosynthesis in pondweed. Identify the variables, describe how the rate is measured, and state and justify your prediction. 2. A student counts pondweed bubbles once at each of five lamp distances and reports that the rate rises as the lamp gets closer. Evaluate the reliability and validity of this investigation, and describe two specific changes that would improve it. 3. Design an investigation to predict how changing carbon dioxide concentration affects the rate of photosynthesis in pondweed. State your independent, dependent and three controlled variables, outline how you would change the independent variable across at least five values, state your prediction, and justify it using the limiting-factor model.

Site memory/retrieval cue: You can now plan a valid photosynthesis prac and predict its result from the limiting-factor model. With the plant's food-making covered, the next lessons follow the plant's plumbing, how water and sugar are transported through xylem and phloem.
