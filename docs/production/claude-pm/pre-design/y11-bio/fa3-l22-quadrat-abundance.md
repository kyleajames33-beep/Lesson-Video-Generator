## 2. Site lesson content

Whole-page section order: Three Sampling Techniques; The Investigation & Calculating Abundance; Comparing the Techniques & Reducing Error.

Selected material:
- Three Sampling Techniques: Different sampling tools suit different questions: random quadrats estimate overall abundance, while transects reveal how a community changes across a gradient.
- The Investigation & Calculating Abundance: You find the average number per quadrat, then scale it up to the whole area using the ratio of total area to quadrat area.

Practice: Calculate and Choose Pattern B, Work with data Six 0.25 m² quadrats placed randomly in a 800 m² dune contained these counts of a grass: 3, 5, 2, 4, 6, 4. (a) Find the mean per quadrat. (b) Estimate the total population (remember each quadrat is 0.25 m²). (c) A student wants instead to show how the grass changes from the beach to the back dune, which technique should they use and why? Site short-answer demand: Compare the use of quadrats and transects for estimating the distribution and abundance of organisms, and describe how you would use one technique to estimate the abundance of a plant, including how to make the estimate reliable.

Memory aid: The page asks students to retain the quadrat, line-transect and belt-transect uses.

## 3. Number and fact check

Site worked example: (4+6+5+5)/4 = 5 plants per 1 m²; 5 × (2000/1) = 10,000 plants. Activity: (3+5+2+4+6+4)/6 = 4 per 0.25 m²; 4 × (800/0.25) = 12,800. Short answer: (6+2+9+4+0+7+3+5)/8 = 4.5 per 0.25 m²; 4.5 × (1200/0.25) = 21,600. These agree with the page key. No reference constant is used. Source: site L22.

## 4. Misconceptions and traps

Site misconception: - Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: You should place quadrats where you can clearly see the organisms you are counting. ✓ Right: Choosing where to place samples biases the result, it over-counts dense patches.

Source-backed response trap: A quadrat estimate needs its area ratio; a transect is chosen for a gradient. A larger frame alone does not remove placement bias. Source: site L22, misconception, activity and short-answer task. This is a page-based trap, not a claim about NESA marker trends.
