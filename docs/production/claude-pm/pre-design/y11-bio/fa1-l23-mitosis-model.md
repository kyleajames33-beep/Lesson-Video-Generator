## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

- **Mitosis and Cytokinesis:** Four phases that separate the chromosomes, then the cytoplasm splits Mitosis is the carefully ordered separation of the duplicated chromosomes, so that each new nucleus receives one complete, identical set. Prophase: chromosomes condense and become visible, the nuclear membrane breaks down, and spindle fibres form. Metaphase: chromosomes line up along the equator (middle) of the cell. Anaphase: the sister chromatids are pulled apart to opposite poles. Telophase: two new nuclear membranes form around the two identical sets of chromosomes. Then cytokinesis divides the cytoplasm, producing two separate, genetically identical daughter cells .

**Practice:** Account for the phases of the cell cycle, from gap 1 (G1) through to cytokinesis, explaining what happens in each phase.

**Memory aid:** No named mnemonic is stated in the selected page cards. Use the page’s comparison or physical model where it helps recall the mechanism.

**Page misconception:** ✗ Wrong: Mitosis is the whole cell cycle, and it produces four daughter cells. ✓ Right: Mitosis is only the nuclear-division part of the cycle (most of the cycle is interphase). Mitosis followed by cytokinesis produces two genetically identical daughter cells, four cells with halved chromosomes is meiosis, which is a different process.
