## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

- **The Electron Microscope Changes Everything:** Resolution leaps, and the ultrastructure of the cell appears Using electrons instead of light gave roughly a thousand-fold jump in resolution, exposing organelle detail no one had seen before. Developed in the 1930s, the electron microscope resolves to about 0.2 nm . It revealed the cell's ultrastructure, the cell membrane, ribosomes, the cristae of mitochondria, the ER and Golgi, and confirmed prokaryotes lack internal membrane-bound organelles. The TEM shows internal detail; the SEM shows 3D surfaces. This transformed the model of the cell from a simple "box with a nucleus" into a richly compartmentalised structure, directly changing scientific understanding of cell function.

- **Weighing It Up, Strengths and Limitations:** Why "assess" means a balanced conclusion Each technology has trade-offs, so a fair assessment weighs benefits against limitations rather than crowning one as best. The electron microscope's huge resolution comes at a cost: specimens must be dead, dehydrated and viewed in a vacuum, so you cannot watch living processes; equipment is expensive and complex; images are black-and-white . The light microscope, though lower in resolution, lets you observe living , moving, coloured cells cheaply. Newer technologies (fluorescence and confocal microscopy) now let scientists see specific molecules glowing inside living cells. **Page activity:** Evaluate Band 6 Assess the Impact, A Balanced Judgement Pattern D, Structured evaluation Write a short paragraph that ASSESSES the impact of the electron microscope on scientific understanding of cells. Include: (1) one specific thing it revealed; (2) how that changed the model of the cell; (3) one limitation (e.g. specimens must be dead, in a vacuum, no live cells). End with a one-sentence judgement.

**Practice:** Assess the impact of the development of the electron microscope on scientific understanding of cell structure and function.

**Memory aid:** No named mnemonic is stated in the selected page cards. Use the page’s comparison or physical model where it helps recall the mechanism.

**Page misconception:** ✗ Wrong: A microscope with higher magnification always shows more detail. ✓ Right: Beyond a certain point, more magnification only produces "empty magnification", a bigger but blurry image. Resolution , not magnification, sets how much detail you can actually see. The electron microscope mattered because of its resolution, not just its power.
