## 2. Site lesson content

Whole-page section order: Structural Adaptations for Water Balance; The Investigation, Method; Making It Reliable and Valid.

Selected material:
- Structural Adaptations for Water Balance: Australian plants face hot, dry conditions, so many have evolved physical leaf features that cut the rate of water loss while still allowing some gas exchange.

Practice: Process Your Results Pattern B, Work with data A class measured leaf mass over an hour. The Eucalyptus leaf went from 2.00 g to 1.88 g; the soft privet leaf went from 2.00 g to 1.40 g. Calculate the percentage mass (water) lost by each leaf, state which has the better water-balance adaptations, and name two leaf features that could explain the difference. Site short-answer demand: Describe a method you could use to compare the structural water-balance adaptations of two Australian plants, and explain how you would make the investigation valid and reliable.

## 4. Misconceptions and traps

Site misconception: - Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: A leaf loses water "evenly" all over its surface, so the wax coating makes little difference. ✓ Right: Most water leaves through the stomata, not the cuticle.

Source-backed response trap: A lower mass loss in detached leaves does not prove one species is generally better adapted. Hold leaf area and conditions constant, repeat, and note the gas-exchange trade-off. Source: site L10, misconception, activity and short-answer task. This is a page-based trap, not a claim about NESA marker trends.
