Site short-answer questions (3): 1. Describe the function of the primary hormones that regulate blood glucose, and analyse how diabetes affects the body's ability to maintain homeostasis. 2. In a glucose-tolerance test, Person A (non-diabetic) records blood glucose in mmol/L of 5 at 0 min, 8 at 30 min, 6 at 60 min and 5 at 120 min. Person B (untreated diabetic) records 9, 15, 16 and 14 at the same times. Analyse the two data sets and explain what they show about the role of insulin in maintaining homeostasis. 3. Using reliable secondary sources, discuss the impact of diabetes on the body's ability to maintain homeostasis. Refer to both types of diabetes, to the immediate consequences of losing glucose control, and to what treatment restores.

Site memory/retrieval cue: You can now describe the hormones that regulate blood glucose and the stress response, analyse hormone data to show a hormone's role, and discuss how diabetes disrupts homeostasis. The final lesson generalises all of this into optimal range and tolerance limits.

## 3. Number and fact check

The site glucose series (mmol/L) is A: 5, 8, 6, 5; B: 9, 15, 16, 14 at 0, 30, 60, 120 min. A returns to baseline; B ends 5 above its own baseline and 9 above A. The comparison is numerically correct.

## 4. Mistakes and traps

The site names this misconception: Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: Diabetes is caused by eating too much sugar, and insulin "is" sugar. ✓ Right: Insulin is a hormone, not a sugar, it is the signal that lowers blood glucose. This is a page-backed teaching trap, not a claim about NESA marking trends.
