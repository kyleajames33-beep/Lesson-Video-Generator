## 2. Site lesson material

Source: `C:/Users/kygs/hscscience/subjects/biology/year11/fa2-cells-to-systems/lesson07.sa.html` and `docs/production/claude-pm/site-extracts/bio-y11-fa2-l07.md`. Site section order: 1. Xylem, Water Going Up; 2. Phloem, Sugar Going Anywhere; 3. Structure Suits Function; 4. Why ring-barking kills a tree. Relevant ideas:

- Phloem, Sugar Going Anywhere: Phloem is built from living cells because transporting sugar against gradients and in changeable directions requires active, energy-using cells rather than empty pipes.
- Structure Suits Function: Each structural feature of xylem and phloem exists because it makes that tissue better at its particular transport job.
- Why ring-barking kills a tree: If you cut a complete ring of bark from around a tree trunk, "ring-barking", the tree dies, but slowly and from the bottom up.

Practice demand: Site review sample: What does xylem transport, and in which direction?

Site short-answer questions (3): 1. Compare the structures and functions of xylem and phloem in the plant transport system. Refer to at least three structural differences and link each to its function. 2. Explain why xylem can function while its cells are dead, but phloem cannot. 3. A complete ring of bark is removed from around a tree trunk. Over the following months the trunk swells just above the cut, the leaves stay green for some time, and the roots eventually die. Analyse this result using the structures and functions of xylem and phloem, and state what it shows about the plant transport system.
