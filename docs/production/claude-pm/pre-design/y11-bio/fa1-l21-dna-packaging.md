## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

- **DNA in Prokaryotic Cells:** One circular chromosome, free in the cytoplasm, plus plasmids In a prokaryote, the DNA exists as a single circular chromosome lying free in the cytoplasm, often accompanied by small extra rings of DNA called plasmids. The single circular chromosome sits in the nucleoid region, not enclosed by a membrane. Many prokaryotes also carry plasmids : small extra circles of DNA that can be exchanged between cells and often carry genes such as antibiotic resistance. Prokaryotic DNA is generally not wound around histone proteins. Prokaryotic DNA: a single circular chromosome lying free in the cytoplasm (nucleoid), plus small circular plasmids; no nucleus and generally no histone packaging.

- **DNA in Eukaryotic Cells:** Many linear chromosomes, wound on histones, inside a nucleus In a eukaryote, DNA exists as several linear chromosomes wound tightly around histone proteins and enclosed within the membrane-bound nucleus. Eukaryotes have much more DNA , organised into several linear chromosomes . Because the total length is enormous, the DNA is wound tightly around histone proteins to package it compactly, and the whole set is enclosed in the nucleus . Eukaryotic DNA: several linear chromosomes, each wound around histone proteins for compact packaging, all enclosed within the membrane-bound nucleus.

- **Comparing the Two Forms:** Different packaging, same underlying molecule The DNA molecule is identical in both cell types; only its shape, number, packaging and location differ. Compare: prokaryotic DNA = one circular chromosome, free in cytoplasm, plasmids present, no histones; eukaryotic DNA = several linear chromosomes, in the nucleus, wound on histones, no plasmids. Both share the same double-helix structure, nucleotides and A–T/C–G base pairing.

**Page activity:** Apply Band 4 Construct the Two Diagrams Pattern A, Structured construction In your book, construct two labelled diagrams side by side: one of DNA in a prokaryotic cell and one in a eukaryotic cell. Label, on the prokaryote: circular chromosome, nucleoid, plasmid. On the eukaryote: linear chromosomes, histones, nucleus. Underneath, write one sentence listing what BOTH forms still share.

**Practice:** With the aid of diagrams, compare the forms in which DNA exists in prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells. Include at least one feature they share.

**Memory aid:** No named mnemonic is stated in the selected page cards. Use the page’s comparison or physical model where it helps recall the mechanism.

**Page misconception:** ✗ Wrong: Prokaryotic and eukaryotic DNA are chemically different molecules. ✓ Right: The DNA molecule is the same in both, a double helix of nucleotides with A–T and C–G base pairing. What differs is only its form and packaging : circular and free in prokaryotes, versus linear, protein-wound chromosomes inside a nucleus in eukaryotes.
