## 2. Site lesson material

Source: `C:/Users/kygs/hscscience/subjects/biology/year11/fa2-cells-to-systems/lesson03.sa.html` and `docs/production/claude-pm/site-extracts/bio-y11-fa2-l03.md`. Site section order: 1. The Levels of Organisation; 2. Why Each Level Exists; 3. The Hierarchy in Plants; 4. Why surgeons think in levels. Relevant ideas:

- The Levels of Organisation: Living things are organised in a hierarchy where each level is constructed from the level beneath it, building up from tiny organelles to a whole organism.
- Why Each Level Exists: Grouping units at each level creates abilities that the individual units could not achieve alone, which is why complex bodies need every rung of the ladder.
- The Hierarchy in Plants: The hierarchical pattern is universal to multicellular life, so plants are organised on exactly the same ladder, just with their own organelles, tissues and organs.
- Why surgeons think in levels: When doctors describe disease, they pinpoint exactly which level of organisation has failed, and that decides the treatment.

Practice demand: Site review sample: Which sequence orders the levels of organisation from smallest to largest?

Site short-answer questions (3): 1. Explain the hierarchical organisation of organelles, cells, tissues, organs and systems in organisms. Use one worked example to show how each level builds on the one below. 2. Explain, with one example each, why the heart is classified as an organ rather than as a tissue, and why the circulatory system is classified as a system rather than as an organ. 3. Choose either the respiratory system or the digestive system. Trace one named structure from that system up through every level of the hierarchy, from organelle to organism, and for each step state the new function that appears at that level and could not be performed by the level below.
