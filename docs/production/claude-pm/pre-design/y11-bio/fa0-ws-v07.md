## 3. Biology context and stop boundary

A quadrat survey conclusion written as claim, evidence, reasoning; peer review with a published case that overturned a consensus (bacteria and stomach ulcers, 1980s); binomial names in italics, SI units, significant figures; the same finding written for a report and for a community noticeboard.

Stop: An evidence-based argument (claim, evidence with data, reasoning that links them, limitation); the role of peer review in checking methods and conclusions, and what it does not guarantee; choosing a medium for an audience; scientific notation, nomenclature and units; correct terminology for the audience and purpose. Course-end video for the Year 11 Working scientifically set.

## 4. Numbers and facts

Quadrat survey (fa3-v26 style): mean 5.2 plants per m² in the shade zone, 1.1 in the sun zone, 10 quadrats each (illustrative).

## 5. Misconceptions and traps

'Peer review proves a result is true'. A conclusion with no data in it. A claim stronger than the evidence ('proves', 'all'). Binomial names not italicised or the species name capitalised. The same wording for every audience.
