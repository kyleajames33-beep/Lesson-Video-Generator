## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

- **Facilitated Diffusion:** When a molecule needs a protein doorway, but still no energy Charged or large polar molecules cannot cross the lipid core, so they pass through specific membrane proteins while still moving down their gradient. Facilitated diffusion is the passive movement of ions or large polar molecules (e.g. glucose) down their concentration gradient, through a specific channel or carrier protein in the membrane . Because it follows the gradient, it needs no energy from the cell, the protein simply provides a route through the hydrophobic core . The rate depends on how many transport proteins are available.

**Page activity:** Apply Band 4 Design the Practical Pattern B, Plan an investigation Outline a method to demonstrate diffusion (e.g. potassium permanganate crystal in water, or agar cubes with dye) AND osmosis (e.g. Visking/dialysis tubing filled with concentrated sugar solution placed in water, or potato cylinders in different sugar concentrations). For each, state what you would measure and the expected result.

**Practice:** Explain the processes of simple diffusion, osmosis and facilitated diffusion. Then outline how you could demonstrate osmosis using Visking tubing, stating what you would measure.

**Memory aid:** No named mnemonic is stated in the selected page cards. Use the page’s comparison or physical model where it helps recall the mechanism.

**Page misconception:** ✗ Wrong: Facilitated diffusion needs energy because it uses a protein. ✓ Right: Facilitated diffusion is still passive , it moves substances DOWN their concentration gradient, so no ATP is required. The protein just provides a route through the hydrophobic core; it does not pump. Active transport, which goes against the gradient, is the one that costs energy.
