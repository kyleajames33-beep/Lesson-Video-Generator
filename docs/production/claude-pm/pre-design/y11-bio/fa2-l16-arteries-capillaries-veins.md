## 2. Site lesson material

Source: `C:/Users/kygs/hscscience/subjects/biology/year11/fa2-cells-to-systems/lesson16.sa.html` and `docs/production/claude-pm/site-extracts/bio-y11-fa2-l16.md`. Site section order: 1. Three Vessels, Three Jobs; 2. Comparing the Three Vessels; 3. Using Models, Strengths and Limits; 4. Varicose veins and failing valves. Relevant ideas:

- Three Vessels, Three Jobs: Each vessel's wall is built for the pressure and the task it must handle: high-pressure delivery, low-pressure return, or close-up exchange.
- Comparing the Three Vessels: Seeing the three cross-sections together makes the link between wall thickness, lumen width and the pressure each vessel handles obvious.
- Using Models, Strengths and Limits: A model is a deliberate simplification that lets us see a relationship clearly, but every simplification leaves something out, so models must be used and judged with care.
- Varicose veins and failing valves: When the valves in leg veins weaken, blood pools instead of being pushed back to the heart, stretching the vein into the bulging, twisted shape of a varicose vein.

Practice demand: Site review sample: Which feature is characteristic of a capillary?

Site short-answer questions (3): 1. Using models or diagrams, explain how the structure of an artery, a capillary and a vein each relates to its function. 2. A student models the three blood vessels with a thick springy rubber tube, a bundle of fine porous tubing and a floppy tube containing a paper flap. State what each part represents, and evaluate one limitation of the model as a demonstration of the structure-function relationship. 3. Blood leaves the heart in a large artery and returns in a large vein, but it moves most slowly in the capillaries, which are by far the narrowest vessels. Analyse why blood slows in the capillaries despite their narrowness, and explain how each of the three vessel types is structured for the job it does.

Site memory/retrieval cue: You can now use a model to demonstrate how each vessel's structure suits its function, and judge the model's limits. Next lesson looks inside the blood itself, its plasma and cells.
