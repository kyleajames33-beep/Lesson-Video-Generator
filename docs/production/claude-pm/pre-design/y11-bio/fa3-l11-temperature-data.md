## 3. Number and fact check

The short-answer data give a 30 °C air rise, X body temperature +29.0 °C and Y +1.0 °C. X nearly tracks air (29/30 = 0.967 °C per °C); Y changes little (1/30 = 0.033). This supports X as ectotherm and Y as endotherm. Site fact check: the card presents 36–38 °C as an endotherm pattern while naming mammals and birds together. Anchor the 37 °C graph to a named mammal, not all endotherms. No reference constant is used. Source: site L11.

## 4. Misconceptions and traps

Site misconception: - Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: Ectotherms are "cold-blooded", their blood is always cold. ✓ Right: A basking lizard can be hotter than a nearby mammal.

Source-backed response trap: Do not call the two heat strategies better or worse from a single temperature point. Compare the whole body-temperature trend and food cost. Source: site L11, misconception, activity and short-answer task. This is a page-based trap, not a claim about NESA marker trends.
