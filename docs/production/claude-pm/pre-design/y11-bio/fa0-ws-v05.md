## 3. Biology context and stop boundary

The bead model's known true count (260) gives a real percentage error; the three repeats and added class repeats give mean, range and standard deviation; one class value of 520 (a counting slip) is the outlier; accuracy against precision shown with marks that wash off (precise but biased).

Stop: Describing relationships qualitatively and quantitatively; patterns, trends and inconsistencies; percentage error = |experimental − accepted| ÷ accepted × 100; range; mean; standard deviation as the typical distance of readings from the mean (calculator method shown, formula on screen); identifying an outlier and deciding with a stated reason whether to exclude it; validity, reliability, accuracy and precision distinguished on one picture; suggesting improvements and further investigations. ## 4. Numbers and facts

Bead model (fa3-v28): repeats 250, 288, 223; mean 253.7 (about 254); true count 260; percentage error = |253.7 − 260| ÷ 260 × 100 ≈ 2.4 per cent; range 223 to 288 (65); sample standard deviation s ≈ 32.7 (n − 1; population form ≈ 26.7). Class set adds 266, 241 and 520: 520 is the outlier (a counting slip: two scoops counted); mean with it 298, without it 253.6. Field of view check (fa1-v06 recall) as a second percentage error: measured 4.2 mm against a stated 4.5 mm, error ≈ 6.7 per cent.

## 5. Misconceptions and traps

Percentage error divided by the experimental value; a negative percentage error reported without saying under or over; 'standard deviation is the range'; deleting an outlier with no reason; 'precise means accurate'; 'more decimal places means more accurate'.
