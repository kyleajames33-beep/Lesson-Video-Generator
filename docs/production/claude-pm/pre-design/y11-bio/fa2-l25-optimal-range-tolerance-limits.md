## 2. Site lesson material

Source: `C:/Users/kygs/hscscience/subjects/biology/year11/fa2-cells-to-systems/lesson25.sa.html` and `docs/production/claude-pm/site-extracts/bio-y11-fa2-l25.md`. Site section order: 1. Optimal Range vs Tolerance Limit; 2. Worked Example, Body Temperature; 3. Why the Limits Exist, and Generalising; 4. Heatwaves and the limits of the human body. Relevant ideas:

- Optimal Range vs Tolerance Limit: A physiological variable has a narrow band where the body works best, a wider band where it merely copes, and a hard limit beyond which it can no longer survive.
- Worked Example, Body Temperature: For human body temperature, the zones translate into definite numbers: about 37 °C is optimal, the body copes within a few degrees of this, but past roughly 42 °C (or below ~28 °C) survival is threatened.
- Why the Limits Exist, and Generalising: The reason a critical limit exists is chemical: the enzymes that run every cell process only work within a narrow range and are permanently destroyed beyond it.
- Heatwaves and the limits of the human body: Australia's increasingly severe heatwaves are a public-health emergency precisely because of tolerance limits.

## 3. Number and fact check

The site marks 36.5 to 37.5 °C as optimal and danger boundaries near 28 °C and 42 °C. These are not fixed fatal thresholds: Merck describes possible survival below 28 °C with treatment and heatstroke at about 40 °C or above.

## 4. Mistakes and traps

The site names this misconception: Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: Homeostasis keeps a variable at exactly one fixed value at all times. ✓ Right: Homeostasis keeps a variable within a narrow optimal range, fluctuating slightly around a set point. Site science finding: the stated temperature limits are drawn as hard failure thresholds, and denaturation is called permanent. This is too categorical. See [Merck on hypothermia](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/injuries-poisoning/cold-injury/hypothermia) and [Merck on heatstroke](https://www.merckmanuals.com/home/injuries-and-poisoning/heat-disorders/heatstroke). This is a page-backed teaching trap, not a claim about NESA marking trends.
