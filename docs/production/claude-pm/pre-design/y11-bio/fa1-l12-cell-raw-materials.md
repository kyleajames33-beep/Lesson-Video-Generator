## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

- **Materials for Energy: Glucose, Oxygen and Carbon Dioxide:** The fuel, the burner and the by-product A cell's most urgent requirement is energy, and three of the six materials are tied directly to releasing it. Glucose is the cell's main fuel, it is broken down in cellular respiration to release the energy stored in its chemical bonds . Oxygen is required because it is the reactant that allows glucose to be fully broken down in aerobic respiration, releasing the most energy . Carbon dioxide is the waste product of respiration that must be removed, but in a plant cell it is a raw material taken IN for photosynthesis . This is the key idea: the same substance can be an input or an output depending on the cell and the reaction.

- **Materials for Building and Structure: Amino Acids and Ions:** Not everything is burned, some materials are built into the cell Beyond energy, a cell must build proteins and maintain the chemical conditions for its reactions, and two materials do this job. Amino acids are the building blocks of proteins, cells join them in chains to make enzymes, membrane proteins and structural proteins . Ions (such as Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺, Cl⁻) are required because they are needed for nerve impulses, muscle contraction, enzyme activity and maintaining cell structure and water balance . Without the right ion balance, enzymes fail and the cell cannot signal or hold its shape.

- **Water, the Material Behind Every Reaction:** Solvent, reactant, and the reason inputs and outputs are relative Water is the most abundant requirement of all, because every other material has to dissolve and react within it. Water is required because it is the solvent in which all the cell's chemicals dissolve and reactions occur, and it is also a reactant in many reactions (e.g. a reactant in photosynthesis and a product of respiration) . This shows the central principle of the dot point: each material relates to a specific cell requirement, and whether it is taken in (input) or released (output) depends on which reactions that cell is carrying out . **Page activity:** Understand Band 4 Relate Each Material to a Requirement Pattern A, Structured matching In your book, draw a two-column table headed Raw material and Cell requirement . Add a row for each of the six: carbon dioxide, oxygen, water, ions, amino acids and glucose. In the second column, state precisely what the cell uses each for, and note whether it is normally an input, an output, or can be both.

**Practice:** Relate glucose, oxygen, amino acids, ions and water to the requirements of a cell. For each, state what the cell uses it for.

**Page misconception:** ✗ Wrong: Cells only need glucose and oxygen, water and ions are just "filler". ✓ Right: Water is the solvent for every reaction and a reactant in many; ions are essential for nerve impulses, enzyme activity and structure. A cell deprived of water or key ions stops functioning just as fast as one starved of fuel.
