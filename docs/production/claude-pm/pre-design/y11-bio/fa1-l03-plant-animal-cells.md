## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

- **Same Toolkit, Different Tools:** What both share, and what each keeps for itself Plant and animal cells are both eukaryotic, so they share the core organelles, the differences are a short, predictable list. Both plant and animal cells have a cell membrane, cytoplasm, a nucleus, mitochondria, ribosomes, endoplasmic reticulum and a Golgi body. The differences are: plant cells additionally have a cellulose cell wall, chloroplasts and a single large central vacuole; animal cells have neither a wall nor chloroplasts, smaller and more numerous vacuoles, and (typically) lysosomes and centrioles.

- **Why a Plant Cell Looks the Way It Does:** Wall, chloroplasts and vacuole follow from the plant's job A plant cell makes its own food and cannot move, so it needs to capture light, store the products, and hold itself rigid, and that explains its three extra structures. The cell wall gives the plant cell a fixed shape and stops it bursting when water enters, essential because plants rely on turgor (water pressure against the wall) to stay upright instead of a skeleton . The chloroplasts are present because a plant is an autotroph: it must capture light energy to make its own glucose by photosynthesis . The large central vacuole stores cell sap, nutrients and wastes and presses outward on the wall to maintain turgor .

- **Why an Animal Cell Travels Light:** No wall, flexible shape, and specialised animal organelles An animal is a heterotroph that moves and consumes food, so its cells are built for flexibility rather than rigidity. An animal cell has no cell wall , which lets it change shape, move, and engulf material (e.g. white blood cells engulfing pathogens) . It has no chloroplasts because it does not photosynthesise, it obtains glucose by eating. Its vacuoles are small and temporary , and many animal cells contain lysosomes for digestion and centrioles that organise the spindle in cell division. The principle is general: a cell stocks the organelles its function requires, structure follows function .

**Page activity:** Analyse Band 5 Function-First Contrast Pattern B, Reason-linked comparison In your book, write three sentences in the form: "A plant cell has a [organelle] that an animal cell lacks, because the plant cell needs to [function]." Use the cell wall, chloroplasts and central vacuole. Then write one sentence on an organelle an animal cell may have that a plant cell does not (e.g. centrioles), again linked to function.

**Practice:** Contrast the structure of a plant cell and an animal cell. For each of two organelles found only in plant cells, explain how it relates to the function of the cell.

**Memory aid:** No named mnemonic is stated in the selected page cards. Use the page’s comparison or physical model where it helps recall the mechanism.

**Page misconception:** ✗ Wrong: Plant cells do not have mitochondria because they make their own food in chloroplasts. ✓ Right: Plant cells have BOTH chloroplasts AND mitochondria. They make glucose by photosynthesis, but they still respire that glucose in mitochondria to release usable energy, including at night when there is no light.
