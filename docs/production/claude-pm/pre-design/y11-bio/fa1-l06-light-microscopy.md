## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

- **From First Lenses to the Light Microscope:** What the light microscope revealed, and could not The first microscopes revealed that living things are made of cells at all, a discovery impossible without the technology. In the 1660s Robert Hooke used an early compound microscope to see and name "cells" in cork, and Antonie van Leeuwenhoek observed living single-celled organisms ("animalcules"). The light microscope (resolution ~200 nm) made the cell theory possible: it revealed that organisms are made of cells, and let scientists see nuclei, chloroplasts and whole cells . But its resolution is limited by the wavelength of visible light, so finer internal detail, membranes, ribosomes, cristae, stayed invisible.

**Page activity:** Evaluate Band 6 Assess the Impact, A Balanced Judgement Pattern D, Structured evaluation Write a short paragraph that ASSESSES the impact of the electron microscope on scientific understanding of cells. Include: (1) one specific thing it revealed; (2) how that changed the model of the cell; (3) one limitation (e.g. specimens must be dead, in a vacuum, no live cells). End with a one-sentence judgement.

**Practice:** Assess the impact of the development of the electron microscope on scientific understanding of cell structure and function.

**Memory aid:** No named mnemonic is stated in the selected page cards. Use the page’s comparison or physical model where it helps recall the mechanism.

**Page misconception:** ✗ Wrong: A microscope with higher magnification always shows more detail. ✓ Right: Beyond a certain point, more magnification only produces "empty magnification", a bigger but blurry image. Resolution , not magnification, sets how much detail you can actually see. The electron microscope mattered because of its resolution, not just its power.
