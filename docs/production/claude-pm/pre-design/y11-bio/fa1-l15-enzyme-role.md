## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

- **What an Enzyme Is:** A reusable protein catalyst that speeds reactions up An enzyme is a biological catalyst, a (usually protein) molecule that dramatically speeds up a specific chemical reaction in a cell without being used up by it. Because an enzyme is a catalyst , it emerges from the reaction unchanged and can be used over and over. A single enzyme molecule can convert thousands of substrate molecules per second.

- **Lowering Activation Energy:** How enzymes actually make reactions faster Every reaction needs a minimum "push" of energy to get started; enzymes work by lowering that barrier, so reactions proceed much faster at normal cell temperatures. Without an enzyme, only the small fraction of molecules with enough energy can overcome the barrier, so the reaction is slow. The enzyme provides an alternative pathway with a lower activation energy , allowing far more collisions to succeed, so the reaction rate rises enormously, even at a mild ~37 °C.

- **Why Cells Depend on Enzymes:** Control, speed and specificity all in one molecule Enzymes do more than speed reactions up, their specificity lets a cell control thousands of different reactions precisely and independently of one another. Living things contain thousands of distinct enzymes, each catalysing one step in the cell's chemistry. Because each enzyme is specific to its substrate, the cell can switch individual reactions on or off, for example, by making more of one enzyme, or by blocking another, giving fine control over metabolism.

**Page activity:** Apply Band 4 Map a Real Enzyme Reaction Pattern B, Worked application The enzyme catalase breaks down hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) into water and oxygen. In your book, identify the enzyme, the substrate and the products for this reaction. Then explain, in two or three sentences, why a tiny amount of catalase can keep breaking down a large volume of hydrogen peroxide.

**Practice:** Explain the role of enzymes in cells. Refer to activation energy, reusability and specificity in your answer.

**Memory aid:** No named mnemonic is stated in the selected page cards. Use the page’s comparison or physical model where it helps recall the mechanism.

**Page misconception:** ✗ Wrong: An enzyme is used up in the reaction it speeds up, so the cell must constantly make more. ✓ Right: A catalyst, including an enzyme, is not consumed by the reaction. It is released unchanged after the product forms and can catalyse the same reaction again and again. This is why a tiny amount of enzyme can convert a huge amount of substrate.
