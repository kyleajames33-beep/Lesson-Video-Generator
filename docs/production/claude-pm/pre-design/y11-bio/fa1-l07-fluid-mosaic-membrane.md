## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

- **The Phospholipid Bilayer:** Why phospholipids form the membrane's "fluid" backbone The membrane self-assembles because each phospholipid has one end that loves water and one that avoids it. A phospholipid has a hydrophilic (water-loving) phosphate head and two hydrophobic (water-fearing) fatty-acid tails . In water, phospholipids automatically arrange into a bilayer : heads face the watery environment on both sides, tails point inward away from water, forming a hydrophobic core . This core lets small non-polar molecules (O₂, CO₂) and lipids pass easily, but blocks ions and large polar molecules, making the membrane partially (selectively) permeable . The bilayer is "fluid" because the phospholipids drift sideways.

- **Proteins in the Mosaic:** Integral, peripheral, channels and glycoproteins Proteins scattered through the bilayer give the "mosaic" its variety and do the jobs the lipid alone cannot. Integral proteins span the whole bilayer; many act as channels or carriers that transport ions and polar molecules across the membrane . A protein channel is a water-filled pore that lets specific ions or polar molecules pass through the hydrophobic core . Peripheral proteins sit on the surface and support the membrane and assist in signalling or enzyme activity . Glycoproteins (protein + carbohydrate) act in cell recognition and signalling, they are the cell's identity tags .

- **Cholesterol and the Working Whole:** Stability, and why the whole model matters A membrane that is too fluid would leak and a too-rigid one would crack, so cholesterol tunes its fluidity. Cholesterol molecules sit between the phospholipid tails and stabilise the membrane and regulate its fluidity, keeping it from becoming too fluid when warm or too rigid when cold . Putting it all together, the fluid mosaic model explains how the membrane is a partially permeable gatekeeper: the lipid bilayer blocks most substances, while the embedded proteins provide selective routes in and out, and glycoproteins handle recognition, structure and function fit at every level .

**Page misconception:** ✗ Wrong: The cell membrane is a solid, fixed sheet with proteins glued in place. ✓ Right: The membrane is fluid , the phospholipids and many proteins drift sideways within the layer, like objects floating on a liquid. "Mosaic" describes the scattered pattern of different proteins. This movement is essential to its function.
