## 2. Site lesson content

Whole-page section order: Abiotic and Biotic Features; The Investigation, Collecting Data; Analysing & Discussing the Ecosystem.

Selected material:
- The Investigation, Collecting Data: Each abiotic factor has a measuring instrument, and biotic abundance is sampled with quadrats placed in a planned way.
- Analysing & Discussing the Ecosystem: Analysis means organising the data, calculating averages, and explaining the living community in terms of the conditions you measured.

Practice: Activity 1 Analyse Band 5 Link the Data Pattern B, Work with data A class recorded two zones of a creek bank. Shaded zone: light 200 lux, soil moisture high, ferns and mosses abundant. Open zone: light 18 000 lux, soil moisture low, grasses and a few hardy shrubs. Use the abiotic data to explain the difference in the plant communities, and state one feature of this ecosystem your data reveals. Site short-answer demand: Describe a practical method to collect abiotic and biotic data from a local ecosystem, and explain how you would analyse the data to discuss the ecosystem's features.

## 3. Number and fact check

The activity compares 200 lux shaded and 18,000 lux open. Open light is 90 times the shaded measurement. Moisture is qualitative, so no moisture difference can be calculated. No reference constant is used. Source: site L19.

## 4. Misconceptions and traps

Site misconception: - Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: An ecosystem is just the collection of living things in an area. ✓ Right: An ecosystem is the living community and the non-living environment, plus the interactions between them.

Source-backed response trap: An ecosystem survey needs abiotic measurements and biotic observations at the same sites. A single shaded spot cannot represent the whole bank. Source: site L19, misconception, activity and short-answer task. This is a page-based trap, not a claim about NESA marker trends.
