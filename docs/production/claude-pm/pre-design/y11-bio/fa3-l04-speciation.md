## 2. Site lesson content

Whole-page section order: What Counts as a Species; How One Species Becomes Two; From Speciation to Biodiversity.

Selected material:
- What Counts as a Species: Before we can explain how diversity arises, we need a clear test for when two groups count as different species.
- How One Species Becomes Two: Speciation usually begins when a population is split, after which different selection pressures push each fragment in a different evolutionary direction until they can no longer interbreed.

## 4. Misconceptions and traps

Site misconception: - Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: A new species appears suddenly when an individual is born "different enough" from its parents. ✓ Right: Speciation happens gradually to whole populations, not to single individuals.

Source-backed response trap: Do not equate a visible difference with a new species. Link isolation, divergence and reproductive isolation to the named finches. Source: site L04, misconception, activity and short-answer task. This is a page-based trap, not a claim about NESA marker trends.
