## 2. Site lesson content

Whole-page section order: Two Kinds of Pressure; Abiotic Pressures at Work; Biotic Pressures at Work.

Selected material:
- Two Kinds of Pressure: Every selective pressure comes from the environment, and the environment splits cleanly into two halves: the things that are alive and the things that are not.
- Abiotic Pressures at Work: An abiotic pressure changes which variants survive by setting a physical challenge that some individuals are better built to meet.
- Biotic Pressures at Work: Living things in the environment pressure a population by competing with it, eating it, or infecting it, and each interaction favours particular variants.

Practice: Activity 1 Analyse Band 5 Classify and Justify Pattern C, Sort, then explain For each factor below, state whether it is abiotic or biotic, then name one trait it would select for in a named population: (a) increased frequency of bushfire, (b) introduction of a fast-running predator, (c) rising soil salinity, (d) competition for nesting sites. Write your answers as a short labelled list.  Site short-answer demand: Using one abiotic and one biotic example, demonstrate how factors in the environment exert selective pressures on a population.

## 4. Misconceptions and traps

Site misconception: - Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: A selective pressure is always something harmful, like a predator or disease. ✓ Right: A selective pressure is anything that makes some variants reproduce more than others.

Source-backed response trap: Do not label the pressure alone. State whether it is biotic or abiotic, name a trait and explain who reproduces more. Source: site L02, misconception, activity and short-answer task. This is a page-based trap, not a claim about NESA marker trends.
