## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

- **Boundary, Control Centre and Powerhouse:** Cell membrane, cell wall, cytoplasm, nucleus and mitochondria Before the specialised departments, a cell needs a boundary that controls what enters, a control centre that holds the instructions, and a power supply. The cell membrane is a phospholipid bilayer that surrounds the cell and controls what enters and leaves, it is partially (selectively) permeable . The cell wall , present in plant cells (made of cellulose) but absent in animal cells, is a rigid outer layer that gives shape and support. The cytoplasm is the jelly-like fluid filling the cell where many reactions occur and organelles are suspended.

- **The Protein Production Line:** Ribosomes, endoplasmic reticulum and the Golgi body Making and exporting a protein is a sequence: build it, fold and modify it, then package and ship it, and a different organelle owns each step. Ribosomes are tiny structures (free in the cytoplasm or attached to the ER) that are the site of protein synthesis (translation) . The endoplasmic reticulum (ER) is a membrane network: rough ER (studded with ribosomes) synthesises and folds proteins, while smooth ER synthesises lipids and detoxifies . The Golgi body is a stack of flattened membranes that modifies, packages and dispatches proteins and lipids in vesicles for transport or secretion .

- **Digestion, Storage and the Solar Panel:** Lysosomes, vacuoles and chloroplasts A cell also needs to break down waste, store materials, and (in plants) capture light energy, three more membrane-bound organelles take care of these. Lysosomes are membrane-bound sacs of digestive enzymes that break down worn-out organelles, waste and engulfed material ; their membrane keeps those enzymes safely contained. Vacuoles are fluid-filled, membrane-bound sacs used for storage of water, nutrients and wastes , in plant cells a single large central vacuole also provides turgor pressure that supports the cell. Chloroplasts , found only in plant (and some protist) cells, contain the green pigment chlorophyll and are the site of photosynthesis,…

**Page activity:** Understand Band 3 Structure → Function Table Pattern A, Structured recall In your book, draw a two-column table headed Organelle and Structure + Function . Write one row for each of the eleven syllabus structures: cell wall, cell membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, mitochondria, chloroplast, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi body, ribosomes, lysosomes and vacuoles. For each, give one structural detail AND its function in a single sentence.

**Practice:** Describe the structure and function of the mitochondrion, the rough endoplasmic reticulum and the Golgi body, and explain how the last two work together when a cell exports a protein.

**Memory aid:** No named mnemonic is stated in the selected page cards. Use the page’s comparison or physical model where it helps recall the mechanism.

**Page misconception:** ✗ Wrong: The mitochondrion "makes energy" out of nothing. ✓ Right: Energy is not created. The mitochondrion transfers chemical energy stored in glucose into ATP through aerobic respiration. Organelles transform energy and matter; they never create it.
