## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

- **The Outer Layers:** Capsule, cell wall and cell membrane A bacterium is protected and bounded by up to three concentric layers, working from the outside in. The capsule is a slimy protective layer outside the cell wall that helps the cell resist drying out, evade the host's immune system and stick to surfaces (not all bacteria have one). The cell wall (made of peptidoglycan) is a rigid layer that gives the cell shape and prevents it bursting . The cell membrane beneath it is a partially permeable phospholipid bilayer that controls what enters and leaves, and also hosts the enzymes of respiration (since there are no mitochondria).

- **Inside: DNA, Plasmids and Ribosomes:** Cytoplasm, nucleoid, plasmid and ribosomes Without a nucleus or organelles, a prokaryote runs its genetics and protein synthesis directly in the cytoplasm. The cytoplasm is the jelly-like fluid where reactions occur and structures are suspended . The nucleoid is the region holding the single circular chromosome, the cell's essential genes, not enclosed by a membrane . A plasmid is a small separate ring of extra DNA (often antibiotic-resistance genes) that can be copied and passed between cells . Ribosomes (70S, smaller than the eukaryotic 80S) are the site of protein synthesis .

- **Moving and Connecting: Flagella and Pili:** The structures that project beyond the wall Two kinds of projection reach beyond the cell wall, one for movement, one for attachment and gene transfer. Flagella are long whip-like tails that rotate to propel the cell, allowing it to move toward favourable conditions (e.g. nutrients) . Pili are short hair-like projections used to attach to surfaces and other cells, and to transfer DNA (such as plasmids) between bacteria, a route for spreading antibiotic resistance . Both are optional structures: not every prokaryote has them.
