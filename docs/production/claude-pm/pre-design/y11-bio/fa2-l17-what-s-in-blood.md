# What's in Blood?: what s in blood

Site lesson: L17. Learner question: What's in Blood? Why would a cell sacrifice its control centre and bend its own shape, and how does that help it do its one job?

## 1. Exact syllabus content

> B25-FA2-G3-05: Explain the relationship between the structure of plasma, red blood cells, white blood cells and platelets and their function within blood

## 2. Site lesson material

Source: `C:/Users/kygs/hscscience/subjects/biology/year11/fa2-cells-to-systems/lesson17.sa.html` and `docs/production/claude-pm/site-extracts/bio-y11-fa2-l17.md`. Site section order: 1. Plasma, The Transport Fluid; 2. The Three Cellular Components; 3. Why "Structure Fits Function" Matters Here; 4. Anaemia. Relevant ideas:

- Plasma, The Transport Fluid: About 55% of blood is plasma, and its structure as a mostly-water liquid is exactly what makes it a universal transport medium.
- The Three Cellular Components: Each cellular component has been shaped, sometimes literally, for one specialised role: carrying oxygen, defending against disease, or sealing wounds.
- Why "Structure Fits Function" Matters Here: The dot point asks you to explain the relationship between structure and function, so the real skill is justifying why a given feature exists, not just naming parts.
- Anaemia: too little haemoglobin: In anaemia, a person has too few red blood cells or too little haemoglobin, so the blood cannot carry enough oxygen.

Practice demand: Site review sample: Which component of blood defends the body against pathogens?

Site short-answer questions (3): 1. Explain the relationship between the structure and function of red blood cells, white blood cells and platelets within the blood. 2. A red blood cell is biconcave, has no nucleus and is flexible. Explain how each of these three structural features contributes to its function. 3. A person with iron-deficiency anaemia has a normal number of red blood cells but a low haemoglobin content in each. Analyse why this produces tiredness and breathlessness, and explain what this case shows about the relationship between the structure of a blood component and its function.

Site memory/retrieval cue: You can now explain how each blood component's structure fits its function. Next lesson follows the oxygen-carrying red cells to the lungs, where alveoli and capillaries are organised for efficient gas exchange.

## 3. Number and fact check

The page states plasma is about 55% of blood and about 90% water. These are approximate proportions, with no worked calculation.

## 4. Mistakes and traps

The site names this misconception: Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: Red blood cells carry oxygen because they are red, and all blood cells are full cells with a nucleus. ✓ Right: Red cells carry oxygen because they are packed with haemoglobin, the red colour is a side-effect of the iron in it. This is a page-backed teaching trap, not a claim about NESA marking trends.
