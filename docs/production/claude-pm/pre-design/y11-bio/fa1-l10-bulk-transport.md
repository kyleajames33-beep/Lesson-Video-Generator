## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

- **Bulk Transport: Endocytosis and Exocytosis:** Moving things too big for any channel, in and out by vesicle Large particles cannot fit through any protein, so the membrane itself folds around them to carry them in or out. Endocytosis is the bulk uptake of material: the membrane folds inward around the substance, pinching off a vesicle that carries it into the cell (phagocytosis engulfs solids, e.g. a white blood cell engulfing a pathogen; pinocytosis takes in liquid). Exocytosis is the reverse: a vesicle moves to the membrane, fuses with it, and releases its contents outside the cell (e.g. a gland cell secreting a hormone). Both require energy to move and fuse the vesicles.

Complete a row for simple diffusion, facilitated diffusion, active transport, endocytosis and exocytosis. Then write one sentence stating what all the active processes have in common.

**Practice:** Explain the processes of active transport, endocytosis and exocytosis, and state clearly how each differs from passive transport.

**Memory aid:** No named mnemonic is stated in the selected page cards. Use the page’s comparison or physical model where it helps recall the mechanism.

**Page misconception:** ✗ Wrong: Active transport and facilitated diffusion are the same because both use proteins. ✓ Right: Both use membrane proteins, but the direction and energy differ. Facilitated diffusion is passive and moves substances DOWN the gradient (no ATP). Active transport moves substances AGAINST the gradient and REQUIRES ATP. The protein is the only thing they share.
