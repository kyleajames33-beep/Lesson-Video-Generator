## 2. Site lesson content

Whole-page section order: Two Directions of Change; Analogous vs Homologous Structures; Worked Examples of Each.

Selected material:
- Two Directions of Change: Evolution can make species more alike or more different, depending on whether their environments are similar or different, these are the two opposite patterns.
- Analogous vs Homologous Structures: The internal structure of a body part reveals its history: shared underlying plans point to common ancestry (divergence), while similar function on different plans points to independent evolution (convergence).
- Worked Examples of Each: Each pattern is best learned through a clear worked example that shows the selective pressure and the relatedness involved.

Practice: Classify the Examples Pattern C, Sort, then justify For each pair, state whether it shows convergent or divergent evolution and whether the structures are analogous or homologous: (a) the fin of a shark and the fin of a dolphin; (b) the forelimbs of a horse and a human; (c) the gliding membrane of a sugar glider and that of a North American flying squirrel; (d) the spines of a cactus and the leaves of an oak. Give a one-line reason for each. Watch pair (d) carefully Site short-answer demand: Using one example of each, explain the difference between convergent and divergent evolution. Refer to analogous and homologous structures in your answer.

## 4. Misconceptions and traps

Site misconception: - Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: If two animals look similar, they must be closely related. ✓ Right: Outward similarity can result from convergent evolution between unrelated species facing the same environment.

Source-backed response trap: Similar function does not always mean common origin. The cactus spine and oak leaf are homologous modified leaves despite different uses. Source: site L08, misconception, activity and short-answer task. This is a page-based trap, not a claim about NESA marker trends.
