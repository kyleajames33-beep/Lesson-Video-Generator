## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

- **The Lock-and-Key Model:** A rigid active site shaped to one substrate The lock-and-key model explains enzyme specificity by saying the active site has a fixed, exact shape that only the correct substrate can fit, just as one key fits one lock. Proposed by Emil Fischer in 1894, this model pictures the enzyme's active site as having a rigid, fixed shape. Only a substrate whose shape is exactly complementary can slot in, forming the enzyme–substrate complex . Once the reaction is done, the product is released and the active site is unchanged.

- **The Induced Fit Model:** A flexible active site that moulds around its substrate The induced fit model improves on lock-and-key by saying the active site is flexible and changes shape slightly as the correct substrate binds, moulding around it for a tighter, more effective fit. Proposed by Daniel Koshland in 1958, this model recognises that enzymes are flexible. The active site is not a perfect match at first, when the correct substrate approaches, the active site changes shape to grip it more closely. This tighter binding strains the substrate's bonds, helping lower the activation energy even more effectively than a rigid fit would.

- **Comparing the Two Models:** Both explain specificity, but one fits the evidence better Both models explain why enzymes are specific, but they differ in whether the active site is rigid or flexible, and knowing both lets you discuss how scientific models are refined by evidence. Both models explain enzyme specificity and the forming of an enzyme–substrate complex. They differ only in the active site: lock-and-key = rigid, exact match; induced fit = flexible, moulds around the substrate. Induced fit is now preferred because enzymes are known to be flexible proteins.

**Page activity:** Analyse Band 5 Two Diagrams, One Comparison Pattern C, Compare and contrast In your book, draw a simple labelled diagram of each model (lock-and-key and induced fit). Below them, write a two-row comparison: one row on what is the SAME (specificity, active site, complex forms) and one on what DIFFERS (rigid vs flexible active site). Finish with one sentence stating why the induced fit model is considered more accurate.

**Practice:** Use diagrams of the lock-and-key model and the induced fit model to explain how an enzyme interacts with its specific substrate. Identify one way the models are similar and one way they differ.

**Memory aid:** No named mnemonic is stated in the selected page cards. Use the page’s comparison or physical model where it helps recall the mechanism.

**Page misconception:** ✗ Wrong: In the induced fit model, the enzyme changes shape so it can fit ANY substrate. ✓ Right: The active site only changes shape to grip the correct substrate more tightly. The enzyme is still specific, a wrong-shaped substrate cannot trigger that adjustment and will not bind productively. Induced fit refines lock-and-key; it does not make enzymes unspecific.
