## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

- **Unzipping at the Replication Fork:** Each strand becomes a template for a new partner DNA replication begins when the double helix is unzipped at a replication fork, exposing each strand so it can act as a template for building a new complementary strand. The enzyme helicase breaks the hydrogen bonds and unwinds the helix, forming a Y-shaped replication fork . Because the bases are now exposed, free nucleotides can pair with them following the complementary base-pairing rule (A–T, C–G). Replication starts as helicase unzips the helix at the replication fork; each exposed parent strand acts as a template, and free nucleotides pair with it by complementary base pairing (A–T, C–G).

- **The Enzyme Team and the Two Strands:** Primase, DNA polymerase, ligase, and the leading vs lagging strand A team of enzymes copies the exposed strands: primase starts each new strand, DNA polymerase adds nucleotides, and ligase joins fragments, with one strand built continuously (leading) and one in pieces (lagging). Primase lays down a short primer so DNA polymerase has a starting point; DNA polymerase then adds matching nucleotides along each template. The leading strand can be built in one continuous piece; the lagging strand is built in short fragments, which ligase then stitches together. **Page misconception:** ✗ Wrong: Each new DNA molecule is made entirely of brand-new nucleotides, with both old strands discarded. ✓ Right: Replication is semi-conservative . Each new double helix keeps one original ("parent") strand and one newly built strand. The original strand acts as the template for the new one, which is why the copies are so accurate.
