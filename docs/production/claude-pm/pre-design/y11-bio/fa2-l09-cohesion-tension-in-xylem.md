## 2. Site lesson material

Source: `C:/Users/kygs/hscscience/subjects/biology/year11/fa2-cells-to-systems/lesson09.sa.html` and `docs/production/claude-pm/site-extracts/bio-y11-fa2-l09.md`. Site section order: 1. Tension, The Pull from Transpiration; 2. Cohesion, Why the Column Doesn't Break; 3. Osmotic Pressure and the Whole Theory; 4. Why air bubbles can kill a drought-stressed tree. Relevant ideas:

- Tension, The Pull from Transpiration: When water evaporates from the leaves it leaves behind a pulling force in the xylem, because the escaping molecules tug on the ones still attached below them.
- Cohesion, Why the Column Doesn't Break: Water molecules attract one another strongly enough that a column of water in a narrow tube behaves like a single thread that can be pulled without breaking.
- Osmotic Pressure and the Whole Theory: Water enters the root by osmosis, is held together by cohesion, and is pulled up by the tension of transpiration, three forces acting as one continuous system.
- Why air bubbles can kill a drought-stressed tree: Because xylem water is under tension, literally being stretched, it is vulnerable.
