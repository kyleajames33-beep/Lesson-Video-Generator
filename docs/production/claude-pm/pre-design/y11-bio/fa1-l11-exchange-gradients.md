## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

- **Gases: Oxygen and Carbon Dioxide:** Small and non-polar, they diffuse straight through A substance's characteristics decide its route, and small non-polar gases have the easiest path of all. Oxygen (O₂) and carbon dioxide (CO₂) are small, non-polar molecules, so they dissolve in the lipid core and cross by simple diffusion, no protein and no energy needed . The direction follows the gradient set by respiration: a respiring cell uses O₂ (low inside → O₂ diffuses IN) and produces CO₂ (high inside → CO₂ diffuses OUT) . The steeper the gradient, the faster the diffusion, which is why active cells need a constant blood supply to keep gradients steep.

- **Glucose and Salts: Needing a Protein:** Polar and charged, channels, carriers, sometimes pumps Polar molecules and charged ions are repelled by the hydrophobic core, so they must use membrane proteins, and the direction of their gradient decides whether energy is needed. Glucose is a large polar molecule, so it cannot cross the lipid core; it moves by facilitated diffusion through a carrier protein, down its gradient (e.g. into a respiring cell that is using it up) . Salts (ions such as Na⁺, Cl⁻) are charged, so they cross through protein channels by facilitated diffusion when moving down their gradient, or by active transport (with ATP) when a cell must move them against the gradient (e.g. ion uptake in root or kidney cells).

- **Wastes Out, and the General Rule:** Putting gradient and characteristics together Wastes build up inside a cell, so they move out down their gradient, and the same two-factor logic explains every exchange. Wastes such as CO₂ and urea are produced by the cell, so they are at higher concentration inside than outside and diffuse OUT down their gradient (CO₂ as a small non-polar gas crosses freely). The general rule that answers this whole dot point is: a material's movement across a membrane depends on (1) the direction of its concentration gradient, set by the cell's activity, and (2) its characteristics, small non-polar molecules diffuse freely, large polar molecules and ions need proteins, and movement against a gradient…

**Page misconception:** ✗ Wrong: Oxygen always moves into cells and carbon dioxide always moves out, no matter what. ✓ Right: The direction depends on the gradient , which is set by the cell's activity. In a respiring cell, O₂ is used up (low inside) so it diffuses in, and CO₂ builds up (high inside) so it diffuses out. The substance always moves down its gradient, the cell's metabolism is what creates that gradient.
