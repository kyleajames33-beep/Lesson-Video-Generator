## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

- **Measuring Cell Size with a Mini-Grid:** The magnification equation, worked through A grid lying on the stage is magnified with the specimen, so reading a cell against it gives the real size straight away; the equation is what converts between a real size and a magnified image. The key equation is magnification = image size ÷ real size, which rearranges to real size = image size ÷ magnification . Which way you use it depends on what you measured. From the stage grid: the grid sits beside the specimen and is magnified by the same amount, so its squares are a ruler marked in real micrometres ,  a cell spanning 4 squares of 100 µm is 400 µm across, and there is nothing to divide. The equation then works forwards as a check: at ×100…

- **Specialised Cells Vary in Size:** Why measuring a variety of cells matters Cells are specialised for different jobs, and their sizes and shapes vary widely, measuring several reveals this range. A red blood cell is about 8 µm across; a human cheek cell about 60 µm ; an onion epidermal cell can be 200–300 µm long; a nerve cell may be over a metre long. Measuring a variety of specialised cells with the mini-grid method shows that cell size relates to function, and that repeating the measurement on several cells improves the reliability of an estimate. Always state results to an appropriate number of significant figures and include the unit.

**Page activity:** Apply Band 4 Calculate Three Cell Sizes Pattern C, Worked calculation set Using real size = image size ÷ magnification, calculate each cell's real size and write it in µm. (a) image 1200 µm at ×400; (b) image 0.9 mm at ×100 (convert to µm first); (c) image 2400 µm at ×600. Show your working for each, including the unit conversion in (b).

**Practice:** Outline how you would prepare a wet mount slide of onion epidermis and identify the cytoplasm, cell wall and nucleus. Then calculate the real size of a cell whose image is 1500 µm at ×500 magnification, showing your working.

**Memory aid:** No named mnemonic is stated in the selected page cards. Use the page’s comparison or physical model where it helps recall the mechanism.

**Page misconception:** ✗ Wrong: To find the real size, you multiply the image size by the magnification. ✓ Right: You divide . Real size = image size ÷ magnification. The image is the magnified, larger view; the real cell is smaller, so dividing brings it back down to its true size.

## 3. Number and science check

The page gives 4 grid squares × 100 µm = 400 µm, which appears 40 mm at ×100. Its 30 mm image at ×100 gives 300 µm. The hook’s 30 mm at ×400 gives 75 µm. The activity results are 1200 ÷ 400 = 3 µm; 0.9 mm = 900 µm and 900 ÷ 100 = 9 µm; 2400 µm ÷ 600 = 4 µm. The short-answer result is 1500 µm ÷ 500 = 3 µm. The syllabus specifies field-of-view diameter ÷ cells across. The page emphasises image size ÷ magnification, so the practical needs an explicit field-of-view example.
