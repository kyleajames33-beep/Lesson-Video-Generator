## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

- **Three Kinds of Solution:** Hypertonic, hypotonic, isotonic, and the direction of water Tonicity compares the solute concentration outside the cell with inside, and that comparison tells you which way water will move. A hypertonic solution has more solute (less water) than the cell, so water moves OUT of the cell by osmosis and the cell shrinks . A hypotonic solution has less solute (more water) than the cell, so water moves INTO the cell and it swells . An isotonic solution has equal solute concentration, so there is no NET movement of water and the cell stays the same size . Remember: water always moves toward the higher solute concentration.

- **Animal Cells: No Wall, No Safety Net:** Lysis, crenation, and why isotonic is "just right" An animal cell has only its membrane to contain it, so gaining or losing too much water has serious consequences. In a hypotonic solution, water floods in and the animal cell swells and bursts (lysis / haemolysis for red blood cells) because there is no wall to resist it. In a hypertonic solution, water leaves and the cell shrivels (crenation) . In an isotonic solution the cell stays normal, which is why this is the only safe environment for animal cells.

- **Plant Cells: The Wall Changes Everything:** Turgid, flaccid and plasmolysed A plant cell's rigid wall resists swelling, so the same osmosis produces very different, non-fatal outcomes. In a hypotonic solution, water enters but the wall stops the cell bursting; it becomes firm and turgid, the ideal, supportive state for a plant cell . In an isotonic solution there is no net change and the cell may become slightly flaccid (losing turgor). In a hypertonic solution water leaves, the membrane pulls away from the wall, and the cell becomes plasmolysed , the cause of wilting. The rule for predicting: find which side has more solute, conclude which way water moves, then apply the cell type, walled (plant) cells resist bursting; wall-less…

**Page activity:** Apply Band 5 Predict the Outcomes Grid Pattern C, Prediction table Draw a 2×3 grid: rows = animal cell, plant cell; columns = hypotonic, isotonic, hypertonic. In each box, write (a) the direction water moves and (b) the resulting state, using the correct term (lysis, normal, crenation, turgid, normal/flaccid, plasmolysed).

**Practice:** Predict and explain the effect of placing (a) an animal cell and (b) a plant cell into a hypotonic solution, and into a hypertonic solution. Use the direction of water movement in your reasoning.

**Page misconception:** ✗ Wrong: A "hypertonic solution" means a solution with lots of water that floods into the cell. ✓ Right: Hypertonic means more SOLUTE (and therefore LESS water) than the cell. So water moves OUT of the cell into the hypertonic solution, and the cell shrinks. The "tonic" refers to solute concentration, not water.
