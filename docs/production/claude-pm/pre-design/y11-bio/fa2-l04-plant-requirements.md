## 2. Site lesson material

Source: `C:/Users/kygs/hscscience/subjects/biology/year11/fa2-cells-to-systems/lesson04.sa.html` and `docs/production/claude-pm/site-extracts/bio-y11-fa2-l04.md`. Site section order: 1. Gas Requirements; 2. Water Requirements; 3. Mineral Nutrient Requirements; 4. Why fertiliser bags carry three numbers. Relevant ideas:

- Gas Requirements: A plant runs two opposite gas-using processes, photosynthesis and respiration, so it requires both carbon dioxide and oxygen, just at different times and places.
- Water Requirements: Water is needed for far more than photosynthesis: it carries dissolved substances, keeps cells firm, and is itself a reactant.
- Mineral Nutrient Requirements: Glucose supplies carbon, hydrogen and oxygen, but a plant must build proteins, DNA and chlorophyll too, and these need extra elements that come from soil minerals.
- Why fertiliser bags carry three numbers: Every bag of garden fertiliser is stamped with an "N-P-K" ratio, the proportions of nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium it supplies.

Practice demand: Site review sample: Which raw materials does a plant require for photosynthesis?

Site short-answer questions (3): 1. Describe the nutrient, water and gas requirements of plants for photosynthesis and respiration. For each requirement, identify the process it serves. 2. A plant is kept in continuous darkness for 48 hours. Describe how the gases the plant requires, and the gases it exchanges with the air, differ between this period and a bright sunny day. 3. A hydroponic grower supplies a crop with light, warmth, water and dissolved nitrate, phosphate and potassium, but forgets magnesium. After three weeks the older leaves are pale yellow and growth has slowed. Analyse this result by relating the missing nutrient to the plant's requirements for photosynthesis and respiration.

Site memory/retrieval cue: You can now describe every raw material a plant takes in and match each to photosynthesis or respiration. Next lesson zooms in on photosynthesis itself: the conditions it needs to run.
