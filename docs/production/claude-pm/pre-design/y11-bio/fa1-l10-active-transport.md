## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

- **Active Transport: Pumping Uphill:** Against the gradient, using a carrier protein and ATP When a cell needs more of a substance than its surroundings hold, diffusion cannot help, so the cell pumps it in using energy. Active transport is the movement of a substance across a membrane against its concentration gradient (from low to high concentration), using a carrier (integral) protein and energy from ATP . The carrier protein binds the substance, ATP is broken down to change the protein's shape, and the substance is released on the other side. Because it works against the gradient, active transport always requires energy , unlike all the passive processes. It lets cells accumulate nutrients (e.g. mineral ions into root cells)…

- **Why It Is Worth the Energy:** The pay-off for active processes Energy buys the cell abilities diffusion cannot provide: concentrating scarce nutrients, expelling wastes, and handling bulky materials. Active processes let a cell accumulate nutrients against the gradient (active transport), take in large particles or pathogens (endocytosis), and secrete proteins, hormones or wastes (exocytosis) . The trade-off is energy: every active process consumes ATP, so cells doing a lot of transport, like kidney, gut and nerve cells, contain many mitochondria to supply it . This is a recurring theme in biology: structure (many mitochondria) supports a high-energy function (active transport).

**Page misconception:** ✗ Wrong: Active transport and facilitated diffusion are the same because both use proteins. ✓ Right: Both use membrane proteins, but the direction and energy differ. Facilitated diffusion is passive and moves substances DOWN the gradient (no ATP). Active transport moves substances AGAINST the gradient and REQUIRES ATP. The protein is the only thing they share.
