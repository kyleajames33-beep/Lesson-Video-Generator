## 2. Site lesson content

Whole-page section order: Two Tempos of Evolution; Reading the Two Models off a Tree; What the Fossil Record Says; Why tempo matters for predicting the future.

Selected material:
- Two Tempos of Evolution: Evolution by natural selection is not in dispute here, what these two models argue about is how fast change is spread out through time.
- Reading the Two Models off a Tree: On a phylogenetic tree, time runs up the page and each branch is a lineage, so the slope and the timing of the splits reveal which tempo of evolution is being modelled.
- What the Fossil Record Says: The fossil record does not crown one model the winner, different lineages show different tempos, so both gradualism and punctuated equilibrium can describe real evolutionary history.

## 4. Misconceptions and traps

Site misconception: - Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: Punctuated equilibrium means evolution happens "all at once" and contradicts natural selection. ✓ Right: Both models work entirely through natural selection and common descent.

Source-backed response trap: Punctuated equilibrium means long stasis plus geologically short change, not an instantaneous jump in one individual. Source: site L14, misconception, activity and short-answer task. This is a page-based trap, not a claim about NESA marker trends.
