## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

- **Three Factors That Change Enzyme Efficiency:** Temperature, pH and substrate concentration An enzyme's efficiency, how fast it converts substrate, depends strongly on three measurable conditions: temperature, pH and substrate concentration. Temperature: raising it increases the rate up to an optimum , then high heat denatures the enzyme (its active site changes shape permanently) and activity collapses. pH: each enzyme has an optimum pH; too acidic or alkaline also denatures it. Substrate concentration: more substrate raises the rate until all active sites are busy (saturation), after which adding more makes no difference.

**Page activity:** Create Band 5 Write Your Own Method Pattern D, Plan and conduct Choose ONE factor (temperature, pH or substrate concentration). In your book, write a complete plan for an experiment using catalase and hydrogen peroxide: state your hypothesis, the independent/dependent/controlled variables, a numbered method (at least 4 steps), how you will repeat trials, and one safety precaution. If you can, conduct it and record your results in a table.

**Practice:** Plan a laboratory experiment to demonstrate the effect of temperature on the efficiency of a selected enzyme. Identify the independent, dependent and controlled variables, outline a method, and explain how you would make the experiment valid and reliable.

**Memory aid:** No named mnemonic is stated in the selected page cards. Use the page’s comparison or physical model where it helps recall the mechanism.

**Page misconception:** ✗ Wrong: To see a bigger effect, change two variables at once (e.g. raise both temperature and substrate amount). ✓ Right: A valid experiment changes only ONE independent variable at a time. If you change two, you cannot tell which one caused the change in the result, the experiment becomes invalid. Run separate experiments for temperature, pH and substrate concentration.
