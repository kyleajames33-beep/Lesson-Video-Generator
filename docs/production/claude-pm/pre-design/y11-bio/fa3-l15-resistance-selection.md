## 2. Site lesson content

Whole-page section order: Resistance Is Natural Selection in Fast-Forward; Two Case Studies; Evaluating the Evidence, and the Cost; Why this is on every hospital wall.

Selected material:
- Resistance Is Natural Selection in Fast-Forward: A resistant population is not produced by the drug; it is produced by selection acting on variation that already exists in the population.
- Two Case Studies: Bacteria and Mosquitoes: Antibiotic resistance in bacteria and DDT resistance in mosquitoes are two instances of one process, only the organism and the human chemical differ.
- Evaluating the Evidence, and the Cost: A secondary source is only as trustworthy as its methods, so analysing resistance means judging the data as well as reading the trend.

## 4. Misconceptions and traps

Site misconception: - Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: Bacteria "become resistant" because the antibiotic teaches them to fight it, or because they "want" to survive. ✓ Right: A few individuals are already resistant due to random mutation before the antibiotic is used.

Source-backed response trap: The chemical selects resistant variants; it does not create a purposeful response. The activity trend is illustrative, not observed surveillance data. Source: site L15, misconception, activity and short-answer task. This is a page-based trap, not a claim about NESA marker trends.
