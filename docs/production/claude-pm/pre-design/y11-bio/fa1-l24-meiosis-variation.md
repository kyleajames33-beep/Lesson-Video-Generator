## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

- **Three Sources of Variation:** Crossing over, independent assortment and random segregation The genetic variety of gametes comes from three distinct processes that rearrange the chromosomes during meiosis. Crossing over occurs in prophase I : homologous chromosomes pair up and exchange equivalent segments with each other, producing new combinations of alleles on each chromosome . Independent assortment occurs in metaphase I : each pair of homologous chromosomes lines up at the equator independently of the other pairs, so the maternal and paternal chromosomes are mixed randomly between the gametes . - **Why It All Adds Up to Variation:** Three processes, combined with fertilisation, make every offspring unique The three processes do not act alone, they combine, so the number of genetically different gametes an individual can make is enormous. Crossing over, independent assortment and random segregation work together so that each gamete carries a unique combination of alleles, and an individual can produce a huge number of genetically different gametes . When a random sperm fertilises a random egg, the genetic possibilities multiply again. **Page misconception:** ✗ Wrong: Meiosis produces two identical cells, just like mitosis, only with fewer chromosomes. ✓ Right: Meiosis produces four cells, each with half the chromosome number, and each is genetically different because of crossing over, independent assortment and random segregation. Identical daughter cells are the result of mitosis, not meiosis.

## 3. Number and science check

Human 2n = 46 and n = 23. Independent assortment gives 2^23 = 8,388,608 combinations before crossing over. A two-pair model yields 2^2 = 4.
