## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

- **What Photosynthesis Does:** Turning light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose Photosynthesis is the process that captures light energy from the Sun and uses it to build energy-rich glucose from simple raw materials. It takes place inside chloroplasts , which contain the green pigment chlorophyll . Chlorophyll absorbs light, mostly red and blue wavelengths, and this absorbed energy powers the reaction.

- **The Summary Equation:** Reactants → products, in words and in balanced symbols A summary equation captures the whole process in one line: it lists what goes in (reactants), what comes out (products), and the energy input that drives it. Word equation: carbon dioxide + water → (light energy, chlorophyll) glucose + oxygen.

- **Photosynthesis and Respiration: A Cycle:** Why the two equations are almost mirror images The products of photosynthesis are the reactants of respiration, and vice versa, together they cycle carbon, oxygen and energy through living things. Respiration's summary equation is C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O (energy released). Read it next to photosynthesis and you can see they run in opposite directions: photosynthesis stores energy by building glucose and releasing oxygen; respiration releases that stored energy by breaking glucose down using oxygen.

**Page activity:** Understand Band 3 Label and Balance Pattern A, Structured recall In your book, write out the balanced symbol equation for photosynthesis from memory. Then label each substance as a reactant or a product, and add an arrow note showing where light energy and chlorophyll act. Finally, write one sentence in plain English describing what the equation tells us happens to the energy.

**Practice:** Use the summary equation to describe the process of photosynthesis. In your answer, state the balanced equation, identify the reactants and products, and explain what happens to energy.

**Memory aid:** No named mnemonic is stated in the selected page cards. Use the page’s comparison or physical model where it helps recall the mechanism.

**Page misconception:** ✗ Wrong: Plants get their food (mass) from the soil through their roots. ✓ Right: The bulk of a plant's new mass comes from carbon atoms in carbon dioxide drawn from the air, fixed into glucose during photosynthesis. The soil mainly supplies water and small amounts of mineral ions, not the carbon that builds the plant body.
