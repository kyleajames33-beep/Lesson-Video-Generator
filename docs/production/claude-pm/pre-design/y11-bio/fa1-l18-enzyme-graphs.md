## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

- **The Temperature Curve:** Rise to an optimum, then a sharp fall As temperature rises, enzyme activity increases to an optimum, then drops sharply because the enzyme denatures. Up to the optimum, more heat means molecules move faster, so enzyme and substrate collide more often and the rate rises. At the optimum (≈37 °C for human enzymes) the rate is highest. Beyond it, the heat breaks the bonds holding the enzyme's shape, the active site is permanently distorted ( denatured ), and the rate falls steeply to zero.

- **The pH Curve:** A bell-shape peaking at the optimum pH Enzyme activity peaks at an optimum pH and falls away on either side, because pH values too far from the optimum also denature the enzyme. Each enzyme has an optimum pH suited to where it works, for example, pepsin in the stomach works best near pH 2, while most cytoplasmic enzymes prefer pH 7. Moving too far either side of the optimum disrupts the bonds that hold the active site's shape, so activity falls. pH graph: a bell-shape peaking at the optimum pH; on both sides the rate falls because the wrong pH denatures the enzyme, distorting the active site.

- **The Substrate-Concentration Curve:** Rise, then a plateau, and why it is NOT denaturation As substrate concentration increases, the rate rises and then plateaus, not because the enzyme is damaged, but because all the active sites become saturated. At low substrate concentration, many active sites are empty, so adding substrate raises the rate. Once all active sites are working flat-out, the enzyme is saturated , adding more substrate cannot increase the rate, so the line plateaus. Only adding more enzyme would raise it further. Substrate graph: rate rises with substrate, then plateaus once the enzyme is saturated (all active sites occupied). This plateau is NOT denaturation, the enzyme is undamaged; it is simply working at…

**Page activity:** Analyse Band 5 Explain Each Region Pattern E, Data analysis Imagine a temperature graph for an enzyme: rate rises from 0 °C to a peak at 40 °C, then falls to zero by 60 °C. In your book, split the curve into three regions (0–40, the peak, 40–60) and write one sentence for each explaining what is happening to the enzyme and substrate at the molecular level. Use the words collisions, optimum and denaturation.

**Practice:** A graph shows enzyme activity rising as substrate concentration increases, then levelling off to a plateau. Analyse the graph to explain its shape, and contrast why this plateau differs from the fall seen on a temperature graph.

**Memory aid:** No named mnemonic is stated in the selected page cards. Use the page’s comparison or physical model where it helps recall the mechanism.

**Page misconception:** ✗ Wrong: The substrate-concentration graph levels off because the enzyme has denatured. ✓ Right: The substrate graph plateaus because the enzyme is saturated , all active sites are busy, so adding more substrate can't speed things up. The enzyme is perfectly fine. Only the temperature and pH graphs fall because of denaturation.

## 3. Number and science check

The site graph has an example optimum near 37–40 °C. The activity graph rises to 40 °C and falls to zero by 60 °C. This is an example enzyme, not a universal optimum.
