## 2. Site lesson material

Source: `C:/Users/kygs/hscscience/subjects/biology/year11/fa2-cells-to-systems/lesson02.sa.html` and `docs/production/claude-pm/site-extracts/bio-y11-fa2-l02.md`. Site section order: 1. Calculating SA; 2. Why a High SA; 3. SA; 4. Why small animals can't stop eating. Relevant ideas:

- Calculating SA:V: As an object grows, its volume grows faster than its surface area, so the surface available for each unit of inside contents shrinks.
- Why a High SA:V Means Fast Exchange: Materials enter and leave a cell only through its surface, so a cell with more surface per unit of volume can supply and clear its interior faster.
- SA:V Sets the Limit on Cell Size: Because exchange cannot keep up once a cell grows too big, organisms either keep their cells small or adapt their shape to boost surface area.

Practice demand: Predict colour penetration in 1, 2 and 3 cm agar cubes, calculate SA:V, and name controls.

Site short-answer questions (3): 1. Explain the effect of the surface-area-to-volume ratio on the rate of movement of materials into and out of a cell. Use a worked SA:V comparison of a small and a large cube to support your explanation. 2. A student cuts agar cubes of side 1 cm, 2 cm and 3 cm from one block, places them in the same dilute acid for 10 minutes, then cuts each open and measures how far the colour change has travelled. Calculate the SA:V ratio of each cube, predict which cube will be colour-changed through the greatest proportion of its volume, and state two variables that must be controlled. 3. An amoeba about 0.5 mm across survives by exchanging all of its materials directly across its own surface. A whale of the same shape but a million times longer could not. Analyse, using surface-area-to-volume ratio, why increasing size forces an organism to abandon direct exchange across its body surface, and explain what this implies about the maximum size of a single cell.

Site memory/retrieval cue: You can now calculate SA:V and explain how it controls the rate of exchange and limits cell size. Next lesson climbs the ladder from a single cell up to whole body systems.

## 3. Number and fact check

Cube SA = 6L², volume = L³. L=1 gives 6/1 = 6:1; L=2 gives 24/8 = 3:1; L=3 gives 54/27 = 2:1. The site agar activity uses 1, 2 and 3 cm cubes. Its arithmetic agrees.

## 4. Mistakes and traps

The site names this misconception: Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: As a cell gets bigger, both its surface area and volume increase, so exchange keeps up fine. ✓ Right: Both do increase, but volume increases faster (with the cube of length) than surface area (with the square). This is a page-backed teaching trap, not a claim about NESA marking trends.
