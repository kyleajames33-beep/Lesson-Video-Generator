## 2. Site lesson content

Whole-page section order: Which Abiotic Factors, Where; Reading a Comparison Table; Making the Comparison Fair & Meaningful.

Selected material:
- Which Abiotic Factors, Where: Aquatic and terrestrial ecosystems share some abiotic factors but each also has factors that are specific to it.
- Reading a Comparison Table: Laying the abiotic data out in a table makes the differences between ecosystems jump out, and lets you explain the life each supports.
- Making the Comparison Fair & Meaningful: A meaningful comparison uses consistent methods and then explains the biological consequence of each abiotic difference.

Practice: Activity 1 Analyse Band 5 Compare Three Ecosystems Pattern B, Work with data Abiotic data: Mountain stream , temp 8–12 °C, dissolved O₂ high, pH 7.2, salinity ~0 ppt. Estuary , temp 14–26 °C, dissolved O₂ moderate, pH 7.8, salinity 5–25 ppt (variable). Mangrove mudflat , temp 18–34 °C, soil waterlogged and saline, low soil O₂, pH 7. Compare the salinity and temperature stability of the three, and explain which would be hardest for organisms to live in and why. Site short-answer demand: Compare the abiotic factors of one aquatic and one terrestrial ecosystem, and explain how two of these differences affect the organisms that can live there.

## 4. Misconceptions and traps

Site misconception: - Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: The same abiotic factors matter equally in every ecosystem, so you measure the same things everywhere. ✓ Right: Some factors are shared (temperature, pH, light), but others are specific: dissolved oxygen, salinity and turbidity matter in aquatic systems, while soil type, wind and humidity matter more on land.

Source-backed response trap: Compare measurements that make sense in both habitats. Dissolved oxygen is useful for aquatic water, not dry scrub soil in the same units. Source: site L20, misconception, activity and short-answer task. This is a page-based trap, not a claim about NESA marker trends.
