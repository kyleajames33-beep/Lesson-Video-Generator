## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

- **The Summary Equation:** Reactants in, products out, energy released A single summary equation captures what cellular respiration achieves: it takes glucose and oxygen and releases carbon dioxide, water and usable energy. In words: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy (ATP) . As a balanced chemical equation: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + energy (ATP) . The reactants are glucose and oxygen; the products are carbon dioxide and water, with energy released and captured as ATP . This is aerobic respiration because it uses oxygen, and it occurs largely in the mitochondria .

- **Reading the Equation Both Ways:** What each side tells you, and why it is only a summary The equation is a compact description, so understanding it means knowing what every reactant and product represents and remembering it stands for many smaller reactions. Left side (reactants): glucose is the fuel and oxygen is needed to fully break it down. Right side (products): carbon dioxide and water are the waste products, and energy is released and stored in ATP for the cell to use. The equation is only a summary, in reality respiration is a long sequence of small, enzyme-controlled steps, so the energy is released gradually and safely rather than all at once . This is why it is described as a controlled release of energy.

- **When Oxygen Runs Out: Anaerobic Respiration:** A back-up pathway with a different equation When oxygen is scarce, cells can still release some energy from glucose, but the pathway and its products change. Without oxygen, cells use anaerobic respiration . In animal (muscle) cells: glucose → lactic acid + a small amount of energy (ATP). In yeast and plant cells: glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide + a small amount of energy (ATP) . Because the glucose is only partly broken down, anaerobic respiration releases far less energy than aerobic respiration, which is why oxygen-using cells produce so much more ATP . This is why you breathe hard after a sprint, your muscles ran short of oxygen and built up lactic acid.

**Page activity:** Apply Band 4 Write and Label the Equation Pattern B, Apply & annotate From memory, write the balanced summary equation for aerobic cellular respiration. Then label each substance as a reactant or a product , and write one sentence stating what happens to the energy. Finally, write the same process as a word equation.

**Practice:** Use the summary equation to describe the process of aerobic cellular respiration. Include the balanced equation and explain the role of each reactant and product.

**Memory aid:** No named mnemonic is stated in the selected page cards. Use the page’s comparison or physical model where it helps recall the mechanism.

**Page misconception:** ✗ Wrong: Cellular respiration "creates" energy, and respiration is the same thing as breathing. ✓ Right: Energy is not created, it is released from the bonds of glucose and transferred to ATP (some is lost as heat). And cellular respiration is a chemical reaction inside cells; breathing (ventilation) just supplies the oxygen and removes the CO₂ it needs.
