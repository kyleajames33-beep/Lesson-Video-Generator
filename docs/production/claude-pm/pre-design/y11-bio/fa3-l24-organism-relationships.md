## 2. Site lesson content

Whole-page section order: The Six Relationship Types; Reading a Predator–Prey Graph; How Competition Affects Populations.

Selected material:
- The Six Relationship Types: Every relationship between two organisms can be classified by its effect (+ benefit, − harm, 0 no effect) on each partner.
- How Competition Affects Populations: Competition for a limited resource reduces the growth or survival of the competitors, capping population size and sometimes excluding the weaker species.

Practice: Activity 1 Analyse Band 5 Classify and Analyse Pattern A, Classify, then analyse data (a) Classify each relationship (+/−/0 for each): a remora fish riding a shark and eating its leftovers; a cuckoo laying eggs in another bird's nest; a fungus and an alga forming a lichen. (b) A graph shows a feral cat population peaking about 6 months after a mouse plague peaks. State the relationship and explain, using the lag, which species is controlling which. Site short-answer demand: Using examples, distinguish between predation, competition and mutualism, and analyse how a predator–prey relationship affects the populations of both species over time.

## 4. Misconceptions and traps

Site misconception: - Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: A predator and a parasite are basically the same thing because both harm another organism. ✓ Right: A predator kills its prey quickly and usually eats it whole or in part; a parasite lives on or in its host, feeding off it over time without (usually) killing it immediately, a dead host is no use to most parasites.

Source-backed response trap: Classify both organisms’ effects, then use a time lag to explain why a predator peak follows a prey peak. Source: site L24, misconception, activity and short-answer task. This is a page-based trap, not a claim about NESA marker trends.
