## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

- **Interphase: G1, S and G2:** The cell prepares before it ever divides Most of a cell's life is spent not dividing but preparing, growing and copying its DNA so that division can be safe and accurate. The cell cycle begins with interphase , made of three phases. In gap 1 (G1) the cell grows, makes proteins and organelles, and carries out its normal functions. In synthesis (S) the DNA is replicated, so each chromosome becomes two identical sister chromatids. In gap 2 (G2) the cell grows further, makes the proteins needed for division, and checks the replicated DNA for errors. Only after G2 is the cell ready to enter mitosis.

- **Why the Order Matters, and Checkpoints:** Accounting for the cycle as a controlled, ordered sequence Accounting for the cell cycle means explaining not just what happens in each phase but why the order is essential for producing identical cells. The cycle is ordered for a reason: DNA must be replicated in S phase before mitosis, or the daughter cells would not each receive a complete set of chromosomes; mitosis must separate the chromatids before cytokinesis, or the split cell would not contain a full nucleus . The cell uses checkpoints (especially between G2 and mitosis) to verify the DNA is fully and correctly copied before division proceeds. **Page misconception:** ✗ Wrong: Mitosis is the whole cell cycle, and it produces four daughter cells. ✓ Right: Mitosis is only the nuclear-division part of the cycle (most of the cycle is interphase). Mitosis followed by cytokinesis produces two genetically identical daughter cells, four cells with halved chromosomes is meiosis, which is a different process.
