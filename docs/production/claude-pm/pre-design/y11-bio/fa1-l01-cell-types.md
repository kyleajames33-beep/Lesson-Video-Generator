## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

- **The Two Fundamental Cell Types:** One line divides all cellular life: the membrane-bound nucleus Every cell that has ever been found belongs to one of two groups, and the dividing line is simply whether the DNA is wrapped inside its own membrane. A prokaryote (Greek: "before the kernel/nucleus") is a cell with no membrane-bound nucleus and no membrane-bound organelles, its single circular DNA molecule lies free in the cytoplasm in a region called the nucleoid . All bacteria are prokaryotes.

- **Comparing the Architecture:** Size, DNA, organelles, feature by feature The presence of a nucleus comes bundled with a whole set of other differences in size, DNA shape, and internal organisation. Prokaryotes: no nucleus, free circular DNA + plasmids, no membrane-bound organelles, small, 70S ribosomes. Eukaryotes: membrane-bound nucleus, linear chromosomes, membrane-bound organelles, larger, 80S ribosomes. Shared by both: membrane, cytoplasm, ribosomes, DNA.

- **How We Know, Using a Variety of Sources:** Why the electron microscope changed the story What we can claim about a cell's structure is limited by the tool we use to look at it, better resolution revealed detail that earlier scientists simply could not see. A light microscope can resolve points about 200 nm apart, enough to see a cell and its nucleus, but not fine internal membranes. The electron microscope resolves to around 0.2 nm , a thousand times finer, and this is what revealed that prokaryotes lack the internal membrane-bound compartments eukaryotes have.

**Page activity:** Understand Band 3 Build a Comparison Table Pattern A, Structured comparison In your book, draw a three-column table with the headings Feature , Prokaryotic , Eukaryotic . Complete a row for each of: nucleus, DNA shape, organelles, typical size, ribosome type, and one example organism. Then add a final row titled "Shared features" and list everything the two cell types have in common.

**Practice:** Compare the cellular structures of a prokaryotic cell and a eukaryotic cell. In your answer, identify two structures they share and two ways in which they differ.

**Memory aid:** No named mnemonic is stated in the selected page cards. Use the page’s comparison or physical model where it helps recall the mechanism.

**Page misconception:** ✗ Wrong: Prokaryotic cells are "primitive" and can't do much because they have no organelles. ✓ Right: Prokaryotes are highly successful and carry out all life processes, they just do so without compartmentalising them into membrane-bound organelles. "Simpler structure" is not "less capable".
