## 2. Site lesson material

Source: `C:/Users/kygs/hscscience/subjects/biology/year11/fa2-cells-to-systems/lesson13.sa.html` and `docs/production/claude-pm/site-extracts/bio-y11-fa2-l13.md`. Site section order: 1. Why Animals Need Organ Systems; 2. The Four Management Systems; 3. The Circulatory System Ties It Together; 4. Why a failing kidney affects the whole body. Relevant ideas:

- Why Animals Need Organ Systems: Because animals are large and use energy quickly, materials cannot simply diffuse far enough or fast enough, so specialised organ systems take over the jobs of supply and removal.
- The Four Management Systems: Each of the four needs is managed mainly by one organ system, built from a team of organs suited to that job.
- The Circulatory System Ties It Together: No system reaches the body's cells directly, they all hand their materials to the blood, which is the shared transport network linking supply, exchange and removal.
- Why a failing kidney affects the whole body: Because the organ systems are interdependent, a problem in one ripples through the rest.

Practice demand: Site review sample: Which organ system removes nitrogenous wastes such as urea?

Site short-answer questions (3): 1. Describe the organ systems required for nutrient, water, gas and waste management in animals, and explain how the circulatory system connects them. 2. Trace a glucose molecule from a meal to a leg muscle cell, and then trace the carbon dioxide that cell produces out of the body. Name every organ system involved at each stage. 3. Describe the organ systems required for nutrient, water, gas and waste management in animals, and analyse why a large active animal cannot manage these four needs by diffusion across its body surface alone.
