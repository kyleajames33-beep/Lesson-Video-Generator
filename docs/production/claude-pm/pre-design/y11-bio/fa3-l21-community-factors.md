## 2. Site lesson content

Whole-page section order: Abundance, Distribution & Diversity; How Abiotic Factors Shape Communities; How Biotic Factors Shape Communities.

Selected material:
- Abundance, Distribution & Diversity: When ecologists describe a community, they focus on three measures, how many, where, and how varied, and all three are shaped by abiotic and biotic factors.
- How Abiotic Factors Shape Communities: Every species can survive only within a tolerance range of each abiotic factor, and the factor in shortest supply limits where and how many can live.
- How Biotic Factors Shape Communities: Living organisms compete, eat one another and spread disease, and these interactions strongly shape who lives where, in what numbers, and how varied the community is.

Practice: Classify and Explain the Factors Pattern A, Classify, then justify For each factor, state whether it is abiotic or biotic and which measure it most affects (abundance, distribution or diversity): (a) a sudden frost; (b) a new predator arriving; (c) a region with rich, deep soil; (d) two bird species competing for the same nest holes. Give one line each explaining the effect. Site short-answer demand: Explain, using examples, how both abiotic and biotic factors influence the abundance, distribution and diversity of species in an ecosystem.

## 4. Misconceptions and traps

Site misconception: - Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: Only the physical environment (abiotic factors) decides which species live where. ✓ Right: Living (biotic) factors are just as important.

Source-backed response trap: Abundance is number, distribution is location, and diversity is variety. A predator can change all three through food-web effects. Source: site L21, misconception, activity and short-answer task. This is a page-based trap, not a claim about NESA marker trends.
