## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

- **Somatic Cells, the Body Cells:** Diploid cells that build and maintain the organism Somatic cells are all the body cells of an organism except the sex cells; they are diploid, carrying two full sets of chromosomes. Skin, muscle, nerve, bone and liver cells are all somatic . In humans each carries 46 chromosomes (23 pairs), they are diploid (2n) . Their role is to make up the body's tissues and organs, and to grow, repair and replace themselves. They do this by dividing through mitosis , which produces two genetically identical diploid cells.

- **Gametic Cells, the Sex Cells:** Haploid cells specialised for reproduction Gametic cells (gametes) are the sex cells, sperm and egg, which are haploid, carrying only one set of chromosomes, and are specialised for fertilisation. Each human gamete carries 23 chromosomes , one of each pair. It is haploid (n) . Gametes are made by meiosis , the division that halves the chromosome number. Their role is to fuse at fertilisation : a haploid sperm joins a haploid egg to form a diploid zygote (46), restoring the full set. Gametes are also structurally specialised, sperm have a tail for swimming; eggs are large with food stores. - **Comparing Structures and Roles:** Why halving then doubling keeps chromosome number constant Somatic and gametic cells have complementary roles: somatic cells maintain the body's chromosome number through mitosis, while gametes halve it through meiosis so that fertilisation restores it exactly. Because gametes are haploid, fertilisation of two of them gives a diploid zygote, so the chromosome number stays constant at 46 from one generation to the next, instead of doubling each time. Somatic = diploid body cells (mitosis) for growth and repair; gametic = haploid sex cells (meiosis) for reproduction. Halving in gametes then fusing at fertilisation keeps the species' chromosome number constant across generations.

**Page misconception:** ✗ Wrong: A gamete has half a chromosome's worth of DNA because chromosomes are split in half. ✓ Right: A gamete has half the number of chromosomes (one of each pair), not half-chromosomes. Each chromosome it carries is complete. Humans have 23 whole chromosomes in a gamete, versus 23 pairs (46) in a somatic cell.

## 3. Number and science check

Human somatic cells have 46 chromosomes (23 pairs); gametes have 23. Diploid gamete fusion would produce 92, then 184, then 368 across successive generations.
