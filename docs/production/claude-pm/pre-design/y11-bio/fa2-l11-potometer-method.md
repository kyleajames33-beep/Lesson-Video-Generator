# Measuring Transpiration Rate: potometer method

Site lesson: L11. Learner question: How does a potometer track water uptake?

## 2. Site lesson material

Source: `C:/Users/kygs/hscscience/subjects/biology/year11/fa2-cells-to-systems/lesson11.sa.html` and `docs/production/claude-pm/site-extracts/bio-y11-fa2-l11.md`. Site section order: 1. The Potometer Method; 2. A Fair Test for One Factor; 3. Validity, Reliability and Accuracy; 4. From a classroom potometer to whole-forest sensors. Relevant ideas:

- The Potometer Method: A potometer works because every drop of water the leaf loses must be replaced by water drawn in through the cut stem, so tracking the water pulled in tells us how fast it is leaving.
- A Fair Test for One Factor: To demonstrate the effect of one environmental factor, we change only that factor and keep everything else the same, so any change in rate can only be due to it.

Practice demand: Calculate uptake rates with and without a fan, then give a conclusion and reliability improvement.

Site short-answer questions (3): 1. Describe how you would conduct a practical investigation using a potometer to demonstrate the effect of wind on the rate of transpiration, and explain how you would make the investigation valid and reliable. 2. A student using a potometer records that the bubble moves 18 mm in 6 minutes with no fan and 40 mm in 5 minutes with a fan. Calculate the rate of water uptake for each condition, state what the comparison demonstrates, and explain one reason the calculated rate slightly overstates the true rate of transpiration. 3. A student concludes from a single potometer reading in still air and a single reading with a fan that "wind doubles the rate of transpiration in all plants". Evaluate this conclusion, identifying two weaknesses in the evidence and stating what would need to change for a claim of this kind to be supported.

Site memory/retrieval cue: You can now design, run and evaluate a potometer investigation that demonstrates how an environmental factor changes transpiration. Next lesson goes inside the leaf, stem and root to see the micro-structures that carry water, nutrients and gases.

## 3. Number and fact check

No fan: 18 mm / 6 min = 3 mm/min. Fan: 40 mm / 5 min = 8 mm/min. The difference is 5 mm/min; the fan rate is 8/3 times the no-fan rate. Water uptake is a proxy for water loss.

## 4. Mistakes and traps

The site names this misconception: Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: A potometer measures transpiration directly. ✓ Right: A potometer measures the rate of water uptake. We assume that almost all the water taken up is lost in transpiration, so uptake is a close proxy. This is a page-backed teaching trap, not a claim about NESA marking trends.
