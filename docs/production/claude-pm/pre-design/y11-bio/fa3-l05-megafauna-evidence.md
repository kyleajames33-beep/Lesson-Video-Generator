## 2. Site lesson content

Whole-page section order: Australia's Giant Animals; Rock Art and Oral Traditions; Why Multiple Sources Matter.

Selected material:
- Australia's Giant Animals: During the Pleistocene, Australia was home to a range of giant animals, its megafauna, and Aboriginal Peoples were already living here while many of these animals still walked the land.
- Rock Art and Oral Traditions: Aboriginal evidence for megafauna comes mainly through rock art that depicts the animals and oral traditions that describe creatures matching the extinct species.
- Why Multiple Sources Matter: No single source gives the whole picture; combining Aboriginal knowledge with the fossil record produces a fuller and more reliable account of the megafauna.

Practice: Activity 1 Analyse Band 5 Two Lines of Evidence, One Conclusion Pattern C, Compare evidence sources A scientist concludes that Genyornis lived in an area until relatively recently. Memory aid: The page uses three evidence lines: rock art, oral tradition and fossils. Identifications are contested and must remain attributed.

## 4. Misconceptions and traps

Site misconception: - Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: Only Western science, through fossil bones, can provide real evidence about extinct animals; Aboriginal accounts are just stories. ✓ Right: Aboriginal Peoples have lived on this continent for tens of thousands of years, overlapping with the megafauna.

Source-backed response trap: Rock art cannot date a fossil by itself. Keep contested species identifications attributed, and cross-check art, oral tradition and physical finds.
