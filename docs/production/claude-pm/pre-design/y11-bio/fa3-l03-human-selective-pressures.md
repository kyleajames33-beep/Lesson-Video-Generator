## 2. Site lesson content

Whole-page section order: Humans as a Selective Force; Chemicals; Deliberate vs Accidental Selection.

Selected material:
- Humans as a Selective Force: Humans change the environment so often and so strongly that our activities have become a major source of selective pressure, but the underlying mechanism is exactly the one from Lesson 1.
- Chemicals: Antibiotic & Pesticide Resistance: A chemical such as an antibiotic does not target the susceptible cells' ability to mutate, it simply removes them, leaving the already-resistant minority to multiply.
- Deliberate vs Accidental Selection: Human-induced pressures fall on a spectrum from deliberate selective breeding to entirely unintended consequences of other activities.

Practice: Explain a Human-Induced Example Pattern A, Structured explanation Overfishing that targets the largest cod has been linked to populations that now mature at a smaller size. ## 4. Misconceptions and traps

Site misconception: - Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: Spraying a pesticide makes the insects become resistant, the chemical causes the mutation. ✓ Right: The resistance allele already exists in a few individuals by chance before spraying.

Source-backed response trap: Herbicide and antibiotics select resistant variants already present; they do not make each exposed organism develop a useful mutation. Source: site L03, misconception, activity and short-answer task. This is a page-based trap, not a claim about NESA marker trends.
