## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**L12 order:** Connect: predict the alcohol reaction; Explain: dehydration forms alkenes; Represent: substitution forms haloalkanes; Respond: classify oxidation products; Feedback: control products with conditions; Apply: conditions, evidence and pathways.

**L12 key ideas:** 1. Dehydration is the exact reverse of hydration, water is eliminated across the C-OH bond and an adjacent C-H bond, regenerating the C=C double bond, and the conditions that drive this reversal are the opposite of those that drive hydration.
2. Dehydration of Alcohols, Making Alkenes.
3. The reverse of the haloalkane substitution from L10, instead of converting a haloalkane to an alcohol with NaOH, you can convert an alcohol to a haloalkane with HX, and the conditions and halide reactivity order are what HSC specifically tests.
4. Substitution of Alcohols with HX, Making Haloalkanes.
5. Oxidation of alcohols is the single most important reaction for understanding how the organic functional group classes connect, primary alcohols become aldehydes and then carboxylic acids, secondary alcohols become ketones, and tertiary alcohols refuse to react, and the reason is structural, not arbitrary.
6. Oxidation of Alcohols, The Classification-Dependent Reaction.
7. Putting dehydration, substitution, and oxidation together with the production reactions from L10 reveals that alcohols are the central hub of the organic reaction map, every arrow into and out of alcohol connects to a different functional group class.
8. Alcohols at the Centre, The Reaction Map Hub.

**Worked examples:** Source examples are integrated into the teaching and practice; checked examples and their numbers are recorded in section 3.

## 3. Number and science check

Balanced transformations checked: ethanol -> ethene + water; ROH+HX -> RX+H2O; primary alcohol+[O] -> aldehyde+H2O; primary+2[O] -> acid+H2O; secondary+[O] -> ketone+H2O. Diagnostic unknown C3H8O can be propan-1-ol or propan-2-ol. Distillation removes aldehyde; excess oxidant/reflux gives acid. Tertiary alcohols resist the ordinary dichromate oxidation taught here.
