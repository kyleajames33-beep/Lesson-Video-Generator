# Module 6 pre-design: Benefits of Genetic Technologies, Agricultural, Medical and Industrial Uses. How do benefits and trade-offs differ across these applications?

Sources: [L17 extract](../site-extracts/bio-m6-l17.md) and [site page](https://hscscience.com.au/subjects/biology/year12/module6/lesson17.sa). These are site teaching notes for pre-design.

## 1. Exact 2017 syllabus content

> evaluate the benefits of using genetic technologies in agricultural, medical and industrial applications (ACSBL086)

Source: [NESA Biology Stage 6 Syllabus (2017)](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/science/biology-stage-6-2017), Module 6.

## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**Teaching order.**

**L17:**

1. A claim of benefit needs a purpose, beneficiary and comparison with an alternative.
2. Agricultural applications include yield, resistance and nutrition, with possible biodiversity trade-offs.
3. Medical uses include production of proteins such as insulin and genetic testing to guide treatment.
4. Industrial uses include enzymes and biological manufacturing at scale.
5. The page ranks benefits by evidence, alternatives and who receives them.

**Worked examples and numbers from the extracts.**

**L17:**

- A seven-mark evaluation compares modified microorganisms making industrial enzymes and chymosin, recombinant medicines, and insect-resistant and herbicide-tolerant crops.
- A five-mark justification compares recombinant human insulin with extraction from pigs and cattle; the page says porcine insulin differs by one amino acid and bovine insulin by three.

**Practice.**

**L17:** Benefit sorting, multiple choice, true or false, two guided extended answers and three short answers. Representative question: “Evaluate the benefits of using genetic technologies across agricultural, medical and industrial applications.”

The extracts record no named mnemonic unless shown above. Keep any useful contrast or question from the page; do not manufacture a memory aid.

## 3. Number and science check

The page gives one amino-acid difference for porcine insulin and three for bovine insulin relative to human insulin. This is a comparison claim, not an effectiveness measure. Source for flagged corrections: [Module 6 site corrections](../site-corrections-m6.md).

## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes

**L17:**

- Higher output automatically means higher biodiversity. Crop genetic uniformity can reduce resilience.
- All modified crops have the same benefits and safety profile. The page says effects depend on the trait, assessment and setting. - “Helps people” is a complete evaluation. The page asks who benefits and compared with what.

These traps come from the linked site lessons. They are not attributed to NESA markers without a specific exam report.
