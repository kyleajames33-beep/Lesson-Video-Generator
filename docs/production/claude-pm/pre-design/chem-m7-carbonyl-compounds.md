## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**L13 order:** Connect: meet the carbonyl family; Explain: locate and name the carbonyl; Represent: connect structure to properties; Respond: interpret distinguishing tests; Feedback: explain the oxidation boundary; Apply: solve carbonyl unknowns.

**L13 key ideas:** 1. The carbonyl group (C=O) is identical in both aldehydes and ketones, what differs is its position in the molecule and what is bonded to the carbonyl carbon, and that single structural difference has cascading consequences for naming, properties, and every reaction both compounds undergo.
2. IUPAC Naming Summary.
3. Intermolecular Forces.
4. Boiling Point Ladder, C4 Compounds.
5. Because aldehydes and ketones have the same molecular formula at the same carbon count (functional group isomers), you cannot distinguish them by formula or molecular mass, you distinguish them by chemistry, specifically by reactions that exploit the single structural difference: the H on the carbonyl carbon.
6. Test 1, Tollens' Reagent (Silver Mirror Test).
7. Test 2, Fehling's Solution (or Benedict's Solution).
8. Complete Test Results Summary.

## 3. Number and science check

Carbonyl carbon in aldehyde RCHO bears H; in ketone RCOR it bears two carbon groups. Carbonyl oxygen accepts hydrogen bonds from water; simple aldehydes/ketones lack OH/NH donors. Carbonyl isomers can share molecular formula and mass. Tollens silver mirror and Fehling/Benedict red precipitate distinguish the simple compounds taught on the page. No arithmetic example in extract. Fresh Tollens reagent must not be stored; exact disposal belongs to practical preparation.
