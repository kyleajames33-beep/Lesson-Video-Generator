## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**Teaching order.**

**L05:**

1. Strength is extent of ionisation; concentration is amount dissolved per litre; both affect [H⁺].
2. The page lists HCl, HNO₃, H₂SO₄ first ionisation, HClO₄, HBr and HI as strong acids; HF is weak.
3. NaOH, KOH, Ca(OH)₂ and Ba(OH)₂ are the four listed strong bases; the latter two give two OH⁻ per dissolved formula unit.
4. Strong species use → and weak species ⇌ in the page's ionic equations.
5. Equal-concentration pH measurements compare the different amounts of ions formed by strong and weak acids or bases.
**L06:**

1. Four student explanations revisit complete versus partial ionisation at equal concentration.
2. A concert analogy separates total acid concentration, fraction ionised and resulting [H⁺].
3. Base solubility is separate from the dissociation of material that has dissolved, illustrated by NaOH, Ca(OH)₂ and NH₃.
4. Salt ions from weak parents can react with water, making salt solutions acidic or basic.
5. The page reviews four exam errors: incorrect strong-acid arrows, calling dilute acid weak, setting [H⁺] equal to weak-acid concentration and omitting strong bases.

**Worked examples and numbers from the extracts.**

**L05:**

- Classifications and equations: HBr → H⁺ + Br⁻ (strong); HF ⇌ H⁺ + F⁻ (weak); Ba(OH)₂ → Ba²⁺ + 2OH⁻ (strong); NH₃ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻ (weak).
- Four 0.10 mol/L acids: HNO₃ and HCl both pH 1.0; HNO₂ pH 2.1; CH₃COOH pH 2.9. The page attributes the difference to ionisation, not a concentration difference.
**L06:**

- HNO₃ → H⁺ + NO₃⁻ is strong; HNO₂ ⇌ H⁺ + NO₂⁻ is weak; NH₄NO₃ gives an acidic solution because NH₄⁺ donates H⁺ while NO₃⁻ is treated as a spectator.
- 5.0 mol/L CH₃COOH, Ka = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵: [H⁺] ≈ 9.49 × 10⁻³ mol/L, pH ≈ 2.02. 0.001 mol/L HCl: [H⁺] = 0.001 mol/L, pH = 3.0. The lower pH of the acetic acid solution does not make it the stronger acid.
- Four-grid activity compares dilute/concentrated strong/weak acids with particle models.

**Practice.**

**L05:** Classify substances, write ionic equations, interpret pH observations and correct strength versus concentration claims. Representative: “A student measures the pH of four solutions, each at 0.10 mol/L, and obtains: HNO₃ = pH 1.0; HNO₂ = pH 2.1; HCl = pH 1.0; CH₃COOH = pH 2.9.”
**L06:** Judge four student claims, draw particle grids, classify salts, fix equations and compare pH values. Representative: “A student claims that a 5.0 mol/L solution of acetic acid (Ka = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵) is a stronger acid than a 0.001 mol/L solution of HCl”.

The extracts record no named mnemonic unless shown above. Keep any useful contrast or question from the page; do not manufacture a memory aid.

## 3. Number and science check

For a monoprotic 0.10 mol/L strong acid, pH = 1.00. The weaker acids can have higher pH at the same analytical concentration. The L06 comparison gives √(1.8 × 10⁻⁵ × 5.0) = 9.49 × 10⁻³ mol/L and pH 2.02; 0.001 mol/L HCl has pH 3.00. Arithmetic checks. ## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes

**L05:**

- “Concentrated” means “strong”: concentration and degree of ionisation are separate.
- HF must be strong because HCl is: the page classifies HF as weak.
- Ca(OH)₂ is weak because little dissolves: its dissolved portion dissociates completely.
- Weak and strong equations can use the same arrow: the page assigns equilibrium arrows to weak species.
**L06:**

- More concentrated acid is intrinsically stronger: strength and concentration differ.
- Low solubility makes Ca(OH)₂ weak: its dissolved fraction dissociates completely.
- Every salt solution is neutral: NH₄NO₃ is acidic and acetate salts are basic.
- Weak-acid pH follows [H⁺] = c: partial ionisation requires equilibrium treatment.

These traps come from the linked site lessons. They are not attributed to NESA markers without a specific exam report.

## Further site corrections for Brief 76

L05: the ionised fraction of a weak acid changes with dilution even when Ka at fixed temperature does not. The memorised four hydroxides are not every strong base; LiOH and Sr(OH)₂ are further examples. Mg(OH)₂ has low solubility, but its dissolved fraction dissociates into ions. See [the exact draft pairs](../site-fixes-draft-m6.md).
