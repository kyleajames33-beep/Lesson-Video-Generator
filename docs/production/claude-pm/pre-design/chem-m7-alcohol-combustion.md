## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**L11 order:** Connect: explain the 53% energy result; Explain: design a reliable spirit-burner investigation; Represent: convert temperature rise into molar enthalpy; Respond: diagnose systematic and random error; Feedback: compare alcohol trends, fossil fuels and biofuels; Apply: calculate and evaluate a complete investigation; Apply: calculate, evaluate and design.

**L11 key ideas:** 1. 53%, Where Did the Other Half Go?
2. A spirit burner calorimeter is a deliberately simple piece of equipment, simple enough to introduce systematic errors, and understanding exactly what you are measuring (and what you are not) is the key to both accurate results and explaining your discrepancy.
3. The Spirit Burner Calorimeter, Method & Measurements.
4. The calculation has four variables, q, m, ΔT, and n, and the most common errors come not from the formula itself but from unit conversions, sign conventions, and confusing the mass of water with the mass of fuel.
5. Calculating Molar Enthalpy of Combustion, Step by Step.
6. The discrepancy between experimental and theoretical enthalpy of combustion is not a mistake, it is chemically meaningful, and explaining it correctly with specific sources of error is one of the highest-value skills tested in this practical investigation.
7. ing the Discrepancy, Why Experimental ΔHc < Theoretical.
8. The trend in combustion enthalpy with chain length is completely predictable from bond chemistry, and the comparison between alcohols and fossil fuels as energy sources is one of the most directly HSC-relevant applications of this chemistry.
