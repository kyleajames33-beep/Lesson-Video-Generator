## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**L06 order:** Connect: map the four alkene additions; Explain: the pi bond is the reactive site; Represent: hydrogenation and halogenation; Respond: predict hydrohalogenation products; Feedback: hydration, conditions and misconceptions; Apply: evidence, equations and product prediction.

**L06 key ideas:** 1. The pi bond is the reactive site, electron-rich, exposed, and ready to break.
2. Hydrogenation adds hydrogen across a double bond to produce a saturated product, halogenation adds a halogen molecule, and both reactions are the foundation of major industrial processes you encounter every day.
3. Hydrogenation.
4. Halogenation & the Bromine Water Test.
5. When an asymmetrical reagent like HBr adds to an asymmetrical alkene, there are two possible products.
6. The Rule in Plain English.
7. Hydration of an alkene converts a hydrocarbon into an alcohol by adding water across the double bond, it is the industrial route to ethanol and to many other alcohols, and it is the reverse of the dehydration reaction you will meet in L12.
8. Hydration vs Fermentation for Ethanol Production.

**Worked problems:** Problem: Write balanced equations and state all conditions for: (a) propene + hydrogen; (b) ethene + bromine; (c) but-1-ene + water. 1 Problem: (a) Write the major product of the reaction between but-1-ene and HBr, and name it. (b) Write the minor product. Markovnikov's rule is enrichment beyond the NSW syllabus; the required skill is writing the addition equation. 1 Problem (6 marks): A student adds an unknown compound X (molecular formula C 4 H 8 ) to bromine water and observes the orange-brown colour disappears. The student concludes: "Compound X must be an alkene because it decolourised bromine water." (a) Evaluate the student's conclusion. (b) Compound X, treated with H 2 O in the presence of H 3 PO 4 at 300°C and high pressure, produces a single alcohol. Identify the structural formula and IUPAC name of the alcohol. (c) If compound X had been but-2-ene, how would the alcohol product differ? 1

What is the major product?” “What does the bromine water test confirm? What is it unable to confirm, and why?”

## 3. Number and science check

Equations checked: propene + H2 -> propane; ethene + Br2 -> 1,2-dibromoethane; but-1-ene + water gives mainly butan-2-ol; but-1-ene + HBr gives mainly 2-bromobutane. Site conditions include Ni at 150 to 200 degrees C and hydration with H3PO4 at 300 degrees C/high pressure. These are example operating conditions, not universal constants. Site error, Worked Example3: C4H8, bromine decolourisation and hydration do not identify butan-2-ol uniquely. 2-methylpropene gives 2-methylpropan-2-ol. Specify an unbranched starting alkene. The answer says cycloalkanes do not decolourise bromine but uses their possible molecular formula to call the observed decolourisation insufficient; rewrite the evidence and conclusion together.
