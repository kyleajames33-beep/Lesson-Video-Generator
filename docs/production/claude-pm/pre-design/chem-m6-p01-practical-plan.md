# Chemistry Module 6 P01 practical plan (draft for Claude's review)

**Aim and route.** Prepare red cabbage indicator, test acidic and basic solutions, then reverse one colour change. `C6-IQ1-02`: “conduct an investigation to demonstrate the preparation and use of indicators as illustrators of the characteristics and properties of acids and bases and their reversible reactions (ACSCH101)”. Site L02 and L15. Prerequisites: chem-m6-c02 and chem-m6-c13. The investigation demonstrates indicator response, not an exact pH measurement.

**School method.** Teacher chops about 20 g red cabbage and steeps it in 100 mL hot water for 10 minutes, then cools and filters it. Place 2.0 mL extract in each of three labelled wells. Add 0.5 mL of 0.10 mol/L HCl to well A, distilled water to B, and 0.5 mL of 0.10 mol/L NaHCO3 to C. To a fresh 2.0 mL extract aliquot, add 0.10 mol/L HCl dropwise until pink, then 0.10 mol/L NaHCO3 dropwise until purple or blue returns. Use separate droppers. Record colours beside reference wells and measure each final pH with a calibrated meter if available. The return is a pH-driven reversal of indicator colour; the bicarbonate neutralises acid and releases CO2, so the final mixture is chemically changed.

**Variables and expected results.** Independent: solution added. Dependent: observed colour and optional measured pH. Control extract volume, cabbage batch, drop volume, lighting and time. Typical data, not a calibration: 2.0 mL purple extract at pH about 7; acid well pink at pH about 2; bicarbonate well blue-green at pH about 8.3; reversed sample returns towards purple as pH rises. The well contents are diluted by addition, so a water reference separates dilution from pH response. HIn ⇌ H+ + In−: added acid favours the protonated form; base removes H+ and favours the other form. Anthocyanins are mixtures, so do not assign one exact transition pH.

**Analysis and calculation.** Added acid before reaction: 0.00050 L × 0.10 mol/L = 5.0 × 10⁻⁵ mol HCl. Added bicarbonate in a matched 0.5 mL dose is also 5.0 × 10⁻⁵ mol. Neutralisation is approximately 1:1, but real final pH depends on drop count and CO2 loss, so measure it. Colour is categorical; calculate no concentration from its shade.

**VRA.** Validity: the water reference and common extract batch isolate the effect of added acid/base. Reliability: repeat each condition with three wells and report agreement of colours and pH. Accuracy: calibrate the meter with pH 4 and 7 buffers and rinse/blot between wells; colour alone does not give accurate pH.

| Hazard and classification | Control |
| --- | --- |
| Hot water scald; knife cuts during teacher preparation | Teacher prepares and cools extract before student handling. |
| 0.10 mol/L HCl and 0.10 mol/L NaHCO3: low hazard at these concentrations, but splashes can harm eyes | Safety glasses, small well volumes, no tasting. See [RSC universal indicator microscale](https://edu.rsc.org/experiments/universal-indicator-microscale-11-16-years/4020613.article) for 0.1 mol/L HCl and [RSC cabbage preparation](https://edu.rsc.org/experiments/making-a-ph-indicator-using-red-cabbage/422.article). |
| Bicarbonate plus acid releases CO2 and can froth | Leave wells open; add dropwise. |

**Errors and fixes.** A different amount of extract changes colour intensity: use measured 2.0 mL aliquots. A dropper shared between acid and base cross-contaminates samples: dedicate and label droppers. Repeating a contaminated method does not restore validity.

**HSC-style question idea.** A student's acid well turns pink and the bicarbonate well turns green. Design a further test that demonstrates reversibility and explain what the water well controls.
