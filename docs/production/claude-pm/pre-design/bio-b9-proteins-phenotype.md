## Site sequence and material

Order: What proteins actually do (enzymes, structural, transport, receptor, antibody); Sequence, shape, function; The missing step; Same genotype, different outcome; Pick a protein, trace the trait; Explain and Connect; Height and Nutrition; independent practice; Retrieve and reflect. Orientation and worksheet links precede the teaching sections.

- Trace a causal chain: DNA sequence changes, amino acid sequence may change, protein folding and function may change, and a trait may change. A protein can be an enzyme, structural fibre, transporter, receptor or antibody. The site warns that genes do not directly become traits. A genotype sets biological possibilities while environment changes expression or development. Human height involves many genes and nutrition; the Himalayan rabbit's pigment enzyme works in cooler body regions, producing darker ears, nose, feet and tail.
- Worked sickle-cell example: beta-globin amino acid 6 is valine instead of glutamate. This is one amino acid substitution in the beta chain, associated with a single DNA base substitution. There is no numerical calculation. The page compresses this to a four-link chain; explain the altered haemoglobin behaviour before a whole-body phenotype claim. Its 5-mark protein-shape answer names hydrophobic interactions, charge attraction and cysteine disulfide bonds, then a critical-site substitution and loss of fit. Its height answer says similar genetic potential does not ensure the same adult height when nutrition and health differ.
- Questions: choose a protein category and connect its job to an observable trait; explain different heights given similar genetic potential; assess gene and environment effects (7 marks); explain protein structure and function (5 marks); evaluate the height and nutrition statement (5 marks); MC, true/false and short answers (12 marks). Misconceptions: genotype fixes every trait with no environmental input; environment normally rewrites genotype during development; every amino acid substitution must abolish protein function. Retrieval cues: proteins, genotype-to-protein-to-phenotype path, environment and exam trap. No named mnemonic; the causal pathway itself is the memory scaffold.

## Checks, exam traps and objects

Position 6 is an ordinal residue number, not a calculation. No constant appears. The site hook calls Beadle and Tatum's 1941 work proof of a one-gene-one-enzyme link. Better: it supported the original hypothesis, later refined because genes can encode non-enzyme products and alternative transcripts. The correction is conceptual, not numerical. [NESA 2022 Q31(b) feedback](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2022) requires the named EGFR protein from the stimulus, then a link from gene mutation through protein structure and function to altered cell division. Avoid a generic DNA-to-trait arrow. [NESA 2023 Q25(b) feedback](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2023) also asks students to use the supplied mRNA codon chart directly.

Real visual candidates: red blood cells for haemoglobin, hair for keratin, muscle fibres, a Himalayan rabbit with dark cool extremities, hydrangeas under different soil conditions, and a nutrition/height comparison.
