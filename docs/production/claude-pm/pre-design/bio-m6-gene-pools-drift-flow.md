## 1. Exact 2017 syllabus content

> evaluate the effect of mutation, gene flow and genetic drift on the gene pool of populations (ACSBL091, ACSBL092)

Source: [NESA Biology Stage 6 Syllabus (2017)](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/science/biology-stage-6-2017), Module 6.

## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**Teaching order.**

**L07:**

1. The gene pool is all alleles in a population; genetic change includes altered allele presence or frequency.
2. Mutation adds new alleles, usually changing frequency slowly by itself.
3. Gene flow transfers alleles between populations when migrants breed.
4. Genetic drift changes allele frequencies by chance, with stronger effects in small populations and after founder or bottleneck events.
5. Relative effect depends on context: new allele creation, migration rate and population size.

**Worked examples and numbers from the extracts.**

**L07:** The page describes separated Lake Victoria cichlid sub-populations and divergence in opsin genes. A few founders colonising an island illustrate chance sampling; a disease reducing a population illustrates a bottleneck. A scenario with two large populations exchanging many migrants is assigned to gene flow, while a population reduced to a few survivors is assigned to drift.

**Practice.**

**L07:** Process identification, relative-importance scenarios, checks, multiple choice and three short answers. Representative: “Define a gene pool and explain how mutation affects it.” “Evaluate why genetic drift can have a stronger short-term effect than mutation in a very small isolated population.”

The extracts record no named mnemonic unless shown above. Keep any useful contrast or question from the page; do not manufacture a memory aid.

## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes

**L07:**

- A gene pool belongs to one individual. It is a population-level collection.
- Genetic drift selects the best allele. Its frequency change is random, not adaptive.
- Gene flow creates a new allele. It moves alleles between populations.
- Allele frequencies change only randomly. Mutation, selection, drift and gene flow have different effects.

These traps come from the linked site lessons. They are not attributed to NESA markers without a specific exam report.
