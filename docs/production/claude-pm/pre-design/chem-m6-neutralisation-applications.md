## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**Teaching order.**

**L04:**

1. Antacid hydroxides and carbonates neutralise stomach HCl; carbonate produces CO₂.
2. Lime raises acidic soil pH; toothpaste bases neutralise acids produced in the mouth.
3. Ammonia accepts protons from H₂SO₄ or HNO₃ to make ammonium fertiliser salts.
4. Slaked lime or limestone removes SO₂ from flue gas and reduces acid deposition.
5. Wastewater neutralisation adjusts discharge pH to protect aquatic ecosystems.
6. Particle diagrams and full/net ionic equations show which species react and which ions remain spectators.

**Worked examples and numbers from the extracts.**

**L04:**

- Industrial equations: NH₃ + HNO₃ → NH₄NO₃; Ca(OH)₂ + SO₂ → CaSO₃ + H₂O; 2HCl + CaCO₃ → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂.
- Antacid choice: the page compares CaCO₃, Mg(OH)₂ and Al(OH)₃ for a patient with kidney disease and recommends Al(OH)₃ under its stated chemical criteria, while adding a clinical caveat about aluminium.
- Environmental response: coal with 3% sulfur and flue gas near 2000 ppm SO₂; Ca(OH)₂ scrubbing gives CaSO₃, and the page cites 90–99% removal.

**Practice.**

**L04:** Balance industrial equations, compare antacids for a patient, evaluate a scrubber and model neutralisation with particles and equations. Representative: “A patient with kidney disease cannot excrete large amounts of calcium or magnesium ions. They experience chronic acid reflux.” The task evaluates CaCO₃, Mg(OH)₂ and Al(OH)₃.

The extracts record no named mnemonic unless shown above. Keep any useful contrast or question from the page; do not manufacture a memory aid.

## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes

**L04:**

- Mg(OH)₂ or Al(OH)₃ antacids produce CO₂: the page says hydroxides form salt and water, while carbonates release CO₂.
- One NH₃ fully neutralises H₂SO₄: two NH₃ molecules accept its two protons.
- SO₃ is the primary flue gas target: the page uses SO₂.
- Naming a wastewater neutraliser alone explains its value: give an equation and environmental pH consequence.

These traps come from the linked site lessons. They are not attributed to NESA markers without a specific exam report.

## Site corrections for Brief 76

L04: the 1956 Clean Air Act did not mandate Ca(OH)₂ scrubbing in every furnace. See [the exact Module 6 site fix draft](../site-fixes-draft-m6.md).

L04 exam worksheet 04: SO₂ is an acidic oxide in the displayed scrubber equation. Do not label it a Brønsted-Lowry proton acceptor.
## Further site corrections for Brief 76

L04: anhydrous ammonia can be injected directly into soil as fertiliser. Two NH₃ per H₂SO₄ are needed for ammonium sulfate; a 1:1 ratio can make ammonium hydrogen sulfate. Discharge pH is governed by the applicable licence, not a universal 6.5–8.5 law. Exact replacements and sources are in [the site fix draft](../site-fixes-draft-m6.md).
