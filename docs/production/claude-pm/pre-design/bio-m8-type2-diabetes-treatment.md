## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**Page sections in order:** Warm up first; Predict first: what should treatment target?; What goes wrong in Type 2 diabetes; Key vocabulary, translated; The treatment framework: from root cause to symptom relief; Match each action to the mechanism; Pharmacological treatments: name the molecular target; Targeted therapy versus chemotherapy; Surgery and lifestyle: structural fixes and remission; Emerging therapies, and how to judge them; Evaluate future research, not just novelty; Choose your route; Exit check; Multiple Choice; Short Answer, 15 marks; Check what actually stuck; Take the full module quiz; Race Through Type 2 Diabetes Management!; How did your thinking change?.

**Teaching points in order:** 1. Mechanism to management to future directions. 2. Treatment only makes sense once you can name the mechanism it targets. 3. Cystic fibrosis was described in 1938 and the CFTR gene was identified in 1989, yet the first truly effective treatment, the CFTR modulator Trikafta, only reached Australian patients through the PBS in 2022. 4. Some disease is structural and no drug can reverse it. 5. The dot point instructs you to investigate the treatment, management and future directions for a non-infectious disease.

**Site examples and practice:**  Named page examples: Worked example: matching diabetes strategies to purpose; Worked example: evaluating a combined plan. “A patient has high blood glucose because body cells respond poorly to insulin. Which target best matches the mechanism?” “Which treatment best addresses the root cause of its disease?”

## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes

Link insulin resistance to management target, then distinguish control of glucose from reversal of existing organ damage. NESA feedback: 2025 Q25 requires a judgement tied to the stated claim and plotted evidence. See [exam research](../research/question-types-bio-m8.md) for the official paper links and marker context.
