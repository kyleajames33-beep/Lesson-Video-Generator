## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**Teaching order.**

**L08:**

1. Biotechnology deliberately uses organisms, cells or biological processes for human purposes.
2. Traditional methods include fermentation, domestication and selective breeding without direct DNA editing.
3. Modern methods include recombinant DNA, cloning, DNA profiling, genetic markers and gene editing.
4. Agriculture, medicine and industry all use biotechnology; engineered insulin connects traditional medicine to modern microbial production.
5. The page traces past observation and selection to present genetic methods and possible future precision.
**L09:**

1. Ethical evaluation asks who benefits, who bears costs or risks, and which values affected groups hold.
2. Plant biotechnology may improve yield, pest resistance, drought tolerance or nutrition; seed ownership, crop diversity and environmental effects also matter.
3. Animal biotechnology may improve disease resistance or medical use; welfare, stress and suffering become central questions.
4. The page offers five questions covering the problem addressed, beneficiaries, risks, welfare or environmental harm, and equitable access.

**Worked examples and numbers from the extracts.**

**L08:** The page describes Pasteur’s 1857 yeast and glucose experiment with ethanol and carbon dioxide and compares it with modern industrial fermenters of 300,000 litres. It contrasts animal-derived insulin with human insulin made by engineered microorganisms. The classification activity labels brewing beer and selecting sheep for finer wool traditional, and inserting a human gene into bacteria for insulin modern.
**L09:** Golden Rice, reported in 2000, is described as engineered to supply beta-carotene to help address vitamin-A deficiency. The page maps the concerns of farmers, public-health workers, researchers, regulators and affected communities. A weak judgement, “This biotechnology is good because it increases production,” is rewritten to include stakeholders and harms as well as benefits.

**Practice.**

**L08:** Interactive biotechnology timeline, traditional-or-modern classification, agriculture/medicine/industry sector map, multiple choice and three short answers. Representative: “Define biotechnology and give two different examples.” “Compare traditional biotechnology with modern biotechnology.”
**L09:** Ethics decision tree, stakeholder map for plant and animal cases, judgement rewrite, multiple choice and three short answers. Representative: “Explain why stakeholder analysis is useful when judging biotechnology.” “Compare the main ethical issues raised by plant biotechnology and animal biotechnology.”

The extracts record no named mnemonic unless shown above. Keep any useful contrast or question from the page; do not manufacture a memory aid.

## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes

**L08:**

- Biotechnology means only modern gene engineering. Fermentation and selective breeding also qualify.
- Modern methods replaced traditional methods. Both continue to be used.
- The field applies only to medicine. The page also gives agricultural and industrial uses.
**L09:**

- Increased yield alone settles the ethics. The page asks about ownership, environmental effects and distribution.
- GM crop debates concern only biological performance. Seed access and local biodiversity also feature.
- Animal productivity is enough to justify a technology. Welfare remains a separate consideration.
- All cultures universally accept biotechnology. Different groups can weigh benefits and concerns differently.

These traps come from the linked site lessons. They are not attributed to NESA markers without a specific exam report.

## Site corrections for Brief 76

L08 cites 14,400-year-old bread-like remains at Shubayqa 1 beside yeast fermentation, but the original archaeological study does not establish fermentation or leavening. Pasteur's exact sealed-flask protocol in the site page is unverified; retain his broader fermentation contribution. See [the exact Module 6 site fix draft](../site-fixes-draft-m6.md).
## Site corrections for Brief 76

L08 worked answers use the early insulin example as if an intact human insulin gene was placed into bacteria with an exactly specified outcome. The original route expressed synthetic A and B chain coding sequences separately. They also conflate unintended effects of an edit at the intended site with off-target changes. Use the exact replacements and sources in [the Module 6 site fix draft](../site-fixes-draft-m6.md).

L09 worked ethics answer treats every Bt cotton seed as patented and every farmer as unable to save it. Patent and licence rules vary by product and jurisdiction. Use the qualified replacement in the draft.
