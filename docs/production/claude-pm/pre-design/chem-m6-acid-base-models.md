## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**Teaching order.**

**L01:**

1. Lavoisier's oxygen model, Davy's hydrogen model and Arrhenius's aqueous-ion model each meet an observation they cannot explain.
2. Brønsted-Lowry defines an acid as an H⁺ donor and a base as an H⁺ acceptor, including reactions without water.
3. HCl donates a proton to water; NH₃ accepts one from water, leaving OH⁻.
4. The donor becomes its conjugate base and the acceptor its conjugate acid; each pair differs by one H⁺.
5. H⁺(aq) is shorthand for hydrated protons; the page writes H₃O⁺ when showing water's role.
6. Lewis electron-pair theory is introduced as a further model beyond the stated syllabus scope.

**Worked examples and numbers from the extracts.**

**L01:**

- HPO₄²⁻ + H₂O ⇌ H₂PO₄⁻ + OH⁻: acid H₂O, base HPO₄²⁻; conjugate pairs H₂O/OH⁻ and HPO₄²⁻/H₂PO₄⁻.
- HCl(g) + NH₃(g) → NH₄Cl(s): Arrhenius cannot describe the gas reaction; HCl is the proton donor and NH₃ the acceptor. The ionic transfer is HCl + NH₃ → Cl⁻ + NH₄⁺.
- Six-mark evaluation compares the two models using NH₃(aq) and the gas-phase HCl/NH₃ reaction.

**Practice.**

**L01:** Model comparison, identify proton transfers and conjugate pairs, choose arrow notation, multiple choice, short answer and extended response. Representative: “For the reaction HPO₄²⁻ + H₂O ⇌ H₂PO₄⁻ + OH⁻, identify (a) the Brønsted-Lowry acid and base on the left side; (b) the conjugate acid and conjugate base on the right side; (c) both conjugate pairs.”

## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes

**L01:**

- Arrhenius was “proved wrong”: the page calls it limited to aqueous systems.
- NH₃ contains OH⁻: it accepts H⁺ from water, which then becomes OH⁻.
- H₂SO₄ and SO₄²⁻ are a conjugate pair: the first conjugate base is HSO₄⁻.
- Any two reactants form a conjugate pair: paired species lie on opposite sides and differ by one H⁺.

These traps come from the linked site lessons. They are not attributed to NESA markers without a specific exam report.
