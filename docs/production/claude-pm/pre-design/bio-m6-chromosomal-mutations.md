## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**Teaching order.**

**L04:**

1. Chromosomal mutations alter large regions or chromosome number, potentially affecting many genes.
2. Deletion removes a segment, duplication adds a copy, inversion reverses a segment, and translocation moves it.
3. These changes can alter gene content, dosage, order, breakpoints or regulatory context.
4. Nondisjunction can change whole-chromosome number; Down syndrome with an extra chromosome 21 illustrates gene dosage.
5. A larger affected region can have broader effects, although exact phenotype depends on the genes and breakpoints involved.

**Worked examples and numbers from the extracts.**

**L04:** An early Down syndrome karyotype has 47 rather than 46 chromosomes, with an extra chromosome 21 from meiotic nondisjunction. The comparison activity asks which is more likely to affect many genes: a missense substitution in one gene or deletion of a large chromosome segment, and asks why the outcome cannot be predicted perfectly. The visualiser asks students to classify segment order and identify changes retaining the same total amount of DNA.

**Practice.**

**L04:** Visualiser, classification checks, true/false, structural-change naming, severity comparison, multiple choice and short answers. Representative: “A segment breaks off and reattaches in reverse orientation.” “A missense substitution in one gene versus a deletion removing a large chromosome segment. Which is more likely to affect many genes, why, and why is the exact outcome not perfectly predictable?”

The extracts record no named mnemonic unless shown above. Keep any useful contrast or question from the page; do not manufacture a memory aid.

## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes

**L04:**

- Chromosomal mutation is another name for a one-base change. It acts at chromosome-segment or whole-chromosome scale.
- All structural mutations have identical effects. Deletion, duplication, inversion and translocation act differently.
- A bigger change has a perfectly predictable severity. Consequences depend on which genes and controls are affected.

These traps come from the linked site lessons. They are not attributed to NESA markers without a specific exam report.

## Site corrections for Brief 76

L04 worked answers classify every inversion and translocation as material preserving. Specify balanced rearrangements; unbalanced rearrangements can gain or lose DNA. A balanced carrier often has no symptoms, but a breakpoint can interrupt a gene or alter expression. Chromosomal tests can detect rearrangements; karyotyping is not the only method.
