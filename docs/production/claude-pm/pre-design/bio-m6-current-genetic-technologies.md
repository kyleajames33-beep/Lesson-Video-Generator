# Module 6 pre-design: Current Genetic Technologies That Induce Genetic Change. What does each current technology change, and what is its advantage?

## 1. Exact 2017 syllabus content

## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**Teaching order.**

**L13:**

1. Artificial insemination and pollination control which gametes combine without normally changing DNA sequence.
2. Whole-organism cloning aims to copy a donor's nuclear genotype; gene cloning makes many copies of a selected sequence.
3. Recombinant DNA technology joins selected DNA to a vector, and transgenic organisms contain introduced DNA.
4. Reproductive control, copying and direct insertion have different uses and limits.

**Worked examples and numbers from the extracts.**

**L13:** No numerical worked example. A technology map compares artificial insemination, artificial pollination, whole-organism cloning, gene cloning and recombinant DNA by what changes, where each acts and why it is used.

**Practice.**

**L13:** Technology classification, multiple choice, comparisons and three short answers. Representative question: “Compare one reproductive technology with one DNA-level technology in terms of mechanism and outcome.”

The extracts record no named mnemonic unless shown above. Keep any useful contrast or question from the page; do not manufacture a memory aid.

## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes

**L13:**

- Every genetic technology directly edits DNA. Reproductive technologies usually direct parental contribution.
- Gene cloning makes a whole organism. It copies a DNA sequence.
- Clones must have identical phenotypes. Environment and development can differ.

These traps come from the linked site lessons. They are not attributed to NESA markers without a specific exam report.

## Site corrections for Brief 76

The Biology bank item `bio-y12-m6-l13-q0131` asks for the first recombinant human insulin method but describes generic single-gene insertion. The original bacterial method expressed synthetic DNA for separate A and B chains, then combined them. See [the Module 6 site fix draft](../site-fixes-draft-m6.md).

L13 embedded Activity 2 presents gene cloning as a required separate step before recombinant DNA insertion. In standard plasmid cloning, a DNA insert is joined to a vector, then the recombinant vector is copied in host cells. Use the corrected sequence in the draft.

L13 body also calls early commercial human insulin a direct insertion of the human insulin gene into bacteria. The original bacterial demonstration expressed synthetic A and B chain coding sequences separately; use the corrected historical case in the draft.
