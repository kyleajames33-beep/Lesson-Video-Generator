## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**L16 order:** Connect: nitrogen compounds in everyday chemistry; Explain: classify and name amines; Represent: connect bonding to properties; Respond: write weak-base and salt equations; Feedback: explain amide resonance and non-basicity; Apply: compare amines and amides.

**L16 key ideas:** 1. The nitrogen atom in an amine has a lone pair that is freely available for proton acceptance, unlike the lone pair on the nitrogen of an amide, which is locked into the adjacent carbonyl, and this single structural difference separates the most reactive organic bases from some of the most inert.
2. Every amine reaction follows from one principle: the lone pair on nitrogen attacks a proton donor and forms a new N-H bond, whether the proton comes from water (giving basic solution) or from an acid (giving an ammonium salt).
3. Adding a carbonyl group directly next to the nitrogen of an amine transforms it from one of the most reactive organic bases into a compound that is essentially neutral in water, one structural change, reactivity reversed completely.
4. For each compound, classify the amine as primary, secondary, or tertiary, and write the equation for its reaction with HCl: (a) (CH₃)₂CHNH₂ (b) (C₂H₅)₂NH (c) (CH₃)₃N.

## 3. Number and science check

Amines classify by carbon groups bonded to N. Tertiary amines lack NH donors but accept hydrogen bonds from water. Amide N lone pair is delocalised into C=O. Site boiling ladder: propane -42, trimethylamine3, propan-1-amine48, propan-1-ol97, propanoic acid141, propanamide213 degrees C; ethylamine16.6 versus dimethylamine7.4. Rounded electronegativities O3.5/N3.0. These are reference values, not worked calculations; named phase-change record verification remains needed before recording. Do not confuse soluble with miscible. No solubility correction is claimed: [ICSC isobutylamine](https://www.inchem.org/documents/icsc/icsc/eics1253.htm) confirms miscibility for this branched C4 example. [NIST propylamine](https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/cbook.cgi?ID=C107108&Type=TBOIL) gives321K, consistent with48 degrees C. Primary/secondary/tertiary labels alone do not fix all isomer boiling rankings.
