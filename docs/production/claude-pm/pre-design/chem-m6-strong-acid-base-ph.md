## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**Teaching order.**

**L08:**

1. pH = −log[H₃O⁺]; one pH unit is a tenfold concentration change.
2. Strong monoprotic acid gives [H₃O⁺] = c; the page uses 2c for dilute H₂SO₄ and notes its second ionisation is actually partial.
3. Strong base calculations find [OH⁻], then pOH = −log[OH⁻] and pH = 14 − pOH at 25°C.
4. Dilution conserves moles, lowers ion concentration and moves pH toward neutral.
5. For strong acid/base mixtures, subtract reacting moles and divide the excess by combined volume before finding pH.
**L11:**

1. Identify whether a solution contains a strong or weak acid or base before choosing a calculation.
2. Strong acids and bases use direct ion concentrations; weak acids and bases use an equilibrium calculation and a 5% assumption check.
3. For mixed strong acid and strong base, calculate reacting moles, then divide excess moles by the combined volume.
4. Partial neutralisation of a weak acid leaves HA and A⁻; the page selects Henderson-Hasselbalch.
5. Check five common errors: wrong strong or weak method, unchecked approximation, pOH reported as pH, wrong mixing volume, and an ICE table used for a buffer.

**Worked examples and numbers from the extracts.**

**L08:**

- 0.025 mol/L HNO₃: pH 1.60; 0.0040 mol/L Ca(OH)₂: pH 11.90; 0.80 g NaOH in 500 mL: pH 12.60.
- 25.0 mL 0.200 mol/L HCl diluted to 500 mL: pH 2.00; diluted to 5000 mL: pH 3.00. At pH 3.40: [H₃O⁺] = 3.98 × 10⁻⁴ and [OH⁻] = 2.51 × 10⁻¹¹ mol/L.
- 40.0 mL 0.150 mol/L HCl + 60.0 mL 0.0800 mol/L Ba(OH)₂: 3.60 × 10⁻³ mol OH⁻ excess in 0.100 L; pH 12.56. Using only acid volume gives erroneous pH 12.95.
**L11:**

- Three students with 0.100 mol/L acids: HNO₃ gives pH 1.00; CH₃COOH, Ka 1.8 × 10⁻⁵, gives pH 2.87; HF, Ka 6.8 × 10⁻⁴, fails the 5% approximation and gives pH 2.10 by quadratic.
- At 25°C, 0.0400 mol/L KOH gives pH 12.60; 0.0400 mol/L HBr gives 1.40; 0.0400 mol/L HCN, Ka 6.2 × 10⁻¹⁰, gives 5.30.
- 35.0 mL of 0.200 mol/L Ba(OH)₂ mixed with 65.0 mL HNO₃ gives pH 12.63 at 0.150 mol/L acid and 12.94 at 0.0800 mol/L acid.
- For 0.100 mol/L HCl, CH₃COOH (Ka 1.8 × 10⁻⁵), and NH₃ (Kb 1.8 × 10⁻⁵), pH values are 1.00, 2.87 and 11.13. Mixing 40.0 mL HCl with 60.0 mL 0.100 mol/L NaOH gives 12.30. Mixing 25.0 mL acetic acid with 25.0 mL NaOH gives an equivalence-point pH of 8.72. The page says equal Ka and Kb for acetic acid and ammonia do not make them conjugates.

**Practice.**

**L08:** Calculate pH from concentration or mass, reverse pH to ion concentrations, dilute, mix and correct student calculations. Representative: “40.0 mL of 0.150 mol/L HCl is mixed with 60.0 mL of 0.0800 mol/L Ba(OH)₂. (a) Calculate the pH of the resulting mixture at 25°C.”
**L11:** Multiple choice, short answers, error diagnosis, calculation practice, a predict-then-reveal mixing task and review prompts. Representative question: “50.0 mL of 0.200 mol/L CH₃COOH (Ka = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵) is mixed with 20.0 mL of 0.200 mol/L NaOH. (a) Calculate the moles of CH₃COOH remaining and CH₃COO⁻ formed. (b) Calculate the pH.”

The extracts record no named mnemonic unless shown above. Keep any useful contrast or question from the page; do not manufacture a memory aid.

## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes

**L08:**

- A 0.3 pH change is negligible: it doubles or halves [H₃O⁺].
- H₂SO₄ gives one H⁺ per formula unit in the page's dilute-solution convention: it uses two.
- Negative pH is impossible: concentrated acid can have pH below zero.
- Use only acid volume after mixing: use total mixture volume.
**L11:**

- [H⁺] = c for weak acids; weak acids require an equilibrium calculation.
- H₂SO₄ is monoprotic; the page says both protons contribute in dilute solution. - pH equals pKa for any weak acid; the page restricts that equality to half-equivalence.
- Equal Ka and Kb values make an acid and base conjugates; conjugates differ by one proton.
- An ICE table starts with zero A⁻ in a partly neutralised solution; the page uses Henderson-Hasselbalch instead.

These traps come from the linked site lessons. They are not attributed to NESA markers without a specific exam report.
