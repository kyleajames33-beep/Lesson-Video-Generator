## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**Teaching order.**

**L09:**

1. Weak acids partially ionise, so their equilibrium [H⁺] is not simply the initial concentration.
2. An ICE table records initial, change and equilibrium amounts; Ka = x²/(c − x) for a simple monoprotic acid.
3. The page approximates x = √(Ka c) when the ionised fraction is under 5%, then checks that fraction.
4. Weak bases use Kb and [OH⁻]; conjugate constants satisfy KaKb = Kw.
5. Measured pH can be converted to [H⁺] and then Ka.
6. Partial neutralisation of a weak acid creates a buffer; the page uses the Henderson-Hasselbalch ratio of conjugate base to remaining acid.

**Worked examples and numbers from the extracts.**

**L09:**

- 0.150 mol/L propanoic acid, Ka = 1.3 × 10⁻⁵: [H⁺] = 1.40 × 10⁻³ mol/L, pH 2.85, ionisation 0.93%.
- 0.250 mol/L CH₃COOH, Ka = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵: pH 2.67. Acetate Kb = 5.56 × 10⁻¹⁰; 0.250 mol/L sodium acetate pH 9.07.
- HX at 0.0500 mol/L and pH 2.78: [H⁺] = 1.66 × 10⁻³ mol/L, Ka = 5.70 × 10⁻⁵. Mixing 40.0 mL HX with 10.0 mL 0.0500 mol/L NaOH gives the page's buffer answer pH 3.77.

**Practice.**

**L09:** Build ICE tables, test the simplifying assumption, calculate Ka/Kb, evaluate salt pH and identify buffers. Representative: “Calculate the pH of 0.150 mol/L propanoic acid (CH₃CH₂COOH, Ka = 1.3 × 10⁻⁵) at 25°C. Check whether the simplifying assumption is valid.”

The extracts record no named mnemonic unless shown above. Keep any useful contrast or question from the page; do not manufacture a memory aid.

## 3. Number and science check

Propanoic acid: √(1.3 × 10⁻⁵ × 0.150) = 1.40 × 10⁻³ mol/L, pH 2.85 and ionisation 0.93%. HX at pH 2.78 has [H⁺] 1.66 × 10⁻³ mol/L; Ka = x²/(0.0500 − x) = 5.70 × 10⁻⁵. The page has a sign error in the shortcut pH expression; correct form is (pKa − log₁₀ c)/2. Relevant site-correction register entries: L09. Use the corrected statements in that register. Source for flagged corrections: [Module 6 site corrections](../site-corrections-m6.md).

**Site correction:** Site L09 writes pH ≈ pKa/2 + log(c)/2. The concentration sign is wrong; pH ≈ (pKa − log₁₀ c)/2 under the weak-acid approximation. See [Module 6 site corrections](../site-corrections-m6.md) for the source and replacement statement.

## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes

**L09:**

- pH equals pKa for every weak acid: the page limits this to equal acid/base amounts at half-equivalence.
- A small Ka always permits approximation: concentration also matters; verify x/c.
- KaKb = Kw for unrelated acids and bases: it applies to a conjugate pair.
- Every neutralisation salt has pH 7: acetate is basic and ammonium salts acidic.

These traps come from the linked site lessons. They are not attributed to NESA markers without a specific exam report.
