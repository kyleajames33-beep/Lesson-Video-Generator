## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**Teaching order.**

**L01:**

1. Mutation changes DNA sequence and can create a new allele; its occurrence is random with respect to need.
2. Natural selection can then change the frequency of an existing resistant allele under antibiotic exposure.
3. A gene is a functional DNA region, an allele is a version of that gene, and a gene pool is the alleles in a population.
4. Meiosis and fertilisation usually reshuffle existing alleles into new combinations rather than create alleles.
5. Later population processes, including selection, drift and gene flow, affect an allele’s fate; later lessons address human biotechnology.

**Worked examples and numbers from the extracts.**

**L01:** The page contrasts a new DNA base change with siblings inheriting different combinations and antibiotic treatment increasing resistant bacteria. Its six-mark model traces a mutation from one cell through inheritance to an allele in the population gene pool. Its five-mark distinction uses an HbS haemoglobin substitution as a new allele and meiotic crossing over plus fertilisation as reshuffling.

**Practice.**

**L01:** Warm-up, interactive mutation classifier, fill-in and true/false checks, two classification and language-repair activities, multiple choice, three short answers, and two guided extended responses. Representative: “Define mutation, allele and gene pool, then explain how they are linked.” “Evaluate the statement: ‘Antibiotics cause bacteria to mutate so they can survive.’ Use the antibiotic resistance anchor in your answer.”

The extracts record no named mnemonic unless shown above. Keep any useful contrast or question from the page; do not manufacture a memory aid.

## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes

**L01:**

- Bacteria mutate because they need antibiotic resistance. The page separates random mutation from subsequent selection.
- Fertilisation makes brand-new alleles. It combines existing alleles.
- A gene pool is the genes in one organism. It belongs to a population.
- Every mutation changes phenotype. Many have no visible effect.

These traps come from the linked site lessons. They are not attributed to NESA markers without a specific exam report.
