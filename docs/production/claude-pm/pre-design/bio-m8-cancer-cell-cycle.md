## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**Page sections in order:** Warm up first; Predict first: damaged DNA; Three checkpoints, three different questions; Cancer vocabulary, translated; Build the cancer pathway; Accelerator and brake: a useful model; Named oncogenes: the accelerators, with figures; Named tumour suppressors: the brakes, and Knudson's two-hit model; Carcinogens: three categories, one endpoint; HPV in detail: a biological carcinogen Australia is beating; Why Australia leads the world in skin cancer; Benign, malignant and metastasis; Metastasis in molecular detail, and why it kills; Choose your route; Exit check; Multiple Choice; Short Answer, 15 marks; Check what actually stuck; Take the full module quiz; Defeat the Boss, Cancer Showdown!; How did your thinking change?.

**Teaching points in order:** 1. From control failure to spread. 2. A healthy cell does not divide whenever it likes. 3. A stop, repair or cell-death response can weaken, allowing damaged cells to survive. 4. A carcinogen is any agent that raises cancer risk by increasing mutations in cell-cycle genes.

**Site examples and practice:**  “A checkpoint detects severe DNA damage. Which response best protects the organism?” “Why can one mutant oncogene allele drive division while one working tumour suppressor allele still protects the cell?”

## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes

Follow DNA damage to failed checkpoints, excess cell division and possible spread. A carcinogen raises risk; it does not guarantee cancer. NESA feedback: 2025 Q25 requires a definite evidence-based judgement of a disease claim. See [exam research](../research/question-types-bio-m8.md) for the official paper links and marker context.
