### Source algebraic-techniques-and-inequalities/lesson01.sa.html, `we-2`

PROBLEM 2 · FLIP RULE Solve $-2x \geq 10$. 1 Divide both sides by $-2$. Because we are dividing by a negative , flip the sign: $x \leq -5$ The flip rule activates: $\geq$ becomes $\leq$. 2 Check: substitute $x = -6$: $-2(-6) = 12 \geq 10$ ✓ $-6 \leq -5$ is true, so $-6$ is in the solution set. The check confirms. 3 Answer: $x \leq -5$ All values less than or equal to $-5$ satisfy the original inequality.

### Source algebraic-techniques-and-inequalities/lesson01.sa.html, `we-3`

PROBLEM 3 · BRACKET INEQUALITY Solve $4(x - 1) > 12$. 1 Divide both sides by 4: $x - 1 > 3$ Dividing by positive 4, no flip. Alternatively, expand first: $4x - 4 > 12$. 2 Add 1 to both sides: $x > 4$ Undo the $-1$. 3 Check: substitute $x = 5$: $4(5 - 1) = 16 > 12$ ✓. Answer: $x > 4$ All values greater than 4 satisfy the inequality. Note: $x = 4$ does not satisfy it (strict inequality).

## 4. Misconceptions and HSC-style traps

- Forgetting to flip when dividing by a negative.
- Flipping when subtracting a negative number or when dividing by a positive.
- Open versus closed circle for strict and non-strict signs.
- Arrow direction drawn from the variable side rather than the solution side.

These follow from the functions and official content, not claims about how often markers see them.

## 6. Two original candidate practice questions

### Question 1

Solve 5 - 3x <= 17 and show the solution on a number line.

**Worked answer:** -3x <= 12, dividing by -3 flips: x >= -4. Closed circle at -4, arrow to the right.

### Question 2

Solve (2x + 1)/3 > x - 1.

**Worked answer:** 2x + 1 > 3x - 3, so -x > -4, flip: x < 4. Open circle at 4, arrow to the left.
