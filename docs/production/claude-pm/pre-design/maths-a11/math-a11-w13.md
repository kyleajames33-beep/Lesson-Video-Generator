PROBLEM 1 · BREAK-EVEN A stall pays $\$200$ for the site and $\$3$ per drink, selling each at $\$5$. Find the break-even quantity and the profit on 250 drinks. 1 $C = 200 + 3n$ and $R = 5n$, where $n$ is the number of drinks Both models over the same variable. 2 Break-even: $5n = 200 + 3n$, so $2n = 200$ and $n = 100$ Set cost equal to revenue. 3 $P = 2n - 200$; at $n = 250$, $P = 500 - 200 = 300$ dollars profit Profit is the margin per drink times quantity, less the fixed cost.

### Source working-with-functions/lesson19.sa.html, `we-2`

PROBLEM 2 · ROUNDING AT BREAK-EVEN A workshop has fixed costs of $\$340$ and makes $\$8$ profit per item. How many items must it sell to make a profit? 1 Profit $P = 8n - 340$ Margin per item times quantity, less fixed costs. 2 Break-even: $8n = 340$, so $n = 42.5$ Not a whole number. 3 $42$ items still loses money, so it must sell at least $43$ Round up, since the goal is to be strictly above break-even.

## 6. Two original candidate practice questions

### Question 1

Streaming service A charges $12 a month plus $2 per movie rental. Service B charges $20 a month plus $1 per rental. For how many rentals do they cost the same, and which is cheaper for 10 rentals?

**Worked answer:** 12 + 2r = 20 + r gives r = 8 (both cost $28). For 10 rentals A costs $32 and B costs $30, so B is cheaper.

### Question 2

A school band pays $360 to set up a T-shirt order. Each shirt costs $7 to make and sells for $15. Find the break-even number of shirts and the profit if 60 are sold.

**Worked answer:** 15n = 360 + 7n gives n = 45 shirts. Profit on 60: 15(60) - (360 + 7(60)) = 900 - 780 = $120.
