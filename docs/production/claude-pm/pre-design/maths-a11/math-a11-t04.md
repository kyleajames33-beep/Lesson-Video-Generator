### Source trigonometry-and-measure-of-angles/lesson05.sa.html, card 05 "From special triangles to any quadrant"

For any angle $\theta$, the reference angle $\alpha$ is the acute angle between the terminal arm and the $x$-axis: Quadrant II: $\alpha = 180° - \theta$ Quadrant III: $\alpha = \theta - 180°$ Quadrant IV: $\alpha = 360° - \theta$ Then: find the exact value at the reference angle, and apply the correct sign from ASTC. This lets you find exact values for $120°$, $225°$, $300°$, and even $-30°$ without ever touching a calculator.

## 4. Misconceptions and HSC-style traps

- Using θ − 180° in the second quadrant (it gives a negative "related angle"; the related angle is 180° − θ there).
- Getting the size right and the sign wrong: cos 150° is negative because x < 0.
- Taking ± from a square root and keeping both, instead of letting the quadrant choose.
- Writing sin(−θ) = sin θ by analogy with cos.

These follow from the functions and official content, not claims about how often markers see them.

## 6. Two original candidate practice questions

### Question 1

Find the exact value of sin 240° − cos 300° + tan(−135°).

**Worked answer:** sin 240° = −sin 60° = −√3/2; cos 300° = cos 60° = 1/2; tan(−135°) = −tan 135° = −(−1) = 1. Total: −√3/2 − 1/2 + 1 = (1 − √3)/2.

### Question 2

Given cos θ = −8/17 and 180° < θ < 270°, find the exact values of sin θ and tan θ.

**Worked answer:** Draw the triangle 8, 15, 17 (8² + 15² = 17²). In the third quadrant x and y are negative, so sin θ = −15/17 and tan θ = (−15)/(−8) = 15/8.
