## 4. Misconceptions and HSC-style traps

- Mixing M (mega) and m (milli).
- A negative power read as a negative number (10⁻³ = −1000).
- Scientific notation with a first part of 10 or more (25.6 × 10¹⁰).
- Counting leading zeros as significant figures.
- Converting prefixes by the wrong power (GB to TB as ÷ 100).

## 6. Two original candidate practice questions

**Question 1.** (a) Write 4 TB in bytes using scientific notation. (b) A strand of spider silk is 7 µm wide. Write this width in metres using scientific notation.
Worked answer: (a) 4 × 10¹² bytes. (b) 7 × 10⁻⁶ m. Slip: "7 × 10⁶ m" (the sign of the power).

**Question 2.** A phone has 64 GB of free space. Each song file is 4.8 MB. (a) Write 64 GB in bytes using scientific notation. (b) How many whole songs fit? (c) Write your answer to (b) in scientific notation correct to 2 significant figures.
Worked answer: (a) 6.4 × 10¹⁰ bytes. (b) 6.4 × 10¹⁰ ÷ (4.8 × 10⁶) = 13 333.3, so 13 333 songs. (c) 1.3 × 10⁴. Slip: "64 ÷ 4.8 = 13.3 songs" (prefixes ignored).

(Design note: the spider silk in question 1 is replaced in the design by a phone screen's pixel, about 50 µm wide, to keep the anchor on everyday technology.)
