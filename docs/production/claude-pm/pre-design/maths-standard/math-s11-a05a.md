## 4. Misconceptions and HSC-style traps

- Using the slant side as the height.
- Forgetting the ½ for a triangle or trapezium.
- Counting an internal line in a perimeter.
- Adding when a part is cut out.
- Mixing perimeter (m) and area (m²) units.

## 6. Two original candidate practice questions

**Question 1.** A triangular shade sail has a base of 3.2 m and a perpendicular height of 4.5 m. Its sloping edge is 4.8 m long. Find the area of the sail.
Worked answer: ½ × 3.2 × 4.5 = 7.2 m². Slip: "½ × 3.2 × 4.8 = 7.68 m²" (slant side as height).

**Question 2.** An L-shaped floor is a 7 m by 5 m rectangle with a 3 m by 2 m rectangle removed from one corner. Find its area and its perimeter.
Worked answer: area 35 − 6 = 29 m²; perimeter 7 + 5 + 4 + 2 + 3 + 3 = 24 m (the same as the full rectangle, because the two cut edges replace the two removed edges). Slip: "24 − 3 − 2 = 19 m".
