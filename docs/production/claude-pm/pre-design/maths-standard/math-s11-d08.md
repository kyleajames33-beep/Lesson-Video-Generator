## 4. Misconceptions and HSC-style traps

- Ignoring the key (is 2 | 4 twenty-four or 2.4?).
- Reading the left side of a back-to-back plot inwards (4 | 2 read as 42 instead of 24).
- Counting dots as values (each dot is one data value; the axis gives the value).
- Reading a line graph between points as data (it shows the trend, not measured values).
- Sector angle taken as the amount (135° is not 135 students).
- Divided bar lengths taken as amounts without dividing by the whole length.

## 6. Two original candidate practice questions

**Question 1.** A sector graph shows how 40 students travelled to a concert: train 135°, car 90°, bus 81°, walk 54°. (a) How many students travelled by train? (b) What percentage of the students walked?
Worked answer: (a) 135 ÷ 360 × 40 = 15. (b) 54 ÷ 360 = 0.15 = 15%. Slip: "(a) 135 students".

**Question 2.** A back-to-back stem-and-leaf plot shows two cafés' daily coffee sales over 10 days. Café A (left): 38, 42, 45, 47, 51, 53, 55, 58, 62, 66. Café B (right): 29, 34, 36, 41, 44, 48, 49, 52, 57, 71. (a) Which café had the highest single day? (b) Which café sold 50 or more coffees on more days?
Worked answer: (a) Café B, 71. (b) Café A: 6 days (B: 3). Slip: "(b) Café B, because it had the highest day".
