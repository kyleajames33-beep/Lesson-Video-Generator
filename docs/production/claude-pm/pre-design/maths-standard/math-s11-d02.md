## 4. Misconceptions and HSC-style traps

- Thinking "anonymous" just means not printing names (a combination of details can still identify someone).
- Thinking bias is only about who is sampled (the wording and the options can bias results too).
- Treating ethics as optional politeness (consent and honest reporting are requirements).
- Assuming one kind of household (own bedroom, own phone, two parents, English at home).
- Reporting only the results that support the issue.

## 6. Two original candidate practice questions

**Question 1.** A gym's survey asks: "Full name and mobile number: ______" and "Don't you agree our new opening hours are better?" Identify one privacy problem and one bias problem, and fix each.
Worked answer: privacy: the name and number aren't needed and link answers to people; remove them (or keep them separate and confidential). Bias: "Don't you agree" leads; ask "How do the new opening hours suit you?" with balanced choices. Slip: "it's fine, members agreed to join the gym".

**Question 2.** A council's survey about a new sports field is online only, in English only, and asks "Which sport does your son play?" Give two ways it fails to respond to diverse groups, and a fix for each.
Worked answer: online only and English only miss people without internet or with little English: add paper copies and translations; "your son" assumes a family with a son who plays: ask "Which sports do people in your household play?" with "none" and "prefer not to say". Slip: "it's fair because anyone can open the link".
