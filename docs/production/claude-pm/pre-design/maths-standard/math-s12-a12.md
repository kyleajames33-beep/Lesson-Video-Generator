**Stop:** Recognise inverse variation: x × y stays the same (the constant of variation k), so y = k/x; test a table by multiplying each pair; find k from one pair; graph y = k/x from a table by hand and with an app, avoiding x = 0; the graph is a hyperbola with two separate branches that approach both axes (x = 0 and y = 0) but never meet them; for k > 0 the branches are in the first and third quadrants, for k < 0 in the second and fourth; in practical problems only the first-quadrant branch is used. ## 4. Misconceptions and HSC-style traps

- Calling any decreasing relationship "inverse variation" (a falling line is not; test the product).
- Testing a table with ratios (that is direct variation) instead of products.
- Joining the two branches through the origin, or letting the curve touch an axis.
- Substituting x = 0.
- Thinking the graph "stops" on the screen edge instead of carrying on towards the axes.

These follow from the content, not claims about how often markers see them.

## 6. Two original candidate practice questions

### Question 1

The table shows values of x and y.

| x | 2 | 3 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| y | 24 | 16 | 12 | 8 | 6 |

(a) Show that y varies inversely with x. (b) Write the equation. (c) Find y when x = 16.

**Worked answer:** (a) Each product x × y = 48 (2 × 24, 3 × 16, 4 × 12, 6 × 8, 8 × 6), so the product is constant. (b) y = 48/x. (c) y = 48/16 = 3.

### Question 2

Complete a table of values for y = 6/x for x = −6, −3, −2, −1, 1, 2, 3, 6. Sketch the graph. Write down the equations of the asymptotes and the quadrants the graph lies in.

**Worked answer:** y = −1, −2, −3, −6, 6, 3, 2, 1. The graph is a hyperbola with two branches; asymptotes x = 0 and y = 0; it lies in the first and third quadrants (k = 6 > 0).

Only the question and the table go on the card. Hide the answer and the product column until feedback. "Pause here to answer." then a 10-second countdown. Feedback stays brief: answer, key reason, one crossed-out mistake (for Question 1: "y/x = 12, so y = 12x", crossed, "inverse: multiply, don't divide"). Answers were checked with SymPy.
