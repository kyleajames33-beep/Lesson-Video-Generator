**Stop:** A ratio compares two or more quantities measured in the same unit, written with a colon, and order matters (cordial to water is not water to cordial); a fraction compares a part with the whole: in a : b there are a + b equal parts, so the first quantity is a/(a + b) of the whole; equivalent ratios (multiply or divide every term by the same number); simplest form by dividing every term by the highest common factor, with a check by multiplying back; the ratio of two quantities in different units: convert to one unit first (250 g : 1 kg = 250 : 1000 = 1 : 4); the two different fraction questions (part of the whole, 3/7; one part compared with the other, 3/4). "A ratio compares parts with each other. A fraction compares one part with the whole. ... In the ratio $3:4$ there are $3+4=7$ parts altogether. ## 4. Misconceptions and HSC-style traps

- Reading a : b as the fraction a/b of the whole (2 : 3 as 2/3 instead of 2/5).
- Writing the terms in the wrong order (water to cordial when the question asks cordial to water).
- Simplifying before converting units (250 g : 1 kg as 250 : 1).
- Stopping at an equivalent ratio that is not simplest (45 : 60 = 9 : 12).
- Mixing up "fraction of the whole" (3/7) with "one part as a fraction of the other" (SA 2 (b): 3/5 of the longer piece). Both are legitimate questions; the wording decides.

These follow from the content, not claims about how often markers see them.

## 6. Two original candidate practice questions

### Question 1

A smoothie recipe uses 300 mL of milk and 0.45 L of yoghurt. (a) Write the ratio of milk to yoghurt in simplest form. (b) What fraction of the smoothie is milk?

**Worked answer:** (a) 0.45 L = 450 mL, so 300 : 450; HCF 150 gives 2 : 3. (b) 2 + 3 = 5 parts, so milk is 2/5 of the smoothie.

### Question 2

In a class of 28 students, 12 catch a bus to school and the rest do not. (a) Write the ratio of bus students to students who do not catch a bus, in simplest form. (b) Jordan writes "12 : 28 = 3 : 7, so 3/7 of the class catch a bus." Explain what Jordan's ratio compares, and whether his fraction is correct.

**Worked answer:** (a) 28 − 12 = 16 do not catch a bus; 12 : 16 = 3 : 4. (b) Jordan compared bus students with the whole class, not with the other students. That is a part-to-whole comparison, so his fraction 3/7 is correct; the bus to non-bus ratio is 3 : 4, which also gives 3/7 because 3 + 4 = 7.

Only the question goes on the card. Hide the answer until feedback. "Pause here to answer." then a 10-second countdown. Feedback stays brief: answer, key reason, one crossed-out mistake (for Question 1 (b): "2/3", crossed, "2 parts of 5 altogether"). Answers were checked with SymPy.
