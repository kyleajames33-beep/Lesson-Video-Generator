**Stop:** A penalty rate is a multiplier on the ordinary hourly rate for overtime (hours beyond ordinary hours), weekends and public holidays; the multiplier is always given (time-and-a-quarter 1.25, time-and-a-half 1.5, double time 2, double time and a half 2.5); the multiplier gives the whole new rate (1.5 × $28.60 = $42.90), not an extra half that is then forgotten; work each block of hours at its own rate, then add; tiered overtime (the first 2 hours at 1.5, then 2); allowances (meal, tool, travel) are added as flat amounts and never multiplied; the ordinary-time equivalent (4 hours at double time pays the same as 8 ordinary hours); checking a pay slip line (lesson04). ## 4. Misconceptions and HSC-style traps

- Time-and-a-half as "an extra half" added to the hours, then forgotten (site Trap 01).
- Adding all the hours and applying one rate (site Trap 02: 38 + 5 = 43 hours at one rate).
- Multiplying an allowance by the penalty rate (site Trap 03).
- Applying the overtime rate to all hours instead of only the hours beyond 38.
- Mixing up the order in tiered overtime (double time on the first hours).
- Thinking penalty rates are a bonus the employer chooses to pay; in Australia, awards set minimum penalty rates (one sentence; HSC questions give the rate).

These follow from the content, not claims about how often markers see them.

## 6. Two original candidate practice questions

### Question 1

Sam works at a bookshop and is paid $28.40 per hour on weekdays. Saturday work is paid at time-and-a-quarter and Sunday work at time-and-a-half. One week Sam works 20 hours on weekdays, 6 hours on Saturday and 5 hours on Sunday. Calculate Sam's pay for the week.

**Worked answer:** weekdays 28.40 × 20 = $568.00; Saturday 28.40 × 1.25 = $35.50, × 6 = $213.00; Sunday 28.40 × 1.5 = $42.60, × 5 = $213.00; total $994.00.

### Question 2

A warehouse worker earns $30.20 per hour for 38 ordinary hours. Overtime is paid at time-and-a-half for the first 2 hours and double time after that. One week the worker works 42 hours and receives a $15.60 meal allowance for the overtime shift. Calculate the gross pay for the week.

**Worked answer:** ordinary 30.20 × 38 = $1147.60; first 2 overtime hours 30.20 × 1.5 × 2 = $90.60; next 2 hours 30.20 × 2 × 2 = $120.80; allowance $15.60; total $1374.60.

## 8. Site corrections and decisions for Claude

See `../../site-corrections-maths-standard.md`, section 11 (MS-76: rates below the 2026-27 minimum). No arithmetic error in lesson02 or lesson04's pay examples.

Decisions for Claude:

- **Syllabus wording** names overtime, weekends and public holidays. lesson02 teaches overtime well but uses weekends only inside `we-2`; give weekends and public holidays their own block of the roster.
- **Multipliers.** Teach 1.25 as well as 1.5, 2 and 2.5: real retail Saturday rates are time-and-a-quarter, and the syllabus says "given an hourly rate", so any multiplier may appear.
- **Rates below the minimum.** Prefer drills 1 and 3, `we-3` (Jade, $29.50) and lesson04 `we-1` (Hana, $27.50); if `we-2` (Alinta, $18.60) is used, call it a junior rate on screen.
- **Ordinary-time equivalent** (card 06 insight) is a useful one-line check; keep it short.
- Pace: comfortable (one point, several cases); about 8 minutes.
