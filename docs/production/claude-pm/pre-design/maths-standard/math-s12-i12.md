### Source lesson08, card 08 ("Buy now pay later, and why term matters more than rate")

"The syllabus asks you to compare three ways of paying later: buy now pay later, a short-term loan and a long-term loan. They are not three points on one scale, because BNPL is not priced like a loan at all."

"Take a $3000 purchase. A personal loan charges 12% p.a. compounded monthly; BNPL charges nothing for 12 months provided every instalment is paid on time."

| Option | Term | Repayment | Total paid | Interest |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BNPL, paid on time | 12 months | $250.00 | $3000.00 | $0.00 |
| Short-term loan | 2 years | $141.22 | $3389.28 | $389.28 |
| Long-term loan | 5 years | $66.73 | $4003.80 | $1003.80 |

"Same debt, same interest rate, and the long-term loan costs two and a half times as much interest. The only thing that changed is how long the money is borrowed for. The smaller repayment is what makes it feel cheaper, and it is exactly what makes it dearer."

"The BNPL condition is doing all the work. That $0 holds only while every instalment lands on time. Miss one and a late fee applies, typically around $10 per missed payment, and any balance still owing after the interest-free period starts attracting interest. On a rate of 18% p.a. simple, $800 still owing for a further 6 months adds 800 × 0.18 × 0.5 = $72. BNPL is the cheapest option in the table and the easiest one to lose money on."

"Compare pay-later options on TOTAL amount paid, not on the repayment."

## 3. Recalculation

- 1% a month on $3 000: 24 months, repayment $141.22 (141.2204...), total $3 389.28, interest $389.28; 60 months, repayment $66.73 (66.7334...), total $4 003.80, interest $1 003.80. Ratio 1 003.80 ÷ 389.28 = 2.58 ("two and a half times" holds). A month-by-month run with the rounded $66.73 leaves a 61st payment of $0.27, so a real 5-year loan adjusts its last payment; the table's totals are the standard repayment × number of payments.
- BNPL: 12 × $250 = $3 000. Late interest example: $800 × 0.18 × 0.5 = $72.
- Practice values: $1 440 at 1.5% a month: 12 months $132.02 (total $1 584.24, interest $144.24); 24 months $71.89 (total $1 725.36, interest $285.36). $8 000 at 1% a month: 24 months $376.59 (total $9 038.16, interest $1 038.16); 48 months $210.67 (total $10 112.16, interest $2 112.16).

## 4. Misconceptions and HSC-style traps

- Choosing the option with the smallest repayment as the cheapest.
- Thinking the same interest rate means the same cost whatever the term.
- Thinking BNPL is free whatever happens. It is interest-free only while every instalment is on time, and some plans charge an account fee even then.
- Comparing a BNPL plan's fee with a loan's repayment instead of with the loan's interest.
- Leaving out fees (establishment, account, late) when totalling a loan or a plan.

These follow from the content, not claims about how often markers see them.

## 6. Two original candidate practice questions

### Question 1

A phone costs $1 440. Three ways to pay are offered:

- BNPL: 4 fortnightly instalments of $360, no interest or fees if every instalment is paid on time, $10 late fee for each missed instalment;
- a 12-month loan with repayments of $132.02 per month;
- a 24-month loan with repayments of $71.89 per month.

(a) Find the total paid for each option if every payment is on time. (b) Find the BNPL total if two instalments are missed. (c) Which option would you recommend for someone who is paid fortnightly and can afford $360 a fortnight? Justify with numbers.

**Worked answer:** (a) BNPL $1 440.00; 12 months: 12 × $132.02 = $1 584.24; 24 months: 24 × $71.89 = $1 725.36. (b) $1 440 + 2 × $10 = $1 460.00. (c) BNPL: it costs $1 440, which is $144.24 less than the 12-month loan and $285.36 less than the 24-month loan, and the $360 instalments fit a fortnightly pay, so they can be paid on time.

### Question 2

A car repair costs $8 000. A lender charges 12% p.a. (1% per month). The repayments are $376.59 per month over 2 years, or $210.67 per month over 4 years. (a) Find the total interest for each term. (b) How much more does the 4-year loan cost? (c) Explain why the longer loan costs more, even though the interest rate is the same.

**Worked answer:** (a) 2 years: 24 × $376.59 = $9 038.16, interest $1 038.16. 4 years: 48 × $210.67 = $10 112.16, interest $2 112.16. (b) $2 112.16 − $1 038.16 = $1 074.00. (c) With smaller repayments, the balance falls more slowly and money is owed for twice as long, so interest is charged on more dollars for more months.

## 8. Site corrections and decisions for Claude

No error in the parts used. Decisions for Claude:

- **BNPL plan realism.** The site's BNPL runs 12 monthly instalments with no fee. Many short BNPL plans are 4 fortnightly instalments; longer plans for larger purchases often charge a monthly account fee. Keep the site's $3 000 table as the spine, and add one sentence and one stamp: "if the plan has an account fee, add it to the total" (the Year 11 rule).
- **Responsible framing.** State the costs plainly with the numbers: $0 if every instalment is on time, $10 for each missed one, $72 if $800 is still owed for 6 months at 18%; a 5-year loan costs $1 003.80 against $389.28 over 2 years. Since June 2025 BNPL is regulated as credit (a credit check, and missed payments can appear on a credit report). No lecture.
- **Repayments are supplied.** The loan repayments come from "the lender's calculator"; the video does not derive them (no formula; i15 uses a repayment table).
