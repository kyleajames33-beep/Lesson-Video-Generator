## 4. Misconceptions and HSC-style traps

- Sizing the fund on all spending (including discretionary) instead of living expenses.
- Rounding the weeks or months down.
- Adding variances without their signs.
- Judging a category by its percentage variance alone.
- Treating any one strategy as the whole answer.

## 6. Two original candidate practice questions

**Question 1.** A person's living expenses are $2480 a month and their surplus is $1180 a month. (a) Find the target for a 3-month and a 6-month emergency fund. (b) How many whole months of saving does the 3-month fund take?
Worked answer: (a) $7440; $14 880. (b) 7440 ÷ 1180 = 6.3, so 7 months. Slip: "6 months" (rounded down: 6 × 1180 = $7080, short).

**Question 2.** Priya budgets $700 for groceries, $320 for eating out and $180 for entertainment each month. One month she spends $763, $272 and $225. Her usual surplus is $1500 a month and her living expenses are $3280 a month. (a) Find the net variance and her surplus that month. (b) Her hours are casual. Recommend a 3-month or 6-month fund and justify your answer with figures.
Worked answer: (a) +63 − 48 + 45 = +$60 overspent; surplus $1440. (b) Six months, $19 680, because casual hours can drop without notice; it takes about 14 months at $1440 (13.7 rounded up), against 7 months for a 3-month fund ($9840). Slip: "variance = 63 + 48 + 45 = $156" (signs dropped).
