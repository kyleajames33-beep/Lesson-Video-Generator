### Source lesson03, hook and revisit

"A house plan is drawn at $1:100$. The kitchen bench measures $3.6\text{ cm}$ on the plan and benchtop costs $\$320$ per metre. Can you price the bench without ever visiting the house?" Revisit: "$360\text{ cm}$, or $3.6\text{ m}$, and at $\$320$ per metre it costs $3.6 \times 320 = \$1152$."

### Source lesson03, key term and card 06 "Quantities and costs from a plan"

"Building plan: A scale drawing of a floor viewed from above, used to read off room sizes and work out materials. Like this: a bedroom measuring $4\text{ cm}$ by $3\text{ cm}$ on a $1:100$ plan is $4\text{ m}$ by $3\text{ m}$, so it needs $12\text{ m}^2$ of carpet." "You measure a room on the plan, scale it up, find an area, then multiply by a rate."

Worked example 5: "A room measures $5.2\text{ cm}$ by $3.5\text{ cm}$ at $1:100$ ... Scale each side separately ... Area $= 5.2 \times 3.5 = 18.2\text{ m}^2$ ... Multiply the two real lengths. Never the plan lengths." Worked example 6: "Tiles cost $\$45$ per square metre ... $18.2 \times 45 = 819$ ... Cost $= \$819$". Tips: "Scale the sides, not the area", "Read the rate's unit: Per metre needs a length; per square metre needs an area", "Estimate is not a free pass". Fill the gaps: "A bedroom measures $4.4\text{ cm}$ by $3.0\text{ cm}$ on a plan drawn at $1:50$" (2.2 m, 1.5 m, 3.3 m², $132; see MS-39 note on realism).

## 3. Recalculation

- Hook: 3.6 × 320 = $1152. Worked examples 5 and 6: 5.2 × 3.5 = 18.2 m²; × 45 = $819. Fill the gaps: 2.2 × 1.5 = 3.3 m²; × 40 = $132. SA 3: 4.2 × 3.0 = 12.6 m²; × 38 = $478.80. SA 1 (a): 1410 cm = 14.1 m. Drill 5: 10 m². Drill 1: 15 m.
- Question 1: 2.8 m × 2.0 m = 5.6 m²; + 10% = 6.16 m²; ÷ 1.44 = 4.28, so 5 boxes; $290. Question 2: walls 2 × (3.8 + 3.2) × 2.4 = 33.6 m²; door 0.82 × 2.04 = 1.6728 m²; window 1.5 × 1.2 = 1.8 m²; 30.1272 m²; two coats ÷ 12 m² per litre = 5.02 L, so two 4 L tins, $178.
- Deck anchor: 4.2 m × 3.0 m at $65 per m² = $819.

## 4. Misconceptions and HSC-style traps

- Multiplying plan lengths and scaling the area once (× 100 instead of × 10 000; drill 5's option B).
- Using an area when the price is per metre (the bench) or a length when it is per m².
- Rounding boxes or tins to the nearest whole number instead of up (4.28 boxes is 5 boxes).
- Forgetting to subtract doors and windows, or forgetting the second coat.
- Treating the plan's door swing or window symbol as a wall gap of the wrong size.

These follow from the content, not claims about how often markers see them.

## 6. Two original candidate practice questions

### Question 1

On a plan drawn at 1 : 50, a bathroom floor measures 5.6 cm by 4.0 cm. Floor tiles are sold in boxes that cover 1.44 m², at $58 a box. The tiler adds 10% to the floor area for cuts and breakages. (a) Find the real floor area. (b) How many boxes are needed, and what do they cost?

**Worked answer:** (a) 5.6 × 50 = 280 cm = 2.8 m; 4.0 × 50 = 200 cm = 2.0 m; area 5.6 m². (b) 5.6 × 1.1 = 6.16 m²; 6.16 ÷ 1.44 = 4.28, so 5 boxes; 5 × $58 = $290.

### Question 2

A bedroom on a 1 : 100 plan measures 3.8 cm by 3.2 cm. The ceiling is 2.4 m high. The plan shows one door (0.82 m by 2.04 m) and one window (1.5 m by 1.2 m). Paint covers 12 m² per litre per coat and comes in 4 L tins at $89 each. Two coats are needed on the walls. (a) Find the wall area to be painted. (b) How many tins are needed, and what is the cost?

**Worked answer:** (a) Real room 3.8 m by 3.2 m; walls 2 × (3.8 + 3.2) × 2.4 = 33.6 m²; minus door 1.67 m² and window 1.8 m² gives about 30.13 m². (b) Two coats: 60.25 m² ÷ 12 = 5.02 L, so 2 tins (8 L); $178.

Only the question goes on the card (with a small coded plan for Question 2 showing the door and window symbols). Hide the answer until feedback. "Pause here to answer." then a 10-second countdown. Feedback stays brief: answer, key reason, one crossed-out mistake (for Question 1 (b): "4 boxes", crossed, "4.28 boxes: buy 5"). Answers were checked with SymPy. Prices and coverage are realistic NSW 2026 examples, labelled as examples.
