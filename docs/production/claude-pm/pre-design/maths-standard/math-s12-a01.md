### Source lesson01, card 02 (key idea)

"Simultaneous equations describe two conditions that must both be satisfied at the same time. A solution is a pair of values $(x, y)$ that makes both equations true. Depending on the lines, there may be one solution, no solutions or infinitely many. For example, $x+y=2$ and $x+y=3$ cannot both be true, so there is no solution. The equations $x+y=2$ and $2x+2y=4$ describe the same line, so there are infinitely many solutions."

### Source lesson01, card 05 (graphical method)

Rearrange both equations into $y = mx + b$ (or use a table of values), graph both on the same axes, identify the intersection, write $x = \ldots,\ y = \ldots$ and verify in both original equations. The card adds: "the approved calculators for Mathematics Standard 2 do not have graphing capability. When graphing by hand, draw at least two clearly marked points on each line and use a ruler."

### Source lesson01, `we-1`

PROBLEM 1 · GRAPHICAL METHOD. Solve $y = 2x - 1$ and $y = -x + 5$ graphically. Verify algebraically. 1 Line 1: gradient 2, $y$-intercept $-1$; Line 2: gradient $-1$, $y$-intercept 5. 2 Tables: Line 1 $(0,-1), (1,1), (2,3)$; Line 2 $(0,5), (2,3), (4,1)$. "Notice both lines pass through $(2, 3)$." 3 Read the intersection: $(2, 3)$, so $x = 2,\ y = 3$. 4 Verify: $3 = 2(2) - 1$ ✓ and $3 = -(2) + 5$ ✓.

### Source lesson01, short answer SA 3 (practical reading)

"Two mobile plans cost the same for a certain number of minutes. Plan A costs $\$15$/month plus $\$0.05$ per minute. Plan B costs $\$5$/month plus $\$0.10$ per minute." Answer: $C = 0.05m + 15$ and $C = 0.10m + 5$; $m = 200$ minutes; $C = \$25$.

Also usable: drill 1 ($y = x + 3$ and $y = -2x + 6$ meet at $(1, 4)$), SA 1 ($y = 2x - 3$ and $y = -x + 6$ meet at $(3, 3)$) and the quick check (lines meeting at $(3, -1)$ means both equations are true there). The café hook, `we-2` and `we-3` belong to a02.

## 3. Recalculation

- `we-1`: y = 2x − 1 and y = −x + 5 meet at (2, 3); table rows (0, −1), (1, 1), (2, 3) and (0, 5), (2, 3), (4, 1) are correct.
- Drill 1: (1, 4). SA 1: (3, 3).
- SA 3: 0.05m + 15 = 0.10m + 5 gives m = 200 and C = 25.
- Key idea: x + y = 2 and x + y = 3 have no solution; x + y = 2 and 2x + 2y = 4 give every point of one line.

## 4. Misconceptions and HSC-style traps

- Reading the crossing as one number ("the answer is 2") instead of a pair, or swapping x and y.
- Checking the point in only one equation.
- Plotting one point per line, or joining points freehand, so the crossing drifts.
- Reading a crossing between grid lines as the nearest whole numbers without saying it is an estimate.
- In context, stating the coordinates without saying what they mean ("200 minutes, both plans cost $25").

These follow from the content, not claims about how often markers see them.

## 6. Two original candidate practice questions

### Question 1

Complete a table of values for x = 0, 1, 2, 3 for each of y = x + 2 and y = 8 − 2x. Draw both lines on the same axes. Write down the point of intersection and check it in both equations.

**Worked answer:** y = x + 2 gives 2, 3, 4, 5; y = 8 − 2x gives 8, 6, 4, 2. The lines cross at (2, 4). Check: 4 = 2 + 2 ✓ and 4 = 8 − 2(2) ✓.

### Question 2

Driving school A charges a $90 booking fee plus $55 per lesson. Driving school B charges $70 per lesson and no fee. The graph shows the total cost of each school for 0 to 10 lessons. (a) Write down the point of intersection. (b) Explain what it means. (c) Which school is cheaper for 10 lessons, and by how much?

**Worked answer:** (a) (6, 420). (b) For 6 lessons both schools cost $420. (c) A costs 90 + 55 × 10 = $640 and B costs 70 × 10 = $700, so A is cheaper by $60.

The card shows the graph with both lines, unlabelled at the crossing. Only the question goes on the card. Hide the answer and which school is cheaper until feedback. "Pause here to answer." then a 10-second countdown. Feedback stays brief: answer, key reason, one crossed-out mistake (for Question 1: "x = 2" alone, crossed, "give the pair"). Answers were checked with SymPy.
