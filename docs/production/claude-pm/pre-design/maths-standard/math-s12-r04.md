**Stop:** A map scale 1 : n is a ratio with no units: 1 of anything on the map stands for n of the same thing on the ground (1 cm for n cm, 1 mm for n mm); this is r01's ratio idea with "same unit" built in; map to real: multiply by n, then convert the units; real to map: convert the real length to the map's unit, then divide by n; a scale bar (a line labelled with the real distance it stands for, still right after zooming or photocopying); finding the scale from one measured pair, n = real ÷ map in the same unit (Year 11 lesson10 `we-3`); using a real distance in a travel-time problem (SA 2). ### Source lesson03, key terms and card 04 "A scale is a ratio with the units stripped off"

"Scale: A ratio comparing a length on a drawing with the matching real length, written drawing : real." "A scale is written $1:n$ and carries no units at all. That is deliberate. It means one of anything on the drawing stands for $n$ of the same thing in reality." "Going out to the real world you multiply by $n$. Coming back to the drawing you divide by $n$. Convert the units only after that, never before." (MS-39: worked example 2 converts first.) "$\text{real} = \text{plan} \times n \qquad \text{plan} = \dfrac{\text{real}}{n}$"

Worked example 3, a map scale: "Two towns are $6.4\text{ cm}$ apart on a $1:50\,000$ map ... $6.4 \times 50\,000 = 320\,000\text{ cm}$ ... $= 3200\text{ m} = 3.2\text{ km}$. Check: $3.2\text{ km} = 320\,000\text{ cm}$ and $320\,000 \div 50\,000 = 6.4\text{ cm}$." Box: "Multiply first, convert second."

## 3. Recalculation

- Lesson03: 6.4 × 50 000 = 320 000 cm = 3.2 km; drill 3: 200 000 cm = 2 km; SA 2: 750 000 cm = 7.5 km, 7.5 ÷ 18 = 5/12 h = 25 min.
- Year 11 lesson10: 8.4 × 25 000 = 210 000 cm = 2.1 km; 3.5 km = 350 000 cm, ÷ 25 000 = 14 cm; 2700 ÷ 4.5 = 600, so 1 : 600; quick check 7.5 × 400 = 3000 cm = 30 m; hook 3.2 × 50 000 = 1.6 km, paddock 6 cm² × 50 000² = 1.5 × 10¹⁰ cm² = 1.5 km².
- Question 1: 18.4 × 25 000 = 460 000 cm = 4.6 km; 4.6 ÷ 3 = 1.533 h = 92 min (1 h 32 min); 1.2 km = 120 000 cm, ÷ 25 000 = 4.8 cm. Question 2: 2 km on 4 cm gives n = 50 000; 7.3 × 50 000 = 365 000 cm = 3.65 km.

## 4. Misconceptions and HSC-style traps

- Dividing by n when going from the map to the ground (drill 1's option D).
- Converting to metres before scaling, then dividing by 100 again.
- Stopping at centimetres or metres when the question asks for kilometres.
- Reading 1 : 50 as "bigger" than 1 : 500 (it shows more detail but a smaller real length for the same map length).
- Squaring is needed for areas, not lengths (only if the area note is used).

These follow from the content, not claims about how often markers see them.

## 6. Two original candidate practice questions

### Question 1

A bushwalking map has a scale of 1 : 25 000. A walking track measures 18.4 cm on the map. (a) Find the real length of the track in kilometres. (b) A walker averages 3 km/h. How long will the walk take, in hours and minutes? (c) A lookout is 1.2 km from the car park. How far apart are they on the map?

**Worked answer:** (a) 18.4 × 25 000 = 460 000 cm = 4.6 km. (b) 4.6 ÷ 3 = 1.533 h = 1 h 32 min. (c) 1.2 km = 120 000 cm; 120 000 ÷ 25 000 = 4.8 cm.

### Question 2

A road map has a scale bar on which 4 cm stands for 2 km. (a) Write the scale as a ratio in the form 1 : n. (b) Two towns are 7.3 cm apart on the map. Find the real distance between them in kilometres.

**Worked answer:** (a) 2 km = 200 000 cm; 200 000 ÷ 4 = 50 000, so 1 : 50 000. (b) 7.3 × 50 000 = 365 000 cm = 3.65 km.

Only the question goes on the card. Hide the answer until feedback. "Pause here to answer." then a 10-second countdown. Feedback stays brief: answer, key reason, one crossed-out mistake (for Question 1 (a): "0.000736 km" or "18.4 ÷ 25 000", crossed, "map to ground: multiply"). Answers were checked with SymPy.
