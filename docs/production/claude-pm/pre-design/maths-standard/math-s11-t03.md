## 4. Misconceptions and HSC-style traps

- Writing longitude first.
- Dropping the minus sign (south becomes north; 33.86°N, 151.21°E is in the Pacific Ocean).
- Thinking latitude can exceed 90°, or longitude 180°.
- Reading a bigger latitude as "further north" in the southern hemisphere (it is further from the equator, so further south).
- Reading degrees and minutes as decimals (33° 52′ is not 33.52°).

## 6. Two original candidate practice questions

**Question 1.** A map app shows a pin at −27.47, 153.03. Write its coordinates using N or S and E or W, and name the two hemispheres it is in.
Worked answer: 27.47°S, 153.03°E; southern and eastern hemispheres (Brisbane). Slip: "27.47°N".

**Question 2.** Darwin is at about (12.5°S, 130.8°E) and Adelaide at about (34.9°S, 138.6°E). (a) Which city is further from the equator? (b) Which city is further east?
Worked answer: (a) Adelaide (34.9° > 12.5°). (b) Adelaide (138.6° > 130.8°). Slip: "(a) Darwin".
