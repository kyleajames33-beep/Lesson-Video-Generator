## 4. Misconceptions and HSC-style traps

- A slanted boundary used as a height.
- Cuts that create pieces with no known height.
- The internal cut lines counted in the perimeter.
- Double counting an overlap or leaving a gap between pieces.
- Forgetting to convert m² to hectares (÷ 10 000).

## 6. Two original candidate practice questions

**Question 1.** A block of land is a 30 m by 25 m rectangle with a right-angled triangular corner removed, the triangle's shorter sides being 10 m and 8 m along the boundaries. Find the area of the block.
Worked answer: 30 × 25 − ½ × 10 × 8 = 750 − 40 = 710 m². Slip: "750 + 40 = 790 m²" (added, not cut away).

**Question 2.** A block is a trapezium. Its front boundary is 45 m, its back boundary is 30 m and parallel to the front, and its left side is 40 m long at right angles to both. Find (a) the length of the slanted right side, (b) the perimeter, (c) the area in hectares.
Worked answer: (a) √(40² + 15²) = √1825 = 42.72 m. (b) 45 + 40 + 30 + 42.72 = 157.72 m. (c) ½ × (45 + 30) × 40 = 1500 m² = 0.15 ha. Slip: "slanted side = 40 m" (the height used as the slant).
