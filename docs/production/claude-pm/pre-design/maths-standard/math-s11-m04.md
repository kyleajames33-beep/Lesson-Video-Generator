## 4. Misconceptions and HSC-style traps

- The smallest repayment taken as the cheapest method.
- Forgetting the deposit, or adding it on top of the cash price.
- Miscounting repayments ("two years of monthly repayments" is 24).
- Dividing the extra by the total instead of the cash price.
- Justifying "cheapest" for a buyer who cannot use that method.

## 6. Two original candidate practice questions

**Question 1.** A fridge has a cash price of $1400. On terms, it costs a 10% deposit and 24 monthly repayments of $58. (a) Find the total cost on terms. (b) Find the extra paid as a percentage of the cash price, to one decimal place.
Worked answer: (a) 140 + 24 × 58 = 140 + 1392 = $1532. (b) 132 ÷ 1400 × 100% = 9.4%. Slip: "total = 24 × 58 = $1392".

**Question 2.** The same fridge can be bought with buy now, pay later: 10 fortnightly instalments of $140 and a $7 account fee for each month money is owed (the plan runs for 5 months). Maya's fridge has broken and she needs a new one this week. She has $500 saved and can put aside $140 a fortnight. (a) Find the total cost with buy now, pay later. (b) Justify which method best suits Maya, using your figures.
Worked answer: (a) 1400 + 5 × 7 = $1435. (b) She can't pay $1400 cash. Buy now, pay later costs $1435 against $1532 on terms, $97 less; its $140 instalments match what she can save each fortnight, and it is paid off in 5 months, not 24; it only stays at $1435 if no payment is missed. Slip: "terms, because the deposit is smallest today".
