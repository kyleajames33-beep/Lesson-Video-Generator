## 4. Misconceptions and HSC-style traps

- Dividing the total by one term instead of the sum of the terms (480 ÷ 5).
- Treating a given share as the total (the 12 kg of sand divided by 4).
- Multiplying instead of dividing to find one part (drill 5's option D).
- Swapping the shares (giving $180 to the person who paid $50).
- Bare numbers without units, and no check that the shares add back to the total.

These follow from the content, not claims about how often markers see them.

## 6. Two original candidate practice questions

### Question 1

A potting mix uses compost, soil and sand in the ratio 3 : 5 : 2. (a) A gardener makes 60 L of mix. How much of each does she use? (b) Another week she has only 9 L of compost and plenty of soil and sand. What is the most potting mix she can make?

**Worked answer:** (a) 3 + 5 + 2 = 10 parts; one part = 60 ÷ 10 = 6 L; compost 18 L, soil 30 L, sand 12 L (check 60 L). (b) 9 L of compost is 3 parts, so one part is 3 L and the whole mix is 10 × 3 = 30 L.

### Question 2

Three friends rent a holiday house for a long weekend for $1560. Ava stays 2 nights, Ben and Chloe stay 3 nights each, and they agree to share the cost in the ratio of nights stayed. (a) Find each person's share. (b) Ava's brother says they should just split it three ways. How much more would Ava pay under his plan?

**Worked answer:** (a) 2 : 3 : 3 is 8 parts; one part = $195; Ava $390, Ben $585, Chloe $585 (check $1560). (b) $1560 ÷ 3 = $520; Ava would pay $130 more.

Only the question goes on the card. Hide the answer until feedback. "Pause here to answer." then a 10-second countdown. Feedback stays brief: answer, key reason, one crossed-out mistake (for Question 1 (b): "9 ÷ 10 × ...", crossed, "9 L is the compost share, not the total"). Answers were checked with SymPy.
