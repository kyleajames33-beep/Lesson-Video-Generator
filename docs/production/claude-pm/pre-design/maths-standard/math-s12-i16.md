## 4. Misconceptions and HSC-style traps

- "Interest-free" means the card never charges interest. It means no interest on purchases only if the whole closing balance is paid by the due date.
- Paying "most" of the balance keeps the interest-free days. On most cards, paying less than the full closing balance loses them, and interest is charged from each purchase date.
- Cash advances get interest-free days. They do not, and they usually attract a fee and a higher rate from the day of withdrawal.
- A credit card is "free money" up to the limit. It is a loan: the balance owed is reduced by repayments and grows with interest and new spending.
- Comparing rates per month with rates per year (e.g. 1.8% a month against a 10% p.a. loan).

These follow from the content, not claims about how often markers see them.

## 6. Two original candidate practice questions

### Question 1

A credit card has these terms: purchase rate 20.99% p.a.; cash advance rate 21.99% p.a., charged from the day of the withdrawal; up to 55 interest-free days on purchases; annual fee $99; late payment fee $20; foreign transaction fee 3% of the purchase.

(a) Ella pays $2 400 for accommodation overseas with this card. What fee is charged on this purchase? (b) Her closing balance is $2 472 and she pays $2 000 by the due date. Will she be charged interest? Explain. (c) Explain why withdrawing $200 cash with this card costs more than buying a $200 item with it.

**Worked answer:** (a) 3% × $2 400 = $72.00. (b) Yes: the interest-free period applies only if the whole closing balance ($2 472) is paid by the due date; paying $2 000 is not enough. (c) The cash advance has no interest-free days, so interest is charged from the day of the withdrawal at a higher rate, and a cash advance fee may also apply.

### Question 2

$5 000 is owed on each of three loans: a credit card at 20.99% p.a., a personal loan at 12% p.a. and a car loan at 7% p.a. (a) Using the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12), find the interest charged in one month on each. (b) Explain why a credit card is called a reducing balance loan.

**Worked answer:** (a) $5 000 × 0.2099 ÷ 12 = $87.46; $5 000 × 0.12 ÷ 12 = $50.00; $5 000 × 0.07 ÷ 12 = $29.17. (b) Interest is charged only on the balance still owed each period; each repayment reduces that balance, so later interest is charged on less (new purchases add to it).
