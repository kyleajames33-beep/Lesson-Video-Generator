## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**Teaching order.**

**L15:**

1. An indicator is represented by HIn ⇌ H⁺ + In⁻; the two forms have different colours.
2. High [H⁺] favours HIn and low [H⁺] favours In⁻; a visible transition occurs across roughly pKIn ± 1.
3. The equivalence-point pH depends on the salt: strong acid/strong base about 7, weak acid/strong base above 7, strong acid/weak base below 7.
4. Choose an indicator whose colour transition occurs in the steep section of the titration curve.
5. Weak acid/weak base curves have no sharp jump, so the page prefers a pH probe or conductometric method.
**L17:**

1. Four students interpret one weak acid/strong base curve; the page compares their equivalence point, indicator and pKa decisions.
2. A six-question framework asks for acid/base type, starting concentration and volume, equivalence volume, equivalence pH, pKa and indicator.
3. Read all four curve types using the initial pH, buffer region, jump and salt hydrolysis.
4. Keep experimental endpoint separate from stoichiometric equivalence point.
5. Diagnose five mistakes: forcing equivalence to pH 7, confusing endpoint and equivalence, putting the buffer after equivalence, misreading pKa and choosing phenolphthalein automatically.

**Worked examples and numbers from the extracts.**

**L15:**

- CH₃COOH with NaOH: the page gives equivalence pH about 8.7 and selects phenolphthalein (8.3–10.0). HCl with NH₃: pH about 5.3 and selects methyl orange (3.1–4.4). HCl with NaOH: pH 7 and lists methyl orange, bromothymol blue or phenolphthalein.
- For 25.00 mL of 0.0800 mol/L CH₃COOH (Ka 1.8 × 10⁻⁵) titrated with 0.0800 mol/L NaOH, [CH₃COO⁻] at equivalence is 0.0400 mol/L, Kb 5.56 × 10⁻¹⁰, [OH⁻] 4.71 × 10⁻⁶ mol/L and pH 8.67. The page chooses phenolphthalein.
**L17:**

- Adding 0.0500 mol/L NaOH to 40.00 mL HX gives a jump centred at 20.00 mL. The page identifies a strong acid, gives c(HX) 0.0250 mol/L, equivalence pH about 7 and accepts phenolphthalein.
- 25.00 mL propanoic acid (Ka 1.3 × 10⁻⁵) titrated with 0.100 mol/L NaOH: equivalence at 25.00 mL, pH 8.57; half-equivalence pH 4.89; theoretical starting pH 2.94. Methyl orange at 6.25 mL reports 0.0250 instead of 0.1000 mol/L, a 75% underestimate.
- Equal 20.00 mL portions of 0.100 mol/L HCl and HF (Ka 6.8 × 10⁻⁴), each titrated with 0.100 mol/L NaOH: starting pH 1.00 and 2.10; both equivalence volumes 20.00 mL; equivalence pH 7.00 and 7.93. HF has half-equivalence pH 3.17.

## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes

**L15:**

- An indicator is a passive colour marker; its own equilibrium responds to pH.
- Every equivalence point has pH 7; salt hydrolysis can move it above or below 7.
- Choose the indicator whose range is closest to pH 7; the page says use the steep curve region.
- A weak acid/weak base titration always has a suitable visual indicator; the page says it often lacks a sharp endpoint.
**L17:**

- Equivalence always occurs at pH 7 or at the curve's maximum pH.
- Indicator colour change directly proves stoichiometric equivalence. - pKa is the starting, equivalence or neutral pH rather than half-equivalence pH.
- Methyl orange is suitable for a weak acid titration because its colour is vivid.
- A buffer region appears after equivalence in a weak acid/strong base titration.

These traps come from the linked site lessons. They are not attributed to NESA markers without a specific exam report.
