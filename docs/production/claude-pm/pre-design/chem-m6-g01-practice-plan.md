# Draft for Claude's review: chem-m6-g01 practice plan

Main question: Which pH method fits a strong acid or base after dilution or mixing?

Route: after C07, before C08. Entry knowledge: C05 distinguishes strength and concentration; C06 introduces water and conjugate reactions; C07 teaches complete ionisation, pH, pOH, dilution and mole balance. Stop before weak-acid equilibrium. Source site lessons: Chemistry Module 6 L08 and L11. All questions below are original HSC-style tasks. Use 25 °C and Kw = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ throughout. Each question needs a full stem and data card during a pause with a visible countdown, then one reasoning stage at a time. End each walkthrough with the answer, one or two key reasons and one crossed classic mistake.

## Q1. Concentration before pH (3 marks)

**Question.** A 0.00250 mol L⁻¹ hydrochloric acid solution is prepared at 25 °C. Calculate its pH and explain why its concentration can be used as [H₃O⁺].

**Working.** HCl ionises completely and supplies one H₃O⁺ per HCl, so [H₃O⁺] = 2.50 × 10⁻³ mol L⁻¹. pH = −log₁₀(2.50 × 10⁻³) = **2.60**. A small concentration does not make this a weak acid. Cross out “dilute means weak”.

**Source videos:** chem-m6-c05, chem-m6-c07.

## Q2. Dilution preserves moles (4 marks)

**Question.** A student transfers 25.0 mL of 0.120 mol L⁻¹ HCl to a volumetric flask and makes the solution up to 250.0 mL. Calculate the final pH.

**Working.** n(HCl) = 0.120 × 0.0250 = 0.00300 mol. The flask contains 0.00300 mol in 0.2500 L, so [H₃O⁺] = 0.0120 mol L⁻¹. pH = −log₁₀(0.0120) = **1.92**. Cross out “the acid concentration is still 0.120 mol L⁻¹”.

**Source videos:** chem-m6-c07; chem-m6-c05 for the concentration distinction.

## Q3. Strong acid plus strong base (5 marks)

**Question.** Mix 30.0 mL of 0.100 mol L⁻¹ HCl with 20.0 mL of 0.100 mol L⁻¹ NaOH. Assume the volumes add. Calculate the final pH.

**Working.** n(H₃O⁺) = 0.0300 × 0.100 = 0.00300 mol; n(OH⁻) = 0.0200 × 0.100 = 0.00200 mol. Neutralisation leaves 0.00100 mol H₃O⁺. Total volume = 0.0500 L; [H₃O⁺] = 0.0200 mol L⁻¹. pH = −log₁₀(0.0200) = **1.70**. Cross out “average the two pH values”.

**Source videos:** chem-m6-c02 for neutralisation, chem-m6-c07 for mole balance and pH.

## Q4. A dibasic hydroxide changes the mole count (5 marks)

**Question.** Mix 25.0 mL of 0.0800 mol L⁻¹ Ba(OH)₂ with 35.0 mL of 0.100 mol L⁻¹ HCl. Assume complete ionisation and additive volumes. Calculate the final pH at 25 °C.

**Working.** n(Ba(OH)₂) = 0.0250 × 0.0800 = 0.00200 mol, so n(OH⁻) = 0.00400 mol. n(H₃O⁺) = 0.0350 × 0.100 = 0.00350 mol. Excess OH⁻ = 0.000500 mol in 0.0600 L, giving [OH⁻] = 0.00833 mol L⁻¹. pOH = −log₁₀(0.00833) = 2.08; pH = 14.00 − 2.08 = **11.92**. Cross out “one mole Ba(OH)₂ gives one mole OH⁻”.

**Source videos:** chem-m6-c02, chem-m6-c07.
