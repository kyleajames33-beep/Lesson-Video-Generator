### Site example `we-chem-y11-m1-l13-1`

Source transcription below, including any error flagged in section 3 or the corrections log. Numbers are unchanged.

Evaluate the development from Thomson's plum pudding model to Rutherford's nuclear model, addressing: (a) what evidence Thomson's model explained, (b) what new evidence challenged it, and (c) the key features of Rutherford's model that addressed the new evidence.

1 Thomson's model and what it explained Thomson discovered electrons in 1897 via cathode ray tube experiments. Evidence explained: (1) atoms contain negative particles (electrons), (2) atoms are overall neutral. Thomson's model: positive charge spread uniformly throughout atom; electrons embedded within.
2 New evidence that challenged Thomson's model Rutherford (1909–1911): fired alpha particles at thin gold foil. Expected: minor deflections. Actual: Most passed straight through; ~1 in 8,000 deflected at >90°; ~1 in 20,000 reflected back. Thomson's model CANNOT explain large-angle deflections, a diffuse positive sphere would only produce small deflections.
3 Rutherford's nuclear model addresses the evidence Concentrated positive nucleus explains large-angle and back-scatter deflections. Mostly empty space explains why most alpha particles passed through undeflected. Electrons outside nucleus at large distances explains the overall neutral atom.

## 4. Misconceptions and traps

An energy-level model does not mean electrons radiate continuously while sitting in an allowed state. A spectral line is a transition energy, not a photographed orbit or a unique colour for every ion regardless of flame chemistry.

## 6. Two original candidate questions

### Question 1

Two allowed levels have energies minus 5.0 and minus 2.0 in units of 10^-19 J. An electron moves from the higher to lower level. State absorption or emission and calculate the photon energy.

**Worked answer:** Emission. The positive energy released is (-2.0)-(-5.0)=3.0 in units of 10^-19 J, so 3.0 times 10^-19 J.

### Question 2

An atom has allowed levels E1=0, E2=2 and E3=5 in arbitrary energy units. Which photon energies can it emit in a single downward transition among those levels? Explain why a photon of energy 4 is absent from this model.

**Worked answer:** Allowed positive gaps are 2, 3 and 5 units. There is no pair of these levels with a gap of 4. Actual spectra depend on allowed transitions; this simple model tests energy gaps only.
