## Site sequence and material

Order of teaching sections: What a gene actually is; RNA polymerase, the promoter and the strands; Following the copy out of the nucleus; Reading the message in threes; CFTR, base by base; Sequence and Annotate; CFTR Transcription Reasoning; independent practice; Retrieve and reflect. Before teaching, the page has Orient yourself and a worksheet offer.

- A gene is a DNA sequence with coded information for a product, usually a polypeptide. Polymerase binds a promoter, reads one template strand, and builds a complementary mRNA strand. RNA uses U where DNA uses T. The coding strand matches the RNA sequence except T replaces U. In the page's eukaryotic cell, DNA stays in the nucleus and mRNA carries a temporary message to ribosomes. Codons are three RNA bases. Stop before decoding them into amino acids.
- Worked sequence: DNA template `TAC CGA ATT` gives mRNA `AUG GCU UAA`. Independent check: T to A, A to U, C to G; C to G, G to C, A to U; A to U, T to A, T to A. All nine positions agree. The first and last codons are start and stop, but their translation belongs to B8. The page gives no strand directions, so add 3′ to 5′ on the template and 5′ to 3′ on mRNA if the animation uses arrows.
- Four-step CFTR model: expose a template, complementary RNA bases pair, mRNA separates, codons travel for translation. A changed DNA base may alter an mRNA codon before any protein is made. This is a qualitative example; there is no CFTR variant or measured value to recompute.
- Named misconceptions: one gene always means one protein (alternative splicing can produce several transcripts); both DNA strands copied into one mRNA; DNA leaves the nucleus; mRNA permanently replaces DNA. The near-universal code and Nirenberg and Matthaei's 1961 poly-U experiment appear as context, not a required calculation. The page mentions vertebrate mitochondrial exceptions.
- Questions: write and label the mRNA for `TAC CGA ATT`; explain how a CFTR DNA change alters mRNA; assess mRNA's importance (6 marks); evaluate the code's universality (5 marks); multiple choice, true/false and three short answers totalling 12 marks. Retrieval cues: gene, transcription, mRNA, exam trap. Memory cue is the DNA to temporary RNA copy contrast, not a named mnemonic.

## Checks, exam traps and objects

[NESA 2022 Q31(b) feedback](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2022) specifically notes confusion between these processes and missing uracil or complementary pairing.
