## 1. Exact 2017 syllabus content

**C8-IQ1-02**

> conduct qualitative investigations – using flame tests, precipitation and complexation reactions as appropriate – to test for the presence in aqueous solution of the following ions:
> - cations: barium (Ba2+), calcium (Ca2+), magnesium (Mg2+), lead(II) (Pb2+), silver ion (Ag+), copper(II) (Cu2+), iron(II) (Fe2+), iron(III) (Fe3+)
> - anions: chloride (Cl–), bromide (Br–), iodide (I–), hydroxide (OH–), acetate (CH3COO–), carbonate (CO32–), sulfate (SO42–), phosphate (PO43–)

## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

Order: qualitative versus quantitative; named ions; flame tests; net ionic equations; anion tests; hydroxide precipitates; complexation; test sequence.

No numerical worked calculation. Practice observations: white AgNO₃ precipitate suggests chloride; red-brown NaOH precipitate suggests Fe³⁺; acid effervescence suggests carbonate; pale green flame supports Ba²⁺. Retrieval includes Na⁺ yellow, K⁺ lilac, Ca²⁺ orange-red and Cu²⁺ blue-green. Sodium/potassium examples are background and do not replace the eight named syllabus cations. Practice includes equation/observation pairs and distinguishing ions. No named mnemonic recorded.

## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes

Naming reagent without observation; failing to remove interfering ions; contaminating later tests; flame colour treated as unique proof. Research bank section 1, [NESA2023 Q30](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2023).
