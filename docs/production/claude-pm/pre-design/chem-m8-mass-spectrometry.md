## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

Order: vaporisation, electron ionisation, acceleration, magnetic deflection, detection; molecular ion; fragmentation/base peak; isotope patterns.

Worked example: M⁺64, M+2 at 66 one-third height, base peak 49; C₂H₅³⁵Cl has nominal mass 64, loss CH₃15 leaves CH₂Cl⁺49. Practice parent72/fragment43 gives lost mass 29 (CHO or C₂H₅). Chlorine M:M+2 about 3:1; bromine about 1:1;¹³C about 1.1% creates M+1. Hook 46 and small 47 separates molecular ion from isotope. Nitrogen rule is an extra aid. Memory contrast: molecular ion sets mass; base peak sets 100% abundance reference.

Full MC and short-answer sets remain in the linked pages. The read-only module8/question-bank-data.js supplements them.

## 3. Number and science check

Nominal sums reproduce:24+5+35=64;12+2+35=49;72−43=29. Average chlorine atomic mass 35.45 must not replace isotope mass 35 in nominal m/z. The page's base peak 49 is hypothetical until checked against the actual [NIST chloroethane spectrum](https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/cbook.cgi?ID=C75003&Mask=200). Mass loss 29 alone does not uniquely distinguish C₂H₅ from CHO. No equilibrium/rate constant is used.

## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes

Isotope peak mistaken for parent; tallest peak always called molecular ion; neutral fragments shown as detected ions; subtraction reversed; average atomic mass used for isotope peak. Research section 2, NESA2020 Q21.
