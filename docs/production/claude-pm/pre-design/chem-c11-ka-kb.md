## Exact syllabus words

> explore the use of Keq for different types of chemical reactions, including but not limited to:
> - dissociation of ionic solutions
> - dissociation of acids and bases (ACSCH098, ACSCH099)

## Site order and material

Get oriented/Think First (acetic versus hydrochloric acid); Know/Understand/Can Do and formula/misconception cards; Ka as Keq for dissociation; what Ka and Kb sizes mean; dissolving solid and Ksp; extension ΔG° and K; worked examples; checking understanding; applying knowledge; practice and retrieve. Central rule: construct products-over-reactants expressions, omit solvent water or a pure solid, use stoichiometric powers. For HA ⇌ H⁺ + A⁻, Ka = [H⁺][A⁻]/[HA]. For B + H₂O ⇌ BH⁺ + OH⁻, Kb = [BH⁺][OH⁻]/[B]. The page uses larger Ka to rank weak acids and warns Ka is neither pH nor [H⁺]. It previews Ksp with BaSO₄(s) ⇌ Ba²⁺ + SO₄²⁻. A builder uses five acid expressions, with HF as the explicit check.

Worked 1: HF Ka 6.8 × 10⁻⁴ versus HNO₂ Ka 4.5 × 10⁻⁴, so HF ranks stronger on the page values; ammonia Kb expression omits water; BaSO₄ Ksp about 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁰ omits solid. Worked 2: H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI, K 54.3 at 430 °C (703 K) gives ΔG° = −23.3 kJ mol⁻¹; K 794 at 25 °C (298 K) gives −16.5 kJ mol⁻¹. Extension Haber example asserts K 977 at 298 K and 0.013 at 773 K and obtains ΔG° −17.1 and +27.9 kJ mol⁻¹. Extension acetic acid Ka 1.8 × 10⁻⁵ gives ΔG° about +27 kJ mol⁻¹. Extension check K 2.50 × 10⁻³ at 500 K gives about +24.9 kJ mol⁻¹.

Practice: methanoic acid Ka 1.8 × 10⁻⁴ versus HCN 6.2 × 10⁻¹⁰, write expressions and rank (4 marks); Ag₂CrO₄ ⇌ 2Ag⁺ + CrO₄²⁻, write Ksp and explain why adding solid does not change it (3 marks). The page also asks a pH 2.87, 0.100 mol L⁻¹ weak-acid Ka calculation, Kb for acetate from Ka 1.8 × 10⁻⁵, and [H⁺] from [OH⁻] 2.5 × 10⁻³. These cross the page's stated Module 6 boundary. Activities ask for acid/base/solid expressions and ask how Ka changes when acetic acid is warmed. The last question cannot be answered from Ka at 25 °C or ΔG° = −RT ln K alone; it needs the ionisation enthalpy or temperature data. Memory aid: Ka/Kb/Ksp are named applications of one K expression and one exclusion rule.

## Exam traps, objects and overlap

[NESA 2023 Q35 feedback](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2023) warns against assuming weak-acid concentration is unchanged and asks students to connect acid strength to Ka and pKa rather than merely list data. [NESA 2024 Q39 feedback](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2024) identifies incorrect use of Ka and equilibrium concentrations. Site traps: put water or solid in K; confuse K with rate, pH or concentration; use Celsius in RT.

Objects: vinegar bottle, weak-acid beaker, ammonia solution and ionic crystal. Brief 38 requests vinegar and paired strong/weak acid with magnesium plates, but **no `m5x-*` plates exist yet**. The magnesium gas comparison concerns acid reaction rate and concentration, so confirm it serves this lesson's equilibrium idea before using.
