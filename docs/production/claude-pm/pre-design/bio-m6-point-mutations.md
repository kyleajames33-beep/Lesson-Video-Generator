## 2. Site order, ideas, examples, practice and memory aids

**Teaching order.**

**L03:**

1. A point mutation changes one or a few DNA bases by substitution, insertion or deletion.
2. A substitution may be silent, missense or nonsense, depending on the resulting codon.
3. An insertion or deletion that is not a multiple of three can shift the downstream reading frame.
4. Protein and phenotype effects depend on mutation type, position and the role of the affected sequence.
5. The page traces DNA change to codon, amino acid, protein function and possible phenotype change.

**Worked examples and numbers from the extracts.**

**L03:** The sickle-cell example changes the sixth β-haemoglobin DNA codon from GAG to GTG, replacing glutamic acid with valine. The page links this to haemoglobin polymerisation under low oxygen and red-cell sickling. A sequence example shows normal `ATG | AAA | CCT | GGA | TTT` and a deletion regrouping downstream codons as `ATG | AAC | CTG | GAT | ...`. Its interactive uses a 13-codon HBB excerpt: deleting base 5 introduces an early stop; inserting at position 8 restores the downstream frame while residues between edits remain changed. GAA to GAG remains glutamic acid, so is silent.

**Practice.**

**L03:** Interactive mutation simulator, mutation classification, sequence consequence reasoning, multiple choice and three short answers. Representative: “Distinguish between substitution, insertion and deletion point mutations.” “Explain how a substitution can be silent, missense or nonsense.”

The extracts record no named mnemonic unless shown above. Keep any useful contrast or question from the page; do not manufacture a memory aid.

## 4. Exam traps and student mistakes

**L03:**

- Point mutation means small biological effect. A single base change can have major effects.
- Every insertion or deletion affects only one amino acid. A non-triplet change shifts downstream codons.
- Every mutation destroys a protein. Some substitutions are silent or have limited effects.

These traps come from the linked site lessons. They are not attributed to NESA markers without a specific exam report.
