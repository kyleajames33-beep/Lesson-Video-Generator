## Brief 149 v14: net force, graph intercept and proportionality

## Brief 149 v20: equilibrium terminology and normal force

**Source:** `fa1-fundamentals-of-mechanics/lesson19.sa.html`, key terms, card 3 and misconception 2.

**Exact old text:** “A static (equilibrium) object has zero acceleration ... whether it is at rest or moving at constant velocity” and “N = mg only on level ground with no vertical acceleration.”

**Correction:** Equilibrium includes rest and constant velocity; static means at rest. On a level surface with no vertical acceleration and no other vertical force, N=mg; an angled pull changes N. ## Brief 149 v24: incline graph, reliability and inference

**Source:** `fa2-waves/lesson03.sa.html`, card 1, card 2 `we-1`, and card 3. **Exact old text:** “a compression is the longitudinal equivalent of a crest”; “In a sound wave travelling along a tube, successive compressions are 0.40 m apart, and 170 compressions pass a point each second”; “A larger amplitude means particles are pushed further, so compressions are denser”.

**Correction:** Identify the crest analogy as a pressure or density graph, not a displacement graph. State same medium and frequency when comparing displacement amplitude and compression strength. The tube values imply 68 m/s; if air near room temperature is intended, revise the values or name the medium and conditions. Without those conditions the numbers alone do not prove an error.

## Brief 156 v05: wave speed depends on conditions

**Source:** `fa2-waves/lesson04.sa.html`, card 3 and activity 1(2). **Exact old text:** “In one medium the wave speed is set by the medium, so v stays constant”; “A water wave of wavelength 0.5 m travels at 1.5 m s⁻¹”.

**Correction:** Identify fixed-tension string or another nondispersive example for the constant-speed trade-off; surface water-wave speed can vary with wavelength and depth. The numerical water example gives 3 Hz and 1/3 s by definition. If still-water gravity waves are intended, state that model and choose compatible values; surface tension, currents and other conditions are not specified, so the stated numbers alone do not establish an impossibility.

## Brief 156 v06: time-graph peak ambiguity

**Source:** `fa2-waves/lesson05.sa.html`, short-answer Q2. **Exact old text:** “A displacement–time graph for one particle in a wave shows crests at t = 0.2 s and t = 0.8 s”.

**Correction:** Say the crests are successive, or show the intervening trace. Otherwise their separation could be multiple periods and the stated 0.6 s period is not determined by the words alone.

## Brief 156 v07: compressions on a displacement graph

**Source:** `fa2-waves/lesson06.sa.html`, card 3. **Exact old text:** “its peaks line up with the compressions”.

**Correction:** In the sinusoidal displacement snapshot illustrated, the compression centres lie at zero crossings where neighbouring displacements converge; the pressure peaks coincide with these centres. The card's later paragraph already describes the converging crossings. For a general profile the local compression depends on the spatial displacement gradient.

## Brief 156 v09: sound-speed explanation and example conditions

**Source:** `fa2-waves/lesson08.sa.html`, learning intentions and cards 2–3. **Exact old text:** “the more tightly packed and strongly bonded the particles are, the faster they relay the energy” and “Because the speed depends on v = fλ in that medium”.

**Correction:** Sound speed depends on both the appropriate elastic stiffness and density; density alone does not establish which medium transmits sound faster. v=fλ relates speed, frequency and wavelength; it is not the physical mechanism setting the speed. Q3's “a few micrometres” needs an identified sound level, frequency and location or should be a qualitative small-displacement statement.

## Brief 156 v10: microphone traces and perceived loudness

**Source:** `fa2-waves/lesson09.sa.html`, hook, oscilloscope key term and worked example. **Exact old text:** “The double bass and piccolo can share the same loudness (amplitude)” and “An instrument that displays a sound wave as a displacement–time trace”.

**Correction:** A microphone and oscilloscope display a voltage related to local pressure, not particle displacement; state calibration when converting it to pressure. Equal perceived loudness at different frequencies does not require equal amplitude, and a taller trace at another frequency alone does not determine perceived loudness. Compare equal-frequency tones or measure loudness appropriately. The fixed-amplitude energy condition is already logged in `concept-three`.
