## Main question and useful outcome

Main question: *When a question asks you to evaluate, assess or justify, what does a judgement need so that it earns marks?* There is one question, carried throughout. Useful outcome: a student who knows the content writes a verdict, names the criteria, ties each criterion to evidence from the question, weighs a limitation and closes the loop. The owner's brief is served: judgement questions are where knowing the content is least enough, and the video names the two failing shapes (announcing, listing). The worked case (calcium carbonate against sodium hydroxide for an acid spill) reuses C04's base-choosing criteria in a new setting, so only the judgement writing is new.

## Trace from entry knowledge

1. Hook: "10 of 17" large question parts, then 7 of 12 reports, gives a clear reason to care. The count is correct (see the science check).
2. Four verbs: clear paraphrases, labelled; analyse is defined and set aside, as root decided.
3. and 4. Card and reading: the five-row table is the heaviest card in the series. The pulses and row-label underlines help; unused rows need to dim during the build (M1). Scene 4's cue word does not match the narration (M3).
5. Three parts: verdict, criteria, evidence, with the three criteria named for this card. The memory aid is short and maps onto the strip. Good.
6. and 7. Build: each line carries a role tag, a table arrow and a tick, and the balance beam tips per criterion. This is the strongest visual teaching in the video: weighing is shown, not described. The limitation line is justified by a claim about the glossary that the glossary does not make (S3).
8. Weak against strong: convincing. The weak answer has a verdict and a "because", so students see why that is not enough. 1 of 4 (root decision) is labelled illustrative and the lost-marks tag is specific.
9. Guidelines: accurate examples, but the closing sentence generalises beyond them (S4).
10. Assess against evaluate: honest about the overlap. The justify sentence makes an unsupported "often" claim, and the guideline example has no stated takeaway (S2). Three ideas in about 36 s of speech crowd the screen (M2).
11. to 14. Drills: each practises the taught shape. Drill 1 (justify a buffer pair) and drill 2 (assess a claim with "always") both keep every demand, value and the claim visible during "Pause here to answer." and the 10 s countdown. Nothing answer-bearing appears. Drill 1's pKa differs from the value students met in C10 and C11 (S5). Both feedbacks run over 30 s (S1).
15. and 16. Decision rule and notes: separate from new reasoning, with a quiet copy hold. Notes 3 and 4 need the same hedges as scenes 7 and 9 (S3, S4).

## Findings

No blockers.

**S1 (should fix). Scenes 12 and 14: feedback length.**
Quoted (scene 12, end): "The habit to drop: "ethanoic acid, because its pKa is closer." Why does closer matter? Say it." (429 characters, about 32 s). Scene 14 is 447 characters (about 34 s).
Problem: over the owner's 15 to 30 s band.
Replacement (scene 12, end): "The habit to drop: "its pKa is closer," with no reason why closer matters." Scene 14: "Mutation one gives G A G, still glutamic acid, ..." and "The habit to drop: labelling them silent and nonsense, then stopping. That never judges the claim." Both land at about 30 s.

**S2 (should fix). Scene 10: unsupported "often", and a guideline example with no takeaway.**
Quoted: "**Justify** is the same shape, often with the verdict handed to you. In one recent justify question, identifying two changes earned the same as fully justifying **one**."
Problem: the pack does not support "often". In the cited Chemistry 2025 Q23, in Chemistry 2024 Q31 and in this video's own drill 1, the student makes the choice. The student is also left asking "so what?" after the guideline example.
Replacement: "**Justify** is the same shape: whether the conclusion is given or you choose it, your job is the support. In one recent justify question, identifying two changes earned the same as fully justifying **one**. The reason was worth as much as a second change." Chip: "justify → given or chosen, your job is the support".
Understanding enabled: a reason is worth marks in its own right, so every choice needs its "because".

**S3 (should fix). Scene 7 and key note 3: advice presented as the glossary meaning.**
Quoted: "Now weigh a limitation, because evaluate means judging both ways".
Problem: the glossary paraphrase is "judge against criteria or determine value". Weighing both sides is good practice (the Biology 2022 Q27 sample answer does it), but it is not what the word means.
Replacement: "Now weigh a limitation, because a fair evaluation looks both ways". Key note 3: "Evaluate: judge against criteria; weighing strengths against limitations is the safe habit (HSCScience tip)."

**S4 (should fix). Scene 9 and key note 4: two guidelines turned into a general rule.**
Quoted: "So reasons with no verdict lose marks, and a verdict without reasons earns **little**."
Problem: the line generalises from two questions; the pack says band differences are question-specific. Biology 2025 Q34(b) separates "describes" from "evaluates" and has no separate judgement criterion.
Replacement: "In both, answers without a judgement stopped short of the top, and no band paid for a verdict on its **own**." Key note 4: "A verdict on its own earns little, and reasons without a verdict can miss the top marks. Give both."

**S5 (should fix). Drill 1: pKa inconsistent with the prerequisite videos.**
Quoted: "Ethanoic acid has a pKa of four point seven six." Card: "4.76".
Problem: C10 and C11 teach ethanoic acid's pKa as 4.74 (from Ka 1.8 x 10^-5). A student who has just watched C11 will ask which value is right. The choice and reasoning are unchanged.
Replacement: 4.74 on the card, in speech ("four point seven four"), in feedback, on the number line and in the model answer. NH4+ stays 9.25, as in C11's attempt A1.

**M1 (minor). Scenes 3, 6 and 7: five-row table load.**
During the build, keep only the table row in use bright; the others sit dimmed.

**M2 (minor). Scene 10 density.**
Collapse the evaluate and assess chips to their verb words before the justify chip and bar arrive.

**M3 (minor). Scene 4 cue.**
Quoted: "On "copying them" a ghost answer line ..." The narration says "don't just copy them". Change the cue to "copy them".

**M4 (minor). Open decisions.**
Root has decided placement (after C11 and B03), the weak-answer mark (1 of 4), analyse coverage (defined, not drilled) and "10 of 17" (kept with its tag). Record them as decided.

## Retained strengths

The role tags and balance beam make the shape of a judgement visible as it is written. The verdict's tick waits until the last line supports it, which teaches "earn the verdict" without a speech about it. The weak answer has a verdict and a "because", so the lesson goes beyond "add a because". Drill 2's "always" is a well-chosen trap that rewards judging the claim, not labelling the mutations. Every question carries the HSCScience tag, and every tally is labelled illustrative.
