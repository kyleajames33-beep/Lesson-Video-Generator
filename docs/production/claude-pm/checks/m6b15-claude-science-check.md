## Facts confirmed (no change)

- **One bull, tens of thousands of calves, many countries.** Widely used AI sires have tens of thousands (some hundreds of thousands) of daughters across many countries (Interbull and herd-book records). Correct.
- **Collection, dilution, straws, liquid nitrogen.** One ejaculate is extended into several hundred 0.25 or 0.5 mL straws and stored at minus 196 degrees Celsius. "Diluted into hundreds of doses and frozen in thin straws in liquid nitrogen": correct.
- **Keeps for decades.** Live offspring have been produced from semen stored for about fifty years (for example, University of Sydney lambs in 2019 from ram semen frozen in 1968; bull semen from the 1960s has been used). "Keeps for decades": correct.
- **Pollen viability.** Wheat pollen survives minutes to about half an hour in the field; maize up to about two hours; tomato for days when cool; many species can be dried and frozen. "The pollen of many crops survives only hours unless it's specially dried and frozen": correct with "many".
- **Insemination timing and placement.** Semen is placed in the uterine body just past the cervix during heat (bio-m5-b03b facts, resolved in its Codex check). "Places it in her uterus while she's on heat": correct.
- **"No bull is there, so no other sperm can arrive."** Correct for the inseminated cycle in controlled AI (a later clean-up bull is a separate cycle).
- **Emasculation and bagging; tomato and wheat self-pollination.** Wheat is predominantly self-pollinating (cleistogamous florets often shed pollen before opening); cultivated tomato is largely self-pollinating, the style held within the anther cone, and crosses use emasculation of the bud before anthesis, hand pollination and bagging or tagging. "Tomato flowers usually pollinate themselves": correct.
- **Embryo transfer (scene 7).** In MOET a selected cow is superovulated, inseminated, and her embryos are flushed and transferred to recipient cows, so she can have many more calves than she could carry. "Embryo transfer lets one prize cow have many calves, carried by other cows, so leaning on a few mothers narrows a breed too": correct (heavy use of few dams lowers effective size).
- **Consequences of few sires.** Drift and the rate of inbreeding (about 1/(2Ne) per generation) rise; relatedness rises; recessive alleles from a widely used sire appear homozygous more often. Correct.
- **Holstein disorders.** As finding 8. Correct as narrated.
- **Crossing makes new combinations; one variety everywhere narrows.** bio-m5-b03b and b12. Correct.
- **Attempt 2 data.** Hypothetical and labelled; the trend matches published falls in Holstein effective population size. Correct as a hypothetical.
- **Absolute words checked.** "Neither changes any DNA" (correct for both methods as defined); "every calf has one of five fathers" (true by construction); "no other sperm can arrive" (correct for the cycle); "only raises the chance" (removed in Revision 2's wording). No "always" or "completely"; no discontinued-product claims.
- **Memory aids.** "Same aim, different reach" (site L14): correct for one parent's gametes, with the stated limit for finished varieties. "Count the fathers, not the cows": correct under Ne = 4NmNf/(Nm + Nf), with the stated relatedness limit.
- **Em dashes:** none in the design.
