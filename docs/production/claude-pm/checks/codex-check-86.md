# Brief 86: Revision 2 error check

## chem-m6-c05

1. **Scene 2 particle lens, line 63:** The right lens draws one split acetic acid molecule among about 40 while its counter says "about 1 in 80". A student can count 1 in 40, which is twice the stated fraction. **Corrected visual:** Draw one split among about 80, or use a non-countable schematic and keep the "about 1 in 80" counter. The stated pH 2.9 gives [H₃O⁺] = 10⁻²·⁹ = 0.00126 mol/L, or about 1.26% of 0.10 mol/L acid, approximately 1 in 79. Source: the design's pH and concentration data, with the [NIST acetic acid dissociation measurements](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/30/jresv30n5p347_A1b.pdf).

2. **Scene 7 particle grid, line 88 and author note b:** The concentrated weak panel draws 2 of about 40 molecules split, or 5%, although its stated 1.0 mol/L acetic acid example ionises about 0.42%. The dilute weak panel draws 3 of about 12 split, or 25%, although the stated 0.0010 mol/L example ionises about 12.5%. The first error is over tenfold and makes the strong versus weak model misleading. **Corrected visual:** Make the grid schematic without countable molecule fractions, or draw panels whose proportions match the stated concentrations closely enough to preserve the difference. Source: [NIST acetic acid dissociation measurements](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/30/jresv30n5p347_A1b.pdf); using the design's Ka = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵ gives approximately 0.42% and 12.5%.

## bio-m6-b05

1. **Attempt 1 feedback, lines 111 and 113:** "Its offspring are normal" and "offspring normal" go beyond the given information. The somatic eye mutation cannot pass from fly A through its gametes, but that does not establish that its offspring are otherwise normal. **Corrected text:** "Its offspring cannot inherit the white-eye patch from this mutation." Source: [NSW Biology Stage 6 Syllabus (2017)](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/science/biology-stage-6-2017), somatic and germ-line mutation dotpoint; the question's stated cell lineage.

2. **Attempt 2 feedback and notes, lines 121, 123 and 147:** CUU and CUC both code for leucine, so Q preserves that amino acid, but the script concludes that "the enzyme is unchanged". A synonymous substitution can alter splicing, expression or protein folding, so the codon chart alone does not establish unchanged enzyme amount or function. The 4-mark assessment also cannot prove that P is more significant without information about Q's actual effect. **Corrected text:** "Q does not change the amino acid at this position. P reduces mRNA to about one tenth. On the evidence given, P has the clearer effect, so the student's claim that Q matters more because it is coding is unsupported." Keep the question's judgement tied to the evidence. Source: [Kimchi-Sarfaty et al., Science 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17185560/), a primary study of a synonymous change affecting protein function; [BRCA1 synonymous substitution and splicing study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3353946/).

## bio-m6-g01

The question card correctly says the sequences are illustrative; Crick and colleagues studied genetic revertants in bacteriophage T4, not these sequenced mRNA rows. **Corrected text:** "find a deletion and an insertion in an illustrative sequence inspired by Crick's frameshift experiment". Source: [Crick, Barnett, Brenner and Watts-Tobin, Nature 1961](https://www.nature.com/articles/1921227a0).
