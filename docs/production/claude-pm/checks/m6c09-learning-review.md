## Main question and the useful outcome

Main question (as stated): "Swap hydrochloric acid for a weak acid in a neutralisation and you might expect less heat. Ethanoic acid gives almost the same heat, and hydrofluoric acid gives more. What really decides the heat a weak acid releases?"

One main question: yes. Every scene serves "what decides the heat": the particle scene (3) removes the "incomplete reaction" answer, the Hess scene (4) locates the difference in one step, scenes 5 to 8 explain the sign of that step, and scene 9 closes the wrong inference (heat ranks strength). The ammonia scene (8) is the mirror case and stays (project manager decision 3). Useful outcome: a student can explain why a weak acid still reacts completely, write the enthalpy of neutralisation as the ionisation step plus about −57 kJ/mol, explain why that step can absorb or release energy, calculate the step from measured data with the right sign, and say why heat cannot rank acid strength.

## Trace from the entry knowledge (student starting Year 12 Module 6)

Entry: C03 (H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O, about −57 kJ/mol, q = mcΔT, ΔH = −q/n), C05 (weak acids mostly whole), C06 (NH₃ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻), Module 5 (Le Chatelier, hydration energy from C11, Ka from C10), Year 11 Hess's law. The trace follows ethanoic acid from the hook to attempt 1.

1. Hook: the "less heat?" prediction is crossed by two real values in opposite directions. A genuine surprise. The HF bar carries the "never used in school" tag (decision 2). Bright at the end: heading, three labelled bars, the HF tag and the dashed line (S3).
2. Same and different: the C03 callback is one sentence; "most of its molecules are still whole" is the right link. Good.
3. Still reacts completely: causal (hydroxide removes hydronium, the equilibrium shifts right, more molecules ionise) and visible (the counter falls to 0). The crossed "reacts incompletely" chip is earned. The "⇌ shifts right" tag stays bright beside the strip, counter and crossed chip (S3).
4. Two steps: Hess's law is named with its meaning ("the total doesn't depend on the route"). **A careful student will ask in attempt 1(b): "How do I get step one from a measured value?"** The scene only adds forward ("minus fifty seven, plus the ionisation step"); scenes 6 and 8 also add forward. Attempt 1(b) needs the rearrangement, and its crossed mistake is the subtraction order, so the order must be shown before the attempt (S1). "about minus fifty seven, exactly as for a strong acid" pairs "about" with "exactly" (M2).
5. Either way: energy in (a bond breaks) against energy out (water surrounds the new ions), with the Module 5 hydration callback. Causal and in plain words. Good.
6. Ethanoic acid: the two bars cancel; "a foam cup experiment can hardly tell them apart" is useful. Good.
7. HF: the small fluoride ion and its crowded water shell carry the cause. Good. The ledger, close-up, staircase, net label, chip and a pulsing scene 1 bar are all up together (S3).
8. Ammonia: the mirror case works, and the staircase change (step 1 up) is visible. On screen: the bottle label, a four-bar chart, the strip and the staircase (S3). The staircase levels are not specified for the base case (science check M3).
9. Heat is not strength: the methanoic against ethanoic pair (tenfold Ka, same heat) is a strong contrast and a correct use of Module 5's Ka. Two cards, two bars, a crossed chip with caption and a thumbnail tag are bright together (S3).
10. Memory aid: "Same last step, different first step." is reconstructable and never claims a direction. Good.
11 to 14. Attempts and feedback: see below. Attempt 2's supplied +43 kJ/mol is off the −57 convention (S2).
15. Decision rule: four lines, each with its condition. Good.
16. Key notes: useful and small; note 4 mixes three examples with the strength rule (M4); note 3 should carry the rearranged form (S1).

## Findings

### S1. Should fix: show how to find the ionisation step from a measured value before attempt 1 needs it (scene 4, key note 3)

- Exact text (scene 4): "The measured enthalpy is the sum: **minus fifty seven, plus the ionisation step**. So the whole difference comes from step one."
- Student question (attempt 1(b)): "I have −56 and −57. Which do I subtract from which?"
- Replacement: end scene 4 with "So the whole difference comes from step one, and to find it, take the **measured value, take away minus fifty seven**." with a second line "ΔH(step 1) = ΔH(measured) − (−57) kJ/mol" writing under the first, which dims. Key note 3 adds "so ΔH(ionisation step) = ΔH(measured) − (−57 kJ/mol)".
- Understanding enabled: the attempt tests applying the rearranged relation to new data, and the crossed subtraction-order slip refers to a rule the video has shown.

### S2. Should fix: attempt 2 supplies an ionisation value off the −57 convention (project manager decision 1)

- Exact text (scene 13 and its card): "its ionisation absorbs about forty three kilojoules per mole"; feedback "about **minus fourteen**".
- Problem: +43 is the 25 °C value (against −55.8). On the −57 convention the HCN step is about +45 (−12.3 − (−57) = +44.7), and the measured HCN neutralisation is about −12. A student who later meets −12 kJ/mol in a data table finds the video's answer wrong by 2. Details in the science check S1.
- Replacement: supply "about 45 kJ/mol"; answer "about −12 kJ/mol"; crossed "−57 − 45 = −102 kJ/mol".

### S3. Should fix: more than about three bright text items in scenes 1, 3, 7, 8 and 9

- Scene 1: the cup labels and readouts dim with the cups; the HF tag dims when its sentence ends; the chart counts as one item.
- Scene 3: the "⇌ shifts right" arrow fades when the counter reaches 0.
- Scene 7: the ledger and close-up dim when the step 1 arrow flips; drop the recalled scene 1 HF bar (the net arrow carries −68).
- Scene 8: drop the fourth bar; a value chip "HCl + NH₃: about −52 kJ per mol NH₃" merges into the staircase net label; the strip and the bottle label dim once the staircase draws.
- Scene 9: the bars sit inside the cards; the crossed chip dims when the pH thumbnail appears.
- Understanding enabled: during each new step the student sees the current case, the current arrow and one supporting fact.

### M1. Minor: units on the q line and underline both assumptions (feedback 1)

- Replacement: "m = 100.0 mL × 1.00 g/mL = 100.0 g", then "q = 100.0 g × 4.18 J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹ × 6.7 °C = 2800.6 J"; underline "1.00 g/mL" and "4.18 J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹" with the volumes and ΔT.

### M2. Minor: "about minus fifty seven, exactly as for a strong acid" (scene 4)

- Replacement: "about minus fifty seven, the same as for a strong acid".

### M3. Minor: feedback 2 names a mechanism that scene 5 refined

- Exact text: "is **used up** pulling protons off the hydrogen cyanide molecules".
- Problem: scene 5 taught that the step is the balance of bond breaking and hydration, so "pulling protons off" alone is the half-story.
- Replacement: "is **used up** by the ionisation step of hydrogen cyanide".

### M4. Minor: key note 4 mixes the examples with the strength rule

- Replacement: note 4 keeps the three examples; a new note 5 reads "Neutralisation heat does not rank acid strength: use pH or Ka."
