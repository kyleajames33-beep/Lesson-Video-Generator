## Findings

1. **Blocker. Exact SDS/calcium method and guaranteed clear result.** Five 0.05 mL drops of 1% w/v SDS supply 2.5 mg or 8.67 µmol. In 10.25 mL final volume this is 0.846 mM SDS with 9.756 mM calcium. The solution is not still 1% SDS. Original research reports calcium dodecyl sulfate's low room-temperature solubility, a Krafft point near 50 °C and precipitation governed by calcium and surfactant concentrations below the CMC. The 1998 paper reports Ksp = 3.72 × 10⁻¹⁰ M³ at 25 °C; the initial concentration product here is about 6.98 × 10⁻⁹ M³, nearly 19 times that value. This simple comparison is a screening estimate, not a complete activity/micelle calculation, but it directly rules out treating "stays clear" as a validated outcome. Pilot the exact formulation and temperature before promising the clear, tall-froth, >10-minute detergent result. Revise agent, hardness or data from actual observations if needed. [Hornfeck et al., 1998, first page](https://www.nbi.dk/~kell/publ/1998_acsLangmuir_SwollenLAM.pdf), [Baviere et al., 1983](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0021979783901790), [precipitation study](https://www.jmaterenvironsci.com/Document/vol5/vol5_N1/7-JMES-369-2013-ElAbbassi.pdf).
2. **Should fix. Scenes 2 and 17, notes 1.** Calcium chloride does not change "only calcium ions"; it adds twice as much chloride and changes ionic strength. Use "a known amount of calcium chloride creates the hard-water condition". Identify calcium as the proposed cause of stearate precipitation from its chemistry; a matched sodium-chloride comparison can test nonspecific salt effects if the claim is that calcium alone caused every measured difference. Do not claim a control proves the entire mechanism merely because the salt is known.
3. **Should fix. Scenes 7 to 8.** Timing an oil layer measures emulsion stability, not cleaning itself. A large emulsion droplet coated with surfactant should not be described as an ordinary micelle without distinguishing their scales. Use "surfactant coats oil droplets and helps stop them joining" for the animated oil test. Small micelles and micellar oil solubilisation can coexist, but this test does not identify them separately. [OpenStax colloids](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/11-5-colloids).
4. **Should fix. A1.** The observations establish calcium OR magnesium hardness, not a unique calcium precipitate. Accept the corresponding magnesium-stearate equation, or state that Y contains calcium before demanding the calcium equation. Repair "only once the calcium is gone" to "once enough hardness ions have been removed for free soap to remain"; a lasting froth endpoint does not prove literally zero dissolved calcium.
5. **Optional. Risk table concentration labels.** H319 for solid calcium chloride and the listed seven solid-SDS codes are supported; the in-use calcium chloride is about 0.11% w/v and stock SDS is 1% before further dilution. State which classifications concern the solid versus working solution. Eye protection remains sensible, but solid H codes are not automatically the exact classification of every dilute tube. A product-specific 1% SDS SDS and soap formulation were not obtained, so the exact dilute classification is not passed.

## Recalculations and chemistry

Means: (5.8+6.2+6.0)/3 = 6.0 cm; (0.5+0.8+0.5)/3 = 0.6; (6.3+6.6+6.3)/3 = 6.4; (6.0+6.3+6.0)/3 = 6.1. Ranges are 0.4, 0.3, 0.3, 0.3 cm. A1 excess drops 17 - 2 = 15. The illustrative symbol count 10 - 6 = 4 is arithmetically right and must stay labelled illustrative, not measured molecule counts.

Anhydrous CaCl₂ M = 110.98 g/mol gives 1.1098 g/L at 0.01 M. Calcium is 400.8 mg/L; hardness is 1000.9 mg/L as CaCO₃. The stated 350 mg/L calcium converts to about 874 mg/L as CaCO₃. Ten millilitres holds 100 µmol calcium; 2.5 mg sodium stearate is 8.16 µmol and needs 4.08 µmol calcium to precipitate, so calcium is greatly in excess. If using CaCl₂·2H₂O instead, the weighed mass must change; the design correctly specifies anhydrous for its 1.1 g figure.

2C₁₇H₃₅COO⁻ + Ca²⁺ → (C₁₇H₃₅COO)₂Ca conserves atoms and has net charge zero. Hardness from calcium/magnesium and soap scum formation are correct. [USGS hardness](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/hardness-water), [RSC hard-water practical](https://edu.rsc.org/experiments/how-can-hardness-in-water-be-removed/1734.article). USGS categories put >180 mg/L as CaCO₃ in very hard water; 0.01 M is an intentionally extreme test, not typical tap water.

No primary source validates the exact fictional froth heights, 40 s water-control separation, 2 min soap-hard separation or >10 min others for this low dose and these particular tubes. Labelled example data can illustrate analysis, but cannot be used as proof that the recipe produces them. Match tube diameter and capacity as well as volume: froth height is geometry-dependent. Part B should use fresh matched samples or explicitly standardise the prior Part A history for every tube.

## Safety and site

Solid SDS H228/H302/H315/H318/H332/H335/H412 matches [Merck 112533](https://www.sigmaaldrich.com/US/en/product/mm/112533). Calcium chloride solid H319 matches [Safe Work Australia](https://hcis.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/HazardousChemical/Details?chemicalID=800). Cooking oil and ordinary magnesium sulfate are not usually classified hazardous; the exact soap formulation and additives remain unspecified. Glasses, sound tubes, secured stoppers, spill cleanup and local waste instructions are appropriate. CSIS's generic inorganic-salt PPE and spill advice is at printed p 50, but it does not classify every 1% surfactant or independently authorise sink disposal. [NSW CSIS](https://education.nsw.gov.au/content/dam/main-education/asset-management/chemical-safety/5._Volume_2_Appendices.pdf).

L17 exact local passages were checked. The interactive dissolving/emulsifying contradiction and universal "gentler on skin" comparison merit correction. "No significant intermolecular interactions" is too absolute for water/oil; explaining the balance of interactions is better. However, 350 mg/L Ca²⁺ is a possible concentration, not mathematically wrong merely because it is very high. Do not silently relabel it 350 mg/L as CaCO₃, which changes the data by a factor of 2.5. Keep this as a reporting-convention note, not a confirmed error. Proposed log rows are separate. The site's Q8 uses a sulfonate detergent and an evaluation scenario, not this drop-count unknown or unequal-dose diagnosis; no identical complete task found in those comparisons. No all-paper originality guarantee.
