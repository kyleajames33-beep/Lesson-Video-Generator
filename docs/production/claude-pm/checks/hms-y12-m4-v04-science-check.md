## Confirmed
- Root rulings applied: "lactate threshold" only (the site's "anaerobic threshold" as one silent chip); about 85 to 90 per cent of maximum heart rate in trained athletes equated with Year 11's 70 to 80 per cent of maximum aerobic capacity; aerobic zone 70 to 85 per cent of maximum heart rate; every percentage names its maximum. Key note 1 consistent.
- Methods (3): continuous, fartlek, aerobic interval (longer and slightly easier than HIIT, rest shorter than work) and circuit. Correct.
- Volume-driven adaptations (4): capillaries, mitochondrial size and number, aerobic enzymes, plasma volume, stroke volume. Correct. Hard-every-day effects (volume falls, quality falls, injury and illness risk) are consistent with the intensity-distribution literature (Seiler, *Int J Sports Physiol Perform* 2010).
- Aerobic base for sprint sports and 8d: phosphocreatine resynthesis is oxygen-dependent and faster in aerobically trained athletes (Tomlin and Wenger, *Sports Med* 2001); 30 s refills only about half. Correct.
- Interference effect (6) (Wilson and others, *J Strength Cond Res* 2012). Correct.
- 8a/8b: fartlek for the umpire, one sub-threshold interval session for the jogger; marking accepts alternatives. Correct.
- Memory aids sound. No people.

## Findings
1. **Should fix (line 53, scene 2).** Current: "Below the **aerobic training threshold**, roughly seventy per cent of maximum heart rate, the stimulus is too small to force adaptation. You're moving, but not training." Stated absolutely, this is wrong for beginners (ACSM counts moderate intensity from about 64 per cent of maximum heart rate, and light intensity improves fitness in deconditioned people; ACSM, *Guidelines for Exercise Testing and Prescription*, 11th ed., 2021) and sits awkwardly with scene 4, where much useful endurance volume is easy. Corrected (keeps the root ruling's 70 per cent): "Below the aerobic training threshold, roughly seventy per cent of maximum heart rate, the stimulus is usually too small to keep improving the fitness of someone who already trains." "You're moving, but not training." can stay only after this qualification, or be cut.
2. **Should fix (line 68, scene 5).** Current: "A two-thousand-metre rower works just above the threshold, so aerobic intervals near it, limited by their cost." A 2,000 m race (about 6 to 7 min) is rowed near maximal aerobic capacity, well above the lactate threshold, with roughly 70 to 85 per cent of the energy aerobic (Secher, *Sports Med* 1993). Corrected: "A two-thousand-metre rower races well above the threshold, close to maximum aerobic capacity, so aerobic intervals near and above it, limited by their cost."
3. **Note (line 53).** Lactate rises above resting values well below the 85 to 90 per cent point; the point the root ruling names is the upper (maximal steady state) threshold. "build up faster than they're cleared, so the effort can't last" describes that point correctly.

## Verdict
Pass with fixes. 0 blockers, 2 should fix, 1 note.
