## Findings

1. **Should fix, scene 4 and key note 3 give a strength threshold of "about 85 per cent" of one-rep max.** Scene 4 defines a training threshold as "the minimum intensity that produces a training effect", then says "from about eighty-five you build maximal strength", and the key note writes "strength ~85% 1RM". A student copies that loads under 85 per cent do not build strength, which v03 contradicts (hypertrophy at 65 to 85 per cent) and which is false for beginners, who gain strength from much lighter loads. The scale also skips v03's middle band, so a student asks "what does 75 per cent do?" Fix: draw three bands on the strength scale (endurance under about 65, growth 65 to 85, maximal strength 85 and above, grey tag "earlier"), and say "Strength has thresholds too: below about sixty-five per cent you mostly build endurance, and for maximal strength in trained lifters, it's about eighty-five. Beginners get stronger on much lighter loads, because their threshold is lower." Key note 3: "Thresholds: aerobic ~70% max HR; maximal strength ~85% 1RM (trained). Individual, moving." Students should see the strength threshold as relative to the athlete, as the memory card says.

2. **Should fix, 8c's "sixty per cent" sits inside Year 11's aerobic zone.** 8d says that at 60 per cent the walk is "below the aerobic threshold". hms-y11-m2-v12 (Revision 2) teaches "the aerobic training zone, sixty to eighty-five per cent", so a student who did Year 11 says 60 per cent is still training and loses the second mark. Either root aligns Year 11 v12 to 70 to 85 per cent (as v13 and v04 use; see the v04 review), or change 8c here to "only reaches fifty-five per cent of maximum heart rate", which is below either floor. Both stem and feedback name "maximum heart rate" in full. Students should get one answer from either year's teaching.

3. **Should fix, both halves of 8a are the body's own examples in new sports.** Scene 5 says "A hundred-metre sprinter doing only long runs fails that [energy system]"; 8a's 400 m runner who only does long easy runs is the same failure. Scene 3 says "If load, volume and frequency all rise in the same week and the athlete breaks down, you can't tell which change did it"; 8a's swimmer who adds sessions, gym load and shorter recoveries in one week is that sentence. Replace with cases the body has not stated, for example A: "a tennis player whose conditioning is all straight-line running" (specificity, movement pattern: tennis moves sideways and changes direction) and B: "a swimmer who doubles weekly distance in one week, changing nothing else" (overload too fast even with one variable; the 10 per cent guide). The demand and marks stay the same. Students should find the breached principle and its dimension themselves.

4. **Should fix, "anaerobic threshold" and internal video numbers.** Scene 4 says "Above the anaerobic threshold" and "you met the two thresholds in v-four", and shows grey tags "v04" and "v05". Root's Module 2 ruling uses "lactate threshold" only (see the v04 blocker), and students never see file ids. Say "Above the lactate threshold" and "you met the two thresholds last time"; use the grey tag "earlier".

5. **Should fix, scene 4 is dense and the unit for the first threshold is easy to lose.** About 830 characters, with the definition, the aerobic floor and ceiling, a strength scale, "same idea, different units", individuality and the 8 km jog. After finding 1 adds a sentence, split at "Thresholds belong to the athlete" into 4a (the two scales) and 4b (individual and moving, the 8 km jog, floor for one adaptation), with a 1 s settle on the aligned scales. No other narration change.

6. **Note, the hook asks for two answers and a change with a one-second hold.** Add 2 s with the slots lit.

7. **Note, the 10 per cent guide is a rule of thumb.** It is spoken as "a common guide", which is right; the science check should confirm the wording.
