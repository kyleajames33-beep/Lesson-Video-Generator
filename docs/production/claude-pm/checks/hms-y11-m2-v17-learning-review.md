The 45 of 50 shooter is answered by the classification itself ("She's trained the doing half, and the reading half has had no practice at all. It's not nerves. It's the drill."), which shows students why classifying matters. Each scheme is a question (which muscles, where does it start and end, how predictable, who sets the timing), and each answer changes practice. The sprint start as externally paced but closed separates two schemes students usually merge. "Harder isn't open: open needs a decision" is a strong, correct rule.

## Findings

1. **Should fix, "never a swimming stroke" overstates the rule.** The hook asks why a coach would "teach a triple jump in parts, but never a swimming stroke", and scene 3 says "Continuous skills can't" be broken into parts. Swimmers routinely use kick and pull drills, and the site's own wording is "largely unavailable" (its Activity 2 faults a coach who "never swims the full stroke", not one who uses drills). A student who swims will distrust the lesson. Say "but mostly keep a swimming stroke whole" in the hook, and in scene 3: "Continuous skills are mostly practised whole, because the rhythm is the skill. A drill of one part has to be rejoined to the full stroke quickly." v18 scene 3 ("A continuous skill stays whole") needs the same softening.

2. **Should fix, 8b's practice consequence for "gross" does not follow from scene 2.** Scene 2 says practise tired when a skill is limited by its fine part, because fatigue costs precision. 8b then says the rowing stroke is gross, "so practise it tired". A student following scene 2 would not reach that answer. Change 8a's first scheme to closed or open: "Classify the rowing stroke in a 2000 m race as closed or open, and as continuous, discrete or serial, and explain one way each should shape practice." Model: closed (stable water and lane, nobody interferes), so repeat a consistent stroke many times; continuous, so practise whole, varying rate and distance. Students should be able to derive each consequence from its scene.

3. **Note, 8c leans on scene 4's last two sentences and the memory card.** "A faster or harder closed drill is still closed" is close to 8c's answer, but 8c asks for a change too; acceptable.

4. **Note, plates.** Five of the plates this video names are not yet painted (golf green, triple jump pit, pool block, free-throw line, tennis); scenes 2 to 6 use them as small tiles beside coded scales, so the scales can carry the teaching if they are late.
