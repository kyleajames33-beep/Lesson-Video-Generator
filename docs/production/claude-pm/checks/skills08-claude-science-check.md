## Findings

1. **Blocker. Coverage is equated with immunity.** The card gives 97% two-dose effectiveness and says about 95% must be immune. P2 and P4 nevertheless infer that 95% two-dose coverage is the stated immune threshold. With the simplified all-or-nothing interpretation in that card, 0.95 × 0.97 = 0.9215, not 0.95. In reality effectiveness is a reduction in disease risk, not itself a direct seropositivity measure, making the equation even less justified. Use the recognised approximate 95% TWO-DOSE COVERAGE target consistently, without asserting measured population immunity from that percentage. [CDC effectiveness](https://www.cdc.gov/measles/vaccines/), [CDC coverage target](https://cdc.gov/global-measles-vaccination/data-research/index.html).
2. **Blocker. Unsupported causal ranking and outbreak-immunity contribution.** The closing answer calls vaccination the "main reason" and says outbreak immunity "probably" helped without population size, extent of unrecorded infection or controls. Cases fell from 310 to 180 before the 2018 program. Say the later decline is consistent with a contribution from improved vaccination, but cannot establish its causal share. Point out the pre-existing decline. A merely possible confounder is not a proven cause. This repair is essential because the lesson claims to teach evidence-based assessment. The official 2022 Biology Q27 sample answer explicitly warns that a before-after program association does not establish cause. [NESA 2022 pack](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/biology/2022).
3. **Should fix. Measles transmission sentence.** "Measles spreads by droplets" omits airborne spread and can teach the wrong infection-control model. Replace with "Measles spreads through the air and is highly infectious", or omit the sentence as the author proposes. [CDC Pink Book](https://www.cdc.gov/pinkbook/hcp/table-of-contents/chapter-13-measles.html).
4. **Should fix. Hook audit claim.** The recorded category combines leaving stimulus unused with copying without interpretation. "Every report flagged answers that left the stimulus unused" narrows the category beyond the audit. Use both parts of the definition. Counts 12/12 and 7/12 match common-faults.md, and 7/12 is more than half. Do not present these as student prevalence. This is a correction against the project's actual audit, not a new audit of all feedback.
5. **Optional. The one-size writing rule.** A number in every paragraph and judgement first/last are useful here but not universal NESA requirements. Preserve the memory aid's own limit in the decision rule: qualitative evidence can be a detail rather than a number; an explain-only answer need not make an assess-style verdict. The official marking guidelines assess the response to each demand, not use of PEEL itself.

## Fictional data and biology

Every displayed/voiced value agrees with the table. 2010/2012 coverage 96/95% and cases 12/8; 2014 91% and 40; 2016 87% and 310; 2018 89% and 180; 2020 94% and 25; 2022 95% and 6. From 2012 to 2016 coverage falls 8 percentage points and cases rise 302. From 2018 to 2022 coverage rises 6 points and cases fall 174 (96.7%). No actual regional incidence can be inferred without population and surveillance information. The broad pattern is plausible as fictional data, not a prediction that a particular coverage forces a particular case count.

Two MMR doses are about 97% effective against measles, and immune memory supports rapid response and reduced disease/transmission risk. Natural measles infection commonly produces lasting measles immunity, but its population importance cannot be assumed from 310 cases alone. Vaccination is the safe prevention mechanism; the lesson must not imply infection is a desirable way to build community protection. These are source checks, not clinical recommendations. [CDC vaccination facts](https://www.cdc.gov/measles/vaccines/).

Drill 1: compliance rises 45 to 82%, or 37 percentage points. Infection rate falls 6.0 to 2.5 per 1,000 patient-days, 58.3%, so "fewer than half" is correct. It is a generic all-infection example; don't mislabel it the Australian SABSI benchmark, which uses per 10,000 patient-days and is much lower. No source verifies this exact fictional ward rate as a typical Australian figure. Patient-days are a legitimate denominator; define it briefly if it is used to compare wards. Concurrent cleaning confounds attribution to training. [Australian infection-surveillance guidance](https://www.safetyandquality.gov.au/clinical-topics/infection-prevention-and-control/infection-surveillance-and-monitoring), [Australian national HH study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22107015/).

Drill 2's calcium-stearate chemistry and renewable-fat feedstock are right for ordinary soap; do not infer that every formulation is universally milder or environmentally preferable. The supplied body facts are enough for a justified, context-specific poor-choice verdict.
