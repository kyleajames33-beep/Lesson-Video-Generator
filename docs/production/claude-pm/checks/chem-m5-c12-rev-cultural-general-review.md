Main question traced: *How does water take the toxin out of a cycad seed but leave the food behind?* Useful outcome: the student can explain why cycasin and macrozamin dissolve (OH groups hydrogen bond with water) and starch does not (packed grains), why crushing speeds leaching, why a small unchanged volume of still water leaves toxin behind (equal two-way rates) and why running water keeps removing it (Q below K), and can credit the method to Aboriginal peoples' traditional knowledge without treating it as a recipe.

## Scope and focus

One mechanism, one question, one visual anchor (the two troughs with synced graphs). Entry knowledge is used, not re-taught: C11's two-way dissolving, C8's Q against K, Year 11 hydrogen bonding and surface area. The site's errors are corrected well (no "65,000 years", no "validated by science", no "still water saturates", no "chemically destroyed" crossed mistake). At about 434 s it is a short video by design; there is room to add the causes below.

## Strengths to keep

- **The still-against-running contrast is a real video beat.** Two troughs, the same seed pieces, the return arrows appearing in one and absent in the other, and two graphs side by side (one levels above zero, one keeps falling). Students can predict, watch and compare.
- **The equilibrium model is correct and better than the site's.** Treating it as toxin moving between seed and water, with equal rates long before saturation, and the large-pool point in scene 7 ("far more leaves before the balance is reached") are accurate and useful.
- **The starch trap is excellent.** "Starch stays because it's non-polar" is the error students will make, and "starch has O H groups too" is the right correction.
- **The cultural framing is accurate and respectful.** "This knowledge belongs to the Aboriginal peoples who developed it through traditional food preparation, and the methods differ from place to place" credits the knowledge, avoids one universal method, names no community, and does not rank it against science. "Not a recipe" has its own reason. No Torres Strait Islander practice is claimed without a source.
- **No people, hands, faces, ceremonies or sacred items.**

## Chemistry and fact check

| Claim | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| Cycasin in *Cycas*; macrozamin, a close relative, in *Macrozamia*; "the same toxic part joined to a sugar part" (3) | Correct. Cycasin is methylazoxymethanol (MAM) beta-D-glucoside; macrozamin is MAM beta-primeveroside (a two-sugar unit). The molecule sketch (glucose ring with four OH groups; a second ring for macrozamin) is right. |
| OH groups hydrogen bond with water, so the toxins dissolve well (3, 12) | Correct; both glycosides are water-soluble. |
| Starch grains don't dissolve in cold water (3, 12) | Correct; granules are semi-crystalline, chains held by inter-chain hydrogen bonds, and gelatinise only on heating. Finding 3 adds the cause. "Starch has OH groups too" is correct. |
| Crushing: more surface and a shorter path, so faster (4) | Correct (standard rate reasoning). |
| Still, unchanged small volume: rates become equal with toxin left in the seed (5) | Correct as a distribution equilibrium, toxin(seed) ⇌ toxin(water). Finding 2 removes the saturation implication. |
| Running water: removing the product keeps Q below K, so net movement continues (6) | Correct. The design also rightly makes the running curve flatten gradually (the net rate falls as the seed's toxin runs down). |
| A large still pool keeps toxin dilute, so far more leaves before the balance (7) | Correct. |
| Roasting before leaching; ageing for months; method choice partly linked to water availability (7) | Consistent with the general method types the design attributes to Asmussen (2011) and Beck (1992); Codex to confirm against the sources, as briefed. |
| "strong enough to make people seriously ill" (1); "a mistake can make someone seriously ill" (2) | Correct; cycad toxins cause serious poisoning. |
| Hydrolysis not mentioned | Acceptable: the learner copy never says the toxin leaves only unchanged, so it is silent rather than wrong (author note d). |
