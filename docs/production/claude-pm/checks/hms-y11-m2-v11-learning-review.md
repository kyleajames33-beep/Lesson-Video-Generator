## Findings

1. **Should fix, scenes 2 and 7 re-teach v06.** v06 scene 3 taught carbohydrate to glucose and glycogen, fat at about 37 against 17 kJ per gram, protein for repair; v06 scene 4 taught iron for haemoglobin, calcium with vitamin D, B vitamins for energy release, and sodium and potassium lost in sweat. v10 scene 4 repeated the fat and carbohydrate comparison. v11 states all of it again as new. Open scene 2 with "You met these three in digestion" and bring v06's ledger in already filled (grey), then narrate only the new column: which system each feeds and why. Do the same in scene 7 ("You met this list earlier; here's how each one limits an energy system"). This follows the course's no-double-teaching rule and frees about 15 s.

2. **Should fix, scene 7 states 10c's answer.** "B group vitamins are coenzymes, helpers the enzymes need, so a shortage slows ATP production even when fuel is plentiful" is most of 10d. Keep the gloss and drop the consequence clause ("B group vitamins are coenzymes, helpers the enzymes need"), so students must apply iron's "removes a limit, only if short" logic to the B vitamins themselves.

3. **Should fix, key note 1 says carbohydrate is "the only anaerobic fuel".** Students copy key notes. Phosphocreatine is the ATP-PCr system's fuel (v09; and v10's blocker). Write "the only food fuel usable without oxygen". Scene 2's spoken "the only fuel that can be broken down without oxygen" is acceptable in a macronutrient context, but "food fuel" there too would remove the doubt.

4. **Note, scene 7 says sodium and potassium "trigger contraction".** Calcium triggers contraction (said one sentence earlier). Say sodium and potassium "let nerves and muscle fibres fire".

5. **Note, "about sixty-five per cent of maximum".** State "of maximum effort" (see the summary's units pattern).

6. **Note, plates.** The food plate carries scene 2's three columns and is not yet painted; the coded tanks carry the teaching if it is late.
