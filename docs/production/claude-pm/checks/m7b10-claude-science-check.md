## Facts confirmed (no change)

- **Semmelweis (hook, scene 3, bars).** Vienna General Hospital, First Clinic (doctors and medical students) and Second Clinic (midwives), admissions on alternate days. 1846: First Clinic 459 deaths in 4,010 births, 11.4 per cent; Second Clinic 105 in 3,754, 2.8 per cent. 1841 to 1846 averages about 9.9 and 3.9 per cent ("about one mother in ten", "far fewer"). Chlorinated lime (calcium hypochlorite) handwashing from mid-May 1847; First Clinic mortality fell from 18.3 per cent (April 1847) and 12.24 per cent (May) to 2.38 per cent in June, then about 1 to 2 per cent; 1848 annual rates 1.27 per cent (First) against 1.33 per cent (Second), so "deaths fell to about the midwives' level" and the 1848 bars are correct. "A chlorine solution" is acceptable (the solution releases chlorine). Puerperal fever was mainly caused by *Streptococcus pyogenes*; "Material from the autopsies carried bacteria" is correct as a present-day explanation (Semmelweis's "cadaverous particles"). Germ theory was not accepted until the work of Pasteur, Lister and Koch in the 1860s to 1880s. (searched) Wikipedia, Ignaz Semmelweis (reproduces Semmelweis's yearly tables) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignaz_Semmelweis ; Hungaricana, *Orvostörténeti Közlemények* Suppl. 18-19 (2000), monthly and 1848 rates https://library.hungaricana.hu/en/view/ORSZ_ORVO_OK_Suppl_2000_018_019/?pg=23 ; Whonamedit, Semmelweis (12.24 to 2.38 per cent) https://redux.whonamedit.com/doctor.cfm/354.html ; Semmelweis, *Die Ätiologie, der Begriff und die Prophylaxis des Kindbettfiebers* (1861), tables (for Codex to open).
- **Soap and alcohol rub (scene 3).** Soap and water remove microbes mechanically; alcohol-based hand rubs kill many bacteria and enveloped viruses but are less effective against norovirus and *C. difficile* spores, for which CDC recommends soap and water. "Doesn't reliably kill" is correctly hedged. (searched) CDC, Clinical safety: hand hygiene for healthcare workers https://www.cdc.gov/clean-hands/hcp/clinical-safety/index.html ; CDC, Hand hygiene science https://www.cdc.gov/handhygiene/science
- **Environmental management (scene 4).** Surface cleaning, safe cooking, water treatment and sewerage remove or kill pathogens in the environment; infrastructure works without individual action. Correct and limited to "treated water and sewers".
- **Measles (scenes 5 to 7).** Airborne; "short survival time (less than 2 hours) in the air or on objects and surfaces" (NSW), so "can stay in the air of a room for up to two hours" is correct. Infectious "from 24 hours prior to onset of prodromal symptoms until 4 days after the onset of rash" (about 4 days before the rash); cases excluded "from onset of symptoms to 4 days after onset of rash". Timeline values (first symptoms about day 10, rash about day 14, infectious about day 10 to 18) match. (searched, opened) NSW guideline (finding 2).
- **Post-exposure callback (Doubt d; cut sentence).** "A vaccine within three days of exposure, or an antibody injection within six, can protect them instead." is correct: NSW offers MMR within 72 hours of first exposure, or normal human immunoglobulin up to 144 hours, and such contacts "may return". It is correct if restored; the learning review recommends leaving it out of the narration (learning review finding 10).
- **Border biosecurity and 2020 (scene 8).** Australia screens travellers, cargo, mail and imported animals; travellers must declare risk goods or may dispose of them in terminal bins; imported cats and dogs complete post-entry quarantine at Mickleham, Victoria (minimum 10 days for most). National Cabinet agreed on 27 March 2020 that, from 11:59 pm on 28 March 2020, all arriving travellers complete 14 days of quarantine in designated facilities such as hotels. "Australia made returning travellers quarantine for fourteen days" is correct and dated. (searched) Department of Agriculture travel and import pages (finding 8) and industry advice 145-2020 https://www.agriculture.gov.au/biosecurity-trade/import/industry-advice/2020/145-2020 ; PM Transcripts, National Cabinet statement https://pmtranscripts.pmc.gov.au/node/38492 ; Victorian Premier, "Enforced quarantine for returned travellers" https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/enforced-quarantine-for-returned-travellers-to-combat-coronavirus/
- **Herd immunity threshold (scene 10).** Threshold = 1 − 1/R₀ under even mixing. With R₀ 12 to 18: 91.7 to 94.4 per cent; with R₀ 15: 93.3 per cent ("about ninety-three per cent"); "more than fourteen in every fifteen" is exactly 1 − 1/15. The MJA and the Department of Health give 92 to 94 per cent for measles. R₀ 12 to 18 is the conventional textbook range; published estimates vary widely with setting (Guerra et al., *Lancet Infect Dis* 17:e420, 2017, for Codex to open), so "about fifteen" is fairly hedged.
- **95 per cent target against the threshold.** Australia's national childhood coverage target is 95 per cent, an aspirational coverage target; the MJA notes that 95 per cent coverage "should theoretically result in population-level immunity comfortably exceeding the 92–94% herd immunity threshold", and that 95 per cent "is not a magic threshold". "Health services aim for ninety-five, because no vaccine protects every person who gets it" is correct (two doses about 97 per cent effective; one dose about 93 per cent). (searched) MJA https://www.mja.com.au/node/13194 ; Department of Health, Childhood immunisation coverage https://www.health.gov.au/topics/immunisation/immunisation-data/childhood-immunisation-coverage
- **Why local coverage matters (scene 10).** The threshold assumes even mixing. Unvaccinated people cluster (schools, communities, families), so a local pocket can sit well below the threshold while the national figure is above it; an imported case can start an outbreak there. Australia's measles outbreaks since elimination (verified 2014) have mostly followed importations by unvaccinated travellers into under-vaccinated groups. Correct. (searched) Department of Health (elimination since 2014; five-year coverage 93.76 per cent, below target) (link above)
- **Babies too young to vaccinate (scene 9).** Australian MMR is scheduled at 12 months (MMRV at 18 months), so younger infants rely on others' immunity. Correct (finding 3 covers the attempt).
- **Attempt 1.** Incubation 5 to 14 days; transmission from 2 days before symptoms; release on day 7; latest symptoms day 14; transmission from day 12. "Quarantine should last fourteen days" (after last contact) is correct. The model's judgement, strength, weakness and improvement fit "evaluate".
- **Attempt 2.** 70 < 80 < 92. The model is correct with finding 3; the author note on vaccinated against immune is correct and properly kept out of learner copy.
- **New memory aid: "Isolate the infected, quarantine the exposed."** Correct and matches standard definitions: isolation separates people who are sick or known to be infected; quarantine separates people who were exposed and might be infected but are not sick. Assumption: "infected" means known or suspected (usually symptomatic); a quarantined person who becomes sick moves to isolation, as scene 7 says. Recommended for use as designed.
- **"Herd immunity: break enough links and the chain dies out"** (bank). Correct with the limits the design states (even mixing; local pockets). **"Every control measure breaks a link"** (bank, b03's softened form) is correct as a principle.
- **Absolute words.** "hygiene can't break every link" (true); "Washing your hands does little" (true for aerosols); "no vaccine protects every person" (true); "can't be vaccinated" (true for young infants and some immunocompromised people); "the pathogen can't use them" (hedged by "usually"; acceptable); "only partly effective" (fine). Findings 1, 2, 4, 6 and 7 cover the rest.
