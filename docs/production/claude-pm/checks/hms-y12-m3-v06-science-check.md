## Confirmed
- Type 2 diabetes incidence (5): AIHW *Diabetes: Australian facts* (page updated 13 Nov 2026): "a steady decline in the age-standardised incidence rate for type 2 diabetes" 2000 to 2021, "an overall drop of 45%"; 45,700 new diagnoses in 2021. "Fallen by almost half since 2000" is correct. Site correction 26 confirmed.
- Contributing cause of death; complications; shared risks; income and remoteness gradients; higher and earlier for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples. Correct.
- Dementia (9b; root ruling 1): AIHW *Dementia in Australia* (burden page, updated 30 Jul 2026): 59 per cent of dementia's total burden in 2024 was fatal (YLL). Dementia is the leading cause of death (ABS 2025). The accepted mid or upper position is right; with a fatal majority, the token's default can sit upper-mid.
- Mental health token (large non-fatal burden), comorbidity, 9d (stigma and counting): correct.

## Findings
1. **Blocker (scene 3, line 58).** "Injury sits on the prevention side, because road deaths fell with laws and safer cars, and it mostly affects the young." Falls are the leading cause of injury deaths in Australia (AIHW *Injury in Australia*, 2023 to 24: about 6,400 fall deaths in people aged 65 and over, over 95 per cent of all fall deaths), so injury deaths are concentrated in older people. Injury is the leading cause of death for young people, which is a different claim. Corrected: "Injury sits on the prevention side, because road deaths fell with laws and safer cars. It's the leading cause of death for young people, while falls hit older people hardest." Adjust the token's tag to match.
2. **Should fix (scene 5, line 68; key note 4, line 119).** "Yet prevalence keeps rising". AIHW reports crude prevalence rising (all diabetes, 2.4 per cent in 2000 to 4.3 per cent in 2021) but age-standardised prevalence "relatively stable since 2011". Corrected: "Yet the number of people living with it keeps rising, because people live with it for decades." Key note 4: "new-case rate falling; number living with it rising."
3. **Should fix (scene 6, line 73).** "So there are fewer new cases overall, but more of them in younger adults". Not confirmed: the AIHW release does not report a rising share or rate of type 2 diabetes in younger adults for 2000 to 2021, and its age breakdown shows rates peaking at 65 to 69. Corrected (true either way): "So there are fewer new cases overall, but when it starts in younger adults, they live longer with it." Keep the closing sentence on earlier onset and complications.
4. **Note (9b).** COPD's age-standardised death rate has fallen overall, mainly in males; for females it rose for decades and has only recently levelled. "Its death rate is falling, decades after smoking fell" is fair for the total; no change needed.

## Verdict
Fail until fixed (1 blocker). 1 blocker, 2 should fix, 1 note.
