## Should fix

**D09-SLIDER, scene 4, "Drag its tail out to the right, and the bulk of the data has to shift left to make room, so the peak moves left", and cause chain item (3), "dragging the tail one way pushes the bulk of the data the other way".** This gives a false mechanism. Large values do not push ordinary values anywhere. The peak sits opposite the tail because most values bunch near a typical value (often near a limit, like zero for screen time) while a few stretch far out on one side. A student who learns "the tail pushes the bulk" has learned something untrue. Fix: keep the slider, but let the bulk and its peak stay put while the tail handle stretches outwards, and say: "A few values stretch far out on one side. The many ordinary values stay bunched where they were, so the peak sits on the other side from the tail." Update the cause chain line to match.

## Note

**D09-SLEEPTAIL, scenes 3 and 6.** The sleep data's left tail is presented mainly through "one slept just three", and d18 later calls that 3 an outlier. The left side does thin out from 6.5 through 6 and 5.5 before the 3, so the skew is real, but saying so ("the values thin out from six and a half down to five and a half, then one night of three") would stop students thinking one outlier is the same thing as a skew.
