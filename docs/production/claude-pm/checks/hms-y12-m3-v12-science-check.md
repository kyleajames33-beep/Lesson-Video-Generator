## Findings
1. **Blocker (scene 2, line 53, with the stamp and its grey tag).** "And in Australia, an approved product has been checked for safety and that it contains what it says, not that it works." The TGA states that listed (AUST L) medicines are "not 'approved' by the TGA" and are not individually assessed before sale: the sponsor self-certifies, ingredients must come from a pre-approved low-risk list, manufacture must follow good manufacturing practice, and the TGA may review a product after it is on sale. So no one has checked the product itself. Corrected: "And in Australia, a listed product, with AUST L on the label, can only use ingredients approved as low risk and must be made to quality standards, but nobody has checked that it works." Replace any "approved" stamp wording with "listed".
2. **Note (scene 5a, line 68).** "Many people don't tell their doctor, often because they expect to be dismissed." Surveys also find common reasons are that the doctor didn't ask or it didn't seem important. Optional: "often because nobody asks, or they expect to be dismissed", which also supports scene 5b's fix (ask every patient).
