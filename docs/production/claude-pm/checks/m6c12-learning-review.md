## Main question and the useful outcome

Main question (as stated): "A pH meter can't tell you how much ethanoic acid is in a bottle of vinegar. How does a titration count every acid molecule, which titres can you trust, and how do the balanced equation and n = cV turn a titre into a concentration?"

One main question: yes. Every scene serves "how does titration give the unknown's concentration": the idea and apparatus (2, 3), trusting the known concentration (4), trusting the titres (5), and the calculation with two ratios (6 to 9). Useful outcome: a student can describe a titration and its terms; tell endpoint from equivalence point; explain why a weak acid is fully counted; say what makes a primary standard and why NaOH is standardised; choose and average concordant titres; and calculate an unknown concentration in four steps with 1 : 1 and 1 : 2 ratios, whichever solution is in the burette, to four significant figures.

## Trace from the entry knowledge (student starting Year 12 Module 6)

Entry: Year 11 (n = cV, n = m/M, mole ratios), C02 (acid reactions, balancing), C05 and C08 (weak acids mostly whole), C07 (pH), C06 (carbonate accepts protons), Module 5 (Le Chatelier). By playlist order the student has also seen C09 (a weak acid still reacts completely with hydroxide) and C10 (Ka from pH and concentration). The trace follows the vinegar from the hook to attempt 2.

1. Hook: **"A pH meter won't do it ... You need a method that counts every acid molecule."** A student who has just done C10 and G02 knows that pH plus Ka gives the concentration, and will ask "Why can't I just work back with Ka?" The design never answers, so its premise looks false to the best-prepared viewer (S1). The fix is also a better hook: a reading of 2.4, good to ±0.05, spans about 0.70 to 1.1 mol/L.
2. Idea and apparatus: each term is named on its cue with a plain meaning (standard solution, pipette and aliquot, burette, titrant, titre). The coded burette level falling while the counter rises makes "titre" concrete. Good.
3. Equivalence and endpoint: "You see it as the endpoint" treats them as one event. The design lists "endpoint = equivalence point" as a misconception it addresses "said precisely", but nothing says they can differ (M1). The complete-reaction explanation (hydroxide removes hydronium, the equilibrium shifts right until every molecule has reacted) repeats C09 scene 3 almost line for line, with no callback (S2).
4. Primary standards: causal (NaOH soaks up water and CO₂, so its mass does not give its moles). But the reason a primary standard must be pure, stable and dry is never said in plain words ("so its mass gives its moles exactly"); the science check S1 also fixes the sodium carbonate claim. Mid-scene and at the end, two three-row cards and the arrow are bright (S3).
5. Titres you can trust: the table, the concordance strip and the sliding 0.10 mL bracket show the rule well. The crossed "average every titre" chip is not narrated and is a fourth bright item (S3).
6, 7. Worked example 1: separate task, equation strip and grouped givens; one step chip per stage; the mole bridge fills one box per step. Good staging. "A bit more than the label says" depends on knowing that "5% acidity" means 5 g per 100 mL, which is never said, and the tag jumps from mol/L to "g per 100 mL" without the 55.8 g/L step (M2).
8, 9. Worked example 2: the 1 : 2 ratio is caused ("carbonate accepts two protons", C06) and the unknown in the burette is the real lesson ("It doesn't matter which solution is where"). Strong. Scene 8's thumbnail labels repeat the givens card in full (S3).
10. Memory aid: "Known to moles, ratio, unknown." holds for all three calculations. Good.
11 to 14. Attempts and feedback: see below.
15. Decision rule: repeats key note 4 and the memory aid nearly word for word (M4).
16. Key notes: five, useful. Note 1 says the standard solution is the one "added from a burette", which contradicts scene 9 (M3). Note 2 inherits the sodium carbonate wording (science S1).

## Findings

### S1. Should fix: the hook's premise contradicts C10 (scene 1, main question, description)

- Exact text: "A pH meter won't do it. Ethanoic acid is **weak**, so most of it stays as whole molecules, and the meter only senses the few that have ionised."
- Student question: "In the Ka video I used pH and concentration to get Ka. Why can't I use pH and Ka to get the concentration?"
- Replacement: "A pH meter won't do it well. ... You could work back through **K a**, but a reading of two point four could mean anything from about zero point seven to one point one moles per litre." Add a grey tag "pH 2.35 to 2.45 → c ≈ 0.70 to 1.1 mol/L (via Ka)". Main question and description: "can't tell you accurately".
- Understanding enabled: the student sees why a titration is needed (precision, because the ionised fraction is small and the reading error is squared), and the claim no longer clashes with C10.

### S2. Should fix: scene 3 re-teaches C09's complete-reaction explanation

- Exact text: "Hydroxide removes hydronium, and takes protons from whole molecules too, so the equilibrium keeps shifting **right**, until **every** molecule has reacted."
- Problem: C09 scene 3 taught this with a "whole molecules left" counter falling to zero. Here it returns as new teaching, without a callback.
- Replacement: "And as in the weak acid heat video, the weak acid gets fully counted." followed by the same mechanism sentence; reuse C09's counter at the lens edge with a grey tag "weak acid heat video".
- Understanding enabled: the student connects the titre to an idea they already own, and the scene stays short.

### S3. Should fix: more than about three bright text items in scenes 4, 5 and 8

- Scene 4: the Na₂CO₃ card dims when the NaOH card writes; the NaOH card has two crossed rows only ("soaks up water", "reacts with CO₂") and dims when the arrow draws.
- Scene 5: remove the uncued "average every titre" crossed chip (the crossed 18.90 row carries it); dim the strip once the rows are ringed.
- Scene 8: the thumbnail labels become "unknown" and "standard" (the givens card holds the values); the methyl orange tag dims before the givens write; the thumbnail dims when the mean chip writes.
- Understanding enabled: the student reads the current step and its one supporting fact.

### M1. Minor: the endpoint is close to, not the same as, the equivalence point (scene 3, key note 1)

- Replacement: "the **endpoint**, when a well-chosen indicator changes colour very close to it"; tag "endpoint: the colour change (very close to the equivalence point)". This sets up C13 without teaching indicator choice.

### M2. Minor: say what the label means and show g/L (scene 7)

- Replacement: "The label's five per cent means five grams, so it's a bit more."; tag "0.9285 mol/L × 60.05 g/mol = 55.8 g/L ≈ 5.6 g per 100 mL" beside "5% acidity (5 g per 100 mL)".

### M3. Minor: key note 1 puts the standard in the burette

- Replacement: "an exact volume (aliquot) of one solution reacts with the other solution, added from a burette, until the equivalence point. One of the two is a standard solution. The indicator's colour change is the endpoint, very close to the equivalence point."

### M4. Minor: shorten the decision rule (scene 15)

- Replacement: "So for any titration: average only the concordant titres, write the balanced equation, then go known to moles, ratio, unknown." This also pays for S1 within the length limit.

### M5. Minor: YouTube prerequisites

- Replacement: list C02, C07 and C09 by title.

## Retained strengths

The vinegar as a running case from hook to worked example; the falling burette level and counter that define "titre"; the concordance strip with its sliding 0.10 mL bracket; the mole bridge filling one box per step; the second worked example that swaps the unknown into the burette and states "It doesn't matter which solution is where"; A2 as a clean transfer of the hook's idea; one crossed mistake per feedback; the site's numbers kept; no mark talk; technique left to P06.
