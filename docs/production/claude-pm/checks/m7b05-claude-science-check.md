## Facts confirmed (no change)

- **Oomycete.** *P. cinnamomi* is an oomycete (Stramenopiles), with cell walls mainly of cellulose and glucans; biflagellate zoospores released from sporangia in wet soil swim chemotactically towards root exudates, encyst behind the root tip and secrete adhesive, then germinate and enter; hyphae colonise the cortex and vascular tissue; above-ground yellowing and wilting follow root loss. Spread in water and in soil moved on footwear, vehicles and machinery. Scene 2 is correct. (searched, Hardham sources in finding 3)
- **Hemibiotroph.** An early phase in living cells (haustoria), then necrotrophic. "Feeds on living cells at first" is correct.
- **About 40 per cent susceptible.** Shearer, Crane and Cochrane (2004), *Australian Journal of Botany* 52: 435 to 443: about 2,284 of 5,710 described species of the South-West Botanical Province (40 per cent) susceptible, 800 (14 per cent) highly susceptible. The hook's wording ("susceptible", not "killed") is correct. (searched) https://publish.csiro.au/bt/BT03131
- **Hygiene.** Boot-cleaning stations, cleandown and track and vehicle restrictions in wet conditions are standard dieback hygiene in south-west WA reserves (DBCA signage asking walkers to clean soil from footwear; a 2023 *Scientific Reports* study evaluated brush and disinfectant boot cleaning for *P. cinnamomi*). (searched) https://library.dbca.wa.gov.au/FullTextFiles/PAM02216.pdf ; https://doaj.org/article/08a88e0911364d8e9569e6524a6d6118 ; TAP 2018 background (finding 1)
- **Plant defences.** No circulating immune cells; preformed barriers (walls, suberin, lignin, periderm) against induced responses after detection. Surface pattern-recognition receptors detect conserved pathogen molecules (PAMPs), giving responses that include callose papillae (beta-1,3-glucan, laid between the wall and the membrane at penetration sites), lignification and antimicrobial compounds; intracellular R proteins (mostly NLRs) detect pathogen effectors, usually triggering the HR, a rapid localised cell death that restricts pathogens needing living cells. Phytoalexins are antimicrobial compounds made after attack. Systemic acquired resistance is a salicylic acid dependent whole-plant priming in many plants; the narration does not claim it for banksia. Scenes 3, 5 and 6 are correct with findings 3 and 5. Jones and Dangl 2006 (for Codex to open).
- **Tynan et al. 1998 (verbatim abstract).** Every observation the narration quotes is in the abstract: root tips necrotic within 24 h and hypocotyls by day 5 (*B. coccinea*, with *B. menziesii*); callose deposition delayed in *B. coccinea*; penetration delayed in *B. spinulosa* var. *collina*; necrosis in *B. serrata* from day 3, rarely past halfway up the root by day 9; 2 to 3-week-old seedlings inoculated in vitro with zoospores and studied microscopically; *B. serrata* tolerant. The study was run in South Australia (Adelaide region isolates). Common names and ranges are standard: scarlet banksia (south-west WA), old man banksia (east coast including NSW), hairpin banksia (*B. spinulosa*; var. *collina* in eastern Australia). The hypocotyl is the seedling stem, so "reached the seedling's stem" is correct.
- **"Tolerant isn't immune"** matches the abstract (necrosis still began at day 3).
- **Attempt 1.** Hypothetical values, consistent with published patterns (earlier papillae and smaller lesions in more resistant hosts). Model correct.
- **Attempt 2.** HR lesion biology correct, with finding 4.
- **Absolute words.** "no circulating immune cells" (true), "every cell has to defend itself" (fair: each cell carries its own defences), "the least protected ... exactly where the spores settle" (true for the zone of elongation), "doesn't remove the pathogen from the soil" (true). Flagged: finding 4; finding 1's "neither banksia had met".
- **Memory aid.** "The hypersensitive response is a firebreak" is sound for pathogens that need living cells; the necrotroph limit is noted for authors, and "speed decides whether the break holds" carries the condition.
