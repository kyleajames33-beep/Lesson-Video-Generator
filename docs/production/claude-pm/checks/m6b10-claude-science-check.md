## Facts confirmed (no change)

- **Bt mode of action.** Cry proteins from *Bacillus thuringiensis* are activated in the alkaline midgut of certain caterpillars and bind specific gut receptors, which mammals lack; Bollgard varieties target *Helicoverpa armigera* and *H. punctigera*. The Revision 2 cause ("only works after binding to sites in those insects' guts, which people and most other animals lack") is correct (OGTR risk assessments; Bravo et al., Toxicon 2007;49:423).
- **Bt cotton in Australia.** Ingard (Cry1Ac) was introduced in 1996, Bollgard II (two genes) in 2003-04 and Bollgard 3 in 2016-17; Bt varieties are grown on well over 90 per cent of the Australian crop, so "they've chosen Bt for most Australian cotton" (Revision 2) is correct. "Australian growers once sprayed insecticide many times a season": conventional cotton commonly received ten or more sprays a season against *Helicoverpa* in the early 1990s. Correct.
- **Refuge requirement.** The Bollgard 3 Resistance Management Plan requires refuges (unsprayed conventional cotton, or pigeon pea, which makes up about 80 per cent of refuges), with planting windows, control of volunteers and pupae busting; compliance with the plan is required under the Bollgard 3 Technology User Agreement (fetched: Bayer Bollgard 3 RMP, https://www.crop.bayer.com.au/-/media/bcs-inter/ws_australia/use-our-products/product-resources/cotton-traits/bollgard-3-rmp---northern-australia.pdf; refuge composition, https://thebeatsheet.com.au/a-new-pigeon-pea-variety-for-bollgard-3-refuges/). The plan's stated principle is "keeping susceptible moths available to mate with any resistant survivors", which supports "keeping resistance alleles rare". "Growers must also plant refuges, areas of non-Bt crops": correct (pigeon pea is a non-Bt crop). The APVMA registration record itself was not opened this session; the Codex check should confirm whether the plan is also a registration condition (the narration does not claim it).
- **Licence fee.** Australian growers pay a per-hectare technology fee for the Bollgard traits under the user agreement. "Growers also pay a licence fee each season to the company that owns the trait": correct for Australia (site correction 1 avoids a worldwide patent claim).
- **Resistance by natural selection.** Correct as a mechanism; resistance alleles to Cry2Ab have been monitored in Australian *Helicoverpa* populations, which is why the plan exists. "Could spread until Bt stops working" (Revision 2): correct as a conditional risk.
- **Hornless dairy cattle.** Most Holstein-Friesian cattle are horned; calves are commonly disbudded, which is painful. The Celtic POLLED allele is dominant and makes Angus naturally hornless. Crossing in polled genetics from beef breeds lowers dairy genetic merit, which is why editing was attempted (Carlson et al. 2016; Van Eenennaam commentary). Correct.
- **The plasmid finding (2019).** FDA scientists analysing the edited bull's genome (Buri) found the donor plasmid, including antibiotic resistance genes (neomycin/kanamycin and ampicillin resistance), integrated at the POLLED target site beside one of his two hornless alleles (Norris et al. 2020; MIT Technology Review). Integration made the bull technically transgenic; the company said it had not looked for it. "Government scientists checking one bull's DNA found ... bacterial DNA used in the editing, including antibiotic resistance genes, had been inserted beside the edit": correct. The project manager's decision to keep scene 7 is supported.
- **Conditional animal verdict (Revision 2).** "Justified, but only for animals checked to carry the edit alone": consistent with Young et al.'s conclusion that screening would let breeders keep the two calves that inherited only the natural allele.
- **Embryos and surrogates.** Cloning edited cattle cells needs embryo transfer into surrogate cows with low success per embryo; in Carlson et al. five calves were born and two survived. "Many embryos don't survive": correct.
- **Golden Rice.** Ye et al., Science 2000;287:303; Golden Rice 2 (Paine et al., Nat Biotechnol 2005;23:482). Vitamin A deficiency is a leading cause of preventable childhood blindness (WHO). The card's stem is correct and makes no regulatory claim.
- **AquAdvantage salmon.** Atlantic salmon with a Chinook salmon growth hormone gene under an ocean pout antifreeze protein promoter ("a DNA switch from another fish"); all-female triploids raised in land-based tanks; FDA approval 2015, Health Canada 2016; the company ended its farming operations in 2024 (author note; not on the card). Correct, with finding 7.
- **"Evaluate".** NESA's glossary: "Make a judgement based on criteria; determine the value of". The aid "'Evaluate' needs a verdict" is correct; 11d ends with a supported, conditional verdict.
- **Insulin, "CRISPR in weeks", selective breeding.** Not claimed in this video's narration (the bulls were edited with TALENs, and the narration names no tool).
- **Absolute words checked.** "Both work" (hook): correct for the stated aims. "objects to editing animals at all, whatever the outcome": correctly attributed to "some people". "is it acceptable at all? Evidence can't settle that one": correct (a value question). "nothing's left to object to" (11b): the crossed mistake, correctly. Findings 2, 5, 6 and 8 remove the others.
- **Em dashes:** none in the design.
