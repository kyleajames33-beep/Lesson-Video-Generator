# hms-y11-m2-v11 science check (nutrition for the energy systems)

Revision 2 design checked 24 November 2026 (source-only: script, on-screen text, practice, key notes, memory aids and facts section; no build or playback). Independent check; the design was not edited.

## Confirmed
- Which system each fuel feeds (2), with root ruling 1's wording. Correct.
- Crossover near 60 to 65 per cent of maximum (3). Correct as an approximate concept (Brooks and Mercier 1994; Romijn and others 1993, fat and carbohydrate roughly equal near 65 per cent of VO₂ max).
- Carbohydrate targets (4): 3 to 5 g/kg/day light, 6 to 10 heavy endurance (ACSM, Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics and Dietitians of Canada joint position stand, 2016; AIS). Arithmetic: 180 to 300 g; 570 to 950 g; about a third. Correct.
- Protein 1.2 to 2.0 g/kg/day with a plateau near 1.6 to 2 g/kg; fat 20 to 35 per cent of energy; fat-soluble vitamins (5). Correct.
- Iron (6): haemoglobin and myoglobin; deficiency caps aerobic supply; supplements help only when deficient; excess harmful; blood test first. Correct.
- B vitamins as coenzymes; calcium; vitamin C and iron absorption; sodium and potassium (7). Correct.
- Water (8): about 2 per cent body mass loss impairs endurance (ACSM fluid replacement position stand, 2007); 1.4 kg for 70 kg. Correct.
- 10a/10b (150 to 250 g; 300 to 500 g) and 10c/10d: correct.
- Memory aids sound. No people; plates named correctly.

## Findings
1. **Note (scene 3 narration and "carbohydrate about 90%").** "Above about eighty-five per cent of maximum effort, carbohydrate supplies almost all of it." At 85 per cent of VO₂ max, laboratory studies (Romijn 1993; van Loon and others 2001) still find a meaningful fat share, roughly a quarter. The exact figure was not confirmed from the full text. Safer: "carbohydrate supplies most of it", with the model readout "carbohydrate about 75 to 80%".
2. **Note (scene 8).** "In team sport, the first sign is often a skill error" is plausible but weakly evidenced; "a skill error can be an early sign" is safer.
