## Findings
1. **Blocker (10b feedback and the 10a model).** The case: the same survey run once in the week after exams and once in a normal week, with very different results. The feedback says "It's reliability" and crosses "calling it validity" as the classic mistake. But the post-exam week is an uncontrolled condition that affects responses for "a reason unrelated to what health means to them", which, by scene 7's own definition (did you measure what you meant to measure?) and by NESA science usage (controlled variables are part of validity), is a defensible validity answer. Crossing it marks a reasonable answer wrong. Corrected 10b: "Mainly reliability: run twice, it didn't give the same result. You could also argue validity, because the post-exam week added a condition unrelated to what health means to them. Either way, the repair is to run both at comparable times, with the same instructions. The classic mistake is calling it credibility, but nothing about the source changed." Update the model to accept validity with that reasoning and to cross "credibility". (Similar length to the current 10b, about 28 s.) Alternatively keep "validity" uncrossed and choose a case that is unambiguously reliability (for example two classes running the identical survey in the same normal week and getting different results because the instructions were read differently, though that is close to scene 7's example).
2. **Note (scene 5).** "community consultation and control" could read "community engagement, consent and benefit" to mirror the AIATSIS principles more closely; the current wording is not wrong.

Fail until fixed: 1 blocker, 0 should fix, 1 note.
