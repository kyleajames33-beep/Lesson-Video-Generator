# Visual standard audit: Year 11 Chemistry and Year 12 Physics designs (6 November 2026)

Auditor: Claude (read-only audit; no design files changed). Bar: `../maths-visual-standard.md`, items 2 to 9 (item 1, the painted real anchor, is graded for information only, because the standard applies it to maths). Reference sheets viewed: `../assets/maths-std-ref-chem-m5-g01.jpg`, `../assets/maths-std-ref-bio-m5-b06a.jpg` (the standard) and `../assets/maths-std-d01-v1-rejected.jpg` (the failure). Reference design read: `../lessons/chem-m5-g01.md`.

These are design documents, not builds, so every grade is a judgement of what the design asks the build to show. Type size (item 5) cannot be confirmed from any design: none of the 163 states a type size for the current line (see the scan). It must be checked on the build contact sheet, as the build gate already requires.

## What the reference sheets show (the target)

- g01: a painted campfire, ash and tree before any symbol; the heading writes in the hand font; terracotta heat and indigo spreading carried through givens, working, bars and the memory aid; the answer line is large and boxed; "minus a negative adds" and "every sign flips" in coloured handwriting with a red crossed chip; a particle-spreading panel and a calibration bar give a new picture roughly every scene; the notes are six short bullets plus a framed aid card.
- b06a: painted cells and organelles fill the frame in most of the first eight frames; the picture changes every frame; handwritten tags sit on the plates.
- d01 v1 (failure): the same axes and curve in 8 of 16 frames, small grey chips, no plate, no colour roles, no handwriting, and a paragraph wall of notes.

The common pattern in this week's designs is closer to g01 than to d01: every design has a colour key, a memory aid line and crossed mistakes. The gaps are narrower: plates arrive late or only in the handoff, many middle stretches stay on one board for two minutes or more, the handwritten voice is used once (the memory line), and "simulations" are often build-ups without a live readout.

## Concrete fixes for designs below the bar

### chem-y11-m1-v09 (light upgrade)
- Scene 1: put the existing `m8-mass-spectrometer-v1.png` plate or a painted jar of chlorine-containing salt beside the periodic box so the first frame is not all chips; keep the box.
- Scenes 4, 7, 10: frame the final row ("Ar = 35.48", "Ar(B) = 10.8", "x = 0.78") in a dark-ink box, as g01 does with -2881.
- Scenes 4 and 7: hand-font tags on the working ("divide by 100 first", "fractions add to 1"), amber like the abundances.
- Scene 5: make the beam the hero at full width; the pivot ticker is the live readout.

### chem-y11-m2-v06 (light upgrade)
- Scene 1: open on the existing `y11c-top-pan-balance-v1.png` with "2 g H2" and "1 g O2" written in hand font on two weighing boats (a concrete, wrong instinct), then cross it in scene 3.
- Scene 8: frame "0.60 mol" and add a crossed slip ("0.40 × 2 ÷ 3 = 0.27 mol: ratio upside down").
- Scenes 10 and 11: make the continuous-variation run a live simulation: a volume slider sweeps 5 to 35 mL of acid, the two-colour cup label and a temperature readout update together, and the point drops onto the graph as the slider passes each mixture.
- Scenes 3 to 5: hand-font accents "same atoms, rearranged" and "16 × heavier" on the bars.

### chem-y11-m3-v10 (needs redesign of scenes 1 to 11)
The equation board stays up for about seven minutes, which repeats the d01 failure in chemistry. Keep the equations and checks, but change the stage:
- Scene 1: open on the existing `y11c-cuso4-beaker-al-salt-v1.png` (aluminium in copper sulfate with the brown copper forming), with a zoom lens to the metal surface: aluminium atoms losing three electrons, copper ions taking two. Put the "3e⁻ against 2e⁻" question on that picture.
- Scenes 3 to 5: keep the half-equations, but show each half happening in the lens (zinc atom leaving as Zn²⁺ with two electrons left behind; Cl₂ splitting into two Cl⁻).
- Scenes 6 and 7: a genuine simulation, an "electron bank": two Al atoms and three Cu²⁺ ions on the surface, with live counters "electrons released" and "electrons taken". Students watch 1 Al against 1 Cu²⁺ leave a spare electron (counter mismatch flashes red), then the multipliers change the token counts until both counters read 6. The multiplier margin is then a record of what they saw.
- Scene 11: show the trap as a picture: the stray electron appearing out of nothing on the surface, with the charge counter at +2 against +3 and a red crossing.
- Scenes 7, 10, 15, 17: frame each combined equation; hand-font tags "lost 6 = gained 6", "never subtract".
- Scene 18 is already good (half-cell plates); keep it.

## The three most common weaknesses

1. **Thin handwritten voice (item 6).** 159 of 163 designs use the hand font at most once, almost always for the memory line; 116 cue no pen marks in the script. The Module 5 references annotate the working in coloured handwriting in most scenes. This is cheap to fix in a pass: add two or three hand-font tags and one pen ring per teaching scene, in the role colours.
2. **Long single set-ups and late or thin plates (items 2 and 1).** 80 designs have a camera-continue run over 120 s, and 92 have no plate in scene 1. Plates often appear only in the hook or the handoff (111 designs name a plate in two or fewer teaching scenes). The fix is per scene: split long board stretches with a cut to a plate, a full-width working stage or a simulation.
3. **Simulations without a live readout (item 3).** 39 designs have no simulation wording at all and 80 have two hits or fewer. Many "simulations" are build-ups (points plotting, levels drawing). The fix is to attach one live control and readout to the core idea (a slider, a running counter, a ticker).

Secondary: key notes are short in count (no design over 6 items) but dense in length (51 over 700 characters); 20 of 44 chemistry calculation designs do not frame the answer; no design specifies type size, so item 5 rests entirely on the build gate.

## Recommendation

- **A light upgrade pass, not wholesale redesigns.** Every design already has a colour key, a memory line and crossed mistakes, and most physics designs have real simulations. The pass adds: hand-font tags and pen marks per teaching scene; a framed answer on every worked result; a live readout on one core simulation; a plate in scene 1 where an existing plate fits; key notes trimmed to five short bullets; and a cut where a set-up runs past 90 s. Write type-size targets (44 px current line, 30 px supporting, 26 px floor) into the shared build brief once, not into 163 designs.
- **Redesign (scene-level) only the high-risk set**: about 18 chemistry (the 17 listed plus m3-v10) and 13 physics designs, after a human skim confirms each (the scan is a proxy). Equation-board lessons in Chemistry Modules 2 and 3 are the most likely true redesigns.
- **Build pilots (one per course, per the build gate):**
  - Year 11 Chemistry: **chem-y11-m2-v06** after its light upgrade (top-pan balance hook, live continuous-variation slider, framed answers). It has the most plates, a calculation, a practical and a graph, so it tests the whole bar. Alternative: m1-v09 (best simulation, but only one small plate).
  - Year 12 Physics: **phys-y12-m5-v04** after its minor fixes, provided the launcher plates are painted first. If they are late, use **phys-y12-m8-v14** (all four new plates are simple apparatus) after its memory-aid, notes and live-count fixes.
- Grade each pilot's 16-frame contact sheet beside `../assets/maths-std-ref-chem-m5-g01.jpg` before the owner sees it.
