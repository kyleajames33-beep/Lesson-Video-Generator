## Findings

1. **Should fix, scene 2 shows a trend that scene 3 says cannot be read.** Scene 2 draws the gap lines rising together beside the ABS life expectancy card, so a student reads "the life expectancy gap barely moves". Scene 3 then says the ABS will not compare those estimates over time. A careful student asks: "Then how do we know the gap held?" The improvement evidence is child deaths and some chronic disease deaths, not life expectancy. Fix: label the gap lines' axis "a health measure, e.g. child deaths" (not life expectancy) and move the ABS card to a separate panel at the upper right with a grey tag "a snapshot, 2020–2022". One spoken change: "So on measures like child deaths, both lines rise, and the gap between them barely moves." (about 1 s). Students should keep "trend" and "snapshot" apart, which scene 3 then explains.

2. **Should fix, 9c marking rewards two things the card does not ask for.** The card asks where it acts, how quickly, and what it leaves unaddressed (three demands, 5 marks). The marking adds a mark for the mechanism and a mark for "a design condition or link to self-determination or cultural safety". A student who answers every demand fully can top out at 3 or 4. Add to the demand: "explain how it would improve health, and state one condition for it to succeed." Feedback 9d already gives the mechanism; add "and it works only if the community sets the priorities" (about 2 s). Students should be able to see every mark on the card.

3. **Should fix, the housing plate does not show crowding.** Scene 4's mechanism is "crowded housing ... Crowding alone spreads infection". The painted `hms-insecure-housing-v1.png` shows one mattress for one sleeper in a bare room, moving boxes and a clock at 3:00: it reads as insecure or temporary housing and broken sleep, not as many people sharing a small space. A student looking at it during "crowding spreads infection" sees one person's room. Keep the plate (it fits v20's housing insecurity) but overlay it in code here: three more mattress outlines on the floor and a small tag "7 people, 2 bedrooms" (illustrative), so the picture carries the mechanism; or brief a crowded-room plate if root prefers. Students should see the condition that lets infection pass from person to person.

4. **Note, 9a is close to the taught case.** Scene 2 works "both lines rise, gap barely moves" and the memory card states it; 9a asks students to assess "the programs failed" on the same pattern. The judgement is new but the reasoning is recall. Acceptable as a first, easier item; if a harder transfer is wanted, give a case where the comparison group improves faster, so the gap widens while both rise, and ask whether the program failed.

5. **Note, scene 4 density.** Interaction, the rheumatic heart disease chain, racism's two arrows and cultural safety share one scene (about 800 characters). Each has its own overlay, but dim the housing and income arrows to grey before "And don't leave out racism" so only one mechanism is bright at a time.

6. **Note, hook "Make a guess" has no time.** Add a 2 s quiet hold with the three chips lit before they dim, so the guess is possible. The design is at about 569 s, so take the 2 s from reserve cuts.

7. **Note, "circles its left valve" is ambiguous on this plate.** In the painted heart cutaway the mitral valve (the one rheumatic fever most often scars, as the design's own fact list says) is on the viewer's right, in the red left ventricle; the valve on the viewer's left is the tricuspid. Write the build instruction as "the mitral valve: the red side, viewer's right" so the ring lands on the right structure.
