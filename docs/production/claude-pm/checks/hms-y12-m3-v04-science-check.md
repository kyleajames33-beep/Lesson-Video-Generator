## Confirmed (OECD *Health at a Glance 2025*, Australia country note, November 2025)
- Life expectancy in the leading group: 83 years, 1.9 above the OECD average. Correct.
- Daily smoking among the lowest: 8.5 per cent against an OECD average of 14.8. Correct.
- Alcohol "around average or above": 10.5 litres per capita against 8.5. Correct ("above" is the safer word).
- Overweight and obesity above average and rising; avoidable mortality better than average; cancer survival among the better: consistent with OECD and AIHW international comparisons.
- Child dental (8d, currency item): the Child Dental Benefits Schedule continues in 2026 (children 0 to 17, Medicare-eligible, family receiving a qualifying Services Australia payment; cap $1,158 over two calendar years, indexed each 1 January). "Some children already get subsidised dental care" and the tag "a means-tested child dental benefit and state school dental services" are true.
- Exposure-to-death lag (5, 8b): correct.
- Site correction 25: the wording (a large gap shared with other settler countries, no ranking) is safe. Australian figures confirmed (ABS 2020 to 2022: 8.8 and 8.1 years); the Canadian and New Zealand figures were not re-fetched in this check.

## Findings
1. **Should fix (scene 3, line 57).** "Only a small share of spending goes to prevention" sits in the weaker-than-others column, but the OECD note gives Australia 3.1 per cent of current health spending on prevention against an OECD average of 3.4 per cent: small, but close to average. Corrected: "And, as in most OECD countries, only a small share of spending goes to prevention." Move the chip to a neutral position (or add a grey "like most" tag).
2. **Should fix (scene 3, line 57; key note 3, line 112).** "Out-of-pocket costs ... are higher than in many similar countries, especially for dental care." Australia's out-of-pocket share is roughly 15 to 16 per cent of health spending (AIHW: $46.4 billion in 2024 to 25), near or below the OECD average (about 18 to 20 per cent), though higher than in several Western European countries. As spoken, students may conclude Australia is an outlier overall. Corrected: "Out-of-pocket costs, what patients pay themselves, are higher than in several similar countries, and heaviest for dental care." Key note 3 can keep "out-of-pocket costs" in the weaker list only with that dental focus.
3. **Note (bands).** Release re-check the bands against the next *Health at a Glance* before publication, as the design already requires.

## Verdict
Pass with 2 should fix. 0 blockers, 2 should fix, 1 note.
