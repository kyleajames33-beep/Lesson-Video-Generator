# hms-y11-m1-v10 science check (self-efficacy, health literacy, help-seeking, problem-solving)

Revision 2 design checked 24 November 2026 (source-only: the Revision 2 section, script, practice, key notes and the design's facts section; no build, audio or playback). Independent science and fact check; the design was not edited.

## Confirmed
- Self-efficacy and its four sources, mastery strongest (scenes 2, 3): Bandura (1977, 1997). Correct. Predicting initiation, effort and persistence after setbacks: correct.
- Health literacy as find, understand, evaluate, use (scene 4): matches access, understand, appraise, apply (Sørensen and others 2012) and the Australian Commission on Safety and Quality in Health Care's individual and environmental health literacy. Correct.
- Help-seeking barriers and facilitators (scene 5): Gulliver, Griffiths and Christensen 2010 (BMC Psychiatry). Correct as a list.
- Problem-solving steps (scene 7): D'Zurilla and Goldfried model. Correct.
- Skills-only programs widening gaps (scene 8): same mechanism as v05. Correct.
- Practice 10a/10b (Revision 2 accepts either skill) and 10c/10d: correct.

## Findings
1. **Should fix (scene 5).** "yet it's the skill young people use least. Five barriers explain most of that" contains two unsupported quantitative claims: no study ranks the four skills by use, and "explain most" has no measured basis. Corrected: "yet many young people delay seeking help, or never seek it. Five common barriers stand in the way: ..." (similar length).
2. **Note (scene 3).** "watching an elite athlete does little for a beginner": vicarious experience is weaker when the model is dissimilar (Bandura), so "does less" would be more exact; "little" is acceptable.

## Verdict
Pass with 1 should fix. 0 blockers, 1 should fix, 1 note.
