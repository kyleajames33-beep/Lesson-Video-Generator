## Main question and useful outcome

*How does a chain of amino acids become a trait you can see, and why can the same genes give different results?* Afterwards a student should be able to walk the chain base, codon, amino acid, shape, cell, trait for a stated case (sickle cell), say why an amino acid change can but need not change a protein, and explain a same-genotype, different-phenotype case through its mechanism.

## Trace from entry knowledge

- **Hook and jobs (1, 2).** B6c's chain is recalled in one clause; the hydrangea clones are a strong, visible puzzle. Five jobs, each on a plate or coded molecule.
- **Fold and fit (3, 4).** Causes come before names: amino acids differ, so water-avoiding ones tuck in, charges attract, cysteines link. "Can, not must" is shown with two counter-cases. Good.
- **Sickle cell (5a, 5b).** B6c's chart look is reused; the haemoglobin behaviour (fibres when oxygen is low) comes before the whole-body claim, and "two copies" keeps the necessary condition. This is the strongest scene pair.
- **Genotype to phenotype and the environment (6 to 9).** The struck shortcut arc blocks "genes become traits". The hydrangea mechanism (soil pH, dissolved aluminium, pigment complex) is explained, not asserted.
- **Attempts.** 11a (Himalayan rabbit) requires an inference (cool extremities, so a cold-active enzyme) from visible givens; 11c uses a supplied chart directly (NESA 2023 Q25(b) style). Demands stay on screen; nothing answer-bearing.
