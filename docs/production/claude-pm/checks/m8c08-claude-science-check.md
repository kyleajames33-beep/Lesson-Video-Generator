## Fact and figure checks

- Bromine-water loss is useful evidence for unsaturation within a restricted candidate set, not universally specific toC=C. An alkane can transfer bromine into its organic layer without reaction; retain the P02 "gone, not moved" interpretation wherever layers are visible.
- Hydrogen carbonate/acid and limewater equations balance. Alcohols are too weakly acidic to give this fizz under the stated conditions. Primary/secondary alcohols reduce acidified dichromate; tertiary alcohols do not under the ordinary test. Aldehydes interfere. The alkene negative is not cleared by these facts.
- With sufficient acidity permanganate is reduced to Mn2+, while neutral/mildly alkaline conditions commonly give MnO2. Acidification and temperature affect organic products, so do not imply all oxidations stop at a diol. The site wording needs the acid/neutral distinction.
- Formulae and names in the worked cases were recounted; no imbalance found. Some physical constants are author notes only. The exact alkene-negative premise remains open; no experimental result has been invented.
