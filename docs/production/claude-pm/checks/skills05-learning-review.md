## Main question and useful outcome

Main question: *When a question says calculate, what does a full-mark answer show, and how does showing it protect you when something goes wrong?* The video carries this one question throughout. Useful outcome: a student who can already do the chemistry or the allele count does four things:
- lays the answer out as equation, substitute, solve and sense check;
- states which value they chose and why;
- carries the digits and rounds once, to the right figures;
- writes the unit and the sign.

The worked case reuses C03's enthalpy method, so only the answer writing is new. This keeps the video on the skill and away from re-teaching the chemistry. The video earns attention through three things: the frequency hook, the same-slip pair (0 against 2) and the early-rounding flip (-54 against -55).

## Trace from entry knowledge (s01, s02; Chemistry to C07 with C03; Biology to B13 with B11)

1. **Hook.** Three numeric beats in about 37 s give a reason to care. The narration says "Chemistry papers", but the bars show combined Chemistry and Biology counts (S3). Biology 2024 Q29(b) is described inaccurately (M1).
2. **Card (scenes 2 and 3).** The card is short, carries the tag, and uses the cup plate with a coded readout. The "underline as you use it" choice is good: it ties givens to working and avoids a givens card. A Biology-only student has no Year 11 Chemistry (n = cV, mol L-1, limiting reagent), so scenes 5 to 10 are about 5 minutes of chemistry they cannot follow. The signpost tells them to watch the layout but not to ignore the content (S4).
3. **Four steps (scene 4).** The rail builds on cue, and the aid appears after the steps are named. "One E and three S's" maps onto the rail. It does not encode the order of the S's, but the rail does. This is acceptable.
4. **Heat, moles, answer (scenes 5 to 7).** This is the strongest visual teaching in the video:
   - unit chips travel and cancel;
   - the two-bar comparison shows which reactant runs out;
   - "choice stated" is tagged;
   - the gauge places -54 against about -57, with heat-loss arrows on the cup.

   Each narrated change appears on screen, and earlier rows dim. The mark counter's ticks do not match the illustrative guide used later in scene 10 (S2).
5. **Significant figures (scene 8).** The cause is explained well: subtraction keeps decimal places, so 5.2 has two figures, so the answer is -54. The lens showing "100.0 (4)" is not right under the strict rule this video teaches (M7; science check F2). The scene carries four ideas in about 42 s: the least precise value, subtraction, exact numbers and the guideline ladder. It is at the screen-load limit (M2).
6. **Round once (scene 9).** The -54 against -55 flip is concrete and memorable. The sample-answer strip shows an unrelated iron calculation's numbers, which students cannot interpret (M3). The range example is correctly limited to one guideline.
7. **Working when wrong (scene 10).** The contrast is convincing, and the caveat about carried-forward marks is in plain words. Under the design's own four ticks, though, Answer B would not reach 2. Placing A and B on the NESA ladder also contradicts "A earns nothing" (S2).
8. **Drill 1 and feedback.** The question stays on screen, "Pause here to answer." plays with a 10 s countdown, and nothing answer-bearing is shown. The last line, 14.00 - 1.699 = 12.301, breaks the subtraction rule that scene 8 has just taught: 14.00 has two decimal places. A student who applies the video's own rule writes 12.30 and is then told 12.301 with "three decimal places" (B1). The feedback runs about 34 s (S1).
9. **Drill 2 and feedback.** This is fair after B11, and every demand is visible. "Counts are exact, so no rounding is needed" overgeneralises (S5). The feedback runs about 31 s (S1). The sense check reads as 1 - 0.42 unless the C count is shown (M4).
10. **Decision rule and key notes.** These are separate from new reasoning and have a quiet copy hold. Key note 2 packs four rules into one item (M5).

## Findings

**B1 (blocker). Drill 1 feedback contradicts the subtraction rule taught in scene 8.**
Quoted (scene 12 visual): "pOH = -log(0.0200) = 1.699; pH = 14.00 - 1.699 = 12.301". Scene 8 chip: "+ or -: fewest decimal places".
Likely student question: "14.00 has two decimal places, so why isn't it 12.30?" Taken strictly, Kw = 1.0 x 10^-14 also gives [H3O+] = 5.0 x 10^-13 and pH 12.30. The design keeps 12.301 only by C07's author-note convention ("14 is treated as exact at 25 °C"), which no learner has seen.
Repair (screen only, no narration change): in feedback 1, a grey tag on "14.00" on the pH line: "14.00 at 25 °C: treated as exact here (C07)". Add to the model answer and the author notes: "12.30 is also defensible from Kw = 1.0 x 10^-14 and is not crossed; the crossed habit stays 12.3." Leave scene 8's exact-numbers chip unchanged, because 14.00 is a convention, not a count.
Understanding enabled: the pH rule (decimal places follow [OH-]) and the subtraction rule do not collide, and the student knows why.

**S1 (should fix). Scenes 12 and 14: feedback length.**
Quoted: scene 12 is 448 characters (about 34 s) and scene 14 is 412 characters (about 31 s). The owner's band is 15 to 30 s.
Replacement, scene 12 (about 399 characters, about 30 s): "Moles: zero point two zero zero over forty point zero zero, five point zero zero times ten to the minus three. Divide by the litres: zero point zero two zero zero moles per litre. pOH is **one point six nine nine**, so pH is **twelve point three zero one**. Three significant figures in the hydroxide give **three decimal places**. It's a base, so **above seven**. The habit to drop: rounding to **twelve point three**."
Replacement, scene 14 (389 characters, about 29 s): "Count alleles, not dragons: **five hundred** copies. T alleles: a hundred and ten from C T, plus a hundred from T T, makes two hundred and ten. Over five hundred, that's **zero point four two**. Counts are **exact**, and this divides exactly, so nothing to round. Check: C is zero point five eight, and together they make **one**. The habit to drop: fifty over two hundred and fifty, which **counts dragons**."
The on-screen rows, the 0.2500 L unit chip and the cues are unchanged. Retime the "five hundred" cue.

**S2 (should fix). Scene 10: Answer B's 2 of 4 does not follow the design's own guide, and the ladder placements contradict the narration.**
Quoted (Questions used, mark ticks): "q with units; n(H2O) from the reactant that runs out, with the choice stated; ΔH value with sign and unit; the answer to appropriate significant figures." Answer B gets only the first of these, but the narration says "so B earns **two**". Scene 10 visual: "Answer A's bare line sits beside rung "1?" ... Answer B sits level with rungs 2 to 3."
Likely student question: "Which is it: 0 or 1 for A, and 2 or 3 for B?" A student may also conclude that showing working earns half marks whatever the answer, which is close to a carried-forward promise.
Repair:
- Mark ticks become: "q with units; n(H2O) from the reactant that runs out, with the choice stated; ΔH = -q ÷ n applied, with the negative sign and kJ mol-1; the correct value to two significant figures."
- Scene 7 counter: "3 / 4" lands on the ΔH = -q / n line with its sign, and "4 / 4" on the boxed -54.
- B's second tick reads "ΔH = -q ÷ n, sign and unit".
- Narration: "In our marking guide, A earns nothing. B is still wrong, but its right steps are visible, so B earns two for them." This adds 18 characters.
- Delete the A "1?" and B "rungs 2 to 3" placements. The ladder stands alone with its tag.

Understanding enabled: marks follow correct, visible steps, not the wrong answer.

**S3 (should fix). Scene 1: the narration says Chemistry papers, but the bars show combined counts.**
Quoted: "In the last five HSC Chemistry papers, calculate was the second most common command ..." with bars "explain 57", "calculate 24", "justify 23".
Problem: 57 and 23 include Biology. The Chemistry-only counts are explain 28, calculate 24 and justify 12 (`verb-frequency.md`). The spoken claim (second after explain) holds either way.
Replacement: bars "explain 28", "calculate 24", "justify 12"; the GreyTag reads "question parts with their own marks, NESA Chemistry papers, Section II, 2021 to 2025 (HSCScience count)". The narration is unchanged.

**S4 (should fix). Biology-only viewers and the Chemistry worked example.**
Quoted (scene 2): "If you take Biology, watch the layout: your question is coming."
Problem: about five minutes use n = cV, mol L-1 and a limiting reagent, which Biology-only students have not met. Without permission to let the chemistry pass, they may decide the video is not for them before drill 2.
Replacement (scene 2, +50 characters): "If you take Biology, you don't need the chemistry here. Watch how each line is laid out: your question is coming." In the YouTube metadata, add chapter timestamps (at least "Biology drill"), set from the recording.

**S5 (should fix). Feedback 2: "Counts are exact, so no rounding is needed."**
Problem: the line generalises. Exact counts do not limit the figures, but a count ratio such as 175 / 460 still needs rounding to a sensible length. Students may carry "counts, so never round" to G03.
Replacement: covered by S1's scene 14 text ("Counts are exact, and this divides exactly, so nothing to round"). The scene 14 chip reads: "counts are exact: they don't limit the figures; 210 ÷ 500 = 0.42 exactly".

**M1 (minor). Scene 1: Biology 2024 Q29(b) is not "a population size".**
In the paper, students predict "the number of people that will be affected by diabetes in 2040" from a graph and a global population.
Replacement: narration "like a probability or a number of people from a graph"; chip "number of people affected, from a graph (Biology 2024)".

**M2 (minor). Scene 8 load.**
Dim the lens row and the rule chip before the exact-numbers chip writes. Move the "pH: decimal places = sig figs in [H3O+] (C07)" chip to feedback 1, where it is used. The ladder then arrives with at most three bright items.

**M3 (minor). Scene 9: the sample-answer strip.**
The strip shows an unrelated iron calculation's numbers. Keep the strip, but write it as "a 2022 sample answer: 0.026362 → 0.052724 → 2.9446 → 68.163% → 68.2%". Light only the digit tails (amber on the last line only), and add the tag "a different question: watch the digits, not the chemistry".

**M4 (minor). Feedback 2: show the sense check as an independent count.**
On "one", the C bar writes "C: (2 × 90 + 110) ÷ 500 = 0.58" before the sum to 1.00. Otherwise 0.58 reads as 1 - 0.42, which checks nothing.

**M5 (minor). Key note 2 density.**
Split it into two notes:
- "Significant figures: × or ÷ keeps the fewest significant figures; + or - keeps the fewest decimal places (so ΔT can have fewer figures than the readings). Exact numbers never limit."
- "pH: decimal places = significant figures in the concentration."

That makes five short notes, all essentials.

**M6 (minor). Unit notation.**
C03 and C07 show "kJ/mol" and "mol/L"; s05 shows "kJ mol-1" and "mol L-1" (the HSC paper form). Render the superscripts, and in scene 3 add a grey chip under the target line: "kJ mol⁻¹ = kJ/mol (both fine)".

**M7 (minor). Scene 8 lens on "100.0 (4)".**
See science check F2. Drop "100.0" from the lens row.

## Retained strengths

- Unit chips that travel and cancel make "values with units" visible, not just stated.
- The stated-choice tag ("NaOH runs out first") teaches a habit that markers can see.
- The -54 against -55 flip shows why rounding once matters, with no lecture.
- The same-slip pair isolates visibility as the only difference between the two answers.
- The caveat about carried-forward marks is plain and honest.
- Each drill carries a different precision lesson (logs; counts).
- Every question carries the HSCScience tag.
- The design avoids a pH worked example that would pre-empt g01.

## Verdict

**Not ready for recording until B1 is fixed** (one screen tag and one author note). After B1 and S1 to S5, it is ready for the Codex error check. M1 to M7 are recommended. Repeat this review on the exact voiced preview.
