Main question traced: *Ethene is a gas, yet thousands of ethene molecules joined together make a solid plastic. How does an alkene become an addition polymer, and why do plastics from such similar monomers, like PE, PVC, PS and PTFE, behave so differently?* Useful outcome: given a monomer, the student can draw the repeat unit (and the reverse), explain why polyethylene is a solid thermoplastic, and explain the properties and uses of LDPE, HDPE, PVC, PS and PTFE from how their chains pack and attract.

## Scope and focus

One question in two halves, held together by one chain model: how chains form, then how chains pack and attract. That is a fair single question for the dotpoint, and the chain view box gives the property half one consistent picture (heat, branches, Cl, rings, F sheath). The boundary is right: no radical mechanism, no condensation polymers, no environmental lifecycle, polypropylene only in an attempt.

No double teaching. c04a's weaker second bond is one "Remember" sentence; c03's contact rule and spaghetti aid are a callback and a grey chip; c08b's dipole–dipole is one sentence and a chip. Polymers are first taught here.

The property half has one structural weakness: three scenes use a cause that seems to contradict an earlier scene (findings 1 to 3). A student who has followed the chain model closely is the one most likely to be confused, so these matter more than their length suggests.

## Strengths to keep

- **The hook is a real surprise with a question.** A two-carbon gas becomes a tough solid, and swapping one H for Cl gives grey pipes.
- **The chain zipper shows the mechanism at the right level**: the second bond opens, the spare bonds link, the counter climbs, with one sentence for the initiator.
- **Notation is taught as a reason, not a recipe.** The two-unit sketch comes first, then the bracket stamp; the backbone and side group are named.
- **The reverse rule has its cause.** "Once the double bond is back, each carbon already has its four bonds", with the ghost H crossed. The site's own error (site correction 1) is the crossed mistake in feedback 1.
- **"The covalent bonds inside each chain don't break"** is said positively in scene 5 and crossed in feedback 2.
- **LDPE against HDPE uses the same monomer and repeat unit**, so branching is clearly the only variable.
- **PTFE is taught with polar C–F bonds**, correcting the site.
- **Both attempts are HSC-style and unseen**: 1,1-dichloroethene tests "everything else hangs off", and the chopping board and squeeze bottle is a new context.
