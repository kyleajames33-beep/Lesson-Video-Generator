# Brief 84: error check of exam skills S01 and S02

Checked the Revision 2 designs on 19 October 2026 against the exam-skills research pack, the cited NESA glossary and marking guidelines, and recent official Chemistry and Biology Section II papers. Findings only.

## S01: Read the question before you answer it

1. **The worked question is too close to an official question.** The S01 card uses the same cobalt equilibrium, the same pink-to-blue observation on heating, and the same three-mark value as NESA Chemistry 2024 Q23. Changing the final demand from equilibrium constant to collision theory does not make the stimulus independent. This also conflicts with the design's claim that no question was adapted from a specific NESA question. Replace the worked stimulus with a distinct equilibrium and observation, then rebuild its answer and the S02 callback that reuses it. Source: [NESA Chemistry 2024 paper, Q23](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/noindex/2025-05/2024-hsc-chemistry.pdf), page 15.

## S02: Identify, describe, explain

1. **The 2024 marking guideline is described inaccurately.** Scene 10 says that in a three-mark explain question, "describing the features" reached two marks. The cited Biology 2024 Q24(b) guideline awards two marks for *outlining features of a relevant program or campaign*, and three for explaining how it decreases disease incidence. Because this lesson teaches the difference between command verbs, keep the criterion's verb: say "outlining the campaign's features" reached two, while explaining its effect reached three. Source: [NESA Biology 2024 marking guidelines, Q24(b)](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/noindex/2025-02/2024-hsc-biology-mg.pdf), page 4.

2. **"Identify: a name (one per mark)" presents a false marking rule.** Scene 15 and key note 1 turn the one-mark example into a general rule. The pack itself lists identify tasks worth 4, 7 and 9 marks, and NESA's criteria are question-specific. The 2024 Biology Q21(a) awards two marks for two structures because that question requests both, not because every identify mark always buys one name. Replace the rule with "name the item or items the question asks for; use the marks to check the scope." Sources: [research count](../research/exam-skills/verb-frequency.md), Identify rows; [NESA Biology 2024 marking guidelines, Q21(a)](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/noindex/2025-02/2024-hsc-biology-mg.pdf), page 2; [NESA glossary](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/hsc/student-guide/glossary).

3. **The cobalt callback repeats the S01 near match.** Scene 9 reuses the S01 cobalt equation, heating and blue outcome to illustrate all three verbs. Replace it after the S01 worked question changes, so this second design does not continue to reproduce the 2024 Q23 stimulus. Source: [NESA Chemistry 2024 paper, Q23](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/noindex/2025-05/2024-hsc-chemistry.pdf), page 15.

## Resolution

Applied on 20 October 2026 in `lessons/skills-s01.md` and `lessons/skills-s02.md`, both now at Revision 3 (author). Narration re-counted with the build parser.

### S01

1. **Worked question (resolved).** The cobalt stimulus is replaced by an original iron thiocyanate question: "The equilibrium below is established in a test tube at 25 °C. Fe3+(aq) (pale yellow) + SCN-(aq) (colourless) ⇌ FeSCN2+(aq) (red). A few crystals of iron(III) nitrate, Fe(NO3)3, are added and dissolve. The temperature stays at 25 °C, and the solution becomes a deeper red. Explain this colour change in terms of collision theory. (3 marks)" The system (iron thiocyanate, not cobalt), the change (an added solid at constant temperature, not heating), the observation (deeper red, not pink to blue) and the stimulus are all new. The annotated reading, the three-point model answer (more frequent Fe3+ and SCN- collisions raise the forward rate; the reverse rate is unchanged at that moment; the rates meet again with more FeSCN2+), the weak Le Chatelier answer (illustrative 1 of 3) and the mark ticks are rebuilt. Activation energy and the ridge sketch are removed; the C3 iron tube plates replace the cobalt plates. The design's claim that no question is adapted from a NESA question is now accurate, and the Decisions section records the comparison.
   - Compared against, from the paper text: Chemistry 2024 Q23 (the replaced match), 2025 Q35 (cobalt, collision theory and Le Chatelier, 5 marks), 2021 Q22 (chromate and dichromate colour, 3 marks; a chromate stimulus was rejected for this reason), 2019 Q25(b) and 2022 Q23(c) (removal from a gas equilibrium, using collision theory, 3 marks; a removal stimulus was rejected for this reason), 2024 Q26(b) (I2 added, using collision theory, rate against time, 3 marks), 2020 Q26(b), 2023 Q26(a), 2025 Q29, and the other three-mark Chemistry explain units listed in `verb-frequency.md` (C22 Q24 and Q25, C23 Q23, Q29 and Q35(b), C24 Q33(b) and Q37, C25 Q28(b) and Q30(b)). SCN- and iron(III) appear only in other topics (Chemistry 2020 Q31, a back titration; 2022 Q28, gravimetric analysis). The 2021 to 2025 Biology papers have no equilibrium or collision theory question.
