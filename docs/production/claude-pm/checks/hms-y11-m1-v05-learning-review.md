## Findings

1. **Should fix, 10a repeats scene 8's taught example.** Scene 8 works "eat more vegetables, walk more" with the full mechanism; 10a asks students to explain the same campaign ("eat more vegetables and walk thirty minutes a day"). Students can recite rather than apply. Use an unseen information-only case, for example: "A national campaign tells young people to book a free skin check every year through an online portal. Explain how this campaign could improve the national average while widening the gap between groups." Feedback keeps its shape: acting needs time, internet access, transport and a nearby clinic, spread unevenly by the determinants; better-off groups take it up more; average up, gap wider; crossed mistake unchanged. Students should show they can transfer "needs resources to act on" to a new case.

2. **Should fix, scene 1 hook labels give away the prediction.** The narration asks "What would you want to know to predict which one?" while the two bars are already labelled "lowest socioeconomic areas" and "highest socioeconomic areas". Draw the bars unlabelled with the dated source line, and write the two labels on "how many of those things will either of them ever get to choose?" (after the question). Students get a moment to predict before seeing the answer.

3. **Note, 10d length (about 32 s).** Trim "Pick one group and stay with it, say a rural community." to "Stay with one group, like a rural community."

4. **Note, scene 4 density.** Clustering, feedback and the gradient share one scene; each has its own overlay, so it should hold at playback speed. The "× × ×" readout is abstract; keep the grey tag "they compound" beside it.
