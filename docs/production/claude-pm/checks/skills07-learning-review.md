## Main question and useful outcome

Main question: *When a practical question asks about validity, reliability or accuracy, or asks you to improve a method, how do you name the right quality, point to the feature of this method that affects it, and propose a fix that actually fixes that cause?* The video carries this one question throughout.

Useful outcome: a student who already understands the experiment can
- say which of the three qualities a question is asking about, as three different questions;
- point to one specific feature of the given method and say what it does to the result;
- propose a fix that removes that cause, and recognise "repeat it" as a reliability answer only;
- judge reliability from a results table (spread against gap) and accuracy against a true value;
- write a risk answer as hazard, harm and a control for this substance.

The video earns attention through the hook (three words blurring into one), the target picture with its "wrong target" surprise, the ghost "repeat it and be more careful" chip stamping onto three unrelated set-ups, the reliable-but-not-valid tension inside one worked question, and two drills that each trap a named confusion.

## Findings

### S1. Should fix: the worked data contradict chem-m7-p03, which viewers watched one video earlier

- Exact text (card table): "Ethanol | −702 | −695 | −708 | −702 | −1367"; "Propan-1-ol | −803 | −815 | −797 | −805 | −2021". Scene 6: "Her values are only about half the data values, because heat escapes from an uncovered can in **every** trial." Scene 7: "ethanol caught about **fifty one** percent of its data value, propan-one-ol only about forty."
- Likely student question (Chemistry viewer straight from p03): "In the practical, ethanol with a shield and a lid gave fifty percent. Why does an uncovered can do better?" p03's own ethanol mean is −688 kJ mol⁻¹ (50.3%) with shield and lid, and its A2 unshielded, uncovered can gives 42.7% and 39.2%. 51% for an uncovered can undermines scene 6's fix (add a shield and lid). "About half" for 40% is also a stretch.
- Repair (data, with every dependent line; recomputed in science check F1):
  - Card stem: add "with no draught shield" (see M1 for the tightened stem).
  - Table: "Ethanol | −592 | −585 | −598 | −592 | −1367"; "Propan-1-ol | −693 | −705 | −687 | −695 | −2021". Spreads stay 13 and 18; gap stays about 100 (103).
  - Scene 5 narration: "Ethanol's three trials sit within about **one percent** of their mean, and propan-one-ol's within about one and a half." Grey tag: "largest difference from the mean: about 1.1% (ethanol), 1.4% (propan-1-ol)". Row (a): "Reliable: each alcohol's trials agree within about 1.5% of the mean".
  - Scene 6 narration: "Her values are well under half the data values, because heat escapes from an uncovered, unshielded can in **every** trial." Links "−592 vs −1367", "−695 vs −2021"; chip "under half: no shield, no lid (the combustion practical)".
  - Scene 7 narration: "Look at the numbers: ethanol caught about **forty three** percent of its data value, propan-one-ol only about thirty four." PercentBars "ethanol: 592 ÷ 1367 = 43%", "propan-1-ol: 695 ÷ 2021 = 34%". Row (c): "(34% against 43% caught)".
  - Scene 14 board: "Feature: can raised to 6 cm for propan-1-ol: more heat lost, 34% against 43%". Model answers (a) to (c), Decisions "Why the distance ..." and "Illustrative marks", "Numbers to verify": replace 51% and 40% (and 51.3%, 39.8%) with 43% and 34% (43.3%, 34.4%), and the trial values as above. Close Open decision 3.
- Understanding enabled: the worked question agrees with what p03 taught (no shield and no lid lose more), so the fix in part b is visibly earned.

### S4. Should fix: "Repeats fix reliability, and nothing else" overclaims

- Exact text (scene 14): "Repeats fix reliability, and nothing else." Key note 3: "Repeats improve reliability only." Scene 14 chip: "repeats → reliability only". Scene 8 sign: "accuracy or validity problem → repeats fix nothing". YouTube description: "why "repeat it" fixes reliability and nothing else".
- Likely student question: "In drill 1, Y's three scattered results averaged to exactly −57. Didn't repeating help Y's accuracy?" Averaging repeats reduces random error in the mean; what repeats cannot fix is a systematic error or an uncontrolled variable. The design's own memory aid check says this, but the narration does not.
- Repair:
  - Scene 14 narration: "Repeats help reliability, but they can't fix an error that pushes every trial the same way." (about +48 characters)
  - Scene 14 chip: "repeats → reliability, not a systematic error".
  - Scene 8 sign: "systematic error or validity problem → repeats don't fix it" / "reliability question → explain the repeats".
  - Key note 3: "Repeats improve reliability (and average out random scatter). They don't fix an error that pushes every trial the same way, or a variable that wasn't controlled."
  - YouTube description: "and why "repeat it" fixes reliability but not an error that pushes every trial the same way."
- Understanding enabled: the student keeps the useful rule without being contradicted by a correct exam answer about averaging random error.

### S5. Should fix: scene 5 uses the gap against the spread without saying why it matters

- Exact text: "Better still, use the table: the gap between the two alcohols, about a **hundred** kilojoules per mole, is far bigger than the spread within either set. A judgement, and evidence from the stimulus."
- Likely student question: "Reliability is about repeats agreeing. Why is the gap between alcohols evidence?"
- Replacement: "Better still, use the table: the gap between the two alcohols, about a **hundred** kilojoules per mole, is far bigger than the spread within either set, so the difference isn't just scatter. A judgement, and evidence from the stimulus." (+36 characters.)
- Understanding enabled: the student can say why the comparison is reliable, not only that the trials agree.

## Retained strengths

- Three words as three different questions, with NESA's exact wording kept small and labelled by version.
- The target picture, including the separate "wrong target" for validity.
- Word, feature, fix, with the test line "If your fix would work for any experiment, it probably isn't fixing this one."
- One stimulus carrying all three words, so reliable but not valid is shown, not told.
- Spread against gap as evidence from the table.
- "More trials just give a better mean of the same low values."
- Weak against strong staged as panel, then ladder alone, then one chip.
- Each NESA claim framed as one guideline with year and mark value.
- Two drills that each trap a named confusion, with the Biology drill crossing exactly the habit the reliability guideline rewards.
- Hazard, harm, control as a portable risk frame.
