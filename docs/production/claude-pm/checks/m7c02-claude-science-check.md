## Isomer classes

- Definitions: "same molecular formula, atoms joined in a different order" (structural isomers); chain (different carbon skeleton, same family); position (same skeleton and family, group on a different carbon); functional group (different family). These match the standard school definitions (chemguide: chain isomers "arise because of the possibility of branching in carbon chains"; in position isomers "the basic carbon skeleton remains unchanged, but important groups are moved around"; functional group isomers "belong to different families of compounds (different homologous series)").
- Alkene against cycloalkane: alkenes and cycloalkanes are different homologous series sharing CₙH₂ₙ, so they are functional group isomers under that definition, and school texts commonly give hexene against cyclohexane as the example. Some university texts call this ring-chain isomerism; author note d records that, and the HSC treatment is used. Acceptable. The learner copy should name the ring's family (S1), because the classification depends on "different family".
- Scene 8's check ("Same family: skeleton changed → chain; group moved → position") does not say what to do when both change (for example pent-2-ene against 2-methylbut-1-ene). Convention classes such pairs as chain isomers. No case in the video needs it (M4, author note only).

## Other chemistry claims

- "Methoxymethane, used as a propellant in some spray cans": correct (aerosol propellant in hair sprays and similar products).
- "Camping gas often contains both" (butane and 2-methylpropane): correct for common blends.
- "Moving a group can change how it reacts, and you'll see later that these two alcohols give different products": correct (on oxidation, propan-1-ol gives propanal then propanoic acid; propan-2-ol gives propanone).
- "The double bond is where an alkene reacts, so cyclobutane, with no double bond, won't react the same way ... a simple test that tells these two apart": correct; cyclobutane does not decolourise bromine water at room temperature, while but-1-ene does (c04a and p02 own the test).
- "A functional group is the part of a molecule that gives its family its reactions, like a double bond, or an O H": correct at this level.
- "Shorten once more, to two carbons, and any branch you add just makes a longer chain again": correct (every carbon of a two-carbon chain is an end carbon).
- Names: "methoxymethane" is the preferred IUPAC name of CH₃OCH₃; "2-methylpropane", "2,2-dimethylpropane", "propan-1-ol", "propan-2-ol" are current IUPAC forms.

## Absolute claims

- "Isomers always have the same atoms, so check the formula first": true by definition.
- "They're different compounds, with different names and different properties": true (properties can be close, but they differ).
- "Same name, same molecule": true for constitution under IUPAC rules, which is all this syllabus needs; stereoisomers are out of scope (author note e).
- No other absolute claims found.

## Exam style

A1 ("Classify each pair of compounds as chain, position or functional group isomers.") and A2 ("A student draws structures A, B and C and states that they show three different structural isomers of C₅H₁₂. Evaluate the student's statement.") are HSC Section II style, with original compounds and wording, and are not copies of any NESA item known to this reviewer (no exhaustive search of past papers was made). ## Findings

### S1. Should fix: name the cycloalkane family (scene 6, feedback 1)

- Problem: "functional group isomers" rests on "different family" (scene 8), but the ring's family is never named, and "the group itself is different" has no group to point at in cyclobutane.
- Replacement: as learning S1. Scene 6: "It's also C four H eight, but it has only single bonds, so it's in a different family: the **cycloalkanes**, rings of carbons with only single bonds. One is unsaturated, the other **saturated**." Feedback 1: "Cyclopentane is a cycloalkane, a ring with no double bond, so it's a different family from pent-one-ene: **functional group** isomers."

### M1. Minor: site correction 2 text

- Replace the reason with: "Methoxymethane is not used as an anaesthetic, and 'no enzyme can oxidise it' is unsupported (it is mostly exhaled unchanged because it is a volatile gas). Its refrigerant use (R-E170) is real." Keep the design's "Correct:" sentence.

### M2. Minor: site correction 3 is a rounding note

- Move it under "Note, not an error": "Reference data give −24.8 °C (CRC), often printed as −24 °C; the video says 'about minus twenty five'."

### M3. Minor: methoxyflurane name in correction 1

- The design's "2,2-dichloro-1,1-difluoroethyl methyl ether (CH₃OCF₂CHCl₂)" is a valid functional class name; add the substitutive name "2,2-dichloro-1,1-difluoro-1-methoxyethane" so the record matches current references.

### M4. Minor: author note for the classification edge case

- Add to author note d: "When both the skeleton and the group's position change (for example pent-2-ene and 2-methylbut-1-ene), the pair is classed as chain isomers; no case in this video needs it."
