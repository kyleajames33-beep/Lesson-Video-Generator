## Hydrogen bonding and solubility

- Hydrogen bond: δ+ H on an O–H attracted to a lone pair on a neighbouring O; H donor, lone pair acceptor (scene 7). Correct; the site's Common Error is answered correctly.
- "far weaker than a covalent bond" (about 20 to 25 against about 463 kJ/mol): correct.
- Boiling breaks no covalent bond: correct.
- Series: one OH each, longer chain, more dispersion: correct (64.7, 78.3, 97.2, 117.7 °C).
- Isomers: "usually" falls primary to secondary to tertiary; shape and contact named as the cause; all three hydrogen bond. Correct and correctly hedged. The steric weakening of the tertiary OH's hydrogen bonding is in author note b.
- Water solubility: methanol to propan-1-ol miscible; butan-1-ol about 7 g per 100 mL at 25 °C ("partly"); hexan-1-ol about 0.6 g per 100 mL at 20 °C ("barely"), density about 0.82 g/mL so it floats. Correct. "No sharp cut-off" is correct.
- Mechanism: "the non-polar chain breaks water's hydrogen bonds without replacing them" is the standard HSC account; the hydrophobic effect (entropic at 25 °C) is in author note d. No learner change.
- "Long-chain alcohols mix better with non-polar solvents, like hexane": correct (hexan-1-ol and butan-1-ol are miscible with hexane). If learning S2's trim T1 is applied, the chip carries it.
- Hook: "butane barely dissolves" in water (about 61 mg/L): correct.

## Findings

### S1. Should fix: the memory aid is an unconditional ranking

- Exact text (scene 13 narration and handwritten line; notes screen): "Hydrogen bonding wins, then size."
- Problem: across different sizes it predicts wrongly (methanol 64.7 °C boils far below octane 125.7 °C), and it appears to contradict c03's "size still wins" between series. The Module 7 bank entry it comes from is conditional ("For molecules of similar size").
- Replacement (all three places): "Same size? Hydrogen bonding wins. Same family? Size wins." (Learning S1 gives the timing.)

### M1. Minor: pentan-3-ol boiling point

- Exact text (correction 3, author note b, "Numbers to verify"): "pentan-3-ol (secondary, about 116 °C)".
- Replacement: "pentan-3-ol (secondary, about 115 °C)". Wikipedia gives 115.3 °C. The argument is unchanged.

### M2. Minor: author note on the fair comparison

- Add to author note c: "Propane is slightly more polarisable than ethanol, so its dispersion forces are not weaker; the gap is hydrogen bonding (with a small dipole–dipole contribution from the polar C–O bond, not taught)."

### M3. Minor: neopentyl alcohol is a solid at room temperature

- Add to author note b: "2,2-dimethylpropan-1-ol melts at about 52 °C, so it is a solid at room temperature; its boiling point comparison still holds." Prevents an owner-record reader from querying the counterexample.
