## Facts confirmed (no change)

- **Ragusa, 1377 (hook).** (searched) The Major (Great) Council's decree of 27 July 1377 (the *trentino*, or *trentina*) required people and goods from plague-infected areas to spend one month at Mrkan island or Cavtat before entering the city (Tomić and Blažina 2015). "Made travellers from plague areas wait thirty days before they could enter" is correct. "Ragusa, now Dubrovnik" is correct.
- **Quarantine's name.** The word comes from Venetian *quarantena* or Italian *quaranta giorni* ("forty days"). Correct.
- **Plague incubation.** Bubonic plague usually incubates in 2 to 8 days (CDC) or 1 to 7 days (WHO), so "usually fell ill within a week" is correct. The main route of bubonic plague is the bite of infected fleas, usually from rats. Finding 3 hedges this for the medieval period.
- **"Nobody there knew that bacteria existed."** Correct: bacteria were first seen by van Leeuwenhoek in 1676. "No germ theory for another 480 years" (1377 to the late 1850s and 1860s) is fair.
- **North Head.** First use in 1828, the Quarantine Act in 1832, closure in 1984. Class-based quarters and separate "Asiatic" quarters are recorded in the station's history. Codex should confirm the dates of the Asian quarters and the wording "worse" from the conservation management plan, as the design asks. The finding that the cost was "set by race, not risk" is consistent with that history.
- **Sanitation.** London's main drainage (Bazalgette) was built mostly in the 1860s after the Great Stink of 1858, justified largely by miasma arguments. "Removed real sources of disease" is fair.
- **Snow.** The Broad Street outbreak ran from late August to September 1854. The handle was removed on 8 September, after Snow spoke to the Board of Guardians on 7 September, and deaths were already falling. Correct, apart from finding 1's change to the order of events.
- **Variolation.** In China, nasal insufflation of powdered scabs is documented from at least the 16th century. Skin inoculation is documented in India (*tika*), West Africa and the Ottoman Empire. Mortality of about 1 to 2 per cent against 20 to 30 per cent for natural variola major is the standard range (Boston, 1721: about 2 per cent of 244 inoculated people died, against about 14 per cent of natural cases). Variolated people had real smallpox and could transmit it. All correct.
- **Onesimus and Montagu.** Cotton Mather's 1716 letter records that Onesimus described his inoculation in Africa. Mather called him "Garamantee" (Coromantee, on the Gold Coast, now Ghana), so "West African" is correct (Herbert, *Isis* 66:539, 1975, for Codex to open). Mather and Boylston inoculated people in Boston in 1721. Lady Mary Wortley Montagu had her son inoculated in Constantinople in 1718 and her daughter in London in 1721. All correct.
- **Jenner.** On 14 May 1796, Jenner inoculated James Phipps with cowpox. Cowpox is a related orthopoxvirus and does not cause or spread smallpox. Correct (earlier users such as Jesty, 1774, are an optional author note).
- **Smallpox eradication.** The 33rd World Health Assembly declared eradication on 8 May 1980, after certification in December 1979. The last naturally acquired case was Ali Maow Maalin, Merca, Somalia, with rash onset on 26 October 1977. The 1978 Birmingham laboratory case is correctly excluded by "natural". WHO adopted the bifurcated needle from 1968. All correct.
- **Semmelweis.** In 1847 in Vienna, chlorinated lime hand washing cut maternal deaths. His idea met resistance partly because it conflicted with prevailing theories. "Largely ignored, partly because" is hedged correctly.
- **Surveillance and sewage testing.** Australia has notifiable disease reporting (NNDSS), and SARS-CoV-2 wastewater surveillance ran from 2020 in several jurisdictions, with public alerts from catchments with no known cases. "Sometimes warned ... before any cases were known" is correct as hedged. Codex is to cite one dated alert, as the design asks.
- **Aboriginal community-controlled health services.** NACCHO co-chaired the national Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Advisory Group on COVID-19 (Crooks et al. 2020). "Helped plan the national response" is correct and conservative.
- **Attempt 1.** 8 / 400 = 2 per cent and 300 / 1,200 = 25 per cent, more than a tenfold cut. The individual against community judgement is correct.
- **Attempt 2.** Correct: rats and fleas in the cargo, wharf workers exposed first, passengers not the route. It echoes Sydney's 1900 outbreak, whose first case was a carter at the wharves, without naming it.
- **Absolute words.** "couldn't spread smallpox" (cowpox) and "wasn't beaten by vaccinating everyone" are correct (finding 11 adds a tag). Findings 3, 9, 10 and 13 cover the rest.
