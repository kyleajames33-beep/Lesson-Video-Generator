## Findings

1. **Should fix, attempt 2 (the angle-to-gradient step for an obtuse angle is never taught).** The card asks for "the point where the tangent is inclined at 135°". Scene 6 teaches only gradient to angle for a falling line (the acute angle from tan α = |m|, then θ = 180° − α). Scene 7 goes from angle to gradient only for 45°, and the memory card says "Angle → gradient: tan". With Year 10 tan only (root ruling 2), tan 135° means nothing to the student, so they must invent the reverse of scene 6 during a 10 s countdown. The crossed slip "m = 1" is the predictable result of following the card literally. Fix: add two sentences at the end of scene 6, before the forward reference: "Going the other way, an angle bigger than ninety degrees means a falling line. Take it from a hundred and eighty to get the acute angle, and the gradient is minus the tan of that." Show a grey row "θ = 135° → acute 45° → m = −tan 45° = −1" (about 8 s; scene 4's reserve trim pays for it). Extend the memory card's second line to "Falling line: θ = 180° − the acute angle; m = −tan(acute angle)." and key note 4 to match.

2. **Optional, scene 3 (the driveway breaks its own limit, and the narration never says so).** The steepest gradient is 9/32 ≈ 0.28, above the supposed limit of ¼, and the shaded stretch shows it.
