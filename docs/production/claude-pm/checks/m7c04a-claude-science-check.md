## Bromine water

- Ethene with bromine water gives mainly 2-bromoethanol, with some 1,2-dibromoethane, because water takes part (Chemguide: "most of the product is 2-bromoethanol ... there will still be some 1,2-dibromoethane formed"). The design's note d is correct.
- Narration: "Bromine water is orange because of the bromine in it. When an alkene adds that bromine, the colour disappears." True for either product. Writing ethene + Br₂ → 1,2-dibromoethane is the standard HSC equation and is presented as the reaction with bromine, not as the bromine water product. No change needed; the key note 2 bracket "(room temperature; bromine water turns orange → colourless)" is acceptable at this level.
- "That's the test for a double bond": absolute; alkynes also decolourise bromine water (M2).
- "Orange" for bromine water: standard HSC wording (often "orange-brown" or "yellow-orange"); "colourless" for the result is correct.

## Margarine history (hook)

- 1869: Mège-Mouriès patented oleomargarine, made from beef tallow (patent BF 86480, 15 July 1869).
- Hydrogenation of fats: Sabatier's nickel catalysis (about 1897 to 1901); Normann's fat-hardening patent (German patent 141,029, 1902; British 1903); industrial hardened fat from about 1909 to 1911.
- Chemguide: some margarine is made by partly hydrogenating oils with a nickel catalyst; partial hydrogenation forms trans fats; not all margarine is made this way.
- Narration: "For much of the last century, that's how margarine was made." True (the twentieth century), with no claim about current spreads. Correction 2 is a real site error.

## Absolute claims

- "In an alkane, every carbon already has four single bonds, so there's no spare bond for a new atom": true.
- "two molecules join to make one, and no atoms are lost": true by definition of addition.
- "No catalyst is needed, and it's fast at room temperature" (scene 5, halogens): true for bromine; acceptable for chlorine.
- "This one's also fast at room temperature" (scene 6, HX): overstated for HCl with ethene (M1).
- "That's the test for a double bond" (scene 5): overstated (M2).
- "every atom of the reagent ends up on the two old double-bond carbons" (scene 9): true for addition across one C=C.
- Feedback 2: "But-one-ene doesn't fit, because it can give two alcohols": true only as "two possible alcohols"; against the question's wording "only one alcohol is formed" it is contestable (B1).
- No other absolute claims found.

## Findings

### B1. Blocker: attempt 2 crosses an answer that real chemistry supports

- Exact text (card): "only one alcohol is formed." Narration: "only one alcohol forms." Feedback 2 crosses "X = but-1-ene" and says "But-one-ene doesn't fit, because it can give two alcohols."
- Problem: acid-catalysed hydration of but-1-ene gives almost only butan-2-ol (Markovnikov), so "only one alcohol is formed" is true of but-1-ene in practice, and 2-methylpropene likewise gives almost only 2-methylpropan-2-ol. The site's own enrichment teaches this, so a well-prepared student can answer but-1-ene and be told, in narration, that the chemistry is wrong. The fix is one phrase of the premise, before recording.
- Replacement (card): "When X reacts with steam in the presence of a phosphoric acid catalyst at high temperature and pressure, only one alcohol product is possible." Narration (scene 13): "When X reacts with steam, using a phosphoric acid catalyst at high temperature and pressure, only one alcohol product is possible." Feedback 2 underline: "only one alcohol product is possible".
- Feedback 2 can stay as written ("giving **two** possible alcohols"; "because it can give two alcohols"). Optional author note b addition: "In practice, acid-catalysed hydration of but-1-ene and 2-methylpropene gives almost only the Markovnikov alcohol; attempt 2 therefore says 'possible'."

### S1. Should fix: correction 1(b) overstates the site's error

- Replace correction 1(b)'s first sentence ("But-1-ene can give two alcohols ...") with: "(b) The question's 'single alcohol' cannot identify but-1-ene: but-1-ene can give two possible alcohols (butan-2-ol mainly, by Markovnikov's rule, which the site labels enrichment, and butan-1-ol), while but-2-ene, being symmetrical, gives butan-2-ol as its only possible product. The site's part (c) itself says both alkenes give butan-2-ol, so its data do not decide between them." Keep the "Correct:" text, changing "Only one alcohol forms" to "Only one alcohol product is possible".
- Reason: the owner record should not say that Markovnikov hydration of but-1-ene forms two alcohols in comparable amounts.
