## Facts confirmed (no change)

- **Snow and the pump.** Snow presented his evidence to the parish board on 7 September 1854 and the handle was removed on 8 September. The outbreak was already declining, and the design correctly claims only that removal "broke the water link". Death tolls in sources vary (about 500 in the first ten days; 616 is the commonly cited outbreak total). The hook gives no figure. (searched, sources in finding 7)
- **Direct contact.** Tinea by skin contact (also shared items; "skin touching skin" is one route, acceptable). HIV by sex and mother to child. Correct.
- **Indirect contact.** Norovirus persists on surfaces; cholera in water; *Salmonella* in undercooked poultry; measles particles stay suspended up to about two hours (CDC); TB droplet nuclei remain airborne for hours. Correct.
- **Vectors.** Female *Anopheles* carry *Plasmodium*. "Develops inside the mosquito, so the mosquito is part of its life cycle" is correct (gut-wall oocysts, then sporozoites in the salivary glands). "A vector is alive. A contaminated object is a fomite." Correct.
- **Fimbriae.** Short protein filaments that bind host surface molecules and resist removal by mucus and urine flow (for example uropathogenic *E. coli* type 1 and P fimbriae). Correct.
- **Viral receptors.** SARS-CoV-2 spike binds ACE2 on airway cells; influenza haemagglutinin binds sialic acid receptors. Correct.
- **Scene 7 compare.** "The bacterium can multiply on that surface, while the virus has to get inside the cell." Correct with "can".
- **Pork tapeworm.** Scolex with hooks and four suckers attaches to the small intestine; eggs leave in faeces; pigs eat them and cysticerci form in muscle; undercooked pork infects people; thorough cooking or freezing kills cysts. Correct. (searched, CDC PHIL above)
- **Influenza** infects airway epithelium, and coughing and sneezing expel virus. Correct.
- **Tobacco mosaic virus** is very stable and spreads mechanically on tools and hands. "A coat so tough that it survives on pruning tools" is acceptable (the rigid helical capsid protects the RNA).
- **Controls.** Correct as listed. Surface cleaning has little effect on airborne TB; ventilation and respiratory protection are the controls.
- **Attempt 1, Ross River virus.** An alphavirus carried by mosquitoes (key vectors *Aedes vigilax*, *Ae. camptorhynchus*, *Culex annulirostris*, with others locally), macropods the main vertebrate hosts, not spread directly person to person; cases rise after heavy rain increases breeding sites. Larval control is the standard council approach. The generic banded mosquito plate (inspected) avoids implying *Anopheles*. Correct, with finding 13. (searched) CDC EID above; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_River_fever ; CSIRO mosquitoes and floods https://www.csiro.au/en/research/health-medical/diseases/Infectious-diseases/Mosquitoes-and-floods
- **Attempt 2.** Cholera toxin causes profuse watery diarrhoea that sheds very large numbers of bacteria; spread is faecal-oral through water and food (WHO cholera fact sheet, for Codex to open). Influenza as given. Correct with finding 2.
- **Absolute words.** "nothing in between" (direct), "A vector is alive", "every link is a place where the chain can break", "isn't passed directly between people" (Ross River virus) are true. "Every pathogen" (finding 3) and "one link" (finding 9) are flagged. No other hit is a problem.
- **Memory aids.** "Entry gets it in; transmission gets it across" is sound once "entry" means getting in and holding on at the entry site (learning review finding 2).
