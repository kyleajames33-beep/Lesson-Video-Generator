## Findings

1. **Should fix, the video gives two different answers to its own main question.** The main question is why CVD deaths have fallen. In scene 2 the hook's ink slot fills "treatment keeps people alive", and scene 9's memory line reads "Deaths down, prevalence up: treatment is working". Scene 8 then says "Cardiovascular disease is mostly a prevention story." A student leaves unsure whether deaths fell because of treatment or prevention, and scene 4 has just shown that both lower mortality. Fix: fill the hook's ink slot with "fewer new cases (prevention) and more survive (treatment)", and in scene 2 add "Deaths fall for both reasons: fewer heart attacks happen, and more people survive the ones that do." (about 4 s). In scene 8 say "Cardiovascular disease is largely a prevention story: fewer people now develop it" and keep the science check on "mostly", which the design already flags. The memory line is fine for prevalence, but write it as "Deaths down, prevalence up: survival is improving" so it does not claim treatment caused the whole fall. Students should be able to answer the main question with both causes and know which measure separates them.

2. **Note, 10b introduces "stage at diagnosis".** The feedback asks students to check stage at diagnosis, which the body never mentions. It is correct and useful; add a three-word grey tag on scene 4's diagnosis station, "stage: how far spread", so the idea has been seen before the feedback relies on it.

3. **Note, 10c's spoken demand drops two parts of the card.** The narration asks which measure moves first, who benefits soonest and whether the gap narrows or widens; the card also asks which moves last and for the strongest argument against. The card stays up, but students listen more than they read. Say "which measure moves first and last" and "and the strongest argument against it" (about 2 s).

4. **Note, scene 7 "Heart disease is under-recognised in women" has no because.** The reason ("symptoms can differ") is silent. Speak it: "partly because symptoms can differ, and it's seen as a men's disease." (about 3 s)

5. **Note, scenes 5 and 6 are chip-heavy.** Scene 5 drops about fifteen chips (some silent) and scene 6 writes five-item chips in each band. The tray and circles carry them, but drop the silent extras ("salt", "chronic stress", "hepatitis", "diesel exhaust", "primary care") if the frames look crowded at playback speed.
