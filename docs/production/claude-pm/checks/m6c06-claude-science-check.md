## Arithmetic

- Water: [H₂O] = 997 g/L ÷ 18.02 g/mol = 55.3 mol/L (55.5 with 1000 g/L); 1.0 × 10⁻⁷ ÷ 55.3 = 1.8 × 10⁻⁹. Correct.

## Findings

### S1. Should fix: "two water molecules in a billion are ionised" (scene 9)

- Exact text: "only about **two water molecules in a billion** are ionised at any moment"; tag "about 2 in a billion".
- Problem: each transfer turns two water molecules into ions (one H₃O⁺, one OH⁻). 1.8 × 10⁻⁹ counts the donors; counting every molecule now present as an ion gives about 3.6 × 10⁻⁹, "about four in a billion". "Ionised" leaves the count ambiguous.
- Replacement: "only about **two water molecules in a billion** have given a proton away at any moment"; tag "about 2 in a billion have given H⁺"; record the counting in author note (c).

### M1. Minor: "never takes it back" (scene 5)

- Exact text: "chloride, has almost **no pull** on a proton and never takes it back."
- Problem: Cl⁻'s basicity is negligible, not zero, and C05 scene 4 says "almost never".
- Replacement: "so it almost never takes one back" (included in learning review S1).

### M2. Minor (no change): the hook's single arrow with hydroxide

- HCO₃⁻ + OH⁻ → CO₃²⁻ + H₂O has K ≈ 4.7 × 10³; a single arrow is the HSC convention and the site's form. Author note (a) records it. No learner change.

### M3. Minor (no change): "mildly basic" without a reason (scene 8)

- Correct as a fact (pH about 8.3); the reason (Kb of HCO₃⁻ ≈ 2.2 × 10⁻⁸ greater than Ka2 4.7 × 10⁻¹¹) needs Ka and Kb (chem-m6-c10). The learning review's S5 links it to C01's statement. Salt hydrolysis stays out (decision 2).
