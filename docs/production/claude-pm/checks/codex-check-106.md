## C10: esterification

1. **Scenes 8 and 11, key note 4 and both memory-aid copies: heat is grouped with the catalyst as affecting rate only.** The screen says “catalyst and heat: faster, not more”. A catalyst leaves the equilibrium constant unchanged; a temperature change can change it. The author's note already acknowledges this temperature dependence. Correct the repeated line to: “A catalyst reaches equilibrium sooner without changing the equilibrium yield. Heating speeds the reaction.” Remove heat from the claim about an unchanged yield. Source: [experimental equilibrium investigation of this exact reaction](https://chemlab.truman.edu/physical-chemistry/physical-chemistry-laboratory/k-of-esterification/), which measures equilibrium constants at different temperatures and obtains the reaction enthalpy from them.

## B13: dengue control

1. **Scene 2 narration and key note 1 call the diseases their pathogens.** “Dengue is a virus” identifies a disease as an organism. “Malaria is the other choice: a protozoan, Plasmodium” repeats the problem. Correct to: “Dengue is caused by a virus, spread mainly by Aedes aegypti mosquitoes.” Then: “Malaria is the other choice: it is caused by Plasmodium, a protozoan parasite, spread by Anopheles.” Apply the same distinction in key note 1. Sources: [WHO dengue](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/dengue-and-severe-dengue), [WHO malaria](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/malaria).

2. **Scene 3 visual gives a period example for a point definition.** “2 in 1,000 people ill this week” does not match the stated prevalence “at one time” and “at one moment”. Correct the example to: “2 in 1,000 people ill today.” Source: [CDC, Principles of Epidemiology, lesson 3, section 2](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/csels/dsepd/ss1978/lesson3/section2.html), distinguishing point prevalence from period prevalence.

3. **Decisions, site correction 8: the proposed replacement mislabels the follow-up.** The quoted approximately 70%, 95%, 49% and 51% serotype estimates are not the cumulative 4.5-year estimates in Tricou et al. 2024. Do not forward that replacement as written. Correct to: “Over 4.5 years, TAK-003 had 61.2% efficacy against virologically confirmed dengue overall and 84.1% against hospitalised dengue. Efficacy varied with serotype and previous infection; no DENV-3 efficacy was shown in participants never infected before, and DENV-4 data in that group were insufficient.” The original study’s Figure 2 gives the serostatus-specific estimates, not the four values claimed in the design. Sources: [Tricou et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38245116/), [original article, Figure 2](https://bsmedia.business-standard.com/_media/bs/data/general-file-upload/2025-04/Long-term%20efficacy%20and%20safety%20of%20a%20tetravalent%20dengue%20vaccine.pdf).

## B14: historical disease control

1. **Scene 8 narration and its author note imply drinking-water treatment began after germ theory.** “Germ theory explained why earlier measures worked, so people could aim at the real route. Cholera spreads in water, not bad smells, so cities began treating their drinking water.” Municipal filtration predates germ theory and Koch's identification of the cholera bacterium. London's 1852 Act required filtration by the end of 1855. Correct to: “Germ theory explained why earlier measures worked, so people could aim at the real route. Cholera spreads in water, not bad smells. Filtering and disinfecting drinking water breaks that route.” Replace the author note's claim that filtration spread only after Koch with the actual earlier filtration date and later use of disinfection. Source: [Metropolis Water Act 1852, section IV, original statute](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Vict/15-16/83/pdfs/ukpga_18520083_en.pdf), printed page 419; [Geological Society's source-based history of the Act](https://www.geolsoc.org.uk/the-library/online-exhibitions/monster-soup-water-supply-in-the-metropolis/1852-metropolis-water-act/).
