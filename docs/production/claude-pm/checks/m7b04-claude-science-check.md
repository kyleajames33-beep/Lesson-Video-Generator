## Facts confirmed (no change)

- **Myrtle rust arrival and spread.** First detected April 2010 at a nursery on the NSW Central Coast; along the east coast to far north Queensland and Victoria within a few years (site L06 and standard accounts). Tasmania and the Northern Territory (Tiwi Islands) are confirmed host areas (SA PIRSA page, searched); the 2015 detection years are for Codex to open. **WA:** confirmed 8 July 2022 (DPIRD release) on a *Melaleuca* in a wetland on a pastoral lease in the East Kimberley, found in June surveillance; thought to have arrived on wind from the Northern Territory (DBCA). Scene 6's "in 2022, northern Western Australia" is correct. **South Australia:** spores trapped (2021) but no confirmed disease as of November 2025 (SA Hansard, searched); the narration rightly names no state as free. (searched) https://www.agric.wa.gov.au/news/media-releases/myrtle-rust-confirmed-kimberley ; https://www.wa.gov.au/government/announcements/myrtle-rust-confirmed-the-kimberley ; https://www.dbca.wa.gov.au/management/threat-management/myrtle-rust ; https://www.pir.sa.gov.au/biosecurity/plant_health/emergency_plant_pests/myrtle_rust ; https://hansardsearch.parliament.sa.gov.au/daily/uh/2025-11-13/29
- **Hosts and spread.** Myrtaceae including eucalypts, paperbarks, tea tree and lemon myrtle; wind-borne spores; plant material, equipment, clothing and vehicles (DPIRD release). Correct.
- **Native guava.** Common and widespread before 2010; few adult trees survive; Critically Endangered (finding 11). "Now critically endangered" is correct.
- **Eradication.** National eradication judged not feasible in late 2010; managed since. "Can't be eradicated" matches the official position.
- **Rust and yield; nematodes.** Correct as summaries (finding 7).
- **FMD.** Virus of cloven-hoofed animals; spread by contact, air, fomites and feed; vesicles in the mouth and on the feet; lameness, reduced feeding, milk and weight gain; low adult mortality, higher in young animals. "Most adults survive" correct.
- **UK 2001 costs.** NAO: public sector over £3 billion and private sector more than £5 billion; NAO (2009) "an estimated £8 billion". Thompson et al. (2002): agriculture and the food chain about £3.1 billion; tourism £2.7 to £3.2 billion. "About eight billion pounds, and tourism lost about as much as farming and the food industry" is correct. Direct (in the sick animals) against indirect (control, movement, export, tourism) is consistent with Knight-Jones and Rushton (finding 6). (searched) NAO links above; https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2009/03/0809288.pdf ; Thompson et al. summary https://agrifood.info/Agrifood/Connections/Winter2002/plunkett.htm
- **Australia's FMD status.** FMD-free; last outbreak 1872 (Victoria). Department of Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry FMD page (for Codex to open; recheck on the voicing date).
- **ABARES 2022.** A large multistate outbreak about $80 billion direct economic impact over 10 years (2020 to 21 dollars, 3 per cent discount rate), an update of the 2013 $52 billion estimate (7 per cent discount rate, so not directly comparable), with losses driven mainly by slow recovery of export market access; control costs small by comparison. "Mostly through lost exports" is correct. (searched) https://agriculture.gov.au/abares/research-topics/biosecurity/biosecurity-economics/fmd-update-of-2013-estimate ; https://www.agriculture.gov.au/abares/research-topics/biosecurity/biosecurity-economics/consequences-foot-mouth-disease-outbreak
- **Fire blight.** Detected in 1997 on ornamental *Cotoneaster* in the Royal Botanic Gardens Melbourne and Adelaide Botanic Garden; destroyed; national surveys found no further infection; EPPO lists Australia as "absent, pest eradicated", and DPIRD states Australia is considered free. The design is right not to use it. (searched) https://gd.eppo.int/taxon/ERWIAM/distribution/AU ; https://web-prd.dpird.wa.gov.au/siteassets/documents/biosecurity/plant/ny11001-fire-blight-biosecurity-threat.pdf
- **Attempt 1.** Hypothetical; 2000 / 40 = 50, so "fifty to one" is correct. The model's direct and indirect split and the endemic qualifier are sound.
- **Attempt 2.** Consistent with rust biology (a new virulent strain overcoming the resistance in a widely grown variety; compare bio-m5-b03b's 1998 to 1999 strain).
- **Absolute words.** "every plant is a clone" (Cavendish, true), "no resistant plants in between" (within one variety, true), "can't be eradicated" (official), "Most adults survive" (true), "usually outweigh" (hedged). Only "only germinate" (finding 5) is flagged.
- **Memory aid.** "Count past the farm gate" is sound as a habit; its limit (endemic disease without trade consequences) is carried in the 12a model.
