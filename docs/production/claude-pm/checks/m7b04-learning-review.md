Main question traced: *How does disease damage farm production, and why does the bill so often reach far past the sick plants and animals?* Useful outcome: given an outbreak, the student can name its biological and agricultural causes, sort its effects into direct (in the infected hosts) and indirect (control and markets), and judge which is larger using size, duration and reach, with a reason tied to the case (for example, export dependence).

## Strengths to keep

- **The farm gate ledger is the lesson's argument made visible.** Chips land on the side of the gate where the cost falls, and the memory line ("Count past the farm gate") names the board's own divider. Students can point at where each cost sits at every moment.
- **The cause strip makes "causes" concrete before "effects".** Pathogen, a host it can infect and conditions fill one per cue, and scene 3 adds the farming half with a causal reason (no resistant plants to slow the run). The mixed-planting path that stops after two plants is a real video beat.
- **Two mechanisms, not bare nouns, for yield loss.** Rust takes sugars and green tissue so less grain fills; galls squeeze the xylem so the plant wilts in moist soil. Both are subject-action sentences.
- **Scene 5 answers a site misconception with the correct cause** ("no shared history, little resistance", not "foreign").
- **The UK 2001 case follows the bill.** Most culled animals were never infected; movement, export and tourism costs follow. Scene 9 then gives the reason exporters suffer most, and the 12a model keeps the endemic qualifier for top answers.
- **Both attempts are genuine transfer** with hypothetical data (Country P; a one-variety district), and each feedback is brief with one crossed mistake.
- **Handoff wording matches b05's hook and question.**
