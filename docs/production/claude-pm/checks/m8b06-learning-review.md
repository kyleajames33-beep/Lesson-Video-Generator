## Findings

### 1. blocker: 2 and decision rule

Text: "An infectious disease is caused by a pathogen ... that can pass from one host to another."

The definition makes person-to-person transmissibility necessary for an infectious disease. Tetanus and environmentally acquired infections are counterexamples; b07/b08 will depend on a sound dividing criterion.

Repair: Replace with "An infectious disease is caused by a pathogen. Many, but not all, can pass between hosts. A non-infectious disease is not caused by a pathogen invading and multiplying in the body." For cancer-trigger discussion keep the distinction between transmissible HPV and the non-transmissible tumour. Decision: classify the disease by its cause, then name a trigger separately. Offset extra words by cutting scene 7's final handoff sentence.

### 2. should fix: 10, scene 12 and memory aid

Text: "what changed first"

A chronological earliest event cannot put melanoma into the cancer category: UV exposure came first, while the author insists cancer is the group. The aid conflicts with the successful worked classification.

Repair: Replace with "What kind of disease is it, and what factor contributed?" Then keep gene/protein/cell/body or exposure/tissue/body as the explanatory chain. Retain "cancer, with an environmental trigger" in the example and attempt.

### 3. should fix: 8

Text: "A nutritional disease comes from the diet."

The iron example teaches low iron as dietary by definition, although poor absorption, blood loss and increased demand can produce the same deficiency. This can misclassify a new case.

Repair: Say "Too little available iron can cause a nutritional deficiency disease." In this case explicitly give low intake if diet is the intended cause. Keep the haemoglobin mechanism. Length-neutral.
