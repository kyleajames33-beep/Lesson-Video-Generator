## Findings

1. **Should fix, scene 5 asks students to hear about sixteen gland and hormone names in about 45 s.** Hypothalamus, pituitary, growth hormone, antidiuretic hormone, thyroid, adrenal glands, adrenaline, cortisol, aldosterone, pancreas, insulin, glucagon, ovaries, testes, oestrogen and testosterone, most of them not used again in the video. Narrate the glands and the four session hormones (adrenaline, glucagon, insulin, cortisol); let the thyroid and the sex hormones write silently on the gland board; and introduce antidiuretic hormone and aldosterone in scene 7, where their job (holding water and salt as you sweat) gives them a reason. This saves about 10 s and keeps each name next to its job.

2. **Should fix, 11c is scene 7's "during" and "after" lanes.** "How is blood glucose kept steady during a long session, and what happens after the meal?" is answered by scenes 6 and 7 almost word for word, and 11a (WE b1, stress) is scene 8's whole answer, so neither item tests transfer. Replace 11c with the water and salt case: "A rower trains for ninety minutes in the heat without drinking. Explain how two hormones help her body hold on to water and sodium, and why she still needs to drink. (4 marks)" Model: sweat lowers body water, so the pituitary releases antidiuretic hormone and the kidneys keep water; sodium is lost, so the adrenal glands release aldosterone and the kidneys keep sodium; both are negative feedback; hormones cut further losses but cannot replace what is gone. Crossed mistake: "the hormones replace the lost water". Students should apply negative feedback to a pair of hormones the video only named.

3. **Note, length.** About 568 s; finding 1 frees time for finding 2's longer feedback.

4. **Note, 11a.** The site's WE b1 verbatim; scene 8 teaches its answer. Keep it as the site's question, labelled "from the lesson".
