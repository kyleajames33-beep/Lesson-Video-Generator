## Main question and useful outcome

*Two parents, one pod of seeds: why are the offspring different from each other, and where does a brand-new allele come from?* Afterwards a student should be able to sort the four sources into reshuffling and new alleles, list gamete combinations for two genes on different chromosomes and for two genes on one chromosome with and without crossing over, multiply at fertilisation, state the germ-line condition for an inherited mutation, and separate genetic from environmental variation.

## Trace from entry knowledge

- **Sources (2).** Three named as met in B5b, in one clause; mutation is set apart as the only maker of new alleles. The card-deck analogy is mapped with its limits.
- **Tracing (3 to 6).** This is the new teaching, and it does not re-teach B5b: B5b's coin flip and swap play once each, by name. The same-chromosome case (4) is a real extension: assortment moves whole chromosomes, so only crossing over separates linked alleles, and only in some cells. The 4 x 4 grid and the 2 to the power 46 ticker build on B5b's "more than 8 million".
- **Mutation and environment (7, 8).** Morgan's white-eyed male is a vivid new-allele case; the gut-cell contrast gives the germ-line condition in plain words. The hydrangea recall from B7 sets up the genetic against environmental test (same conditions, or compare clones).
- **Attempts.** 11a models gametes on unseen letters; 11c separates runner clones from seedlings. Demands stay on screen; nothing answer-bearing.
