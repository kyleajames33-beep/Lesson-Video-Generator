Main question traced: *Bt cotton has been grown for decades; a malaria gene drive has never left a lab cage. Both promise big benefits and both could change biodiversity. So how do you weigh benefits against biodiversity risks, and reach a judgement you can defend?* Useful outcome: given an unseen biotechnology proposal, the student asks the same four questions of it, names which answer pulls the verdict (often reversibility), and writes a verdict with a checkable condition (the route stop: "reach a justified conditional judgement from their evidence").

## Strengths to keep

- **One weighing board with two columns** makes "same questions, different verdicts" visible; the balance tips only at the verdict, never as a score.
- **Scene 5's two-lane strip** (a field that switches back, a species that cannot) is the strongest video beat: the student sees reversibility before the word is used.
- **Scene 7 makes "provided that" checkable**: the resistance bar rising into the amber zone and the verdict easing back shows that a condition is evidence, not decoration. This directly targets the HSC marker-reported fault of a judgement with no specific technology or effect.
- **Earlier tools are reused as cells**, not redrawn: b11's evidence ladder, b12's levels icon and baseline chip, b10's spray-count bars.
- **Both attempts transfer.** 9a tests reversibility with a new mechanism (self-limiting release); 9c applies the whole route stop to a new crop with a wild relative and landraces.
