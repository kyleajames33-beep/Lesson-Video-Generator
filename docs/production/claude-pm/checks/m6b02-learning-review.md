Main question traced: *What does a mutagen physically do to DNA, and why doesn't every exposure end in a mutation?* The question has two parts, but they share one causal chain (agent, then damage, then repaired or copied), and every scene feeds it. That counts as one main question. Useful outcome: the student can name a mutagen's group, say what it physically does to DNA, and finish with "copied before repair, so mutation".

## Strengths to keep

- **One strand board throughout.** Each mutagen acts on the same `SlimStrand`: kink, gap, notched base, blob, slab, dashed insert. Students can compare mechanisms against one stable reference, which is the video advantage.
- **Scene 3 (the repair race) is the causal centre and is shown, not told.** The repaired path and the "copied first" path branch from the same damaged spot, and the second copy gives the wrong base its own partner. Scene 9 and the decision rule both return to it.
- **Causes come before names.** The thymines join and the strand kinks before "thymine dimer" is said. An electron flies off before "ionising" is said.
- **Misconceptions are blocked in context.** "UV is a mutation", "exposure = certain mutation" and "natural = safe" are each crossed at the point where they are answered. No separate mistake/fix scene is needed.
- **Attempt 2 is a genuine transfer and data item.** It uses the site's yeast data, and its key idea (counts measure survival, not mutation) is new reasoning rather than a replay.
- **People.** The design has no people or body figures. The skin is a cross-section tissue block, X-rays are shown as the machine and film, and the depth bar is a layered block "no body silhouette". Pass.
