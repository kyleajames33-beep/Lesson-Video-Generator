## Main question and the useful outcome

Main question (as stated): "Ethanol and methoxymethane are made of exactly the same atoms, yet one is a liquid and the other a gas. How can one molecular formula make different molecules, and how do you tell which kind of isomer you are looking at?"

One main question: yes. Useful outcome: a student can define structural isomers, explain why ethanol boils far higher than methoxymethane, classify a pair as chain, position or functional group isomers (or not isomers) with a reason, including a saturated and an unsaturated hydrocarbon, and list every C₅H₁₂ isomer while rejecting duplicates by naming.

## Trace from the entry knowledge (c01 plus Year 11)

Entry: c01's drawings and naming rules; Year 11 hydrogen bonding (H on O, N or F) and "stronger attractions between molecules raise the boiling point". The trace follows C₂H₆O from the hook to scene 3, then C₄H₈ from scene 5 to scene 7, then C₅H₁₂ to feedback 2.

3. Why the arrangement matters: causal and Year 11-anchored. Student question: "Isn't the ether just smaller or lighter?" The narration never says the two have the same atoms and so similar size, which is what makes hydrogen bonding the reason (M3). The mid-scene screen carries more than three bright text items (S3).
4. Chain isomers: the chain shortener is a good move. "the family stays the same" uses "family" for the first time without saying what it means here (M2).
5. Position isomers: but-1-ene and but-2-ene, then propan-1-ol and propan-2-ol with "-ol" in one line. See "The '-ol' choice" below.

## The "-ol" choice (asked by root)

Giving "-ol" a one-line meaning here is a good choice. Reasons:

- The hook already uses "ethanol", so students meet "-ol" anyway; one line explains a name they have just heard.
- The classic HSC slip (calling propan-1-ol and propan-2-ol functional group isomers; site worked example 3) needs an alcohol pair, and it is the slip scene 8 crosses. A hydrocarbon-only replacement ("but-1-ene and cyclobutane are position isomers") is not a slip students actually make.
- It shows that position isomerism applies to groups other than C=C, which hydrocarbon pairs cannot show.
- The cost is low: the line names only the O–H and the locant ("the number says which carbon carries it"), uses c01's locant idea, and leaves alcohol classification and naming to chem-m7-c05.

One improvement: tie it to the hook (M1). Keep the propanol pair; do not take the open-decision fallback.

## Findings

### S1. Should fix: the ring's family is never named, so "different family" has no referent for cyclobutane

- Exact text (scene 6): "Join the four carbons into a **ring** instead, and you get cyclobutane. It's also C four H eight, but it has only single bonds. One is unsaturated, the other **saturated**."
- Student question: "Scene 8 says 'different family? functional group isomers'. What family is cyclobutane in?"
- Replacement (scene 6): "It's also C four H eight, but it has only single bonds, so it's in a different family: the **cycloalkanes**, rings of carbons with only single bonds. One is unsaturated, the other **saturated**." Add the bracket tag "cycloalkane (no C=C)".
- Replacement (feedback 1): "Cyclopentane is a cycloalkane, a ring with no double bond, so it's a different family from pent-one-ene: **functional group** isomers."
- Understanding enabled: the student can apply scene 8's "different family?" question to a ring against an alkene, which is the dotpoint's saturated and unsaturated case.

### S2. Should fix: feedback 2 does not answer "Evaluate"

- Exact text: "So the student shows only two isomers, and has missed the third: **two, two-dimethylpropane**."
- Student question: "So was the student right or wrong?" An HSC "evaluate" answer needs a judgement.
- Replacement: "So the statement is **incorrect**: the drawings show only two different isomers. The third, **two, two-dimethylpropane**, isn't drawn." Answer chip: "Incorrect: only 2 different isomers shown (A = B); 2,2-dimethylpropane not drawn".
- Understanding enabled: the student models the HSC response shape (judgement, then reasons).

### S3. Should fix: scene 3 mid-scene has about five bright text items

- The "from Year 11: H bonded to O, N or F" chip, the grey "no O–H" tag, the caption "H on O attracted to the O of the next molecule", two thermometer values and the returning plates are all on screen together.
- Repair: do not bring the plates back in scene 3 (the hook has shown them); dim the Year 11 chip when the caption writes; keep the thermometers as small grey values. End state unchanged.
- Understanding enabled: attention stays on the dotted H-bond lines, which carry the cause.

### M1. Minor: link "-ol" to the hook

- Scene 5 replacement: "The ending **ol** means an O H, which is why ethanol ends in ol, and the number says which carbon carries it".

### M2. Minor: first use of "family"

- Scene 4 replacement: "The carbon skeleton changes, but the family, here the alkanes, stays the same."

### M3. Minor: make the boiling point comparison fair

- Scene 3, after "so its molecules **can't** hydrogen bond to each other.": "They have the same atoms, so they're about the same size. The hydrogen bonds make the difference." Then "Weaker attractions between molecules mean a lower boiling point."

### M4. Minor: scene 9 placement reasons

- On "carbon two", let the chain shortener's ghost positions grey out visibly with two small tags: "C1: just a longer chain" and "C3: C2 from the other end" (dim before scene 10). No narration change; scene 10 then names both.

### M5. Minor: feedback 1 underlines

- Underline "chain, position or functional group" and each pair's names as they are used, not only "(a)".

### M6. Minor: YouTube title over 100 characters

- Current title is 110 characters. Replacement (98): "Structural Isomers: Chain, Position and Functional Group, and Finding Them All | Year 12 Chemistry".

### M7. Minor: internal references

- "Numbers used": the "Where it appears" column is off by one or more scenes (A1 is 12 and 13, A2 is 14 and 15, duplicates 10, chain pair 4, 11 and 16, position pair 2 is 5 and 8; the C₅H₁₂ set is 9, 10, 12 and 14, not 8). Assets: the camping plate is in scenes 4 and 16, not 15.
