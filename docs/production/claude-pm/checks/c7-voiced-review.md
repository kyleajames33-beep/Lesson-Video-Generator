## Student learning trace (entry knowledge: C3 shifts, C4a gas counts, C4b catalyst changes speed not destination)

- Main question (hook): "Why choose conditions that make less ammonia in the settled mixture?" Useful outcome: justify a stated industrial condition by yield, rate and limit. The hook states the puzzle against the highest-yield setting and the lesson answers it.
- Recall is on a switchboard where each control visibly moves a gauge; the new term "equilibrium yield" is defined on screen as spoken. The removal case, where students may confuse yield with total made, has its own contrast (yield stays put, total rises).
- The causal chain: cold gives a high ceiling but a slow climb, the plant is paid per pass not per settled day, the iron makes the middle temperature fast enough without moving the ceiling, pressure helps both but costs rise faster than gains, only part reacts per pass so condense and recycle. The weakest link is the claim that pressure raises the rate (SHOULD FIX 2 and 3): the student is told, but sees no crowding and no gauge change.

- The attempt is a real transfer (new reaction, a student claim to reject, three conditions to justify) with all conditions visible and a 10 s countdown; feedback names four likely wrong answers with a cause each.
- Honest scope: no K; later-video markers are explicit.
