## Blocker

**D13-RESIST, scene 4, "Every value counts, so one odd day can't take over the way it does with the range", with scene 3's set-up ("We need a measure of spread that listens to every value") and cause chain item (3) ("so one value has less effect").** This teaches that σ resists an extreme value, which is false, and the video's own example disproves it. With the breakdown day, Bus A's σ jumps from 0.58 to 4.14 minutes, larger than Bus B's 3.11. So σ reverses the comparison exactly as the range did (12 against 8), the very failure scene 3 uses to motivate σ. d19 then teaches the opposite, correctly: "an outlier pulls the mean, the range and σ" and "one big distance counts heavily". A student who checks the breakdown data on the calculator, or who reaches d19, meets a contradiction in the strand's core idea about spread. Fix: motivate σ by what it uses, not by resistance. Keep scene 3's breakdown as the range's weakness, then in scene 4 say that σ uses every value's distance from the mean, so it describes how the whole set spreads, not just its two ends. A clean picture: Bus B (0, 1, 2, 6, 7, 8) against a Bus C (0, 4, 4, 4, 4, 8): the same range (8) but σ 3.11 against 2.31, because most of Bus C's days sit right on the mean. Replace "one odd day can't take over" with "An extreme value still pulls σ, as we'll see later; the measure that ignores the ends is the IQR, coming soon." Update cause chain item (3) to "the standard deviation combines every value's distance from the mean into one typical distance, so it reflects the whole dataset, not just its two ends".
