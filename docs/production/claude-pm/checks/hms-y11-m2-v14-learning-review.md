## Findings

1. **Should fix, 9c is the module's fourth pass at the hot-day mechanism.** Sweat lowers plasma volume, stroke volume falls and heart rate rises: v05 scene 9 taught it, v05 12c tested it (the hot netballer), v08 scene 8 and v11 scene 8 retaught it. The design calls 9c "an unworked mechanism", but students have worked it twice. Use 9c for the one distinction this video names and never practises: "After eight weeks of training, a student's resting heart rate has fallen from 72 to 64 bpm. In each session her heart rate rises from about 64 to 150 bpm, and it starts rising before she moves. Classify each change as an immediate response or an adaptation, and explain the rise before she moves. (4 marks)" Model: the resting fall is an adaptation (lasting, months); the session rise is an immediate response (reverses after); the early rise is the anticipatory rise (sympathetic nerves and adrenaline); answering one with the other loses marks. Crossed mistake: "the lower resting heart rate is a response to the session". Cardiovascular drift can stay as a named clause in scene 4 or 5 if wanted.

2. **Should fix, scene 3 and v04 scene 9 give different main triggers for breathing.** v04: "Carbon dioxide, not low oxygen, is the main trigger." v14: breathing rises within seconds "because the brain signals the breathing muscles at the same moment as the working muscles", with carbon dioxide later. Fix v04 as its review proposes, and add one bridge here: "Earlier, you saw carbon dioxide drives breathing. That's true at rest and later in exercise, but the first rise comes from the brain." Students should hold one consistent account across the two videos.

3. **Note, scene 3 is long.** About 880 characters (about 66 s), with ventilation, depth against rate, the drive and the restored hyperventilation point. Keep root's ruling, but give the safety point its own visual beat (the pool block plate, gauges) so it does not read as part of the drive explanation.

4. **Note, "as the brake on the heart comes off" (scene 2).** Tie it to v07 in a word: "as the parasympathetic brake comes off", so students connect the steep rise to the nerve that slows the heart afterwards.
