## Main question and the useful outcome

Main question (as stated): "A pH meter reads 1.00 in a strong acid and 13.00 in a strong base of the same concentration. Where do those numbers come from, how can a base have a pH at all, and what happens to the pH when you dilute a solution or mix an acid with a base?"

One main question: yes, with a caution. The question has three clauses because the route joins two dotpoints (pH and pOH; dilution and mixing). They share one idea, "find [H₃O⁺] or [OH⁻], then take the negative log", and the hook's meter pair carries the first two clauses through scenes 1, 2, 5 and 7, so the video reads as one question: "how do you get the pH of a strong acid or base, including after dilution or mixing?" The temperature scene (6) is the one detour; it is justified because the route moved Kw here and it fixes the "pH 7 always means neutral" misconception. No split needed. Useful outcome: a student can calculate pH, pOH, [H₃O⁺] and [OH⁻] for strong acids and bases, report pH to the right number of decimal places, explain why a base has a pH and why neutral pH changes with temperature, and calculate pH after dilution or after mixing a strong acid with a strong base.

## Findings

### S1. Should fix: add the sulfuric acid condition, and stop the decision rule counting hydrogen ions (project manager decision 1)

- Exact text (scene 18): "So for any strong acid or base: count the hydrogen or hydroxide ions per formula, work in moles"; screen "1 Strong? Then count H⁺ or OH⁻ per formula unit."
- Student question: "Sulfuric acid has two hydrogens. So 0.010 mol/L sulfuric acid is 0.020 mol/L of H⁺?"
- Replacement (scene 8, after the classic slip): "Sulfuric acid is different: only its first hydrogen ion comes off completely. HSC questions usually say to **assume both do**, and only then is the hydrogen ion concentration twice the acid's." Chip: "H₂SO₄: first H⁺ complete, second only partly." then "Only if the question says assume both H⁺ are released completely: [H⁺] = 2 × c." Scene 18 counts hydroxide ions only; screen line 1 "Strong? Then [H₃O⁺] = c (HCl, HNO₃), or count OH⁻ per formula unit." Key note 3 adds "H₂SO₄: [H⁺] = 2c only if told to assume both H⁺ are released completely."
- Understanding enabled: students can answer the HSC-style sulfuric question when its assumption is given, without learning that both protons ionise completely (C05 already said "sulfuric, for its first proton").

### S2. Should fix: say what the log does and why the minus is there (scene 2)

- Exact text: "So the pH is **one**. The log turns each factor of ten into **one pH step**."; visual "no cue word; the narration no longer explains the sign".
- Student question: "How does 10⁻¹ become 1?"
- Replacement: "The **log** of ten to the minus one is its power, minus one, and the minus sign makes it positive: pH **one**. So each factor of ten is **one pH step**." Screen: on "log" a small line "log 10⁻¹ = −1" with the power circled; on "one" it flips to "−log 10⁻¹ = +1".
- Understanding enabled: pH is read as "the power of ten, made positive", which every later step uses.

### S3. Should fix: word the decimal-places rule for [H₃O⁺], and give the three-decimal reason at its first use

- Exact text (scene 3): "So **decimal places in pH equal significant figures** in the concentration."; rule card "Decimal places in pH = significant figures in the concentration."; key note 1 the same. Scene 9 gives "two point zero zero zero" with no reason; scene 12 says "three decimal places for three significant figures".
- Student question (C08): "0.150 mol/L has three significant figures. Why is the propanoic acid pH 2.85?" And here: "Why does 2.000 have three decimal places?"
- Replacement: scene 3 "decimal places in pH equal **significant figures** in the hydronium concentration, two here"; rule card and key note 1 "... significant figures in [H₃O⁺]". Scene 9 "so the pH is **two point zero zero zero**, three decimal places for three significant figures"; drop the repeat in scene 12 (its answer chip tag stays).
- Understanding enabled: one rule that works for strong acids now and for weak acids (x) in C08.
