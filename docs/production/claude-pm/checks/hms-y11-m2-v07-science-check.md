## Findings
1. **Should fix (scene 7 narration, "That's where your wobble is corrected", and on-screen "decided in the spinal cord").** Balance corrections in standing use spinal stretch reflexes first, but most of the correction comes from fast automatic responses through the brain stem and cerebellum (vestibular and long-latency postural responses), still before any conscious decision. Corrected narration: "That's where your wobble starts to be corrected: fast reflexes in the spinal cord, then automatic responses in the brain stem and cerebellum, all before you decide anything." On screen: "corrected before you decide". The hook's question ("where is that decision made?") is then answered as "below conscious control". 11d's model (reflex first, then the cerebellum) already fits.
2. **Should fix (scene 3 narration).** "that chemical step is the slow part of the whole route." One synaptic delay is about half a millisecond, while conduction along a metre of axon takes about 15 to 20 ms, so the chemical step is not the slowest part of a long route. What is true is that each synapse adds a delay. Corrected: "The signal crosses a synapse as a chemical, and each chemical crossing adds a small delay." On screen "each synapse costs time" already fits. Scene 7 and 11b ("more synapses, each a slow chemical step") stay true with this framing; optionally "each adding a delay".
3. **Note (scene 4).** Textbook innervation ratios for eye muscles are usually given as "about ten" (some sources lower). "about ten fibres or fewer" avoids a teacher's query; on screen "eye: about 10 fibres".
4. **Note (scene 7).** "usually passes through one connecting neuron": many reflexes (withdrawal) use several. "at least one connecting neuron" is more exact.

## Verdict
Pass with fixes. 0 blockers, 2 should fix, 2 notes.
