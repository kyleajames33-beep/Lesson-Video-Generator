## Findings

1. **Blocker. NSW permissions and the risk row allowing student dichromate use.** "Many schools let only the teacher handle the solid" and "students use small volumes" are not established NSW permission rules. Public CSIS Appendix F refers dichromate to chromate and describes carcinogenicity and precautions, but the current Appendix D use categories require staff access and were not verified. Before a school method is approved, obtain the actual current entry and local risk assessment for acidified dichromate at the stated concentration. Until then state teacher demonstration subject to the school's approved assessment rather than assert student permission. Do not confuse banned concentrated chromic-acid cleaning mixture with every dilute dichromate oxidation. [CSIS pp 117 to 118](https://education.nsw.gov.au/content/dam/main-education/asset-management/chemical-safety/5._Volume_2_Appendices.pdf). This is an unresolved permission claim, not a claim that dilute dichromate is universally banned.
2. **Should fix. Risk table, incomplete and concentration-mixed H codes.** Add the missing relevant statements using a named product and concentration. The design's dichromate list omits H335 from Sigma P5271; 1 M sulfuric acid also carries H290; tert-butanol includes H332 and H335 (Sigma 360538 also H336); propanal includes H302 and H332; Benedict's reagent is not merely dilute copper sulfate and Sigma 11945 carries H319/H411. Identify stock reagents separately from working mixtures. Do not apply 25% ammonia's full classification to diluted freshly prepared Tollens' solution without its recipe. [Dichromate](https://www.sigmaaldrich.com/FM/en/product/sigald/p5271), [1 M acid](https://www.scharlab.com/ph/en/chemicals/volumetric-solutions/sulfuric_acid_solution_1_mol_l_2_n_7015), [tert-butanol](https://www.sigmaaldrich.com/US/en/product/sigald/360538), [propanal](https://www.sigmaaldrich.com/US/en/product/aldrich/p51451), [Benedict's](https://www.sigmaaldrich.com/CY/en/product/sial/11945).
3. **Should fix. Scene 8 footer and scene 9 receiver.** "All three turned dichromate green" is false if read as propanal, propanoic acid and propanone reacting. Propanone and propanoic acid do not ordinarily reduce dichromate in these tests. Identify the starting alcohols as the species that reduced it. A collected distillate is not "organic product only": water and volatile impurities can co-distil. Label it "product-containing distillate, separated from non-volatile sulfuric acid/chromium salts". These changes retain the useful validity explanation. [RSC alcohol experiments](https://edu.rsc.org/alcohols-and-carboxylic-acids/the-properties-of-alcohols/463.article).
4. **Should fix. Scene 10, faintly acidic aldehyde.** "Probably distilled too slowly" is not diagnosed by a faint red litmus result alone. Acid contamination, entrainment, oxidation during storage and method errors remain possible. Keep it as an investigation prompt: "A red litmus result suggests acid contamination; check prompt distillation, clean apparatus and sample handling." Do not teach one uncertain cause as the expected diagnosis. Reflux product collection and boiling-point readings also support rather than prove identity.

## Verified chemistry and arithmetic

Propan-1-ol/propan-2-ol are C₃H₈O structural isomers; no C3 tertiary alcohol exists. 2-methylpropan-2-ol is C₄H₁₀O and is unchanged in the ordinary warm acidified-dichromate class test, so the solution remains orange. Its near-room-temperature melting point makes pre-warming to dispense sensible. Propanal/propanone are C₃H₆O, propanoic acid C₃H₆O₂, and pentan-2-one C₅H₁₀O. All four [O] equations balance. Boiling points about 48 to 49, 56, 82 and 97 °C are appropriate rounded values; 97 minus 48 = 49 °C. A mixed aqueous distillate need not show the pure compound's exact boiling point. [OSHA propionaldehyde](https://www.osha.gov/chemicaldata/922), [Sigma tert-butanol](https://www.sigmaaldrich.com/US/en/product/sigald/360538).

Propanal gives positive Tollens' and alkaline copper tests; propanone is negative; glucose is a valid positive reducing-sugar control. Glucose's aldehyde form explains its reaction, but it is not a reference acid for litmus. [RSC glucose silver mirror](https://edu.rsc.org/experiments/the-silver-mirror-test-with-tollens-reagent/822.article), [Wuppertal direct propanal/glucose comparison](https://chemiedidaktik.uni-wuppertal.de/de/digitale-medien/videos-zu-klassischen-schulversuchen/allgemeine-chemie-ii-organische-chemie/tollens-probe/).

Other stated stock classifications checked: propan-1-ol H225/H318/H336 ([Sigma 09158](https://www.sigmaaldrich.com/US/en/product/sial/09158)); propan-2-ol H225/H319/H336 ([437522](https://www.sigmaaldrich.com/US/en/product/sial/437522)); propanone H225/H319/H336 ([443638](https://www.sigmaaldrich.com/US/en/product/sial/443638)); propanoic acid H226/H314 and supplier-dependent H335 ([402907](https://www.sigmaaldrich.com/US/en/product/sial/402907?context=prod)); silver nitrate H272/H314/H410 ([Safe Work Australia](https://hcis.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/HazardousChemical/Details?chemicalID=4107)). [Sodium hydroxide 1 M](https://www.sigmaaldrich.com/US/en/product/sial/s2567) H290/H314 is compatible with its corrosive preparation role; the exact concentration is not specified, so its working-mixture classification remains unverified. Fresh Tollens' reagent, prompt disposal and no storage are sound; no school disposal method is approved by this review.

## School method and site check

The 60 °C, five-minute colour-sort and 20-minute reflux are proposed conditions, not a validated local school procedure. The small-volume method must follow the school assessment. No-flame heating, open condensers, clamping, anti-bumping granules, ice-cooled propanal collection and separate waste streams are sensible controls. The current Appendix D entries and exact Tollens'/Benedict's formulations are the remaining safety-source limits.
