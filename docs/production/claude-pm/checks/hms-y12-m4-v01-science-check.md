Independent check; the design was not edited. Line numbers refer to `lessons/hms-y12-m4-v01.md` as of this check.

## Confirmed
- **APSS (flagged).** Checked against the APSS V2 (2019) form itself (ESSA, Fitness Australia, SMA and Exercise is Medicine; copy hosted by SMA). Stage 1 is "COMPULSORY" and may be self-administered; questions 1 to 6 are as scene 3a lists them; a YES to any of the six means "seek guidance from an appropriate allied health professional or medical practitioner prior to undertaking exercise" (scene 4, 8b: correct). If all six are NO, the client proceeds to question 7. Question 7: "Total minutes = (minutes of light + moderate) + (2 x minutes of vigorous/high)"; "less than 150 minutes per week then light to moderate intensity exercise is recommended. Increase your volume and intensity slowly"; "more than or equal to 150 ... continue with your current physical activity/exercise intensity levels". Scene 3a, scene 6's "exactly a hundred and fifty ... so it counts" and key note 3 match. Stage 2 is headed "STAGE 2 (RECOMMENDED)" and is completed with an exercise professional. Question 5's window is "in the last 3 months"; 8a's "unstable for several weeks" sits inside it.
- "No fixed risk categories" (3b): V2 replaced V1's low, moderate and high stratification with professional judgement (as the site states from the User Guide). Accepted; the User Guide text itself was not retrieved (see summary).
- Worked minutes 90 + 2 x 30 = 150. Correct.
- 8a person 2: father's heart attack at 52 (first-degree male relative under 55) is a valid family-history risk factor; smoking likewise. Marking correct; the feedback crosses a fixed category correctly.
- 8c/8d: under 150 weighted minutes, light to moderate and gradual; three walks a week adding about ten minutes each week is a reasonable start. Reasonable alternatives (perceived exertion, interval walking) would also earn marks under the marking as written.
- Acute risk (2): vigorous exertion transiently raises the risk of acute cardiac events, mostly in people with existing, often undiagnosed, coronary disease, while habitual activity lowers long-term risk (Thompson and others, AHA scientific statement, *Circulation* 2007). Correct.
- Memory aids sound with their stated assumptions. No people (tokens only).

## Findings
1. **Note (line 58, scene 3a).** Question 3 also asks about "lose balance" during activity. "feeling faint or dizzy during activity" is acceptable for narration; the gate label could read "faint, dizzy, balance".
2. **Note (line 58).** Question 4's window is "at any time over the last 12 months"; "a recent asthma attack" is acceptable, "in the last year" would be exact.
3. **Note (line 53).** "the extra risk sits almost entirely in people with heart disease they don't know about" is a fair simplification for adults; inherited conditions in young athletes account for the remainder, which the memory aid check already records.

## Verdict
Pass. 0 blockers, 0 should fix, 3 notes. The flagged APSS wording is confirmed against the V2 (2019) form.
