## Main question and the useful outcome

Main question (as stated): "After two minutes of UV, a plate of yeast grows five colonies instead of a hundred and forty two. If some cells did mutate, where would they be? What can colony counts tell you about UV exposure, how do you collect and analyse them fairly, and what can't they tell you about mutation?"

One main question: yes ("what does a colony count measure?"), with the fair test and analysis serving it. Useful outcome: a student can explain why a colony marks a dividing survivor, why mutants sit among the survivors, name the variables and the lamp-off control, calculate means and relative survival, describe the trend with its spread, limit a conclusion to survival, and describe how mutation would actually be measured.

## Findings

No blockers.

### S1. Should fix: the hook presents b02's answered question as open

- Exact text, scene 1: "Fewer colonies, so UV made mutants? Here's a puzzle. If some yeast cells did mutate, where would they be?"
- Student question: "The mutagens video already told us it's survival, not mutation. Why are we asking again?"
- Problem: b02's 11d gave the verdict without a mechanism; this video's real contribution is the "why" (the four fates) and the method. Naming that keeps the student's trust and frames the video as the payoff.
- Replacement (replaces the two sentences): "And you saw that fewer colonies doesn't prove more mutants. But why not? If some cells did mutate, where would they be?" (+14 characters.) Screen: the terracotta chip writes as "fewer colonies = more mutants?" with a small grey tag "mutagens video: not proven".
- Understanding enabled: the student treats b02's verdict as a claim to explain, then gets the explanation in scenes 2 and 3.

### S2. Should fix: "So damaged it can't copy its DNA" seems to contradict b02's copying-past-damage story

- Exact text, scene 3: "So damaged it can't copy its DNA: it stops dividing, and there's **no colony**."
- Student question: "In the mutagens video the copying enzyme went past the damage and put in a wrong base. So why can't this cell copy?"
- Problem: both are true, and the difference is the amount of damage: the copying enzyme stalls at dimers; past one dimer, a cell may copy through (sometimes wrongly); with many, copying cannot finish. Saying so links fate 3 and fate 4 to the same cause.
- Replacement: "So many dimers that copying stalls at them, and the cell can't finish copying its DNA: it stops dividing, and there's **no colony**." (+53 characters.) Visual: on "stalls" a short coded copy-fork marker halts at two kinks on the strand lens.
- Understanding enabled: the student can explain why more UV means both fewer survivors and more mutants per survivor, from one mechanism.

### S3. Should fix: the memory aid can be read as "changed cells aren't counted"

- Exact text: *Colonies count the living, not the changed.* Condition tag: "a mutant that lives still makes a colony".
- Problem: the video's key point is that mutants **are** counted, among the survivors. "not the changed" can be heard as "changed cells don't appear in the count", the opposite. "Living" is also loose (a living cell that cannot divide makes no colony; the author's note says so).
- Replacement: *Colonies count survivors, changed or not.* Condition tag: "survivor = a cell that could still divide". Key note 1 is already consistent.
- Understanding enabled: a compact anchor that carries both halves: the count measures survival, and mutation is invisible inside it.

### S4. Should fix: scene 11's replica-plate picture shows a mutant frequency about 10,000 times too high

- Exact text and visual: "copy each plate's colonies onto a medium that normal yeast can't grow on, using a velvet pad" with the 30 s plate (61 colonies) printing "two coded colonies at matching positions".
- Student takeaway: "about one colony in thirty is a mutant". Real forward-mutation frequencies after UV are of the order of one in 10⁴ to 10⁶ survivors, so the method needs millions of cells on the selective plate, not one plate of colonies (science check S1 has the evidence and the replacement narration).
- Replacement visual: a dense spread (a coded cream haze labelled "about 10 million exposed cells") on `m6-agar-dish-top-v1.png` with the label "selective medium: normal yeast can't grow", from which two coded colonies grow; a second small dish beside it, "diluted: count survivors"; the frequency line "mutants ÷ survivors plated" and the green "compare with unexposed cells" chip. The velvet block plate is no longer needed; b01's recall becomes the grey tag "like the antibiotic dish in the first video".
- Understanding enabled: the student describes a workable test and knows why the survival plates cannot double as the mutation test.

### M1. Minor: scene 3 end-of-scene load

- Once the teal and grey brackets draw, dim rows 1, 2 and 4 text to grey (the bracket and tag carry them). Bright at the end: the teal bracket "counted: survivors", the terracotta tag "mutants hide here", and row 3's "no colony".

### M2. Minor: A1's card does not define relative survival

- HSC items usually define a derived quantity. Add to the card, after "Calculate the relative survival of the yeast at 45 s": "(mean colonies at 45 s as a percentage of the control mean)". Narration unchanged. This removes a marking ambiguity (survival relative to cells plated is also a common definition).

### M3. Minor: A2's feedback and the misconception list (see science check S3)

- The misconception "a more concentrated suspension is a fair fix for low counts" should read "comparing raw counts from plates given different numbers of cells". Plating more cells at high exposure is standard when corrected.

### M4. Minor: length

- S1 +14, S2 +53, science S3 −2, S4 +5, scene 12 wording (science S2, M3) +30. Offset: the author's reserve cut in scene 4 ("the kind in germicidal lamps", −30; the hazard tag carries it); scene 11's replacement is 20 characters shorter. Net about +50 (about 6,300 characters, still about 9.5 minutes). Recount before recording.
