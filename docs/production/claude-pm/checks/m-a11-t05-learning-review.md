## Findings

1. **Optional, scene 4 ("it faces the shorter side, seven, so it's smaller than A").** The reason relies on "the shorter side faces the smaller angle", which is never stated. Add it as the general fact: "In any triangle, the shorter side faces the smaller angle. B faces seven, which is shorter than nine, so B is smaller than sixty-three degrees, and must be acute." t07 then reuses exactly this to explain when a second triangle is possible (a < b).

2. **Optional, scene 4 (the calculator's degrees-and-minutes key).** Most students meet minutes through the calculator's ° ′ ″ key. One clause ("or use your calculator's degrees and minutes key, which does the same conversion") would connect the dial to what they will press; the dial still explains why it is × 60.

Open decision 1 (the obtuse-case proof): keep it as an author note. The moving corner shows the rule holding for obtuse shapes, and t04 has given sin(180° − C) = sin C; speaking the extra case would add 8 s to the densest scene for little gain.
