## Should fix

**N03-FEWEST, scene 4, the hand tag "least total, not fewest stops" placed "beside the 3-stop route that was longer", and the Decisions line "the fewest stops is not the shortest".** In this example the shortest route, T–M–V (14 km), also has the fewest stops. The two longer routes both have three edges. So scene 4 shows the opposite of the tag, and a student may conclude that fewest stops does find the shortest. A1 then says the reverse. Fix (either): (a) keep the numbers, drop the tag from scene 4 and say "here the shortest route also has the fewest stops, but that's luck, as you'll see"; or (b) change one weight so a longer-looking route wins, for example M–V 9 instead of 5 (T–M–V becomes 18, T–L–R–V 16 wins with three edges, T–L–M–V 19); then update the ride time to 16 ÷ 12 = 1⅓ h = 1 h 20 min. Option (b) gives the tag a true picture; it also changes the "5" in n02's handoff table, the scene 2 and 3 tags and the author notes, so (a) is the smaller edit. The student should see the contrast in the worked example before being tested on it in A1.

**N03-INITIALS, scenes 5 and 6, the working rows "P → S → H → W" and "W → S → H → P".** The places are Car park, School, Shops and Pool, so on screen "P" is the Car park and "W" is the Pool. A student reading "P" will think Pool, and "W" matches no name. Fix: use full names in the rows ("Car park → School → Shops → Pool = 9 min") or initials that match (C, Sc, Sh, Po). The student should be able to read a route row without a lookup.
