## Findings

### 1. blocker: 3, 4 and 6

Text: "a small air bubble"

The apparatus is filled and sealed under water, but the method never explains how to introduce the measuring bubble without putting air into the xylem. A student cannot plan the advertised method from it.

Repair: Add to scene 4 after sealing: "Briefly lift the capillary end from its water, then return it, introducing one small bubble there, away from the stem." Show that end and the bubble path. Cut scene 9's windy-paddock aside, keeping uptake-not-loss and the one-shoot limit, to stay under 570 seconds.

### 2. should fix: 10

Text: "a peel can't tell"

A count alone cannot measure transpiration, but an imprint may preserve a pore aperture. Saying the method cannot show opening makes the visual open/closed comparison teach the wrong limit.

Repair: Replace with "A count alone cannot tell how much water is being lost." Remove the open/closed thumbnail and "a peel can't tell" tag; retain the upper/lower count comparison. This is shorter.

### 3. should fix: 10

Text: "one field of view, lower surface, then upper"

One unstandardised field per surface could differ in area or be a selected patch, so it cannot support a reliable distribution comparison.

Repair: Use equal field area and magnification, several non-overlapping randomly chosen fields and leaves from the same species. Screen label 20 and 3 as illustrative equal-area counts, not a species-wide result. Replace some of scene 10's repeated "where, not how fast" wording to fund the method detail.

### 4. should fix: 6 and 11

Text: "Only the air movement changed"

All still trials precede all fan trials on one shoot. Settling reduces transients but does not rule out order, time, temperature or dehydration drift.

Repair: Alternate or randomise condition order with a settling period, monitor temperature/light and repeat the comparison on more shoots before generalising. Keep the reported conclusion about this shoot; screen method instruction is sufficient, with a short replacement for "Only the air movement changed".
