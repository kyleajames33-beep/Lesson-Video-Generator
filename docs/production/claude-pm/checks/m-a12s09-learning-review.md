# m-a12s09 learning review

Revision 1, 4 November 2026. Source review only.

The stored fill histogram matches s08's unanswered handoff. A 12-row peg board offers a visual reason for a central pile-up: many left/right routes reach middle bins, fewer reach an extreme. The lesson then carefully moves from discrete simulated counts to an approximately normal continuous model of measured bottle fills. Symmetry, centre, spread and tail areas are each tied to one graph change. The notation `N(μ,σ²)` is unpacked before its use; σ controls the horizontal spread and inverse peak height. The two attempts keep the questions visible without disclosing their answers. The ending marks at one, two and three σ prepare s10 without pre-teaching its percentages.

1. **Should fix:** A peg board does not prove all sums of many independent effects are normal, or that all fill measurements follow that model. Preserve “often approximately” language and explicitly distinguish finite discrete pegs from the smooth limiting model.
2. **Should fix:** The simulation bins must be described as one seeded example, not measured factory data. The particular histogram shape does not establish model fit for actual production.
3. **Should fix:** When stretching σ from 8 to 12, keep the total shaded density area fixed at 1. The height must shrink to two-thirds as the width grows by a factor of 1.5; do not merely widen an unchanged curve.

The design avoids naming the binomial distribution in learner copy, which is appropriate to the root's scope decision. Root decision 58 retains the 924-of-4096 readout. About 5,627 narrated characters and 525 seconds. Count: 0 blockers, 3 should fix, 0 optional.
