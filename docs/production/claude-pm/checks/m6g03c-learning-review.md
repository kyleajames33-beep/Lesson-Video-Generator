## Main question and the useful outcome

Main question (as stated): "A titration question gives you a weighed standard, a table of burette readings, a sample diluted in a flask, or a tablet. How do you follow the moles from the standard to the answer, and check the titres, the scale-up and the units on the way?"

One main question: yes. All three questions are "follow the moles" chains, each adding one link and one trap. Useful outcome: a student can carry a multi-step titration calculation from a hydrate standard or a diluted or solid sample to a concentration or percentage by mass, choosing titres, writing the ratio, scaling up and checking the size of the answer.

## Findings

No blockers.

### S1. Should fix: scene 3c says "stronger" for "more concentrated"

- Exact text: "That's sensible: it was diluted twenty five times, so it should be much stronger than the aliquot." Screen tag: "250.0 ÷ 10.00 = 25: the cleaner is 25 times the diluted solution".
- Student question: "Stronger? I thought hydrochloric acid is always strong, and strength isn't concentration."
- Problem: C05 taught that strength (extent of ionisation) and concentration are different, and P04 and C12's attempt 2 test that distinction. Using "stronger" for "more concentrated" in a sense check undoes it.
- Replacement narration: "That's sensible: it was diluted twenty five times, so the cleaner should be about twenty five times as concentrated as the diluted solution." (about 25 characters longer).
- Replacement tag: "250.0 ÷ 10.00 = 25: the cleaner is 25 times as concentrated (0.1276 × 25 = 3.190)".
- Understanding enabled: the sense check now also confirms the answer numerically, and the strength/concentration distinction stays intact.

### M1. Minor: scene 2c shows two tables

- Instead of writing C12's titre table beside the card's table, add a third row, "Titre (mL)", directly under the card's two reading rows, filling cell by cell as "final − initial"; then draw the concordance strip under it. One table, with the readings and titres aligned.

### M2. Minor: scene 3c's bright-item load

- Dim the flask bands and counter to grey once the "× 12.5" box fills; dim the "n(HCl) in flask" line when the "c = 0.03190 ÷ 0.01000" line writes; let the "all of it came from the sample" tag fade on its sentence end. Bright at the end: the c line, answer box (a) and the S1 tag.

### M3. Minor: Q2's card lacks a "only acid" condition that Q3's card has

- Q3 states that the other ingredients "do not react with acid" (the author's doubt (c): a necessary condition). Q2 has the same dependency: the answer assumes HCl is the only substance in the cleaner that reacts with NaOH. Add to the card after the first sentence: "Assume hydrochloric acid is the only substance in the cleaner that reacts with sodium hydroxide." (Screen only; the narration need not read it.)

### M4. Minor (optional): make the hook concrete

- Option, replacing the hook's second sentence: "Lose one link, and every number after it is wrong: stop one step early in a dilution question, and your answer can be twenty five times too small." Visual: on "twenty five times" the chip "0.1276 mol/L" greys beside a ghost "3.190 mol/L". (This previews Q2's trap without giving the method.) Keep the current hook if length is tight.

### M5. Minor: "percent" in narration

- 2e "forty percent", 3d "eleven point one percent", 4c "seventy seven point eight four percent", 4d "thirty eight point nine two percent": use "per cent", matching C12 and the Australian Government Style Manual. Screen "%" is unchanged.

### M6. Minor: key note 2's wording

- Exact text: "Leave out the rough run and any titre more than 0.10 mL from the rest".
- Replacement: "2. Titre = final − initial reading. Leave out the rough run and any titre that does not agree with the others within 0.10 mL; average the concordant titres."

### M7. Minor: the shrunk question strip must stay readable

- The walkthrough underlines givens in the question strip, including Q1's reading table. Keep the strip at a size where an underlined value can be read at playback speed (test in the stills pass); if Q1's table cannot be read shrunk, keep the table at full size and shrink only the stem.
