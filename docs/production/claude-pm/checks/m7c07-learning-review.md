## Main question and the useful outcome

Main question (as stated): "E10 petrol is up to ten percent ethanol, yet litre for litre ethanol carries less energy than petrol. What does burning an alcohol tell us about choosing a fuel: its products, its energy per mole and per gram, and where its carbon comes from?"

Useful outcome: a student can balance complete and incomplete combustion equations for an alcohol (counting the OH oxygen), explain soot as too little oxygen, explain why molar enthalpy of combustion rises by about 650 kJ/mol per CH₂, convert to energy per gram and say why alcohols give less, and judge ethanol against petrol on energy, carbon source, production cost and the implications of extracting hydrocarbons.

One main question: yes, with a seam. Scenes 2 to 6 are the chemistry of burning; scenes 7 to 11 are fuel judgement; scene 10 (extraction) is a third strand required by C7-IQ2-06. The hook's E10 puzzle and the "energy" and "carbon" icon chips hold the strands together, and scene 11's criteria card gathers them. Keep as one video; the split with p03 already removes the measurement strand.

## Findings

### S1. Should fix: scene 10's sociocultural line is imprecise about First Nations peoples

- Exact text (scene 10): "And there are **sociocultural** costs, affecting communities and culture, often felt most by people and Aboriginal Country near the site, rather than by those burning the fuel."
- Student question: "Is 'Country' a group of people? What about Torres Strait Islander communities?" The site's card 04B says "Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander land and sea Country"; the board line already says "Country and sea Country near the site". "People and Aboriginal Country" treats Country as a population and drops Torres Strait Islander peoples.
- Replacement: "And there are **sociocultural** costs, affecting communities and culture. They often fall on communities near the site, including Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples and their Country, rather than on those burning the fuel." (about +55 characters.) Board line: "Sociocultural: community health and opposition / Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander land and sea Country".
- Understanding enabled: the student names who bears the cost and why (it falls near the extraction site), in accurate and respectful terms.

### S2. Should fix: scene 9 states lower net CO₂ as certain

- Exact text (scene 9): "So ethanol's case is a renewable source with less net carbon dioxide, against less energy per litre and the resources to grow it."
- Student question: "If production uses fossil energy, does ethanol always add less CO₂?" The scene has just shown production CO₂; net savings depend on the crop, farming, fertiliser and land-use change (science S1).
- Replacement: "So ethanol's case is a renewable source that can add less net carbon dioxide, against less energy per litre and the resources to grow it." (+6 characters.)
- Understanding enabled: the student can argue the benefit without the absolute claim markers penalise.

### M1. Minor: scene 5 divides by molar mass before scene 7 explains why

- Exact visual (scene 5): comparison chip "careful value: 726 ÷ 32.04 = 22.7 kJ g⁻¹".
- Repair (screen only): "careful value: about 22.7 kJ g⁻¹". The division appears in scene 7, where "Divide by molar mass" is spoken. No narration change.

### M2. Minor: scene 9 repeats c06 without a callback

- c06 scene 10 already said "its dilute product takes energy to distil, and growing the crop takes land and water".
- Replacement (scene 9, second and third sentences): "As you saw last video, distilling takes energy, and so do growing, harvesting and fermenting, often from fossil fuels. The crop also needs **land** and water that could grow food." (about +5 characters; keep "energy" bold on "takes energy".)

### M3. Minor: four or more bright text items at the end of scenes 3, 7, 8 and 10

- Scene 7: dim the molar masses on "gram", the "O adds 16 g mol⁻¹" tag on "partly oxidised", and both alcohol and octane bar labels when the litre bars write; end on the partly oxidised tag and the litre bars.
