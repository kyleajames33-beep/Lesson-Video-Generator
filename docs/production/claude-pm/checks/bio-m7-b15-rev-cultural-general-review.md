- **Acknowledgement "in the way they choose"** is the right general rule: some holders want naming, some want a group name, some want nothing published.

## Science check

| Claim | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| "Often, no law made anyone ask." (5) | Fair. Australia has no general national law requiring prior informed consent for using traditional knowledge in research or patents. Access-and-benefit-sharing rules exist only for some jurisdictions and resources (Commonwealth areas under the EPBC Regulations Part 8A; the NT *Biological Resources Act 2006*; Queensland's *Biodiscovery Act 2004* as amended in 2020, which added a traditional knowledge obligation). "Often" is accurate. Codex to confirm the Queensland section number and commencement, as briefed. |
| Patent: "stop others making or selling an invention for about twenty years, if it's new" (5); screen adds "inventive" | Correct (*Patents Act 1990* (Cth): 20 years for a standard patent; novelty and inventive step). |
| "A purified compound in a product can count as new, even when the plant's use is known." (5) | Fair as hedged ("can"). An isolated compound in a formulation can be novel over a known traditional use of the plant. Australian law after *D'Arcy v Myriad* (2015) restricts claims to isolated genetic information, not isolated chemical compounds generally. |
| "spoken knowledge that isn't written where examiners search can look new when it isn't" (5) | Fair. Since 2013 Australian prior art includes public oral disclosures, but examiners search mainly documents, so unwritten knowledge is rarely found. "Can look new" describes the search gap correctly. |
| "Some agreements make the community a co-owner of any patent." (8) | Confirmed. At least one Australian patent is co-owned by an Aboriginal corporation and a university (the Revision 1 to 3 case, not named here). "Some" is accurate. |
| "often a veto" (9) | Not confirmed as a frequency. See finding 1. |
| Nagoya Protocol (10) | Correct as a plain summary of Articles 5 to 7: adopted 2010, in force 12 October 2014; prior informed consent and mutually agreed terms for access to genetic resources, and prior informed consent or approval and involvement of indigenous and local communities for associated traditional knowledge, with fair and equitable benefit sharing. Strictly, Article 6 lets each party decide whether to require consent for access to its genetic resources, so "says ... should be used only with prior informed consent" slightly overstates the genetic resources part; acceptable for Year 12 because the traditional knowledge part (Art 7) is what this video is about. Australia signed on 20 January 2012 and had not ratified as at my latest knowledge; Codex to recheck on the day. |
| AIATSIS Code of Ethics (10) | Correct: *AIATSIS Code of Ethics for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Research* (2020), four principles (Indigenous self-determination; Indigenous leadership; impact and value; sustainability and accountability). The spoken "guides research with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples" is accurate. I believe the Code refers to free, prior and informed consent, but I cannot confirm its exact wording; leave to Codex as briefed. |
| Aspirin and morphine tag (3) | Correct. Aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid, 1897) was developed from salicylic acid, whose history runs through salicin from willow bark; morphine was isolated from the opium poppy in the early 1800s. Neither is tied to Aboriginal knowledge. |
| ICIP meaning (2), biopiracy (4), free, prior and informed consent (6), acknowledgement as the holders choose (7), benefit sharing agreed in advance (7, 8) | Accurate and respectful general statements, consistent with the ICIP literature and the site lesson. "This knowledge has owners" is accurate: the knowledge is held by particular people for particular communities, often collectively. |
| "Most plant extracts screened at random show no useful activity." (3) | Fair as hedged; unchanged from Revision 3. |
