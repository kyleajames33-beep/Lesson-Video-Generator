Main question traced: *how do you build a model of DNA copying and of making a polypeptide, and how do you judge what each model shows and hides?* Useful outcome: the student can build both card models from the video, record the two semi-conservative molecules and the codon table, and write an assessment (represents well, leaves out or distorts, improvement per limit).

## What works (keep)

- One kit, one colour key and one bench from start to finish; the step rail keeps the student oriented, and the right zone holds one card at a time.
- Causes are attached to the method steps: tabs that only fit partners (pairing), tape against touching tabs (strong backbone, weak hydrogen bonds, so strands separate intact), colours (tracking old and new strands), template strand only (one strand is read).
- Scene 6's "try forcing a wrong card in" is a good predict-and-see beat; scene 9's computed "one card" flash links DNA to the bead without re-teaching mutation.
- Scene 12's rule "each change fixes one limit you named" is a useful, reusable assessment habit, and the crossed chip "wrong because it's simplified" blocks a common exam weakness.
- Theory is recalled in clauses with grey source tags (B4, B5a, B6b, B6c); nothing from B5a, B5b, B6a to B6c, B7, B9 or B11 is re-taught. Folding is handed to B7.
- Attempt: the white-card ladders and both demands stay on the card through "Pause here to answer." and the 10 s countdown, with nothing answer-bearing. Feedback is brief (330 characters, about 25 s): answer, the key reason, one crossed mistake.
- No human figures or hands; cards move by themselves.

## Can a student run the practical from the video?

- **Materials:** partly. Card, scissors, tape, pencil and beads are named, but the paper ribosome, the tRNA cards and the yellow RNA cards are never made (finding 1).
- **Steps with reasons:** yes for replication (scenes 3 to 6), except the antiparallel step (finding 2). Transcription and translation steps are clear once the pieces exist.
- **What to record:** yes: a labelled sketch of both molecules (scene 6), the codon, anticodon and amino acid table (scene 9) and the three-column assessment card (10 to 12).

## Learner trace

1. **Hook.** A finished ladder beside the real helix with a "?". Can say why building a model is worth doing: it makes you follow the cell's rules.
2. **Aim and kit.** Key built one row per colour. After finding 1, the ribosome card is in the kit list.
3. **Step 1.** Nucleotide card; shape rule; notches for hydrogen bonds. After findings 1 and 4, can make DNA and RNA cards with three parts named.
4. **Step 2.** Twelve-pair molecule, tape against touching tabs. After finding 2, can show antiparallel strands with arrows.
5. **Steps 3 and record.** Separate at the tabs, green cards on each template, two molecules each one blue plus one green; mismatch tilts; sister chromatids link. Can record and explain semi-conservative replication.
6. **Step 4.** Template strand only, U opposite A, gene closes unchanged. Can explain why the gene can be read again.
7. **Step 5 and results.** Codon window, tRNA cards, beads to the stop codon; table Met-Ala-Phe. After finding 1, the anticodon fit is physical.
8. **Assess.** Strengths with reasons; after finding 3, each limit says what it hides; each improvement fixes a named limit.
9. **Attempt and feedback.** Can explain why one colour hides which strand is old and suggest two colours; the "sequences match" mistake is crossed.
