## Facts confirmed (no change)

- **Vector chain and extrinsic incubation (scene 2).** Dengue is transmitted mainly by *Aedes aegypti* (also *Ae. albopictus*, not established on the Australian mainland); in Australia *Ae. aegypti* is found in Queensland. A mosquito becomes infectious only after the extrinsic incubation period, about 8 to 12 days at typical temperatures. "Passes dengue on only if it bites an infected person, survives while the virus multiplies, and then bites someone else" is correct for transmission from people; vertical transmission in mosquitoes is rare and correctly left out of learner copy.
- **Aedes aegypti biology (scene 3).** Eggs laid singly on container walls just above the waterline; eggs survive drying for months; hatch on flooding; four larval instars and a pupa in water; egg to adult about 7 to 10 days in warm weather; only females take blood, needed for egg production; mainly day-biting and resting indoors in dark places. Correct (Queensland Health dengue pages; WHO dengue fact sheet; for Codex to open).
- **Environmental management (scene 4).** Removing, tipping, covering and screening containers, rainwater tanks and gutters is the core of Queensland's household advice ("tip, toss and cover" as a screen tag only). Correct.
- **Pesticides and nerves (scene 5).** DDT and pyrethroids hold insect voltage-gated sodium channels open, so nerves keep firing; larvicides (including methoprene and *Bti*) and adulticides as described. Correct as "attack insect nerves". Bed nets reduce malaria where *Anopheles* bite at night indoors (finding 5 hedges the timing).
- **Insecticide resistance (scene 6).** DDT resistance in *Anopheles* was reported from 1951 (Greece) and was widespread by the 1960s; the 1969 World Health Assembly abandoned the global eradication goal for control, with resistance, funding and operational problems among the reasons. "Helped end" is fairly hedged. Pyrethroid resistance is the most widespread insecticide resistance in malaria vectors (WHO reports; 2012 global plan: resistance in 64 malaria-endemic countries, pyrethroids most widespread), and has risen since. (searched) summary of the WHO Global Plan for Insecticide Resistance Management https://ohsonline.com/articles/2012/05/21/malaria-progress-endangered-by-insecticide-resistance.aspx
- **DDT eggshell thinning (scene 6).** DDT and its metabolite DDE are persistent and fat-soluble, bioaccumulate and biomagnify, and DDE thins the eggshells of birds of prey (peregrine falcon, osprey, bald eagle), causing breakage and population declines; US ban 1972; the Stockholm Convention (2001) still permits DDT for disease vector control. Correct. The plate brief (peregrine egg) is correct.
- **Sterile insect technique (scene 7).** Males reared, sterilised by irradiation and released; females mated by them lay non-viable eggs; species-specific; male mosquitoes do not feed on blood; the effect lasts only while releases continue in an open population (finding 7 on "can't stop"). Correct.
- **Oxitec self-limiting males (scene 8).** Oxitec's second-generation OX5034 strain carries a tetracycline-repressible gene that kills female offspring before adulthood; male offspring survive and carry the gene, which declines over generations. Released in Brazil and, from 2021, in the Florida Keys under a US EPA experimental use permit. "Some male Aedes carry an inserted gene that kills their female offspring before adulthood" and "releases must keep going" are correct. (searched) Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists, EPA approval for Florida Keys releases, April 2021 https://thebulletin.org/2021/04/paradise-altered-epa-approves-first-release-of-genetically-modified-mosquitoes-in-florida-keys/
- **Gene drive release status (scene 8) rechecked.** No gene drive organism has been released in the wild. Target Malaria's August 2025 release in Souroukoudingan, Burkina Faso, was of genetically modified non-drive males (male-biased offspring), and the government suspended the project on 22 August 2025; Target Malaria's own FAQ says gene drive mosquitoes have never been released and that it hopes to run field trials by about 2030. I found no 2026 report of a gene drive release. "None has been released yet" and the dated tag "no wild release (checked October 2026)" are correct; Codex to reconfirm before voicing. (searched) Target Malaria, "Common questions about gene drive, answered" https://targetmalaria.org/virtual-press-room/press-releases/common-questions-about-gene-drive-answered/ ; *Science*, "After 'humiliating' raid, Burkina Faso halts 'gene drive' project" https://www.science.org/content/article/after-humiliating-raid-burkina-faso-halts-gene-drive-project-fight-malaria
- **Wolbachia (scene 9).** wMel was transferred into *Ae. aegypti*, which does not naturally carry it, by embryo microinjection; the mosquito genome is unchanged, so "It isn't genetic engineering. No gene is inserted" is correct. Wolbachia infects a large share of insect species (often estimated at about 40 to 60 per cent; Zug and Hammerstein, *PLoS One* 7:e38544, 2012, for Codex to open), so "lives naturally in many insects" is correct. It reduces dengue replication and transmission in the mosquito ("much harder for dengue to multiply"; mechanism not settled, correctly unstated). Maternal transmission is nearly complete ("nearly all their eggs"). Cytoplasmic incompatibility: eggs of uninfected females mated with infected males fail to hatch, while infected females' eggs hatch with either male, so the infection spreads once above a threshold frequency and persists without further releases (Hoffmann et al., *Nature* 476:454, 2011, for Codex to open). "So infected females out-breed the rest, and releases can stop" is correct; learning review finding 1 adds the missing half.
- **Sterile males against Wolbachia persistence (scene 10, attempt 2).** Correct. "About 90% of *Aedes* carry *Wolbachia*" two years after releases is typical of established Townsville and Cairns areas.
- **Attempt 1.** Invented data with a monotonic rise; the model's selection chain, proposals and justification are correct; "spray more often or at a higher dose" is correctly named as a wrong chain.
- **New memory aid.** "Fewer mosquitoes, fewer bites, or mosquitoes that rarely pass it on" (finding 3's wording) is correct with the stated assumptions (the vector is the main route; a method can sit in more than one group, for example indoor spraying). Recommended after finding 3.
- **Absolute words.** "only if it bites an infected person" (correct for transmission from people); "Only females bite" (correct); "it only works if nearly every household keeps doing it" (hedged); "releases must continue" (correct for open populations); "the released males only affect their own species" (correct); "No gene is inserted" (correct); "none has been released yet" (correct, dated). Findings 3, 6 and 7 cover the rest.
