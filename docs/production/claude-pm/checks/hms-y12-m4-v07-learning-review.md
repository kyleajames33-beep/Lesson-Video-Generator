## Findings

1. **Should fix, both halves of 8a are the body's own cases.** Scene 2 works "A footballer coming back after a long break" who can still do the skills but not sustain them; 8a's cricketer who returns to full training after twelve weeks off is that case, and 8b's answer ("aerobic fitness has dropped most, skill hasn't, so jumping to full training risks injury") repeats scene 2. Scene 4 says repeating one movement causes "overuse injuries like shin pain" and that aerobic variety means "changing surface or cross-training to spread impact"; 8a's runner on the same loop with shin pain, answered "vary surface ... add a non-impact session", is those two sentences. Replace with cases that need the same principles but a new step of reasoning, for example A: "A basketballer sprains an ankle and, for four weeks, does only upper-body weights" (reversibility: aerobic fitness falls fastest and is untrained; recommend pool or upper-body ergometer intervals at preserved intensity) and B: "A lifter has done the same squat, same sets, same tempo, every session for a year, and the squat has stopped improving" (variety for the plateau reason, not the overuse reason; vary exercise, tempo or scheme while squatting stays the direction). Demand and marks unchanged. Students should use the three reasons for variety and the order of losses, not recognise a scene.

2. **Should fix, narration names internal video numbers.** Scene 4 says "which is v-six's plateau from another angle" and scene 5 "as in v-five". Students never see these ids; the brief says "earlier". Say "which is the plateau from last time, from another angle" and "as you saw earlier".

3. **Note, the hook asks students to agree or disagree, explain and recommend with a one-second hold.** Add 2 s with the three slots lit.

4. **Note, 8b is about 30 s.** If finding 1 is not taken, trim "Recommend off-season maintenance, or" to "Recommend a graded build-up" (about 2 s); the marking accepts either.

5. **Note, scene 5's oxygen-release curve is a new idea in a tiny tile.** "Blood gives up oxygen to muscle more readily when it's warm" is drawn as "a small oxygen-release curve shifting right". Year 11 did not teach dissociation curves, so a beginner sees an unexplained graph. Keep the sentence and replace the curve with two small haemoglobin dots releasing O₂ faster on a warm muscle tile than on a cool one.
