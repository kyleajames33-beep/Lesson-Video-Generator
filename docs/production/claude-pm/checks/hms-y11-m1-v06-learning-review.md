## Findings

1. **Should fix, 10a repeats scene 4's taught example.** Scene 4 works peers in full (task constant, form now continuous, bigger, public and countable); 10a's parent claim is about caring what others think, and 10b's answer is scene 4 almost word for word. Use a constant from scene 2 that was not worked as task against form, for example: "A grandparent says, 'Teenagers today take far more risks than we ever did.'" Feedback: risk-taking rising in adolescence is a constant (reward system before impulse control), so the claim is about form; a defensible version names which risks changed form (some, like smoking and drink driving, have fallen; some, like online risks, are new), supported with trend data by age; crossed mistake "phones made teenagers reckless". Students should apply the task and form split to a case they have not been given.

2. **Should fix, scene 7 uses the bathtub for diagnoses.** Test 2's picture is v03's bathtub with its tap relabelled "diagnoses". In v03 the tap is incidence (true new cases); "more help-seeking raises the count" is v03's scene 4 point (morbidity is counted only if people present, are diagnosed and are recorded). Relabelling the tap teaches that recorded diagnoses and incidence are the same thing. Use v03's three-gate strip ("presents", "diagnosed", "recorded") with more dots passing the first gate, tag "more help-seeking, more counted". The recurring models then keep one meaning each.

3. **Should fix, 10d feedback length (about 34 s).** The longest feedback in the module. Cut "Close with a judgement." and the technology example's second clause ("but one device now carries everything"), keeping the judgement on screen. About 5 s shorter.

4. **Note, scene 2 science claim.** "The brain's reward system tends to mature earlier than its impulse control" is a simplification of the dual-systems model; leave the wording to the science check, keeping "tends to".
