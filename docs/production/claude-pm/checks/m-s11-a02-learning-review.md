## Blocker

**B1. Attempt 2's model answer is wrong for the question as worded, and the lesson's own method gives a different answer.** The card asks "Ignoring mortar, how many whole bricks fit in 1 m³?" and the model answer is 1 000 000 ÷ 1815 = 550.96, so 550. But bricks are solid blocks: 22 cm, 11 cm and 7.5 cm do not divide 100 cm, so a real stack fits 4 × 9 × 13 = 468 bricks in one orientation (and no packing reaches 550). Scene 5 has just taught "convert the lengths first" for tiles (8 across, 6 down), so a student who applies it to the brick gets 468 and is marked wrong. That teaches students to distrust the method they were just shown. Fix (choose one):
(a) Reword to a volume-only question: "(b) Ignoring mortar, about how many bricks have a total volume of 1 m³?" Feedback: "A million divided by eighteen fifteen is about five hundred and fifty. A real stack fits fewer, because the brick's sides don't divide a metre exactly." Keep the crossed slip.
(b) Use a block whose sides divide a metre, so both methods agree, for example a concrete paver block 25 cm by 12.5 cm by 10 cm: 3125 cm³ = 0.003125 m³, and 1 000 000 ÷ 3125 = 320 = 4 × 8 × 10. This also lets feedback show both routes.
