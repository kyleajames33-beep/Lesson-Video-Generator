## Blockers in full

**m06 B1. Attempt 2 is answered before it is asked.** Scene 7 uses the same two cars (A $18 000 at 9 L/100 km, B $22 000 at 6 L/100 km) and shows B's five-year bar finishing below A's ("by year five it has caught up"). A2 then asks which is cheaper over five years, and its picture slot during the countdown is scene 7's bars, so the decision is on screen. Fix: change A2's distance to 10 000 km a year, which reverses the answer. A: 900 L, $2025 a year, 18 000 + 5 × 2025 = $28 125. B: 600 L, $1350 a year, 22 000 + 5 × 1350 = $28 750. Car A is cheaper by $625. Use blank purchase blocks as the picture. This also tests scene 5's point that a per-km comparison depends on the distance driven.

**a02 B1. Attempt 2's model answer is wrong as worded.** "Ignoring mortar, how many whole bricks fit in 1 m³?" has the model answer 1 000 000 ÷ 1815 → 550. A 22 by 11 by 7.5 cm brick does not divide a metre, so a real stack fits 4 × 9 × 13 = 468 in one orientation and no packing reaches 550. Scene 5 has just taught "convert the lengths first" for tiles, and that method gives 468, so a student who uses the lesson's own method is marked wrong. Fix: ask "about how many bricks have a total volume of 1 m³?" (and say a real stack fits fewer), or use a block that divides a metre, such as a 25 by 12.5 by 10 cm paver: 3125 cm³, 320 by volume and 4 × 8 × 10 = 320 by lengths.

**a07 B1. Scene 6's "bad cut" is not bad.** It says a corner-to-corner diagonal splits the house block into two triangles "but neither one has a height you know". In fact one triangle has a marked right angle with legs 20 m and 30 m (300 m²), and the other has the 40 m left side as base with the block's 20 m width as its perpendicular height (400 m²), totalling 700 m² again. The scene tells students something false about the skill being taught. Fix: make the diagonal a third check that works when perpendicular heights are used, and keep the crossed slip as "using a slanted edge (the 22.36 m back fence) as a height".

## Should-fix list (18)

- **m01** SF1 "profit is measured on the cost price" stated as absolute; the site lesson adds "unless the question asks for the selling price". SF2 "× (1 + r%)" may be read as 1 + 3 = 4; show "× (1 + 0.03) = × 1.03" beside it.
- **m02** SF1 A2's UK train ticket "including VAT": UK rail fares are zero-rated. Use a hotel night or clothing.
- **m03** SF1 the memory aid "Free only while every payment is on time" contradicts scene 3's on-time total of $492 (account fee). Keep the line for Year 12 i12 but say and show that account fees cost even when on time. SF2 A1's card (about 60 words, three parts) needs a grouped givens card or a "pause if you need more time" line.
- **m04** SF1 buyer two's lay-by: with $200 paid now, 7 more fortnights is about three months, not "about four".
- **m05** SF1 NSW stamp duty is shown as the current rule with no dated source on screen; add "Revenue NSW, rates at [date]" once Codex confirms. SF2 third-party property cover is given no excess, so the "who pays" table cannot be completed.
- **m06** SF1 the crossed "km per dollar" slip is spoken as "fifty-four hundred" while the screen shows 5408.
- **m07** SF1 a lower cost per day is read as "using a bit less"; rates change, so compare kWh per day.
- **m08** SF1 the car ($104 a week, mostly fixed costs in m06) is grouped as a variable essential; say why or split it.
- **a01** SF1 the ladder's orientation is ambiguous; with the rungs listed "mm, cm, m, km" a builder will put mm at the top and the "× down" arrows will teach the wrong direction. Fix the biggest unit at the top in a01 to a04.
- **a03** SF1 the "backyard pool" is 25 m by 10 m by 1.8 m (450 kL), a lap pool; rename it here and in a09, and drop "filled by a water truck".
- **a04** SF1 scene 6 is overloaded (1,065 characters) and its rounding sentence ("keep the one, two and one ... one point two two") never says why the 1 became 2; add the zeros-between rule and move the E display to its own beat.
- **a05a** SF1 "mulch ... measured in square metres": mulch is bought by volume, and a11 computes it in m³.
- **a05b** SF1 a "running track" with a 183 m perimeter contradicts a01's 400 m track; rename it or use a standard track (straights 84.39 m, radius 36.5 m: about 398 m and about a hectare).
- **a07** SF1 the paddock's apex position (40 m from the left) is never given, so the 80 m and 40 m legs appear from nowhere.
- **a11** SF1 "a straight front, eighteen metres deep" should be "eighteen metres wide"; 18 m is h.

## Cross-video and strand-level patterns

1. **The teaching core is strong in both strands.** Every design gives a reason before a rule (the multiplier from "100% + 3%", ÷ 11 from ten parts plus one, the tile that is nine times too big, π from a rolling pizza, πr² from rearranged slices, the third from three pours). Simulations carry real comparisons rather than decoration: the price ladder stopping $1.20 short, the equal fee block on unequal bars, the distance slider lowering cost per km, the square-metre counter running to 10 000, the leaning parallelogram with a fixed area, the application slider giving 0, 16.4, 21.8. Anchors are varied and everyday (sneakers, phone plans, headphones, a fridge, the red hatchback, a share house, a ribbon, a pizza, a softball diamond, a cereal box, a pond); none is science-themed. Each memory aid is introduced after its idea and, apart from m03 (SF1), is true for the cases it covers.

2. **Arithmetic is clean.** No numeric error was found in any of the 21 designs. The three blockers are about what a question asks (m06, a02) and a false geometric claim (a07), not calculation. Codex's numeric check should still run; it should also be asked to recheck the wording of every "how many fit" or "which is cheaper" question against the answer.

3. **Real-world realism is the main source of should-fix findings** (m02 train VAT, m04 lay-by timing, a01 salt or sodium, a03 and a09 pool size, a05a mulch, a05b track, a06 ramp gradient, a10 floating melon). In a strand that teaches sense checks, a context that fails a student's own sense check undermines the lesson. Suggested habit for the remaining strands: one "would a student who knows this thing object?" pass per anchor.

4. **Feedback rows compress several results into one line** in about half the designs (m01 A2, m03 A1, m07 A1, m08 A1, m09 A1 and A2, a08 A2). The narration separates the results, but the screen does not. AGENTS.md asks for one reasoning stage at a time with result lines aligned to their spoken cues. This is a picture-only sweep: split each row at its semicolons and attach each part to its cue word.

5. **Money figures and dated sources.** Prices are consistently labelled "example" and the "prices change" sentence appears once per video. Dated sources exist in the designs for petrol (m06, NRMA 9 Nov 2026), electricity (m07, AER 2026-27), BNPL regulation (m03, ASIC INFO 285, 10 June 2025) and the m05 fees. The gap is the NSW stamp duty schedule (m05 SF1), which is shown as a current rule without a date on screen. GST, UK VAT and NZ GST rates (m02) and the shelf-price rule are stable but need Codex's dated pages in the check file. The a09 concrete price and a11's "on the reference sheet" are pending Codex.

6. **Joins and neighbours.** Every handoff frame matches the next design's boundary across both strands, including e12 to m01, m09 to a01 and a11 to t01. Two cross-video conflicts were found: a05b's running track against a01's 400 m track, and the a03 pool reused in a09 (fix together). The m09 to a01 hook repeats the same three sentences (a01 N1). No double teaching was found: reverse percentages are taught once (m02), percentage of an amount is recalled not retaught (m01), the trapezoidal shortcut is left to r07 (a11), and the sine area rule is left to Year 12 (a05b, a08).

7. **Year 12 recall (year12DependsOn) is set up word for word** in both strands: "Up 5%: × 1.05", "% ÷ 100 = decimal", the percentage change wording, "revenue and cost" (m01); the BNPL total and "free only while every payment is on time" (m03; see m03 SF1 for how to keep the i12 line without misleading); "save first, or an emergency fund" and the hoist plate (m09); "1 mm = 0.001 m", "1 km = 1000 m", "1 L = 1000 mL" (a01); "1 ha = 10 000 m²" (a02; Year 12 t09's entry line wrongly cites a03, a root edit already listed in the design status); "1 m³ = 1000 L" (a03); "kilo = 1000" (a04); "area = ½ × base × height" (a05a); the hypotenuse definition (a06); "areas by splitting into shapes" (a07); d_f, d_l, h (a11).

8. **Reading load and length.** All designs are under the 570 s cap. The densest scenes (m05 scene 5, m06 scene 4, m01 scene 6, a01 scene 5, a04 scene 6, a11 scene 3) are mostly staged one beat per picture change; only a04 scene 6 needs restructuring. m05 and m06 are within 10 s of the cap; root's split option for m05 remains reasonable if voiced takes run long.

9. **Attempts and feedback** meet the format in all 21: unseen items (except m06 A2), every demand on the card, "Pause here to answer." then 10 s, feedback 17 to 29 s with one crossed slip, no re-teaching. Two cards (m03 A1, m04 A2) need small additions: grouped givens, and the list of methods for "justify which method".
