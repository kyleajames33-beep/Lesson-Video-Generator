## Main question and the useful outcome

Main question (as stated): "Warm an orange oxidising agent with ethanol and it turns green; warm it with 2-methylpropan-2-ol and it stays orange. Where can an alcohol reaction lead, and what decides the product: the reagent, the conditions or the alcohol's class?"

Useful outcome: a student can write the equation and conditions for dehydration (including two alkenes from butan-2-ol), substitution with HX and oxidation; tell alcohol substitution from alkene addition; and predict aldehyde, carboxylic acid, ketone or no reaction from the class and from distil or reflux, with the reason (an H on the C–OH carbon).

One main question: yes. Three reaction types are a lot for one video, but the hub framing ("where can it lead, and what decides it") holds them together, each reaction gets its own strip, and the split from c08b already moved naming, properties and tests out. No further split recommended. There is a seam: the hook's puzzle (oxidation) waits through five scenes of dehydration and substitution before scene 8 answers it (M1).

## Findings

### S1. Should fix: "plenty of water" is the wrong cause for the hydration conditions (and the arrow types differ)

- Exact text (scene 3): "To add water to ethene, we used steam, so plenty of water, at high pressure. To take water out, we use concentrated acid, which soaks up water, and the alkene leaves the mixture. It's the same equilibrium. From Module five, more water pushes it towards the alcohol, and **removing** water pushes it towards the alkene."
- Student question: "c06 said steam is kept below a 1 to 1 ratio. So hydration doesn't use plenty of water?" The industrial process uses excess ethene, not excess steam (science S1). A student who remembers c06 is told something different; a student who does not will learn something false.
- Replacement (scene 3): "To add water to ethene, we used steam, at high pressure. To take water out, we use concentrated acid, which soaks up water, and the alkene escapes from the hot mixture. It's the same equilibrium. From Module five, adding water pushes it towards the alcohol, and **removing** water pushes it towards the alkene." (about −8 characters.)
- Visual: on "steam" the "steam" and "high pressure" chips pulse together (not a "plenty of water" pulse). Add a small grey tag under the dehydration arrow on "escapes": "→: water and ethene removed, so it runs one way". This answers the ⇌ against → question without narration.
- Understanding enabled: the equilibrium link survives (Le Chatelier on water), without contradicting c06's conditions.

### S2. Should fix: "just one product" contradicts c04a

- Exact text (scene 6): "An alkene plus H B r gives just one product. That's addition." Screen: "1 product". Key note 2: "alkene + HX is addition (one product)".
- Student question: "The addition video said propene and HBr can give two possible products. Which is it?" The design means "one product molecule, nothing else formed", but says "one product".
- Replacement (scene 6): "An alkene and H B r join into one molecule, with nothing left over. That's addition." (+11 characters.) Screen tag: "joined into one molecule: nothing else formed". Key note 2: "alkene + HX is addition (they join into one molecule; no water)".
- Understanding enabled: the contrast becomes "joins with nothing left over" against "swaps and makes water", which works for symmetrical and unsymmetrical alkenes.

### M1. Minor: link the hook's tube to scene 7

- Scenes 2 to 6 run about two and a half minutes before the hook returns. Scene 7 introduces dichromate without saying it is the hook's orange solution.
- Replacement (scene 7): "A common oxidant is acidified potassium dichromate, the orange solution from the start." (+34 characters.) On "start" the hook's small two-tube thumbnail flashes top-left and dims.

### M2. Minor: scene 5 carries about eight text items

- Remove the silent "rate: HI > HBr > HCl" chip (Open decision: decline speaking it; it is not syllabus wording, its reason is not taught here, and a silent chip in a full scene is not read). Dim the reflux plate and its label before the c06 thumbnail writes. Then the end state is the equation, the reflux chip and the "OH + H → H₂O" tag, as stated.

### M3. Minor: A2's product name relies on an untaught step

- Scene 8 turns propan-2-ol into "propanone" (locant dropped); A2 expects "butan-2-one". Ketone naming is c08b. A student may write "butanone", "butan-2-one" or a structure, and all should be accepted.
- Repair (screen only): in scene 8, a small grey chip on "propanone": "propan-2-ol → propanone: '-ol' becomes '-one'". In feedback 2, the answer chip reads "butan-2-one (butanone)". No narration change.

### M4. Minor: the temperature puzzle between hydration and dehydration

- Scene 3 shows 300 °C hydration above 170 °C dehydration. c06 taught that a lower temperature favours ethanol. A sharp student will ask why the reverse runs at a lower temperature. The honest answer (different phases and catalysts; concentrated acid removes water) is beyond this video.
- Repair: no learner change. Add to author note a: "Hydration is a gas-phase equilibrium at about 300 °C for rate; dehydration happens in hot concentrated liquid acid, which removes water, while ethene escapes as a gas. The two temperatures are not comparable as Le Chatelier settings." Watch for this in the voiced-preview review.

## Retained strengths

The orange and green aligned pair as a hook that the H counter answers; the water lift and hydrogen strip-off, which show exactly which atoms leave; dehydration as hydration reversed and HX substitution as c06 reversed; water as the visible marker of substitution; the product ladder with the aldehyde's remaining H pulsing; distillation and reflux in real apparatus with the boiling point cause; "green shows oxidation, not the product"; two unseen attempts with marker-reported or reflux traps crossed.
