## Main question and the useful outcome

Main question (as stated): "Two benches titrate the same hydrochloric acid against the same standard. Both get concordant titres, but their means differ by half a millilitre. How do you carry out a titration step by step so the titre can be trusted, and how do you trace what went wrong?"

One main question: yes. Every scene serves "how do you get a titre you can trust?", and the hook's puzzle is paid off in scene 11 and reused in scene 13. Useful outcome: a student can describe and justify each step of a sodium carbonate standardisation, deal with a faulty run correctly, calculate the acid's concentration, and trace any technique error through the titre to the direction of the error in the answer.

## Trace from the entry knowledge (student at playlist order 19, straight after C12)

Entry: C12 (terms, primary standard, concordance within 0.10 mL, the four steps, Na₂CO₃ + 2HCl, "dried in an oven first"), P04 and P05 (volumetric flask, pipette, filler), chem-m5-p01 (VRA, variables, risk), Year 11 (n = m/M, n = cV). The trace follows bench A's acid from the standard to 0.1059 mol/L, then bench B.

1. Hook: two concordant benches, two different means. A concrete, honest puzzle that sets up "concordant is not accurate". Good.
2. Plan: unknown in the burette, standard in the flask, 1 : 2 as a C12 callback in one clause. Not re-taught. Good.
3. Standard: dry, weigh, rinse in, each with its reason. "so its mass is all carbonate" is slightly loose: drying removes water, it does not purify (science check M5). Good otherwise.
4. Make up and invert, then n = m/M and c = n/V on the calculation card. One line each; C12 handed the full mass-to-concentration practice to G03, and this single pass does not take it. Good.
5. Aliquot: rinse with the solution (cause given), filler, last drop (cause given), wet flask (cause given), indicator yellow. Five actions in one scene, with two crossed chips. Manageable only if the first chip dims before the second (M2).
6. Burette: rinse with acid, top below eye level, funnel out, tip filled. Each step has its reason. Good.
7. Reading: "The scale counts down from zero" can be heard as "the numbers get smaller as you go down", the opposite of the truth, and "Look from above and the reading comes out too low" is asserted with no cause. The student asks: "Why low, not high?" (S1).
8. Rough then dropwise to first permanent orange, with the orange flash fading back. A good use of motion: predict, see the flash, see it stay. Good.
9. Results: the faulty run is excluded because its cause is known, then repeated. This applies C12's draft correction 2 properly, and the bubble replay (counter jumps, no liquid enters) shows the cause. Strong scene.
10. Calculation: one compact C12 pass with the mole bridge. A callback, not a re-teach. Good.
11. Bench B: water dilutes the acid, more millilitres react with the same carbonate, titre bigger. "A bigger titre means a smaller answer" skips the reason (same calculated moles divided by a bigger volume). A student who is shaky on c = n/V asks "why smaller?" (M1). The aid "rinse with what's going in" with its three-part condition tag lands after the explanation, as it should.
12. Controlled conditions: "nothing is changed to test its effect" is defensible, but a student taught in chem-m5-p01 to name independent and dependent variables, and who may meet that framing in a school report, asks "so what do I write if the task asks for variables?" (M3).
13. VRA: three cards, with bench B as the accuracy example. Good structure. The validity card puts "dried, exactly weighed standard" under validity; by the NESA definition that is an accuracy point (science check M4). The footer's "NESA glossary (2025)" attribution is unverified (science check S1).
14. Risk: correct rows, rows dim. The final table is dense (seven rows, three columns) but it is a reading hold and returns in the key notes. Acceptable.
15 to 18. Attempts and feedback: see the checklist.
19, 20. Decision rule and key notes: clear. Note 5 joins error tracing and risk (M4).

## Findings

No blockers.

### S1. Should fix: scene 7's scale description is ambiguous and the parallax direction has no cause

- Exact text: "Look from above and the reading comes out too low. That's **parallax**. The scale counts down from zero, with lines a tenth of a millilitre apart".
- Student questions: "Counts down? So the numbers get smaller lower down?" and "Why too low and not too high?"
- Problem: the burette numbers increase downwards; "counts down from zero" can be heard as the opposite. The direction of the parallax error depends on exactly this fact, and it is stated before the scale is described, so the student has no way to reason it out. The sight line visual shows the effect but the narration gives no reason, against the "explain each cause" rule.
- Replacement (scene 7, whole narration, about 420 characters, about 100 more): "Read it with your eye level with the bottom of the **meniscus**. On a burette the numbers get bigger as you go down. Look from above, and your line of sight meets the scale above the meniscus, where the numbers are smaller, so the reading comes out too low. That's **parallax**. The lines are a tenth of a millilitre apart, so estimate to the nearest **half** line, zero point zero five millilitres. This start reading is **zero point four zero**."
- Visual: show the grey "↓ increasing" arrow on "the numbers get bigger" (before the sight line tilts), and on "meets the scale above the meniscus" ring the point where the tilted sight line crosses the scale (at 0.30).
- Offset: apply the author's two reserve cuts (scene 3 "a sealed jar that keeps moisture out", about 40 characters; scene 13's last sentence, about 80 characters).
- Understanding enabled: the student can work out the direction of a parallax error from the scale, rather than memorise it.

### M1. Minor: scene 11 skips the reason a bigger titre gives a smaller answer

- Exact text: "A bigger titre means a smaller answer: zero point one zero three eight, about two percent **low**."
- Replacement: "A bigger titre means a smaller answer, because the same moles of acid are divided by more millilitres: zero point one zero three eight, about two per cent **low**." (about 60 characters more; both feedbacks then reuse the same reason in reverse.)

### M2. Minor: scene 5 holds two crossed chips plus tags

- Dim the step card's reason tag and fade the "blow out the last drop" chip to grey before the silent "rinse the flask with carbonate" chip writes. Bright at the end: the yellow flask, the film tag "water adds no moles" and one crossed chip.

### M3. Minor: give students the words for a "variables" question

- Keep the narration. Add the author's fallback as a grey line on the scene 12 card, under "Measured: the titre": "If a task asks for variables: the titre is what you measure; the aliquot, standard, indicator, endpoint colour and temperature are controlled; nothing is varied to test its effect."
- Understanding enabled: the student can answer a school-report variables question without contradicting the video.

### M4. Minor: key note 5 mixes error tracing and risk

- Replace note 5 with two notes: "5. Trace errors through the titre: water in the burette → titre bigger → c(HCl) too low; wet pipette or damp standard → titre smaller → c(HCl) too high. Concordant titres can still be inaccurate." and "6. Risk: safety glasses (Na₂CO₃ dust, splashes); methyl orange solid is toxic if swallowed; pipette filler."

### M5. Minor: both attempts have the answer "too high"

- A student who gets A1 right can pattern-match A2 from A1's feedback. The design's reason (both run opposite to the taught bench B case, and each traces a different stage) is defensible, so this is a judgement call. If the voiced preview shows A2 feels guessable, swap A2 for: "Before adding the aliquot, a student rinses the conical flask with the sodium carbonate solution. All other steps are carried out correctly. Explain the effect of this on the calculated concentration of the hydrochloric acid." (Answer: extra carbonate, more moles, bigger titre, c(HCl) too low; crossed "rinsing with the solution is correct, so no effect".) Scene 5's chip caption "extra carbonate: more moles" would then be removed so the chip gives no chain.

### M6. Minor: "percent" in narration

- Scene 11 "about two percent low": use "about two per cent low", matching C12's "five per cent" and the Australian Government Style Manual. Captions are generated from the narration text.

### M7. Minor: the vocabulary list names a term the shot plan never shows

- "quantitative transfer (... shown on screen only)" appears in the vocabulary list but in no scene. Either add a grey tag on scene 3's rinse counter, "quantitative transfer: every grain into the flask", replacing "every weighed grain into the flask", or delete the term from the list.

## Retained strengths

The two-bench hook paid off by a traced error; the bubble run excluded for its cause and repeated (C12 correction 2 applied); the sight line that shows parallax without drawing an eye; the orange flash that fades before the permanent orange; the error chain reused in both feedbacks; attempts whose answers run the opposite way to the taught case; "rinse with what's going in" placed after the explanation, with an accurate three-part condition tag; strict placement (no indicator choice, curves or back titration).

**Ready to voice after fixes** (S1 before recording; M1 to M7 are cheap and should be applied, with the reserve cuts to hold length). Repeat this review on the exact voiced preview.
