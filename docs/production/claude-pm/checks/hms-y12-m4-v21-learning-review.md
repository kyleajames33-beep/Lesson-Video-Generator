## Findings

1. **Should fix, 8c is the hook's case with a different joint.** The hook works a player six weeks after a hamstring tear whom the player and coach want on Saturday and the physio has not cleared; scenes 4 and 6 answer it (not yet, which criteria are missing, the medical assessment governs, "A player's wish to play doesn't create clearance"). 8c is a rugby player four weeks after a knee sprain whom the coach wants on Saturday and the player agrees, answered with the same roles and the same missing criteria. Replace with a case that tests a part of scenes 5 and 6 the hook did not use, for example: "A 15-year-old school basketballer was knocked out briefly in Saturday's game. By Wednesday she says she feels fine, her parents sign a note saying she can play Friday, and she has not seen a doctor. Explain who should make the return decision, and what the school's policy should require." (A suspected concussion needs medical clearance before any return; parents give consent but cannot give clearance; the school sets and enforces the policy and carries the duty of care; school settings should be more cautious.) Marks unchanged. Students should apply the responsibility table to roles the hook did not test.

2. **Should fix, narration and screen name internal video numbers.** Scene 3 says "From v-seven, fitness fades fast"; the board shows grey tags "not a diagnosis (v20)", "v07", "v18", "v02", "v12" and "v09". Students never see file ids; the brief says "earlier". Say "As you saw earlier, fitness fades fast" and use "earlier" on every tag.

3. **Note, RICER currency for the science check.** RICER is the syllabus-era standard the site teaches, and the design is careful: ice "limits bleeding, swelling and pain" with no claim that it speeds healing. Newer frameworks (for example PEACE and LOVE) emphasise protecting the injury, avoiding anti-inflammatories and early pain-guided loading, and question prolonged icing. Students sitting the HSC need RICER; the science check should confirm the wording and decide whether one author-note sentence or an on-screen grey tag ("guidance keeps evolving: early, pain-free movement matters too") is warranted. Scene 3's "progressive mobilisation ... started as early as the injury allows" already points the right way.

4. **Note, 8a is a recall item.** "Describe the management ... including what should be avoided and why" for a sprained ankle is answered by scene 2's list and mechanism; the dotpoint demands it, so keep it, but a small twist would test the mechanism rather than the list: add to the stem "That night she plans a hot shower and a long soak in the team's spa" and ask why that is a problem.

5. **Note, 8d is about 31 s.** If finding 1 is not taken, trim "Confidence is unknown" (the line on screen keeps it); about 1.5 s.
