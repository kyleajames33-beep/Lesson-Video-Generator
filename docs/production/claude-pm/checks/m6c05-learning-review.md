## Main question and the useful outcome

Main question (as stated): "Two acids, both 0.10 moles per litre: one reads pH 1.0, the other pH 2.9. If they have the same amount of acid dissolved, why is one so much more acidic, and how is a "strong" acid different from a "concentrated" one?"

One main question: yes. Strength against concentration carries every scene: the hook pair (1 to 5), the strong list (6), the concentration dial and the four-grid model (7), the concentrated-weak surprise (8), bases and limewater as the same idea in a second family (9, 10), and both attempts. The bases and limewater scenes widen the case but answer the same question, and the dotpoint names "acids and bases". Useful outcome: a student can explain a pH difference at equal concentration by complete or partial ionisation, choose → or ⇌, name the common strong acids and bases, and refuse to judge strength from pH when concentrations differ.

## Findings

### S1. Should fix: say that two pH steps multiply to a hundred (scene 3)

- Exact text: "each step of one on the pH scale is **ten times** as many. So a gap of almost two steps means the hydrochloric acid has about **eighty times** as many hydronium ions."
- Student question: "Two steps of ten is twenty, so why eighty?"
- Replacement: "each step of one on the pH scale is **ten times** as many. Two steps would be ten times ten, a **hundred** times. The gap here is almost two steps, so the hydrochloric acid has about **eighty times** as many hydronium ions."
- Visual: the two ×10 brackets merge into "× 10 × 10 = × 100" across 1 to 3 on "hundred", then dim as the 80× bracket writes; the lens tags dim when the chips drop. Bright at the end: two chips and the 80× bracket.
- Understanding enabled: the student reads pH differences as multiplying, which C07 relies on, without any −log (project manager decision 4).

### S2. Should fix: "fraction ionised rises as you dilute it" contradicts the student's new definition of strength (scene 7)

- Exact text: "Acetic acid stays weak at any concentration. Its fraction ionised rises as you dilute it, but in solutions like these it stays far from complete."
- Student question: "If more of it ionises when diluted, is dilute acetic acid stronger?"
- Replacement: "**Dilute acetic acid**, and a slightly bigger fraction of its molecules ionise, but nowhere near all of them, so it's still a weak acid."
- Visual: dial labels become one word each ("strength", "concentration"); drop the "dilute strong acid" tag (the crossed chip's caption says it) and the panel note; the crossed chip dims when the acetic acid lens thins.
- Understanding enabled: weak stays a property of the acid, with the true trend stated plainly and the screen at three items.

### S3. Should fix: the mnemonic's order and the spoken letter list (scene 6)

- Exact text: "learn six: hydrochloric, hydrobromic, hydroiodic, nitric, perchloric, and sulfuric, for its first proton. A handy line is **So I Brought No Clean Clothes**. Each word starts with the key letters: S, I, B r, N, C l, and C l again."
- Problem: the student hears the acids in one order and the mnemonic in another, then has to map six spoken letters; the TTS will read "B r" and "C l" as stacked fragments (conversational pacing rule).
- Replacement: "For this course, learn six. A handy line is **So I Brought No Clean Clothes**, and each word gives the key letters of one acid, in order: **sulfuric**, for its first proton, then hydroiodic, hydrobromic, nitric, hydrochloric and perchloric."
- Visual: the mnemonic writes first; each tile writes under its word as named; the grey tag dims at its sentence end; on "hydrofluoric" the mnemonic and tiles dim except HCl.
- Understanding enabled: one list, one order, with the screen doing the letter matching.

### S4. Should fix: scene 5 holds six text items

- Visual text: two strips, tags "strong: complete" and "weak: equilibrium", crossed chip and caption.
- Repair: the first tag dims as the second strip writes; the second tag dims on "wrong". Bright: two strips and the crossed chip.

### S5. Should fix: scene 8 stacks two sets of labels, chips and tags

- Visual text: "acetic acid, 1.0 mol/L" with "pH ≈ 2.4", "hydrochloric acid, 0.0010 mol/L" with "pH 3.0", the 4× bracket, the crossed chip and caption, the returning beakers with labels, "fair test: same concentration", "weak ≠ safe".
- Repair: merge each label and chip ("acetic acid, 1.0 mol/L: pH ≈ 2.4"); the bracket dims on "pH alone"; the lenses and chips dim when the hook beakers return; the fair-test tag dims on "safe"; the hazard tag names concentrated acetic acid and sits apart from the 1.0 mol/L chip (science check S1).

### S6. Should fix: scenes 9 and 10 crowd the screen

- Scene 9: remove the strip tags (the arrows pulse and the narration names them); the first strip dims when the second writes; pin the pH chips on the plate labels instead of an extra pH scale; dim plates, lenses and chips before the Ca(OH)₂ tile.
- Scene 10: the "undissolved" tag dims when the card writes; shorten the rows to "how much dissolves: little" and "how completely it splits: completely".

### S7. Should fix: drop the second mnemonic from the memory-aid screen (scene 11; project manager decision 5)

- Problem: a fifth text item on the scene that should leave one causal anchor; scene 6 already taught it and the notes screen keeps it.
- Repair: cut the sentence and its visual; keep the mnemonic as the notes screen's second line.
