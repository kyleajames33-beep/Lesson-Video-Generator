## Findings
1. **Should fix (scene 2, line 54).** "But other Australians kept improving too, just as fast. So on measures like child deaths, both lines rise, and the gap between them barely moves." Two problems. (a) Child death rates fall when health improves, so "both lines rise" on a child deaths axis teaches the opposite direction. (b) Closing the Gap reporting on the 2008 to 2018 target found the Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander child mortality rate fell about 7 to 10 per cent while the non-Indigenous rate fell about 33 to 35 per cent, so the gap did not narrow (the 2020 report says it widened): other children improved faster, not "just as fast". Corrected: "But other Australians kept improving too, often faster. So on measures like child deaths, both groups improve, and the gap between them hasn't narrowed." On screen, label the axis "health measure (higher is better)" or plot "child survival", so rising lines mean improvement.
2. **Note (memory line and its aid check).** "Both lines can rise while the gap holds" is sound as stated, with its assumption recorded; with finding 1 the scene shows a faster-improving comparison group, which the aid check already says widens the gap. Optional tag: "if others improve faster, the gap widens".
3. **Note (6).** "Oldest continuing cultures on earth" is the standard phrasing in Australian government and education sources; keep.
