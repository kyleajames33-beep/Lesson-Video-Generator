## Facts confirmed (no change)

- **About 85 per cent less insecticide.** CSIRO's estimate for Australian cotton, credited jointly to Bt varieties and integrated pest management (b10, checked in its Codex check 90). "Together with better pest management": correct.
- **Refuges.** A resistance management plan with non-Bt refuge crops is a licence and stewardship condition in Australia (b10 facts). Correct.
- **Resistance monitoring (scene 7).** CSIRO screens *Helicoverpa* collected from cotton regions each season for resistance alleles to Cry1Ac, Cry2Ab and Vip3A, and the industry plan responds to rising frequencies. "Each season, scientists test caterpillars from cotton regions for resistance alleles": correct; Codex to confirm the programme is current.
- **Reverting.** Growers choose varieties each season; reversion to conventional cotton is possible with more spraying (Revision 2 states the cost). Correct.
- **Malaria deaths.** WHO World Malaria Report: about 600,000 deaths a year (2023 and 2024 estimates), about 95 per cent in the WHO African Region, where children under five are about three quarters of deaths. "Hundreds of thousands of people a year, mostly young children in Africa": correct; recheck the latest report near recording.
- **Gene drive mechanism (recalled).** A homing drive copies itself to the homologous chromosome in the germ line, so carriers pass it to nearly all offspring (caged *An. gambiae* populations collapsed in Kyrou et al. 2018). "Copies itself into nearly all offspring": correct.
- **Reversibility.** Extinction is irreversible; a self-sustaining drive or an established transgene in a wild population can persist (persistence depends on the trait and population). "Could be very hard to remove", "can usually be won back": correct as hedged.
- **Attempt 2.** Hypothetical. Wheat hybridises with some wild relatives (for example jointed goatgrass, *Aegilops cylindrica*); replacement of landraces by uniform varieties is documented (FAO). The model's genetic-level loss, gene flow with selection and conditional verdict are correct.
- **Absolute words checked.** "An extinct species can't be brought back" (correct); "a list doesn't decide" (screen); "isn't a level" (correct, from b12). After findings 1, 3 and 7 no unhedged "stays", "leaves" or "gone" remains. No "always" or "completely" in the narration. No discontinued-project claim in the present tense (the Burkina Faso work is described as halted).
- **Memory aids.** "A benefit can be paused; an extinction can't be undone" (site L12): correct as a weighing rule with the design's stated limits. "Verdict, provided that ..." (bank, "'Evaluate' needs a verdict"): correct.
