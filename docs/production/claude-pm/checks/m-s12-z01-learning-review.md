## Findings

1. **Should fix, scene 4 (the main question's "why" is restated, not explained).** "All three land in the middle, because a symmetric bell has its peak, its middle and its balance point in the same place" says that they coincide, not why. A Standard student will ask "why is the balance point in the middle too?" Fix. Narration, after "the same place": "Fold the bell at the peak and the two halves match. So half the loaves are on each side, which puts the median there, and every heavy loaf has a light partner the same distance out, so it balances there too." Visual: reuse scene 3's dashed fold at 710; on "light partner" two blue dots at 703 and 717 light with equal violet brackets "7 g", "7 g" under the balance beam. Students should be able to explain that symmetry alone forces the median and the mean onto the peak. About 9 s.

2. **Should fix, scene 2 (the "small differences cancel" picture shows two pluses).** The tags "+ dough" and "+ oven time" drift on and "cancel", but both read as additions, and nothing says that a longer bake makes a loaf lighter. The student sees two pluses cancelling. Fix. Narration: "That's because lots of small differences push each loaf a little heavier or a little lighter: a bit more dough, a bit longer in the oven drying it out. On most loaves the pushes partly cancel, so most land near the middle. Only a few get pushes all one way, and those are the loaves out at the edges." Visual: tags "more dough: heavier" (blue, up arrow) and "longer bake: lighter" (blue, down arrow) meet on the loaf outline and leave "about 710 g". Students should understand why the middle is crowded and why the edges are thin on both sides. About 5 s more than now; the named reserve trim (scene 3's last two sentences) covers it.

3. **Should fix, scene 2 (the imagined columns do not fit the same axis).** 2,000 loaves in 1 g columns peak at about 160 loaves (2,000 × 0.0798), twice the 200 loaves' tallest 5 g column (81), so on the stated axis ("number of loaves" 0 to 90) the thin columns run off the top and the bell cannot lie "exactly over" both, which is the scene's visual surprise. Fix: imagine **1,000** loaves, not 2,000. 1,000 × 1 g against 200 × 5 g is a ratio of exactly 1, so the 1 g columns peak at about 80 on the same axis and the one teal bell traces both. Change the narration ("Now imagine a thousand loaves, weighed to the nearest gram"), the label ("imagined: 1,000 loaves, to the nearest gram") and the kit check (1,000 seeded masses from N(710, 25) under the N(710, 25) curve scaled to the count). z04 can then reuse the same 1,000 (z04 finding 1).
