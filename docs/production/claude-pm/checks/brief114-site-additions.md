## Method or precision notes, not demonstrated false statements

- Chemistry L02 P02 note 1: "a precipitate that is not dried to constant mass will give a result that is too high, because the retained water is weighed as if it were analyte." Prefer "weighed as part of the precipitate, so the calculated analyte result is too high". The error direction is right. P02 notes 2 and 3, about checking a known sample and hot-crucible wording, are suggestions rather than new factual errors. Do not add them to the clear-errors table.
- Chemistry L03's short-answer model omits acidification in its chloride/sulfate procedure, although the anion table specifies it. Exact fragments: "To test for chloride ion, add AgNO 3 (aq) to a portion of the unknown solution." and "To test for sulfate ion, add BaCl 2 (aq) to a separate portion of the solution." Add dilute HNO₃ before AgNO₃; dilute HCl or HNO₃ before BaCl₂. This is an incomplete method in an otherwise unspecified unknown, and should be aligned with the table.
- Chemistry L03's "Ca 2+ and Pb 2+ also give white sulfates" and "Pb 2+ also gives a white chloride" need concentration/solubility conditions for a usable test procedure. These are not claims that every concentration must precipitate. Use the official NESA Ksp table to explain when visible solids form.
- Chemistry L03's "Confirm by flame test: yellow-green" is not wrong. Pale green, apple green and yellow-green are legitimate perceived descriptions of a barium flame. Consistency is an editorial choice.
- Chemistry L03's white-powder hook does not establish a pure compound. Its copper evidence does not prove that an otherwise white mixture is impossible. Do not log that colour claim as a confirmed error without more information about sample purity.
- Biology L03's "When blood glucose rises above about 6 mmol/L after a meal, beta cells secrete insulin" is an over-specific teaching trigger if used as a universal onset threshold. Basal insulin exists and release rises progressively. Prefer "As blood glucose rises after a meal, beta cells increase insulin secretion." The page does not explicitly say insulin is absent below 6, so keep the distinction between this clarification and that stronger false claim.
- Biology L03's "the response switches itself off as the variable returns to range" can mean the additional response subsides, rather than every hormone molecule disappears. Prefer explicit reduction towards a background level for insulin. Do not log "beta cells reduce insulin secretion" as wrong; it is correct.
- Biology L04's "The loop switches itself off: negative feedback" does not warrant the author's proposed universal repair that ADH never stops. ADH can become undetectable after water loading. Specify that release decreases as the osmotic stimulus subsides, without a never-zero claim.
- Biology L04's "Aldosterone acts on the distal tubule" is incomplete, not a false exclusive location. Align with the page's fuller "late distal tubule and collecting duct". The "Urine volume decreases" response slot is an effector output; adding the effect on the regulated concentration improves the loop explanation without making that output itself false.
- Biology L04's fixed sweat-rate limit should not imply ADH can replace water below a threshold. ADH conserves water; it does not replace sweat. The exact rate of failure depends on intake, losses, solute and circumstances.
- Biology L05's "Roots in drying soil send it up to the leaves" is not necessarily false in species where root ABA contributes, but should not be the exclusive source model. Leaf ABA is important. [Original leaf ABA study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6052997/).
- Biology L05's permanent-wilting-point phrase, "the level below which roots cannot extract water at all", is a conventional available-water approximation also used by [FAO](https://www.fao.org/4/X0490E/x0490e0e.htm). A definition based on failure to recover turgor is more precise; do not call the existing approximation a demonstrated science error without specifying species and conditions.

- Biology L05's "A 2019 CSIRO survey (Nolan et al.) measured these plants holding leaf water potential within plus or minus 0.2 MPa" and related tenfold ABA figure could not be traced after targeted searches. Record unsupported citation pending a paper, not proven fabrication.
