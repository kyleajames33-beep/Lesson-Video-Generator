| 1 | science | Scene 1: "the corals of the Great Barrier Reef all do the same thing at once" | This falsely includes all reef corals: some spawn outside the mass event and others brood internally ([AIMS](https://www.aims.gov.au/research-topics/marine-life/corals)). | Identify the animals as the many corals taking part in mass spawning. |
| 2 | science | Scene 1: "tiny eggs ... along with clouds of sperm"; visual: "pink dots for eggs, smaller pale dots for sperm" | The hard corals in the asset brief release bundles containing eggs and sperm, which break apart after rising, rather than releasing the separate clouds shown at the polyps ([AIMS](https://www.aims.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-06/on-demand_coral_spawning_a_gamechanger_speeding_critical_research.pdf)). | Show bundles rising first, then breaking apart into eggs and sperm. |
| 3 | science | Scene 4: "most eggs and sperm never meet"; scene 8 narration and CAUSE card: "few eggs are fertilised" | Applied to the salmon example and fish generally, the statement wrongly treats low fertilisation as the norm: Chinook spawning experiments found similar fertilisation rates for natural stream spawning and artificial fertilisation, and published stream studies report high egg fertilisation ([Berejikian and Tezak](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10641-004-1481-0), [Macfarlane et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2690473/)). | Separate gamete losses from the many fertilised eggs and young that die before breeding; remove the claim that only few fish eggs fertilise. |
| 4 | science | Scene 8b: "Each cod laid millions of eggs, so even after nine in ten of the young die, many thousands survive"; scene 10: "Oysters release millions of eggs, so many young survive even after heavy losses" | The calculation applies a loss among living young to eggs laid, skipping fertilisation and earlier deaths, so the stated conditions cannot establish the survivor counts or which population recovers faster ([NOAA: cod recruitment and egg production](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/atlantic-cod)). | State the living-young counts before the storm and compare new surviving young per female, keeping eggs and young separate. |
| 5 | science | Asset brief 7: "a small clutch of five or six white leathery eggs" | A complete central bearded dragon clutch of five or six misrepresents the named species, whose documented clutch size is 11 to 30 ([Australian Museum](https://australian.museum/learn/animals/reptiles/central-bearded-dragon/)). | Show 11 to 30 in a complete clutch, or five or six visible eggs within a partly hidden clutch. |
| 6 | science | Decisions: "harbour seal ... many months of care" | Harbour seal pups stay with their mothers for four to six weeks after birth, so this confuses pregnancy with postnatal care ([NOAA](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/feature-story/share-shore-harbor-seal-pups)). | Distinguish the months of pregnancy from four to six weeks of nursing and care. |

## Resolution (11 Oct)

- Finding 1 (coral wording): the hook now says "many of the corals on the Great Barrier Reef spawn together"; scene 3b says "Many corals".
- Finding 2 (bundles): the hook narration says the bundles float up and break apart; the visual shows bundles rising first, then splitting into pink egg dots and smaller pale sperm dots.
- Finding 3 (fertilisation odds): scene 4 now says many gametes are lost before they meet (swept away, eaten, spread thin) and most fertilised eggs and young are eaten or die before breeding; no claim that few eggs are fertilised. The scene 8 CAUSE card and narration say the same and the EFFECT card keeps "huge numbers make up for these losses".
- Finding 4 (recovery): scene 8b, attempt item 3 and the feedback compare living young. Before the storm ten seal mothers have ten pups and each large female cod's eggs have become thousands of young fish; after nine in ten die, one pup remains for ten mothers and hundreds of young fish per cod; next breeding season each seal mother adds about one pup and each cod adds millions of eggs again. "A large female cod can lay millions of eggs." The oyster and dugong feedback follows the same logic.
- Finding 5 (lizard clutch): asset brief 7 now asks for part of a larger clutch visible in the burrow cut-away (about 11 to 30 eggs); Claude will check the delivered image.
- Finding 6 (seal care): months of pregnancy, then about four to six weeks of nursing and care, in the decisions, scope note and scene 7.
