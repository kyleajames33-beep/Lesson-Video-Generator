## Boiling-point explanations

- Boiling separates molecules and breaks no covalent bond (scene 8): correct.
- "Hydrocarbons are non-polar, so their only intermolecular forces are dispersion forces": the standard HSC treatment. Alkenes and alkynes such as propene (about 0.37 D) and but-1-yne have small dipoles, and author note (i) already records the simplification. No learner change.
- Chain length: more electrons (more polarisable cloud) and more contact area, so stronger dispersion forces (scene 9): correct.
- Branching: same formula, same electrons, more compact shape, less contact, weaker dispersion forces (scene 10): correct, and the standard explanation (site card 4; HSC texts).
- Between series: alkanes, alkenes and alkynes of equal carbon number boil within a few degrees (alkyne slightly higher, alkene slightly lower; author note k); "chain length usually matters far more" is fair with "usually".

## Solubility

- "Water molecules hold each other with strong hydrogen bonds. To dissolve, hexane would have to push in between them, breaking those bonds, and it could only offer weak dispersion forces back." This is the standard HSC account. At 25 °C the very low solubility of alkanes in water is largely entropic (the hydrophobic effect: water ordering around the solute), so the "broken and not replaced" story is a simplification; it does not teach a wrong prediction. Author note only (M2).
- "So hexane stays as a separate layer, on top": correct (water solubility about 9.5 mg/L; density 0.66 g/mL).
- "In heptane ... they mix completely": correct; hexane and heptane are miscible in all proportions.
- Key note 4: "Hydrocarbons do not dissolve in water" is an absolute claim; they are very slightly soluble (M1).

## Absolute claims

- "every covalent bond stays intact" (scene 8): true for boiling.
- "their only intermolecular forces are dispersion forces" (scene 8): HSC simplification; acceptable (above).
- "they mix completely" (scene 12): true for hexane and heptane.

## Findings

### S1. Should fix: the general formulas need the ring condition in learner copy

- Exact text (key note 2): "Alkanes CnH2n+2 (no functional group); alkenes CnH2n (one C=C); alkynes CnH2n−2 (one C≡C)."
- Problem: c02 taught cyclobutane as C₄H₈ and c04a attempt 2 depends on "C₄H₈ could be a ring". Without the condition, the note teaches "CnH2n means an alkene", which is wrong. This caveat meets the AGENTS rule for learner copy (its omission teaches a wrong idea).
- Replacement (key note 2): "Alkanes CnH2n+2 (no functional group); alkenes CnH2n (one C=C, no ring); alkynes CnH2n−2 (one C≡C, no ring). Rings such as cyclobutane also fit CnH2n."
- Optional screen-only chip in scene 6, small and grey, after the alkene chip: "rings (cyclobutane) also fit CnH2n". No narration change.

### S2. Should fix: the series definition says "same functional group" and then says alkanes have none

- As learning S1 (scene 5 and scene 6 replacements and the key note 2 wording there). The IUPAC Gold Book entry for "homologous" reads: "(Of a series of chemical compounds) Having the same functional group(s) but differing in structure by a fixed group of atoms." Treating alkanes as a series with "no functional group" is the HSC convention (site lesson 1 card 3), so the definition must allow for it: "the same functional group if they have one".

### M1. Minor: key note 4 overstates insolubility

- Exact text: "Hydrocarbons do not dissolve in water (they would break water's hydrogen bonds and give back only weak dispersion forces)".
- Replacement: "Hydrocarbons barely dissolve in water (they would break water's hydrogen bonds and give back only weak dispersion forces), so they form a separate layer; they mix with non-polar solvents such as heptane."
- Optional task line, scene 12: "Why does hexane form a layer on water?"

### M2. Minor: author note on the solubility model

- Add to author note (i): "At 25 °C the low solubility of alkanes in water is largely entropic (the hydrophobic effect); 'water's hydrogen bonds broken without comparable replacement' is the standard HSC explanation and predicts the right result."

### M3. Minor: YouTube title over 100 characters

- Replacement as above (76 characters).

### M4. Minor: site correction 4 should name both lines

- Replace with: "L03 investigation card: 'Controlled: pressure held at 100 kPa' and 'Boiling points at 100 kPa'. The tabulated values (methane −161.5 °C to octane 125.7 °C) are normal boiling points at 101.325 kPa. Correct: 'Boiling points at 101.3 kPa (normal boiling points)'. Minor (under 0.5 °C)."
