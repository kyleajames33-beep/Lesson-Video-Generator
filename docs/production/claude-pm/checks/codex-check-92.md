## chem-m6-c15

1. **Scene 16 narration and Visual, decision rule:** The rule tells students to boil off carbon dioxide after *any* insoluble or slow solid is back titrated. The video's aluminium hydroxide attempt makes no carbon dioxide. Boiling is needed for the calcium carbonate case because its acid reaction produces dissolved carbon dioxide that would consume base in the back titre. **Correct narration and visual text:** "Add a measured excess, let the sample react, and titrate what is left. If the reaction makes carbon dioxide, drive it off and cool the flask before the back titre. Subtract moles and use the reaction ratio." Keep the calcium carbonate procedure in scenes 3 to 6 and key note 1. [Kingsborough Community College antacid back-titration procedure](https://www.kbcc.cuny.edu/academicdepartments/physci/documents/phy_and_sci/Experiment10_2.pdf); [University of Massachusetts antacid reactions](https://genchem.chem.umass.edu/chem110/110_Experiment_3.htm).

## chem-m6-c16

1. **Scene 7 narration and Visual, red wine method:** The procedure goes straight from red wine to sodium hydroxide titration. Dissolved carbon dioxide would add to the measured titratable acidity. Both the OIV total-acidity method and the Australian Wine Research Institute method remove dissolved carbon dioxide before the titre; the endpoint is method-specific (pH 7.0 for OIV, pH 8.2 for the Australian method). **Correct narration and visual text:** "First remove dissolved carbon dioxide. Then titrate the wine with standard sodium hydroxide to the stated pH endpoint, read with a calibrated probe. The titre gives the wine's titratable acidity to that endpoint, not the amount of each acid separately." Show the degassing step or state that the sample was degassed, and label the chosen endpoint convention in the worked case. [OIV total acidity method](https://www.oiv.int/standards/annex-a-methods-of-analysis-of-wines-and-musts/section-3-chemical-analysis/section-3-1-organic-compounds/section-3-1-3-acids/total-acidity-%28type-i%29); [Australian Wine Research Institute method](https://www.awri.com.au/industry_support/winemaking_resources/laboratory_methods/chemical/ta/).

## bio-m6-b13

1. **Scene 4 Visual, species row:** A down arrow on the species-diversity row presents the proposed drive as reducing the number of mosquito species. The stated aim is to suppress the population of malaria-transmitting target species enough to stop transmission; a population decline alone does not establish loss of species from an area. **Correct visual text:** "target mosquito population: down if the drive works; local species loss: uncertain". Keep the ecosystem effect as a separate unknown. [Target Malaria, common questions about gene drive](https://targetmalaria.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/20260330_-Target-Malaria-Common-questions-about-gene-drive_ENG.pdf).

2. **Attempt 1 narration, part (b) question and feedback; scene 9b Visual:** "A gene drive" is treated as necessarily self-sustaining and as copying into nearly all offspring. Some gene drives are designed to be self-limiting, and inheritance bias and spread depend on the construct and resistance. **Correct question and feedback:** "Explain why stopping releases alone may not remove a self-sustaining suppression gene drive. A drive designed to bias inheritance above one-half can keep spreading after releases stop; its spread in the wild is still untested." Match the crossed mistake and chromosome visual to this conditional claim. [Target Malaria, gene-drive terminology](https://targetmalaria.org/latest/blog/updating-the-terminology-to-describe-our-genetically-modified-mosquitoes/); [Kyrou et al., caged *Anopheles* suppression drive](https://www.nature.com/articles/nbt.4245); [Target Malaria, resistance to gene drive](https://targetmalaria.org/fact-sheets/resistance-to-gene-drive/).

## bio-m6-b15

1. **Scene 4 narration and Visual, scene 8 model comparison, attempt 1 feedback and key notes:** The design says a flower needs both anthers removed and a bag to ensure the chosen pollen parent. Bagging can protect a cross from stray pollen, but it is not a required step for every controlled cross. UC Davis's tomato crossing procedure uses an unopened flower, removes its anthers and nearby older flowers, applies chosen pollen, then tags the flower without bagging it. The design also shows the bag only *after* chosen pollen is applied, so it cannot protect the exposed stigma between emasculation and pollination when those steps occur at different times. **Correct process wording:** "Remove the seed flower's anthers before they shed pollen, protect it from stray pollen until the chosen pollen is applied, and keep other pollen out while the cross is made. A bag is one way to isolate the flower." In the tomato feedback, describe bagging as an option, or use the UC Davis tomato procedure. [UC Davis Tomato Genetics Resource Center, crossing guidelines](https://tgrc.ucdavis.edu/pollinating); [USDA ARS controlled-cross procedure showing bagging before pollination](https://www.ars.usda.gov/ARSUserFiles/20721500/Operations%20Manual%202020/Chapter%203%20Seed%202020.pdf).
