## Confirmed
- Progressive overload, adaptation in recovery, plateau and overtraining (2). Correct. "On the way up, overtraining looks like the best progress of all, until it collapses" fairly describes overreaching tipping into overtraining (Meeusen and others, ECSS and ACSM joint consensus, *Med Sci Sports Exerc* 2013).
- FITT progressions (3) and one variable at a time. Correct.
- Thresholds (4a, 4b): root rulings applied (aerobic floor about 70 per cent of maximum heart rate in the 70 to 85 per cent zone; lactate threshold; strength about 85 per cent of 1RM in trained lifters; beginners gain from much lighter loads). 4b's "a threshold is a floor for one adaptation, not for all benefit" is correct and important.
- Specificity (5) and precision of application (6). Correct. 100 min x 1.10 = 110 min.
- 8a/8b: correct. Doubling weekly distance far exceeds any usual progression.
- Memory aids sound. No people.

## Findings
1. **Should fix (line 63, scene 4a; line 103, 8d).** Current: "Below about seventy per cent of maximum heart rate, there's too little stimulus, however long you go." "however long you go" is wrong: long sessions at 60 to 70 per cent of maximum heart rate drive real aerobic adaptation, especially in less fit people (ACSM, *Guidelines*, 11th ed.), and most elite endurance volume sits there (Seiler 2010). Corrected: "Below about seventy per cent of maximum heart rate, the stimulus usually becomes too small to keep improving aerobic fitness." In 8d, "so it keeps health benefits but no longer drives new adaptation" becomes "so it keeps health benefits but drives little new aerobic adaptation". The marking's 70 to 85 per cent zone (root ruling) stands; on-screen line 2 "health kept, no new adaptation" becomes "health kept, little new adaptation".
2. **Note (line 58).** The 10 per cent weekly guide has weak evidence (a randomised trial in novice runners found no injury difference; Buist and others, *Am J Sports Med* 2008). "a common guide" is the right register.
3. **Note (line 63).** "Below about sixty-five per cent you mostly build endurance": low loads taken close to failure also build muscle size (Schoenfeld and others, *J Strength Cond Res* 2017). Acceptable as a rule of thumb.
4. **Note (line 73, not science).** "Five questions" is said, but four dimensions are spoken (energy system, movement pattern, speed, context); key note 4 lists five, including "muscle". Root may speak the fifth or say "Four questions".

## Verdict
Pass with fixes. 0 blockers, 1 should fix, 3 notes.
