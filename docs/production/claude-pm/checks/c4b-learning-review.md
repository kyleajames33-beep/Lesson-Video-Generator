## Key notes (suggested wording)

1. Activation energy is the minimum energy a collision needs to react. A catalyst gives the reaction an alternative pathway with a lower activation energy, and it is not used up.
2. The heat of reaction is the energy difference between reactants and products, and a catalyst does not change it. So the catalyst lowers the activation energy of the forward and reverse reactions by the same amount, and at a steady temperature both rates rise by the same factor.
3. A catalyst makes a mixture settle sooner but does not change the mixture it settles at. Before the slower mixture has settled it can give more product; once both have settled, the amounts are the same.
4. Added to a mixture already at equilibrium, a catalyst raises both rates by the same factor, so they stay equal. There is a moment of the change but no response, and nothing shifts.
5. A shift needs the two rates to change by different factors. Adding a substance, heating, or squeezing a gas with different gas counts does that. A catalyst, or squeezing a gas with equal gas counts, does not. The reaction that takes in heat has the higher activation energy, so heating helps it most.

Aid (large): *A catalyst changes the speed, not the destination.* Tag: "same temperature, closed system. Speed: forward and reverse together."
