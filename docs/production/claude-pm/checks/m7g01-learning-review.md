## Main question and the useful outcome

Main question (as stated): "Given a skeletal formula, a molecular formula, a reagent or a test result, how do you name a hydrocarbon, sort its isomers, and predict or work back through its reactions?"

Useful outcome: a student can name skeletal alkenes, numbering from the end nearer the multiple bond; classify a pair as chain isomers with a reason; decide addition or substitution from the bonds and conditions and write one product of each; work back from a polyhalo product to the hydrocarbon; and evaluate a bromine water conclusion with the formula.

One main question: yes, as a practice set held together by the three checks (which bonds, added or swapped, atoms and name), which the decision board then restates. Four questions, rising in demand, each with one named trap.

## Findings

### S1. Should fix: "Adding twice needs a triple bond" is false as stated

- Exact text (4b): "A double bond can open only once, so it would add one chlorine molecule. Adding twice needs a **triple** bond."
- Student question: "Buta-1,3-diene has two double bonds. Wouldn't it add two chlorine molecules too?" Yes, it would (to 1,2,3,4-tetrachlorobutane). The answer is still but-1-yne, but the reason is the positions, which 4c gives.
- Replacement (4b, +38): "A double bond can open only once, so it would add one chlorine molecule. Adding twice needs two extra bonds: a **triple** bond, or two double bonds."
- Replacement (4c, +2): "So both additions happened across the same pair of carbons. That needs a triple bond, between carbon **one** and carbon two." (Cue "one" unchanged.)
- Screen: 4b's grey tag "C=C: adds once; C≡C: adds twice" becomes "2 Cl₂ = 2 extra bonds: C≡C, or two C=C"; 4c's tag "all 4 Cl on C1 and C2" gains a second line "same two carbons → C≡C".
- Key note 4: "Working back: count the added atoms (2X₂ means two extra bonds); if all of them sit on the same two carbons, it was a triple bond there. Bromine water shows unsaturation; for an open-chain hydrocarbon with one multiple bond, the formula decides: alkene CnH2n, alkyne CnH2n−2."
- Model answer 3: "Four Cl atoms means two Cl₂ molecules added, so X has two extra bonds. All four Cl are on C1 and C2, so both additions were across the same two carbons: a triple bond between C1 and C2."
- Understanding enabled: the student learns to use position, not count alone, which is the transferable step (and matches the design's own Numbers to verify, which rules out the dienes by position).

### S2. Should fix: key note 4 drops the formula's condition

- Problem: Q4's walkthrough correctly depends on the stem's "open chain, one multiple bond", but the note a student copies does not carry it. Replacement in S1's key note 4.

### S3. Should fix: Q4's stem cues the wrong answer and differs between voice and card

- Exact text: narration "it decolourises bromine water quickly at room temperature"; card "X decolourises bromine water rapidly at room temperature".

- Replacement (narration, −8): "and it decolourises bromine water at room temperature." Card: "X decolourises bromine water at room temperature." 5b underline: "decolourises bromine water".
