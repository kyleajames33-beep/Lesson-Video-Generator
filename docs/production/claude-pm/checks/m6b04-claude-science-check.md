## Facts confirmed (no change)

- **The 1959 discovery.** Lejeune, Gautier and Turpin reported three cases with 47 chromosomes in January 1959, then nine children in a second note to the Académie des Sciences (16 March 1959, C R Acad Sci 248:1721-1722). The work used cultured fibroblasts in division. Gautier set up the cultures and analysed the metaphases; her role was later acknowledged (her name was misspelt "Gauthier" on the paper). The extra chromosome was small and acrocentric, and was numbered 21 under the Denver convention (1960). "Dividing cells", "counted", "later named chromosome twenty-one" and the "1959, Paris" tag are correct. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marthe_Gautier ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%A9r%C3%B4me_Lejeune ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11725334
- **Chromosome 21 is the smallest human chromosome** (about 46.7 million base pairs; chromosome 22 is about 50.8 million). Correct, despite its number.
- **Down syndrome causes.** About 95 per cent are free trisomy 21 from nondisjunction, about 3 to 4 per cent translocation and about 1 to 2 per cent mosaic. "That's how most cases of Down syndrome arise": correct. Source: https://medlineplus.gov/genetics/condition/down-syndrome/
- **"Roughly half as much again."** Trisomic genes are expressed on average about 1.5-fold, with gene-to-gene variation and some compensation. "Roughly" is spoken. Correct.
- **"Its genes weren't faulty."** Free trisomy 21 adds a normal chromosome. Correct as a teaching statement (individual allele variation aside).
- **Visibility.** Standard G-banded karyotypes resolve changes of roughly 5 to 10 million base pairs; point mutations need sequencing. "The large ones can even be seen down a microscope, while a point mutation can't": correct.
- **Breaks.** Ionising radiation causes double-strand breaks; misjoined ends give deletions, inversions and translocations. Correct (b02 recall).
- **Unequal crossing over** gives a reciprocal duplication and deletion (Sturtevant 1925; Tsubota 1991 molecular confirmation at Bar). Correct, with finding 2's geometry.
- **Bar eye.** *Drosophila* Bar is a tandem duplication of X chromosome region 16A (Bridges 1936, "The Bar 'gene' a duplication"; Muller, Prokofyeva-Belgovskaya and Kossikov 1936). Double (ultra) Bar is a triplication and gives a narrower eye with fewer facets. Bar eyes are narrowed front to back into a vertical strip. "A duplicated stretch of the X chromosome narrows the eye into a bar": correct. Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5105861/ ; https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/genetics-research/article/unequal-crossingover-within-the-b-duplication-of-drosophila-melanogaster-a-molecular-analysis/F42D6B72ED3C04943306F0B15A31241B
- **Deletions are often lethal** because many genes are left with one copy (haploinsufficiency), though some are viable (for example 5p deletion). "Often lethal": correct.
- **Balanced rearrangements.** Balanced reciprocal translocations occur in about 1 in 500 people. Carriers are usually healthy, but a breakpoint can disrupt a gene, and carriers have higher rates of miscarriage and unbalanced offspring. Many inversions have no effect (for example the common pericentric inversion of chromosome 9). Correct.
- **CML and t(9;22).** The Philadelphia chromosome joins *BCR* (22q11) to *ABL1* (9q34), making a constantly active BCR-ABL1 tyrosine kinase that drives myeloid (white blood) cell proliferation. It is acquired in a haematopoietic stem cell, not inherited. "Joining two genes into one whose protein is always active, so white blood cells keep dividing": correct. Source: https://medlineplus.gov/genetics/condition/chronic-myeloid-leukemia/
- **Nondisjunction.** Failure of homologues (meiosis I) or sister chromatids (meiosis II) to separate gives n + 1 and n - 1 gametes; a meiosis I error makes all four gametes abnormal, as drawn. Fertilisation with a normal gamete gives trisomy. Correct.
- **Attempt 11a.** 1 (A B C D E F E F G H) is a duplication of E F; 2 (A B C D G F E H) is an inversion of E F G; 3 (A B C D E F P Q with M N O G H) is a reciprocal translocation. Only 1 changes copy number (three copies of E and F with a normal homologue). Correct, with learning-review finding 4's added condition.
- **Attempt 11c.** Sickle cell: a single-base substitution in HBB (Glu6Val), with haemoglobin fibres at low oxygen in people with two copies. Down syndrome: usually an extra chromosome 21. Correct. On "first arises", see learning-review finding 5.
- **Bread wheat is hexaploid** (2n = 6x = 42). Correct, but the tag is removed for screen load.
- **Memory aids.** The book of chapters, with its spoken limit, and "fail to dis-join" are sound.
- **Site corrections 1 to 6** in the design are scientifically right.
