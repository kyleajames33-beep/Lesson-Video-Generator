## Findings

1. **Should fix. Hydration conditions, scenes 2c and 4a, Q1 answer and note 3.** The design transfers the industrial ethene steam/phosphoric-acid/high-pressure recipe to liquid hexenes without a demonstrated process source. Acid-catalysed hydration is a valid arrow, and the direct-addition product from either end of symmetrical hex-3-ene is hexan-3-ol. Use the supported general reagent wording "H₂O, dilute acid catalyst, heat, ⇌" for these liquid-alkene questions; keep ethene's industrial recipe with ethene. The official 2021 Chemistry Q26 explicitly uses dilute H₂SO₄ for a liquid alkene. [NESA 2021 exam pack](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/curriculum/hsc-exam-papers/chemistry/2021), [OpenStax hydration](https://openstax.org/books/organic-chemistry/pages/8-4-hydration-of-alkenes-addition-of-h2o-by-oxymercuration).
2. **Should fix. Q2 model, overall-yield multiplication.** The route branches at ethanol. Its yield is not simply the product of the four reaction yields as though all material follows one unbranched sequence. With common-route yields y₁ and y₂, acid-branch yield y₃, final yield y₄ and an optimised split, ester per mole of initial ethane is y₁y₂y₃y₄/(1+y₃); the 100% theoretical maximum is half a mole of ester per mole of ethane. No numerical example is needed in learner copy: keep "mixtures, incomplete esterification and losses at several stages lower the overall yield". Recalculation from the two C2 arms and 1:1 ester equation.
3. **Should fix. YouTube alcohol dotpoint quotation.** Quote the stem verbatim ending "including but not limited to (ACSCH128, ACSCH136):", then give combustion, dehydration, substitution with HX and oxidation as separate listed examples outside or in the syllabus's original structure. The current concatenated quotation is a paraphrase presented as verbatim. [Official syllabus](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/noindex/2025-03/chemistry-stage6-syllabus-word.docx).
4. **Optional. Q2 start and practical conditions.** Ethane to chloroethane by UV chlorination, then hydrolysis to ethanol, is chemically reasonable as an abstract route, and further substitution explains poor selectivity. Chloroethane boils at about 12.3 °C. Ordinary room-water-cooled open reflux is therefore not a literal workable school recipe for retaining it. Keep this a paper-planning task; author notes should require suitable gas containment or cooling for any real procedure rather than implying the generic haloalkane apparatus can simply be used. [ATSDR chloroethane profile](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/toxprofiles/tp105.pdf), [NIOSH ethyl chloride](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/npg/npgd0267.html).

## Recalculations and reference facts

C₆H₁₂ alkenes, C₆H₁₄O alcohols and C₆H₁₂O ketones are correct. Using C 12.01, H 1.008, O 16.00 gives 100.156 g/mol for hexanone, 88.104 for ethyl ethanoate and 130.182 for propyl butanoate. The target and intermediates in all three charts have the correct connectivity and locants. All [O] hydration, substitution, chlorination and esterification balances listed in the design conserve atoms; C2 + C2 = C4 and C4 + C3 = C7.

Markovnikov direct hydration of hex-1-ene and but-1-ene favours the secondary alcohol, which oxidises to a ketone. Hex-3-ene is the better constitutional route to hexan-3-one. Symmetry explains one constitutional direct-hydration product, not a demonstrated promise of analytical purity. Aqueous hydroxide hydrolysis of the primary chlorobutanes/propanes keeps the functional group carbon. Primary-alcohol oxidation with excess acidified dichromate under reflux gives the acid; prompt removal gives aldehyde; secondary alcohols give ketones that resist these ordinary oxidation conditions. [OpenStax](https://openstax.org/books/organic-chemistry/pages/17-7-oxidation-of-alcohols).
