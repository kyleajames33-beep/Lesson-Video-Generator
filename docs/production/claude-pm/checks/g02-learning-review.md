## Material findings

**BLOCKER**

1. **Scene 4, fingerprints: "No jump, then curves to a new mixture: temperature."** This is stated for all disturbance graphs. Scene 3c has just shown the opposite on the amount graph: a volume change gives no jump on an amount-time graph, and the curves still bend to a new mixture. A student who learns the scene 4 rule will answer "temperature" for a no-jump amount graph caused by a volume change, which is exactly the hard variant in the research (A4.3, and the 2023 amount-plot item). Scene 4's own concentration clause ("every gas concentration jumps together: volume changed, though amounts do not jump") half-covers it, but it does not say what a no-jump amount graph means.
   - Likely student question: "On an amount graph with no jump, is it temperature or volume?"
   - Repair: split the fingerprint board by axis, with the axis label read first (the draft already says this). Concentration graph: one line jumps = that substance added or removed; every gas jumps together = volume changed; no jump, curves to a new mixture = temperature; nothing at all = catalyst (or nothing). Amount graph: one line jumps = added or removed; no jump, curves to a new mixture = temperature or a volume change, so use a second clue (the other graph, or which way the gases moved). Suggested speech replacing the clauses: "Read the axis label first. On a concentration graph: one line jumps, that substance was added or removed. Every gas jumps together, the volume changed. No jump, temperature. On an amount graph nothing jumps for a volume change either, so temperature and volume look alike and you need a second clue." Panels: a concentration row of four and an amount row of two, the last panel tagged "temperature or volume: look for another clue". The rate-graph clause is cut (see length).
   - What it enables: a graph rule that survives the amount-against-concentration contrast the lesson exists to teach.

2. **Scene 5a, the argon question stem: "at constant temperature. What happens to the colour, and what happens to the temperature?"** The stem supplies "constant temperature" and then asks what happens to the temperature. A student reads it as a trick or concludes the question is badly set. The research's A6.1 has the same flaw. The hidden idea (no net reaction, so no heat is released or absorbed) is real and hard (2025 Q29), but the question has to leave the temperature free to vary.
   - Likely student question: "It says constant, so why ask?"
   - Repair: "Argon is added to a rigid, insulated container holding brown NO2 and colourless N2O4 at equilibrium. The argon is at the same temperature as the mixture. What happens to the colour, and does the temperature change?" Keep the thermometer ringed. In 5b the temperature answer ("no net reaction, so no heat released or absorbed") then answers a real question. Change the aid's condition tag from "temperature steady" to "fixed volume; insulated, argon at the mixture's temperature" for this case only. Key note 3 and the decision board tag follow.
   - What it enables: a fair question whose answer is the reasoning, not a gotcha.

## Memory aid check

(a) "Does Q still equal K? If yes, nothing shifts." (replacing "Did a concentration in K change?"). Works for every case the video shows: argon at fixed volume (concentrations unchanged, Q = K), a catalyst (both rates rise equally, composition unchanged), an added pure solid (not in K), an equal-gas-count squeeze (concentrations change, Q does not), and it fails correctly for the squeeze of 2 gas particles into 1 (Q = 3.75, below K). Assumptions that hold and are stated on screen: closed system, steady temperature, the same equation written the same way, concentrations in mol/L. It gives direction only. It does not apply once the temperature changes K (C9). It reuses "Q chases K" from C8 without adding an acronym. The old wording was true but invited the converse misreading.

(b) "Change, collisions, rate, other rate, net, named species, level." The spoken chain is correct for one concentration changing at a steady temperature, and works on a new case (attempt 1). It does not hold for temperature, where both rates change; 6a states this and the aid tag must too. The aid is a prompt for writing, not an explanation, and should not be shown before 2b has explained each step.

Both aids are drafts from the Module 5 bank; the science and subject-teacher checks remain pending. Neither introduces future content.

### New findings

All should-fix. None prevents the main understanding.

1. **7b line 5: "[N2] and [H2] keep falling from their peak".** Only N2 has a peak (it jumped). H2 never jumped and starts falling from its original equilibrium value; "keep falling" also implies it was already falling. Likely student question: "What peak does hydrogen have?" Repair: "[N2] falls back from its jump and [H2] falls, [NH3] rises, so the forward rate falls and the reverse rate rises until equal." Same length.

2. **6d spoken slip: "Do not write that it shifts right because gas escapes."** This contradicts 2c, which says to use the principle to predict direction and then give the reason. A student asks "so can I write shifts right or not?" Repair: "Do not stop at: it shifts right because gas escapes." The on-screen chip and green correction stay. Same length.

3. **6c pressure row has no reason.** The narration says "high pressure would help" and the row reads "small extra yield", but never says why. The format is condition, effect, reason, so the effect is incomplete. Repair: "Pressure: three gas particles become two, so high pressure would help, but the yield is already high, so the cost is not worth it." About +6 words.

4. **6a: "Subtract: 120 minus 63 is plus 57".** A student may not know which way round. Repair: "forward minus backward: 120 minus 63 is plus 57, so the forward reaction takes in heat" (+3 words). It is the one step in 6a not tied to a rule the learner was given.

5. **Scene 4: pacing and the "second clue".** (a) Six panels (a live-drawn panel 1 with 1 : 3 : 2 steps plus five thumbnails) light in about 23 s of narration. Split scene 4 into 4a (concentration row, four panels) and 4b (amount row, two panels) with a short hold between; no extra words, one stage per scene. (b) "You need a second clue" never says what the clue is. Add "such as the concentration graph, or what the question says was done" (+10 words); 7d already models the first. (c) "Nothing changes, a catalyst" should read "a catalyst, or an inert gas at fixed volume", so a flat graph after argon (5a) is covered; the tag already says "catalyst or nothing".

6. **3d second slip: "levelling off at the old value, or back at the jump".** 3d is the amount graph, where there is no jump, and the visual puts that chip on the concentration graph. Student question: "back at what jump?" Repair: name the graph for each slip ("on the concentration graph, levelling off at the old value or back at the jump; on the amount graph, a jump, or curves that break 2 : 1"), or move that slip to the end of 3c where the concentration graph is described.
