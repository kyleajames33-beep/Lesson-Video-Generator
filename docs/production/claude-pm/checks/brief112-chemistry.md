## chem-m7-p06

Three errors, with two affecting the proposed practical and one affecting an animation cue.

1. **The literature calculation does not establish that the revised SDS tube remains clear.** The dilution arithmetic is correct: 1 mg SDS in 10.25 mL gives 0.338 mM DS-, with 0.976 mM Ca2+, and Q = 1.12 x 10^-10 M3. But the design uses only Ksp = 3.72 x 10^-10. Another primary experimental study reports a concentration-based Ksp of 2.65 x 10^-11 M3 at 30 C, making this recipe about 4.2 times saturation by that measurement. Thus haze is a credible outcome and the previous blocker is not resolved by this calculation. Literature values disagree; do not silently select the value that gives a clear tube. The proposed narration still predicts nearly unchanged froth and no visible scum, so deleting the phrase "stays clear" did not remove the unsupported result. [Balasuwatthi et al., 2004, Table 1 and definition of concentration-based Ksp, pp 33 to 34](https://www.scientificspectator.com/documents/surfactant%20spectator/surfactant%20interaction/2004%20Surfactant%20Ratio.pdf), [Hornfeck et al., 1998](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/la970247i).

   Correct calculation text: "At 0.001 mol/L CaCl2, five 0.05 mL drops of 0.1% w/v SDS give 0.0846 mM DS- after dilution and Q = 6.99 x 10^-12 M3. This is 0.26 of the 2004 measured Ksp, or about 0.37 with 20% larger drops." That concentration is a conservative precipitation screen using the lower measured value, rather than proof of froth heights or oil-separation times. Keep the soap at 0.4% if the aim is visible scum. Then correct the equal-strength claim: compare each agent's hard-water result with its own soft-water control; the different concentrations do not support a direct equal-dose comparison between the agents. Alternatively choose and document a different named detergent suitable for the original comparison.

   Calcium stearate is much less soluble. The ILO/WHO card gives 0.040 g/L at 15 C; a current supplier sheet gives 0.053 g/L at 25 C. Under the simple ion-product model these correspond to Ksp approximately 1.15 x 10^-12 and 2.66 x 10^-12 M3. With the stated 1 mg stearate dose and calcium, the initial Q is about 9.9 x 10^-11. Solving with calcium remaining in excess predicts about 0.8 to 0.9 mg calcium-stearate solid per tube. That supports a scum comparison on a milligram scale, but neither source validates 6 cm froth, 0.6 cm froth or the invented oil times. [ILO/WHO ICSC 1506](https://inchem.org/documents/icsc/icsc/eics1506.htm), [Carl Roth calcium stearate, section 9](https://www.carlroth.com/medias/SDB-2682-IE-EN.pdf?context=bWFzdGVyfHNlY3VyaXR5RGF0YXNoZWV0c3wyNDIwMjN8YXBwbGljYXRpb24vcGRmfGFHWTRMMmd5TlM4NU1UZ3lPVGd4TXpJME9ETXdMMU5FUWw4eU5qZ3lYMGxGWDBWT0xuQmtaZ3w1MDMzNTQxODEzODhhMzRjOGMyMTI0NTI1OTRjMzllZWZhY2M0OWRiYjM2YmJkZDdlZDBjNTllOTJjMjg0MWE0).

   Root decision: no physical pilot will occur, as brief 112 states. Replace the obsolete pilot dependency with the literature screen and label the numerical results as hypothetical example data. Do not present the froth and oil times as observations established by the literature. The current exact class recipe is not ready to certify merely from its original Q/K calculation.

2. **The soap stock is not adequately specified for this room-temperature procedure.** The screen names only "soap solution", but the calculations and mechanism use sodium stearate. Pure sodium stearate has a high Krafft temperature, about 67 to 71 C, so an assumed clear, active 0.4% stock at room temperature cannot be taken for granted. Specify the actual soap formulation and the temperature at which it remains dissolved. If a different fatty-acid salt is used, update its identity and mass calculation; keep stearate explicitly as the model equation rather than imply that it is the measured reagent. [Primary sodium-soap solubility study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0021979705006089), [primary stearate-solubilisation study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0927775708002550).

3. **Scene 7 has a nonexistent narration cue.** Its visual paragraph starts the three long-lasting emulsion timers on "many minutes", but the revised narration no longer contains those words. Correct the cue to the existing phrase "it stays cloudy much longer". Source: scene 7's narration and Visual paragraph in `lessons/chem-m7-p06.md`.

Other revised findings are corrected: the chloride control, oil-dispersion measurement, coated-droplet explanation, acceptable Ca/Mg equations and separate stock/working-solution hazard rows. The SDS solid teacher-preparation choice and the glasses, intact-tube, spill and oily-waste controls are consistent with the cited hazards. The exact staff-only school entries remain unread.
