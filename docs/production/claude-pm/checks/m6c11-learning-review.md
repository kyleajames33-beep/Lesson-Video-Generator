## Main question and the useful outcome

Main question (as stated): "The same splash of acid drops water by five pH units, but a mixture of ethanoic acid and sodium ethanoate by less than a tenth. What does that mixture contain, how does it take up added acid and base, what sets its pH, when does it run out, and why does your blood depend on one?"

One main question: yes, in substance. The route question is "How does a buffer resist a pH change?". The five clauses are the parts of that answer: contents, mechanism, pH, limit, and a natural system (the second dotpoint). The blood scenes (9, 10) apply the same defender idea. They do not open a second objective. The stated question is long for a title card; the hook's spoken version is shorter and works. Useful outcome: a student can say what a buffer contains and why both partners are plentiful; write the equations for added acid and added base; explain the small pH change through the ratio; estimate a buffer's pH and calculate it after an addition; explain why HCl with NaCl fails; explain capacity and why it is not set by the pH; and describe how the blood buffer works with the lungs and kidneys.

## Trace from the entry knowledge (student starting Year 12 Module 6)

Entry: C10 (Ka and pKa, ethanoic acid 1.8 × 10⁻⁵ and 4.74, Ka from pH by the ICE table backwards), C08 (weak acids mostly whole; the ICE table), C07 (pH, the decimal-places rule), C06 (conjugate pairs and conjugate strength), C05 (salts dissolve completely), Module 5 (Le Chatelier). The trace follows the 0.100 / 0.100 mol/L ethanoate buffer from the hook to attempt 2.

1. Hook: two readouts move by very different amounts for the same dose. It is a real surprise, and the closing question ("can it ever run out?") sets up scene 8. Good.
2. What it holds: the weak acid stays whole and the salt dissolves fully. These are two C05/C08 facts that combine into the definition. Good.
3. Added acid: causal (ethanoate, a weak acid's conjugate base, pulls the proton back), visible (the proton hop and the ratio bar slice) and accurate ("almost every added hydronium is used up"). Good.
4. Added base: the mirror case, and the "defender for each attack" line is earned. Mid-scene, the lens, the new equation and the two returned readouts are all bright together (S2). The NaOH dose is spoken as "the same millilitre" without its concentration on screen (M2).
5. Buffer pH: the rearranged Ka line and "the volume cancels" are clear. **A careful student fresh from C08 will ask: "Why don't I need an ICE table? Aren't these the starting amounts, not the equilibrium ones?"** The design never says why the amounts added can be used (S3). The "0.0100 mol of each" also appears without its n = cV origin (M1).
6. After the acid: two stages (new amounts, then pH), with step chips, earlier lines dimming and the ratio bar moving. "Up 22%" against "a hundred thousand times" is a strong contrast. The givens card shows "1.0 × 10⁻³ mol" while the narration and the working use 0.0010 (M4).
7. Ratio sets the pH: four ideas share one scene (direction about pKa, not neutral, the pH = pKa trap, HCl with NaCl). **The "not neutral" example is NH₃ / NH₄Cl "near pH 9", which is the attempt 1 system**, so the attempt's partner identification and its (b) answer are previewed (S1). The crossed "every buffer: pH = pKa" chip is not narrated, and at the end the pH line, the ammonia chip and the crossed chip are all bright (S2).
8. Capacity: the curve against water, the emptying base column and "same pH, one tenth the capacity" together show the idea well. Good, and the best use of the medium in the video.
9. Blood: the cause of the narrow range, the pair, the reaction and the CO₂ leaving to the lungs. The chain is causal and in plain words. Good.
10. Why blood's buffer works: the 20 : 1 tilt is explained through capacity. "The lungs remove carbon dioxide" gives only one direction; a student will ask how blood copes with added base when it has so little carbonic acid (M5). The ratio bar, tag, two brackets, two arrows with tags, the lungs plate and two chips build up together (S2).
11. Memory aid: reconstructable, and the condition ("it can run out") is shown. Good.
12 to 15. Attempts and feedback: see below.
16. Decision rule: four lines, each with its condition. Good.
17. Key notes: five, useful and copyable; note 2's two equations are the right density for a book.

## Findings

### S1. Should fix: scene 7 previews the attempt 1 system and its answer

- Exact text (scene 7): "So a buffer doesn't have to be neutral: ammonia with ammonium chloride buffers near pH **nine**." with the chip "NH₄⁺ / NH₃: near pH 9" and the ammonia bottle.
- Student effect: in attempt 1 the student recognises the pair and recalls "about 9" without finding the acid partner or applying pH = pKa. The design calls A1 a "new pair", but it is not new.
- Replacement: "And a buffer doesn't have to be **neutral**: this one sits at four point seven four." with a chip "this buffer: pH 4.74, not 7". Leave the ammonia bottle for the attempt 1 card.
- Understanding enabled: attempt 1 tests transfer to a weak-base buffer, and its crossed swap mistake becomes a real diagnostic.

### S2. Should fix: more than about three bright text items in scenes 4, 7, 8 and 10

- Scene 4: dim the lens when the meter plates return.
- Scene 7: remove the uncued "every buffer: pH = pKa" crossed chip; the moving marker and "equal: pH = pKa" under the middle carry the idea. Dim each direction label as the next writes, and dim the pH line before the HCl + NaCl chip.
- Scene 8: dim the dose counter and the ratio bar when "capacity" writes.
- Scene 10: dim the "20 : 1" tag when the brackets write, the brackets when the arrows draw, and the ratio bar and arrows when the chips write.
- Understanding enabled: during each new step the student sees the current case, the current change and one supporting fact.

### S3. Should fix: say why the amounts added can be used (scene 5)

- Exact text: "Both are in the same solution, so you can use moles instead of concentrations, because the volume cancels."
- Student question: "In C08 I needed an ICE table. Why are the starting amounts good enough here?"
- Replacement: "Very little of either partner reacts with water, so you can use the amounts you put in. They share one volume, which **cancels**, so moles work too." Add a grey tag "partners barely react with water: use the amounts added".
- Understanding enabled: the student knows the condition behind the shortcut. They also know why it stops working when a partner runs out (attempt 2, buffer Y).

### M1. Minor: show n = cV for the 0.0100 mol (scene 5 givens)

- Replacement: "CH₃COOH: 0.100 mol/L × 0.100 L = 0.0100 mol" and the same for CH₃COO⁻.

### M2. Minor: tag the NaOH dose (scene 4)

- Replacement: a tag "1.0 mL of 1.0 mol/L NaOH" between the meter plates, dimming after its sentence.

### M3. Minor: feedback 2's figures and cue

- Replacement: say "zero point zero zero eight zero" and show "A⁻ 0.0080 mol" (two significant figures, like the data). Change the visual cue "uses it up" to "uses up".

### M4. Minor: one notation for the added amount (scene 6)

- Replacement: givens "added H₃O⁺: 1.0 mL × 1.0 mol/L = 0.0010 mol".

### M5. Minor: the lungs work in both directions (scene 10)

- Exact text: "the lungs remove carbon dioxide within minutes".
- Replacement: "the lungs adjust how much carbon dioxide they breathe out within minutes", with a two-way arrow at the H₂CO₃ column.

### M6. Minor: YouTube prerequisites

- Replacement: "Prerequisites: Conjugate acid-base pairs; Ka and pKa: comparing acid strength." (the route lists C06 and C10).
