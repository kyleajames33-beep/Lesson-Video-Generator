## Main question and the useful outcome

Main question (as stated): "The same titration needs twenty five millilitres of base, but methyl orange changes colour before eight. How does an indicator change colour, why isn't every equivalence point at pH 7, and how do you choose the indicator that changes at the right moment?"

One main question: yes, in practice. The three clauses are steps toward one decision (which indicator fits this titration?): how the colour change works (2 to 4), where the endpoint must land (5 to 9), and the selection rule (10, 11). Useful outcome: a student can explain an indicator's colour change and its reversal with Le Chatelier's principle, say why the change spans a range, predict whether the equivalence point is acidic, neutral or basic from the salt (with an equation), calculate the equivalence pH for a weak acid with a strong base, and choose an indicator whose change falls inside the jump.

## Findings

### B1. Blocker: attempt 1 omits a necessary condition (scene 13)

- Exact text: "Bromothymol blue is a weak acid, HIn. HIn is yellow and In⁻ is blue. A few drops of bromothymol blue are added to a solution at pH 4."
- Student question: "How do I know pH 4 is acidic enough for HIn to win? Where does it change?" Whether HIn dominates at pH 4 depends on the indicator's pKa, which the card does not give. Scene 4 showed the band only dimly, and the design says the attempt states the range "anyway", but it does not.
- Replacement: card adds "Its transition range is pH 6.0 to 7.6."; narration adds "Its colour changes between pH six point zero and seven point six."; feedback 1 says "At pH four, below the range, ..." and underlines the range.
- Understanding enabled: the student can justify both colours from the range and the shift, which is what an HSC marker would accept.

### S1. Should fix: scenes 6 and 9 re-teach C10 scene 9 without a callback

- Exact text: "ethanoate is the conjugate base of a weak acid, so it takes protons back, even from water"; "Its K b is K w over K a, five point six times ten to the minus ten".
- Problem: C10 scene 9 taught this equation and computed this exact Kb. Presented as new, it costs attention and misses the chance to connect.
- Replacement: scene 6 "so, as in the K a video, it takes protons back from water", with a grey tag "Ka and pKa video: ethanoate + water"; scene 9 "Its K b, from the K a video, is K w over K a, ...".
- Understanding enabled: the student sees the equivalence-point pH as an application of a result they already own.

### S2. Should fix: scene 9 result lines and givens

- Exact text: "That gives a p O H of five point two eight, and a pH of eight point seven two." Both lines write on "eight point seven two".
- Replacement: cue "five point two eight" for the pOH line (with its decimal-places tag), "eight point seven two" for the pH line; the givens card dims and relights only the row in use (Ka and Kw for Kb; the kept [CH₃COO⁻] chip for [OH⁻]).
- Understanding enabled: one reasoning stage at a time, with each supplied value tied to the line that uses it.

### S3. Should fix: scene 7 ends with four or more bright items

- "Bright at the end: the three chips (short) and the ammonium equation", plus the grey hydrolysis tag; the left and middle columns are also said to dim, which contradicts three bright chips.
- Replacement: on "acidic" the ammonium equation dims and the three chips relight together as one comparison row ("basic | neutral | acidic"); bright at the end: the three chips only.

### M1. Minor: methyl orange's change starts near the start (scenes 1 and 10)

- Replacement: "the colour drifts from red, through orange, to yellow over the first eight millilitres"; scene 10's orange stretch at about 0.4 to 8 mL; hook chip "changes over 0 to 8 mL ?". See science S1.

### M2. Minor: the memory-aid sentence names one parent

- Replacement: "A strong base with a weak acid makes it basic, a strong acid with a weak base makes it acidic, and two strong parents make it neutral."

### M3. Minor: give feedback 2's phenolphthalein reason from the card

- Replacement: "Phenolphthalein is the trap: it changes above the jump, so it goes colourless while ammonia is still in excess." The 22.5 mL figure stays in the crossed chip's caption.

### M4. Minor: plain meaning for "buffer region" (scene 10)

- Replacement: tag "buffer region: acid and ethanoate both present" (screen only; C14 teaches the term).

### M5. Minor: "pKIn" is never spoken (scene 4)

- Replacement: label "pH = pKa(HIn)", matching the narration's "the indicator's p K a".

### M6. Minor: YouTube prerequisites

- Replacement: add "how a buffer resists a pH change" (C11).
