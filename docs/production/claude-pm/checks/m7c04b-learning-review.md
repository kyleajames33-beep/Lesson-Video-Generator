## Main question and the useful outcome

Main question (as stated): "Hexane and hex-1-ene both meet bromine, but only the alkene reacts in the dark, and when the alkane finally reacts under UV light, it gives off hydrogen bromide too. Why do alkanes substitute while alkenes and alkynes add, and what does each make?"

Useful outcome: a student can explain why an alkane does not add and needs UV light; write a substitution equation with UV light on the arrow and HX as the second product; say why UV light is not a catalyst; tell substitution from addition by counting products; expect a mixture from substitution; write both steps of alkyne addition with H₂, X₂ and HX and the overall equation for excess; and say what bromine water can and cannot show.

One main question: yes. The hook is a real observable contrast (orange stays in the dark, fades under UV, fumes appear), and the two chips it ends on ("Why the light?", "Where does HBr come from?") are answered in scenes 2 and 3. The alkyne half is attached cleanly as the "adds twice" contrast with c04a, and the bromine water scene ties all three families back to the hook's test.

## Findings

### S1. Should fix: the molecular-formula rule is taught without its condition

- Exact text (scene 9): "To tell them apart, use the molecular formula: C n H two n for an alkene, and C n H two n minus **two** for an alkyne."
- Student question: "Buta-1,3-diene is C₄H₆ and decolourises bromine water. Is it an alkyne?" (and a cyclohexene, C₆H₁₀, would be called an alkyne too). This is the omission the design itself flags in site correction 3, and g01 Q4 relies on the condition being known.
- Replacement (+52 characters): "To tell them apart, use the molecular formula. For an open-chain hydrocarbon with one multiple bond, C n H two n is an alkene, and C n H two n minus **two** is an alkyne."
- Screen: under the two formula chips, a small grey tag "open chain, one multiple bond". Key note 4: "Alkenes and alkynes decolourise bromine water at room temperature without UV light; alkanes don't. The test shows a multiple bond, not which one. For an open-chain hydrocarbon with one multiple bond, use the formula: alkene CnH2n, alkyne CnH2n−2."
- Understanding enabled: the student uses the formula as evidence with its condition, which transfers directly to g01 Q4 and to HSC "evaluate" items.

### S2. Should fix: scene 2's visual breaks the wrong bond

- Exact visual (scene 2): "On 'break' one C–H bond flickers; on 'energy' a violet chip writes 'a bond must break: needs energy'." Scene 3 then says UV light "gives the energy to split bromine molecules apart".
- Student question: "Does the UV light break the C–H bond or the bromine?"
- Replacement visual (no narration change): on "break" the drifting Br₂'s Br–Br bond flickers violet (the C–H bonds stay steady); chip unchanged. In scene 3 the "split" beat then pays it off on the same molecule.
- Understanding enabled: one consistent picture: the light splits the halogen, and that starts the swap. It also matches site lessons 5 and 7 ("UV light = energy source (initiation)").

### S3. Should fix: the alkyne model changes shape with no narration

- Exact visual (scene 7): "Below, the ink ball-and-stick ethyne runs the three shapes in turn, linear, flat, tetrahedral, with c03's outline tags." No cue word; the narration is naming at the time.
- Student question: "Why is the model changing?" The "construct models" part of the dotpoint is only shown, never said.
- Replacement: insert after "Each step puts one piece on each carbon." (+136 characters): "Watch the model too: straight-line ethyne goes **flat** after one step, then **tetrahedral** after two, once each carbon has only single bonds." Cue the model: linear while ethyne is on the strip, flat on "flat", tetrahedral on "tetrahedral".
- Understanding enabled: the c03 shapes are applied to a new case, and the student hears why the shape changes (each step removes one extra bond).

### S4. Should fix: alkynes are said to decolourise bromine water "quickly"

- Exact text (scene 9): "Alkenes **and** alkynes both decolourise it quickly in the dark, because both can add." Key note 4 repeats "quickly in the dark".
- Problem: alkynes add bromine more slowly than alkenes (science S2). A student who sees a slow change in the practical may decide the compound is not unsaturated. "In the dark" is also not the point for bromine water; the point is "without UV light".
- Replacement (+18 characters): "Alkenes **and** alkynes both decolourise it at room temperature, without UV light, because both can add." Tile tag for hexane: "no change (no UV)".
