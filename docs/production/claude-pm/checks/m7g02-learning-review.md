## Main question and useful outcome

Main question: *A combustion question gives you a water mass, a temperature rise and a fuel mass that all look alike. How do you give each its job, get the molar enthalpy and the share of heat that reached the water, run the calculation backwards, and decide whether an odd result belongs?*

Useful outcome: a student can put the water's mass and rise into q and the fuel's mass lost into n, get ΔHc in kJ mol⁻¹ with its sign, find the percentage of released heat that reached the water, divide by a stated fraction to find the heat released and the fuel mass, and judge a trial by its cause before excluding it from a mean. One main question, carried by "water in q, fuel in n" and the check strip. It is a hard-practice video, so the walkthroughs are the content (AGENTS.md exception).

## Findings

### S1. Should fix (root decision): use the alternative Q1 masses, giving −365 kJ mol⁻¹

Root decision: Q1 uses the design's alternative final mass (150.54 g) so students meet a new number instead of p03's −383. Arithmetic checked in the science check (F1). Every change:

- Card (2a): "The mass of the spirit burner and methanol decreased from 151.64 g to 150.54 g."
- Hook chips: "150.0 g", "20.0 °C", "1.10 g".
- 2b narration, last two sentences: "Now the fuel. The burner lost one point one zero grams. Divide by thirty two point zero four, and that's **zero point zero three four three three** moles of methanol." Visual: balance readout "151.64 g" then "150.54 g"; "Δm = 151.64 − 150.54 = 1.10 g"; on "one point one zero" the plate appears; "n = 1.10 ÷ 32.04 = 0.034332 mol".
- 2c narration: "Delta H is minus q over n: minus twelve point five four zero kilojoules, over zero point zero three four three three moles. That's minus three sixty five point two six, so minus **three hundred and sixty five** kilojoules per mole. Three significant figures, from the mass lost and the temperature rise. And it's **negative**, because burning releases heat." Visual: "ΔHc = −q ÷ n = −12.540 kJ ÷ 0.034332 mol = −365.26 kJ mol⁻¹"; box (a) "ΔHc = −365 kJ mol⁻¹"; tag "3 s.f.: 1.10 g and 20.0 °C".
- 2d narration: see S3 (it carries both changes). Visual: "heat released = n × 726 = 0.034332 × 726 = 24.925 kJ"; bar "24.93 kJ released"; "12.540 ÷ 24.925 × 100 = 50.3%"; box (b) "50.3%"; tag "check: 365 ÷ 726 = 50.3% too".
- 2e narration: "The classic slip is dividing by the grams instead of the moles. Twelve point five four over one point one zero gives eleven point four, but that's kilojoules per **gram**, not per mole." Chip "ΔHc = −12.540 ÷ 1.10 = −11.4 kJ mol⁻¹"; ring on "1.10 g".
- 4d narration: "That's about forty eight percent of the data value, in line with question one's fifty, so the three that fit look like normal trials."
- Key note 2: "Example: methanol, 12.540 ÷ 24.925 × 100% = 50.3%."

Precision bullet: "Δm 1.10 g (3 s.f.), answers to 3 s.f. (−365 kJ mol⁻¹, 50.3%)". ### S2. Should fix: 4c states one cause as if the data prove it

- Exact text (4c): "Extra mass with no extra heat means fuel left without burning: perhaps the burner was left uncapped and **evaporated**, or some spilled."
- Likely student question: "Couldn't a draught, or a sooty flame, also make the burner use more fuel for the same rise?" Yes: the trials stop at a similar rise, so extra heat lost in trial 3 would also need more fuel, and incomplete combustion gives less heat per gram. The data show that the extra fuel gave the water no heat; they do not show which way it was lost. The video's own rule ("find the cause") is weakened if the cause is asserted.
- Replacement (4c): "So does it belong? Look for the cause. Trial three's temperature rise matches the others, but the burner lost nearly **half a gram** more. Extra mass with no extra heat means that fuel never heated the water. Most likely it left without burning: the burner was left uncapped and **evaporated**, or some spilled. That makes n too big, and delta H **less negative**. It's an anomaly with a likely cause, so leave it out of the mean, and repeat that trial if you can." (+36 characters.)
- Screen: add a small grey tag under the error-chain row: "other possible causes: a draught or a sooty flame in that trial". Model answer (b) and key note 5: "so that fuel gave the water no heat (most likely lost without burning, for example evaporation from an uncapped burner, or a spill)".
- Understanding enabled: the student reads the evidence for what it does show, names the likeliest cause, and still excludes a trial that clearly went wrong.

### S3. Should fix: "normal for an open can" clashes with Q3 and with p03

- Exact text (2d): "That's **fifty two point seven** percent: about half, which is normal for an open can."
- Problem: Q1's card does not mention a shield or lid; Q3's card has both and gives 48%. A viewer of p03 (shielded ethanol 50.3%, unshielded A2 39 to 43%) will expect the open can to do worse, not better. The author raised this doubt (Review notes b).
- Replacement (2d, with S1's numbers): "Part b asks how much of the heat reached the water. If those zero point zero three four three three moles burned completely, they'd release that times seven hundred and twenty six: **twenty four point nine three** kilojoules. The water took in twelve point five four. That's **fifty point three** percent: about half, which is typical for a spirit burner and can. The rest warmed the can and the air."
- Understanding enabled: "about half" is a sense check for the apparatus, without a claim that contradicts the shield lesson.

### M2. Minor: 4c's answer line is too long for the working area

### M3. Minor: "anomalous" appears on the badge before its gloss

- The badge "Table: is it anomalous?" appears at 4b; the gloss "anomalous result: doesn't fit the pattern of the others" appears at 4c. Move the gloss to 4b, under the badge, when the badge appears.
