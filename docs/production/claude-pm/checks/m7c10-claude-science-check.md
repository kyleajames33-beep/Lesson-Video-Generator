## Facts confirmed (no change)

- **Which atoms form the water (scene 2).** In acid-catalysed esterification of primary and secondary alcohols, ¹⁸O from the alcohol ends up in the ester, so the acid supplies the OH and the alcohol the H (Roberts and Urey, *J Am Chem Soc* 60:2391, 1938; for Codex to open). The narration states the outcome only. Correct. "Condensation reaction: two molecules join and release a small molecule" is correct.
- **Ester group (scenes 2 and 4).** –COO–: a C=O carbon bonded to a second oxygen, bonded to the alcohol's carbon chain. The cut at the bond between the carbonyl carbon and the single-bonded oxygen, with the C=O side from the acid and the O plus chain from the alcohol, matches the labelling result. Correct.
- **Names and formulas, all by atom count.** Ethyl ethanoate CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ C₄H₈O₂; methyl propanoate CH₃CH₂COOCH₃ C₄H₈O₂ (from propanoic acid and methanol); ethyl butanoate CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₂CH₃ and butyl ethanoate CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃, both C₆H₁₂O₂, different compounds; pentyl ethanoate C₇H₁₄O₂; butyl propanoate CH₃CH₂COOCH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ C₇H₁₄O₂ (C 3 + 4, H 5 + 9), 130.18 g/mol, from propanoic acid and butan-1-ol. The crossed "propyl butanoate" (CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₂CH₂CH₃) is a real isomer, so "a different ester" is right. Alcohol part first ("-yl"), acid part second ("-oate"): correct IUPAC order.
- **Equation balance.** CH₃COOH + CH₃CH₂OH ⇌ CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ + H₂O (C 4/4, H 10/10, O 3/3). CH₃CH₂COOH + CH₃OH ⇌ CH₃CH₂COOCH₃ + H₂O (C 4/4, H 10/10, O 3/3). Correct.
- **Boiling points and molar masses (scene 5, key note 3).** Ethyl ethanoate 77.1 °C, 88.11 g/mol; pentan-1-ol 138.0 °C, 88.15 g/mol; butanoic acid 163.8 °C, 88.11 g/mol; ethanol 78.3 °C; methyl propanoate 79.8 °C; pentyl ethanoate 149.2 °C. Electrons 48, 50, 48. The order ester < alcohol < acid at equal mass, and the reasons (no O–H, so dipole–dipole and dispersion only; alcohol hydrogen bonds; acid dimers), are correct. Ethyl ethanoate and butanoic acid are functional group isomers (C₄H₈O₂); only the acid fizzes with NaHCO₃. Correct.
- **Conditions and hazards (scene 6).** A few drops of concentrated H₂SO₄ as catalyst, heat under reflux; the reaction is slow at room temperature; condenser returns volatile ethanol (78 °C) and ester (77 °C). Concentrated sulfuric acid is corrosive; ethanol and ethyl ethanoate are highly flammable (ethyl ethanoate flash point about −4 °C, closed cup; PubChem and ICSC); electric heating or a water bath, no flame. "Never a flame" is a correct safety absolute. The catalyst written over the arrow, not as a reactant, is correct.
- **Equilibrium yields (scenes 7 and 8), checked against K about 4.** K = [ester][water] ÷ ([acid][alcohol]); Δn = 0, so amounts can replace concentrations. Equal amounts: x² ÷ (1 − x)² = 4, x = 2/3 = 0.667 ("about two thirds"). Acid 1 mol, ethanol 3 mol: 3x² − 16x + 12 = 0, x = (16 − √112) ÷ 6 = 0.903 ("about nine tenths of the acid"). Both by script. K about 4 is the classic Berthelot and Péan de Saint-Gilles result (1862) and is the textbook value for ethanoic acid with ethanol (measured values about 3 to 4.5 with conditions). Scene 8 correctly says "of the acid". The site's "Keq ≈ 1–4 ... ~50–70%" is consistent. Attempt 2: 0.32 ÷ 0.50 = 64%, implied K = 3.16, plausible for methyl propanoate. Recommend keeping both fractions on screen with "about" (Open decision on yield fractions: keep).
- **Catalyst (scene 8).** A catalyst speeds forward and reverse reactions equally and does not change the equilibrium position; heating changes K only slightly because ΔH of esterification is small (a few kJ/mol). Correct.
- **Concentrated sulfuric acid as a water remover (Decisions, author note c).** In catalytic amounts it binds only a small fraction of the water formed; "takes up a little" is a fair hedge. Finding 1 is about the crossed answer that conflicts with this.
- **Isolation (scene 9).** Ethyl ethanoate solubility in water about 8 g per 100 mL at 20 °C ("doesn't mix well"); it floats (density about 0.90 g/mL); Na₂CO₃(aq) neutralises leftover ethanoic and sulfuric acids with CO₂, so the funnel is vented; dry with anhydrous MgSO₄ or Na₂SO₄; distil. The grey tag "check densities: don't assume" is right. Finding 2 covers the water wash and identification wording.
- **Flow chart (scene 10).** Ethanol to ethanoic acid with acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ (Cr₂O₇²⁻/H⁺), heat under reflux; ethanoic acid with ethanol and concentrated H₂SO₄ under reflux; ethene with steam over a phosphoric acid catalyst at about 300 °C and 6 to 7 MPa. Correct; boxes hold compounds, arrows carry reagents and conditions, which is the form HSC flow chart items expect.
- **Attempt 1 and feedback 1.** Butyl propanoate; propanoic acid and butan-1-ol; "in a straight chain" is supported by the condensed formula. Correct.
- **Ester odours (hook).** Ethyl butanoate is commonly described as pineapple-like; pentyl ethanoate (amyl acetate) as banana-like (also sold as "banana oil"), with 3-methylbutyl ethanoate the main banana ester in the fruit; "often described as" and "usually" are the right hedges. Keep as designed (Open decision on smell claims). (PubChem records for ethyl butyrate and amyl acetate; for Codex to open.)
- **Memory aids.** "Alcohol gets '-yl' (first), acid gets '-oate' (second)" holds for every simple ester. "acid + alcohol ⇌ ester + water" is correct. "Faster, not more" needs finding 3's wording; "main road" needs finding 5's.
- **Absolute words.** "never quite finish" (correct: equilibrium); "can't hydrogen bond to each other" (correct: no O–H donor); "Only the acid fizzes" (correct for the pair); "with all four substances still there" (correct); "The catalyst only speeds it up" (correct); "never a flame" (safety rule, correct).
