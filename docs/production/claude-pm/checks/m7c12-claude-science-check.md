## Facts confirmed (no change)

- **Formulas and molar masses (IUPAC atomic weights, by script).** Stearate CH₃(CH₂)₁₆COO⁻ = C₁₇H₃₅COO⁻ (H 3 + 32 = 35); sodium stearate C₁₈H₃₅NaO₂, 306.47 g/mol; palmitate CH₃(CH₂)₁₄COO⁻ = C₁₅H₃₁COO⁻, so the feedback bracket "C₁₅H₃₁: hydrophobic" is right; glyceryl tristearate C₅₇H₁₁₀O₆ (891.5 g/mol); glycerol C₃H₈O₃, propane-1,2,3-triol; sodium dodecyl sulfate C₁₂H₂₅NaO₄S, 288.38 g/mol.
- **Saponification equation.** C₃H₅(OOCC₁₇H₃₅)₃ + 3NaOH → C₃H₅(OH)₃ + 3C₁₇H₃₅COONa balances: C 57/57, H 113/113, O 9/9, Na 3/3. NaOH is a reactant (3 per triglyceride), not a catalyst. Heat with concentrated NaOH is the usual school method.
- **Why saponification goes to completion.** The carboxylic acid formed is deprotonated by hydroxide (pKa about 4.8 against water 14), and the carboxylate is not attacked by the alcohol, so the reaction is effectively one way. This is the standard HSC reason.
- **Scum equation.** 2C₁₇H₃₅COO⁻(aq) + Ca²⁺(aq) → (C₁₇H₃₅COO)₂Ca(s) balances: C 36/36, H 70/70, O 4/4, Ca 1/1, charge 0/0. Na⁺ are spectators. The crossed equation "C₁₇H₃₅COO⁻ + Ca²⁺ → C₁₇H₃₅COOCa" has charge −1 + 2 = +1 on the left and a neutral product on the right, so it is unbalanced in charge, as the caption says. Calcium and magnesium stearates are practically insoluble in water (calcium stearate about 0.004 g/100 mL). Magnesium "the same" is right.
- **Structure and forces.** Hydrocarbon tail: non-polar, dispersion with oil, hydrophobic. Carboxylate head: ionic, ion–dipole with water (and accepts hydrogen bonds), hydrophilic. "Amphiphilic (amphipathic)" as a quiet tag is right. Tails C₁₁ to C₁₇ is the usual soap range (C12 to C18 acids).
- **Surface and micelles.** Soap particles line the surface with tails pointing out of the water. Above the critical micelle concentration they form micelles with tails in and heads out. SDS CMC is about 8 mmol/L at 25 °C, and aggregation numbers are about 50 to 100 (author note c). The 20-symbol cross-section is a fair illustration.
- **Cleaning action.** Tails dissolve into grease by dispersion, heads stay in water, agitation lifts and breaks up the grease, coated droplets carry like charges and repel, the grease is emulsified (dispersed as droplets), not dissolved, and the rinse carries it away. This is the standard HSC mark scheme. Finding 3 softens "can't join back together".
- **Hard water.** Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ dissolve from rocks such as limestone and dolomite, helped by dissolved CO₂ (author note i). Scum uses up soap, so there is less froth and more soap is needed. Rainwater is effectively soft, so the attempt 2 contrast is valid.
- **Detergent types.** Anionic (sulfonate –SO₃⁻ or sulfate –OSO₃⁻) are the most common class. Cationic (quaternary ammonium) are used in fabric softeners and some antiseptics. Non-ionic (polyethoxylates, R–(OCH₂CH₂)ₙ–OH) are uncharged polar chains that hydrogen bond with water, so there is no charge for Ca²⁺ to bind. All correct.
- **Biodegradability.** Soaps biodegrade readily. Detergents vary: linear alkylbenzene sulfonates biodegrade readily in aerobic conditions but poorly without oxygen, and the early branched alkylbenzene sulfonates (1950s) biodegraded slowly and caused foam on rivers before they were replaced from the mid-1960s. "Varies" is right, and leaving the history out is reasonable (author note f).
- **Detergent wording (Open decision).** "Much more soluble, so far less scum forms" and "detergents mostly don't" are the right strength. They are true as written and will earn full marks; HSC marking guidelines accept "calcium salts are soluble" and do not penalise the more careful "much more soluble".
- **Attempts.** Attempt 1 has every condition on the card (formula, both demands, "refer to the intermolecular forces"). Attempt 2 gives both samples, the calcium ions, the observations and the soap's formula. Feedback 2's model equation is the correct net ionic equation with state symbols.
- **Absolute words.** "break all three ester links" (correct); "three for every fat molecule" (correct); "no charge at all" (correct for non-ionic); "nothing to grab" (correct: no charged head); "can't re-form the ester" (correct in base); "the soap in the scum can't clean" (correct); "can't hydrogen bond" (finding 2); "stays that way" and "can't join back together" (finding 3).
