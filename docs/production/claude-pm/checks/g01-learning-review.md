Under its stated condition (both terms agree, T above zero, delta H and delta S treated as constant) it is correct: delta H negative with delta S positive makes delta G negative at every temperature, and delta H positive with delta S negative makes delta G positive at every temperature. ## Key notes (suggested wording)

1. Enthalpy change (delta H) is the heat a reaction gives out (negative) or takes in (positive). Entropy (S) measures how spread out the particles and energy are.
2. Delta G = delta H - T delta S, with T in kelvin. A negative delta G means the reaction is spontaneous (it can run on its own). A positive delta G means it needs energy fed in.
3. A non-equilibrium system has a net change still going, with no steady balance.
4. Combustion (for example glucose): delta H is negative and entropy increases, so delta G is very negative and the reaction runs essentially to completion. Address both delta H and T delta S in an answer.
5. Photosynthesis is combustion reversed: delta H is positive and entropy decreases, so delta G is large and positive. It runs only while sunlight keeps supplying energy to an open system.
6. Delta G shows where a change is heading, not how fast it gets there.

Aid (large): *Heat out and spread out: it can run on its own. Heat in and packed in: it needs energy fed in all the time.*
