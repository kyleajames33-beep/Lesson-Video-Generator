## Main question and the useful outcome

Main question (as stated): "Oxidise propan-1-ol and distil, and you get propanal; oxidise propan-2-ol, and you get propanone. Both are C₃H₆O, and both are colourless liquids, yet only one turns a test tube into a silver mirror. How do we name aldehydes and ketones, how does the carbonyl's position change their properties, and how can a test tell them apart?"

Useful outcome: a student can decide aldehyde or ketone from the carbonyl's position, name both (with branches), recognise the propanal and propanone isomer pair, explain their boiling points and solubility from their forces, and explain why an aldehyde gives a positive Tollens', Fehling's or dichromate test and a ketone does not, then use a test on a distilled oxidation product to identify the alcohol's class.

One main question: yes, with a seam (as in c05). The silver mirror hook is answered in scenes 8 to 11; naming and forces (scenes 3 to 7) sit between. The single idea "where the C=O sits, and whether its carbon has an H" runs through every part (name, forces, test), and the memory aid says so. Keep as one video.

## Keeping Tollens' and Fehling's (Open decision)

The site's banner calls the tests "identification enrichment" and "not prescribed in Module 7". Recommendation: **keep both, as designed.** Reasons: `C7-IQ4-06` asks students to investigate the products of oxidation, and without a test that tells an aldehyde from a ketone, the investigation has no evidence; HSC-style items commonly give test results as data to interpret; the tests turn the lesson's one causal idea (an H on the carbonyl carbon) into something a student can see; and the design already keeps reagent formulas and equations out of learner copy. Fehling's adds a second, contrasting observation (a coloured precipitate at the base, not a coating) for about 25 s, and the narration stays within budget, so the saving from cutting it is not needed. The narration never claims the tests are prescribed recall, and it should not. No change.

## Findings

### S1. Should fix: the hook recalls a reaction c08a did not show

- Exact text (scene 1): "In the alcohol reactions video, oxidising propan-one-ol, and distilling, gave an aldehyde, propanal. Oxidising propan-two-ol gave a ketone, propanone."
- Student question: "I don't remember propanal. Did I miss part of the last video?" c08a used ethanol to ethanal for the primary case, and propanal only in an author note.
- Replacement (scene 1): "In the alcohol reactions video, oxidising propan-two-ol gave a ketone, propanone. Oxidise propan-one-ol and distil, as we did with ethanol, and you get an aldehyde, propanal." (+about 10 characters.) The grey thumbnail stays; reorder its lines to match (propan-2-ol first).
- Understanding enabled: the callback is accurate, and the student extends c08a's ethanol case to a new primary alcohol, which is the transfer the hook needs.

### S2. Should fix: use pentane, not butane, for a fair size comparison

- Exact text (scene 6): "For molecules of similar size, butane boils near minus one degree, butanal at seventy five, butan-two-one at eighty, which is close, and butan-one-ol at one hundred and **eighteen**."
- Student question (after c05's equal-electron pair): "Butane has fewer electrons. How much of its low boiling point is just size?" Butane has 34 electrons and 58 g/mol; butanal and butanone 40 and 72; butan-1-ol 42 and 74. Pentane has 42 electrons and 72 g/mol, matching the others, and still boils far lower (36 °C).
- Replacement (scene 6): "For molecules of similar size, pentane boils at thirty six degrees, butanal at seventy five, butan-two-one at eighty, which is close, and butan-one-ol at one hundred and **eighteen**." (+1 character.) Strip mark "pentane 36 °C" (tag "dispersion only"), above the 25 °C line. Key note 3: "Similar size: pentane 36 °C < butanal 75 °C ≈ butan-2-one 80 °C < butan-1-ol 118 °C." Author note a: pentane 36.1 °C, 42 electrons, 72.1 g/mol.
- Understanding enabled: the comparison isolates the type of force, as c05 taught, so "the middle" is earned by the forces, not by size.

### S3. Should fix: say what a dipole–dipole attraction is

- Exact text (scene 6): "Between the molecules, the polar C double bond O gives **dipole-dipole** attractions, on top of dispersion forces."
- Student question: "What's a dipole–dipole attraction? c03 only had temporary dipoles." It is Year 11 knowledge, not used since, and the vocabulary list promises a plain meaning at first use.
- Replacement (scene 6): "Between the molecules, the polar C double bond O gives **dipole-dipole** attractions: the slightly positive carbon of one molecule attracts the slightly negative oxygen of the next. That's on top of dispersion forces." (+about 95 characters.) The teal lines already show this; cue them on "attracts".
- Understanding enabled: the student can point to which end attracts which, and can contrast it with c03's temporary dipoles.

### M1. Minor: state the first check in scene 12

- Exact text: "Oxidise an unknown alcohol and distil off the product."
- Replacement: "Oxidise an unknown alcohol. If the dichromate turns green, distil off the product." (+about 30 characters.) The screen chip "stayed orange at the start → tertiary" then matches the narration.

### M2. Minor: scope "one question" to aldehydes and ketones

- Exact text (scene 11): "So all three tests really ask one question."
- Replacement: "So, for an aldehyde or a ketone, all three tests ask one question." (+about 20 characters.) This prevents scene 12's "Don't rely on dichromate" sounding like a contradiction.

### M3. Minor: scene 6's silent chips

- Remove the silent "butanoic acid 164 °C: acids video" chip (forward content, not spoken, while four marks and a crossed chip are present). Keep the c05 chip but write it only after the force view shrinks and dim it before "eighteen".
