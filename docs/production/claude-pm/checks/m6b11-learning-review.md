Main question traced: *A CRISPR treatment for sickle cell disease is already approved, and headlines promise far more. So which future uses of biotechnology are plausible, and how can you tell?* Useful outcome: given an unseen proposed use, the student can explain its mechanism and possible benefit, place it on the evidence ladder, name its uncertainties, and phrase a judgement as "could, if" with the condition named (the route stop: "explain the mechanism, possible benefit and uncertainty").

## Strengths to keep

- **Two cases on two rungs.** The approved sickle cell treatment (top rung) and the caged gene drive (animals rung) make "how far has the evidence got?" a visible comparison rather than a list.
- **"Works in a trial" is separated from "available to everyone"** with concrete causes (chemotherapy, weeks in hospital, price, where patients are), which is the route's centre and the site's key idea 3.
- **The gene drive is built from Module 5 inheritance.** The offspring row filling from four of eight to eight of eight shows the half-and-half rule being beaten; the female fertility target then explains the crash causally (one copy breeds, two cannot).
- **"Could, if" is modelled twice** (scene 9 and 11d), so the evaluate item has a pattern to copy. The scene 2 mechanism (guide pairs, Cas9 cuts, repair adds or loses bases) pays off twice: in the exa-cel edit and the drive's copying.
- **The project manager's decisions are sound.** Scene 2 is needed for the route stop and is kept to one scene; the gene drive gives a well-separated lower-rung case and supplies b13's mechanism; "cheats" is informal but not misleading (the drive does break the usual inheritance ratio), so it stays.
