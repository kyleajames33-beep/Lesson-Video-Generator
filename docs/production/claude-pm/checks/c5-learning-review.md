## Key notes (suggested wording)

1. At a given temperature, the ratio of products to reactants written from the balanced equation, with each concentration raised to the power of its coefficient, stays constant for a settled mixture, even when a disturbance changes every amount. Its value is the equilibrium constant, K.
2. For aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD, K = [C]^c [D]^d / ([A]^a [B]^b), written for the equation as given. Products go on top, reactants underneath. Square brackets go around the whole ion, including its own brackets and charge.
3. Pure solids, pure liquids and water as the solvent do not appear, because their concentration does not change, so it is already part of K. Gases and aqueous species do appear, and so does water as a gas, H2O(g).
4. Rough guides: above about 10^3, the settled mixture is almost all products; below about 10^-3, almost all reactants; in between, measurable amounts of both. K says where the mixture settles, not how fast.

Aid (large): *Products over reactants, coefficients become powers, pure solids and liquids drop out.* Tag: "written for the equation as given, concentrations in mol/L, water as solvent or gas."
