Main question traced: *Bacteria making insulin and an edited human embryo both used genetic technology to promise a health benefit, yet one is accepted worldwide and the other was condemned. So which benefits of genetic technology actually hold up, and how can you tell?* Useful outcome: the student can test any benefit claim with "Better than what? Shown how? Who gets it?", explain why the size of a benefit depends on the alternative it replaced (insulin against growth hormone), explain why farm uses are the most contested (released, not contained), and give a ranked, supported verdict across the three domains.

## Strengths to keep

- **The insulin surprise (scene 4).** Students expect human insulin to work better; the trial bars show it doesn't, so the benefit moves to supply. This is a real "predict, then see" beat, and the bead strips make the one and three amino acid differences visible before the result lands.
- **The insulin against growth hormone contrast (scenes 4 and 5)** shows that "better than what?" sets how big a benefit is. It is the video's best causal move and it transfers directly into attempt 2.
- **Scene 7's sealed tank beside an open field** gives the cause of agriculture being the most contested, not just the conclusion. The site states only the conclusion.
- **One visual system.** The benefit board builds one column per case and enlarges for the ranking, so scene 8 is a look-across comparison, not a new slide.
- **Both attempts are genuine transfer.** The laundry item tests "shown how?" (the saving needs a behaviour change); the vaccine item applies scene 4's insight to a new product. The feedback is brief and each has one crossed mistake.
- **No people.** The embryo case is carried by the injected-egg plate and chips only.
