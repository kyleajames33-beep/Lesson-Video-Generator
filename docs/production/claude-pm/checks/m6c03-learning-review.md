## Main question and the useful outcome

Main question (as stated): "Mixing an acid with a base warms the solution. How do you turn that temperature rise into the heat released per mole of water, and why does it come out negative?"

One main question: yes. The hook experiment (HCl + NaOH, 21.5 to 28.0 °C) carries it from scene 1 through the full worked example (6 to 9) to attempt 2's scaling prediction. Scenes 2 to 4 give the cause (only H⁺ + OH⁻ reacts) and the reason for "per mole of water"; scenes 10 and 11 add the one harder case an HSC question will test (unequal amounts, a two-hydrogen-ion acid). Useful outcome: a student can calculate ΔH of neutralisation from volumes, concentrations and temperatures, choose the right mass and the right moles, give it the right sign and size, and say why heat loss makes a school result less negative.

## Trace from the entry knowledge (student starting Year 12 Module 6)

Entry: C02 acid + hydroxide equations; C01 H⁺(aq) as H₃O⁺; Year 11 q = mcΔT, ΔH = −q/n, n = cV, limiting reagent, net ionic equations. The trace follows the hook experiment.

1. Hook: two clear solutions, no fizz, readout climbs. A Year 11 student can see this and the question "how would you compare different amounts?" is the right one for "per mole". Good.
2. Why it warms: spectator ions get a plain meaning; "the water has less chemical energy than the two ions had" is a plain cause, and the energy step beside the lens is a genuine visual explanation. Good.
3. Exothermic, negative, per mole of water: the two-cup comparison (twice the water, twice the heat) earns the definition before stating it. Good.
4. About −57 whichever pair: the stack of pairs shedding their spectators to the same strip is a strong visual argument. Two problems: the term "strong acid" is never named although students will meet it on the site and in exam wording (S1), and the screen plans three pairs, a strip, a value card, a three-clause condition tag, a dashed chip and a crossed chip (S5). The H₂SO₄ + NaOH pair in this stack also carries a science problem (science check S2).
5. Calorimeter and q = mcΔT: each symbol gets one meaning in turn. Good. Attempt 1 says "polystyrene cup" but the lesson only says "foam cup" (M3).
6. Worked example 1 task and givens: task, strip and grouped givens are separate, as required. But the three-step ladder adds three more lines that stay on screen in scenes 7 to 9 beside the task line, the strip and the givens card (S3).
7. Step 1, the heat: total mass explained by cause ("the heat warms everything in the cup") with a crossed 50.0 g chip. Good.
8. Step 2, the moles: **a careful student will ask where 0.0500 came from.** The narration says "one point zero zero times zero point zero five zero zero" straight after "volume in litres"; the conversion 50.0 mL = 0.0500 L is not said or shown (M1).
9. Step 3: divide, sign, compare. The sign reason is causal ("the chemicals gave heat away"). The answer is left at −54.3 kJ/mol although ΔT (6.5 °C) has two significant figures; a student copying this would lose the precision mark the HSC expects, and the video never says when or why to round (S2). Heat loss as the reason is well placed and visual.
10. Worked example 2, counting: the two-bar comparison with a leftover stub tagged "no reaction, no heat" is the best visual in the lesson. The scene is overloaded, though: task, strip, four-group givens card, a separate H₂SO₄ lens, three n lines, the bars, a chip and a tag, with 567 characters of narration full of spoken numbers (S4).
11. Worked example 2, heat and ΔH: "the leftover solution still gets warmed" answers the student's obvious question. Good. Answer again unrounded (S2).
12. Memory aid: three short, reconstructable rules, each checked against the lesson. The condition tag makes four text items (M2).
13 to 16. Attempts and feedback: see below.
17 and 18. Decision rule and notes: separate from new reasoning; quiet copy hold. Notes 1 and 2 repeat each other (S6).

## Findings

### S1. Should fix: name "strong acid" once (project manager decision a)

- Exact text (scene 4): "For an acid and a base that are completely split into ions, like these two, the reaction is the same one"
- Student question: "The site lesson and exam questions say 'strong acid and strong base'. Is that what you mean?"
- Replacement: "Acids that split completely into ions like this are called **strong acids**, explained in a later video. With a strong acid and a base that's also completely split, the reaction is always the same one: hydrogen ion plus hydroxide ion makes water."
- Visual: a small tag "strong acid: splits completely into ions (later video)" under the HCl tile, dimming with the pairs.
- Understanding enabled: the student links the plain meaning to the exam term without strength being taught here.

### S2. Should fix: round final answers to the precision of the data, and say why once (project manager decision b)

- Exact text (scene 9): "and delta H is **minus fifty four point three kilojoules per mole**."; scene 11 "gives **minus fifty five point two kilojoules per mole**"; scene 14 "**minus fifty five point seven kilojoules per mole**"; key note 5 "ΔH = −54.3 kJ/mol".
- Student question: "How many figures do I give in the exam?" Every ΔT here (6.5, 6.6, 7.4 °C) has two significant figures.
- Replacement (scene 9): "Two point seven one seven kilojoules over zero point zero five zero zero moles is fifty four point three. Delta T has only **two significant figures**, so round to fifty four. ... delta H is **minus fifty four kilojoules per mole**." Scene 11: "gives fifty five point two, so delta H is **minus fifty five kilojoules per mole**." Scene 14: "Divide by the moles of water and round: **minus fifty six kilojoules per mole**." Keep intermediate values unrounded (screen tag "keep the digits; round at the end" in scene 7). Corrected values are listed in the science check S1.
- Understanding enabled: the student rounds once, at the end, to the data's precision.

### S3. Should fix: the three-step ladder becomes a fourth bright text item in scenes 6 to 9

- Visual text (scene 6): "a three-step ladder writes small at the right: "1 q = mcΔT", "2 n(H₂O)", "3 ΔH = −q ÷ n"", then "ladder step 1 lights" (7), "ladder step 2 lights" (8), "ladder step 3 lights" (9).
- Problem: task line, equation strip, givens card, ladder and the current line are five text items during new reasoning.
- Repair: show the ladder in scene 6 as the plan, then collapse it into one step chip under the task line: "Step 1 of 3: q", "Step 2 of 3: n(H₂O)", "Step 3 of 3: ΔH".
- Understanding kept: the roadmap is still visible, at one item's cost.

### S4. Should fix: scene 10 crowds the screen and the ear

- Visual text (scene 10): "On "two" an H₂SO₄ molecule in a small lens releases two teal tokens. Lines write one per cue ... On "run out first" a two-bar comparison".
- Problem: givens card, strip, task line, a lens, three n lines, two bars, a chip and a tag, under the longest narration in the video (567 characters).
- Repair: drop the lens (ring the subscript 2 on the strip and drop two teal tokens from it); light each givens group only while it is used; fold the three n lines into the bars, labelled "H⁺ 0.0500 mol" and "OH⁻ 0.0750 mol". Trim the narration: cut "Now a harder one." and "Count the ions." and say "two hydrogen ions" (as scene 2 does) rather than switching to "protons".
- Understanding enabled: one comparison, the bars, carries the limiting-reactant reasoning.

### S5. Should fix: scene 4 plans six text items at once

- Visual text (scene 4): value card, condition tag "acids and bases fully split into ions; dilute; no precipitate", dashed chip "partly split acids: later video", and a crossed chip "−57 kJ/mol for every acid and base" (caption "only when fully split into ions"), with three formula pairs still lit.
- Repair: dim the pairs once the bracket joins them to the strip; shorten the condition tag to "dilute; no precipitate" (the strong-acid tag now carries "fully split"); merge the dashed chip into the crossed chip's caption, "partly split acids differ: later video".

### S6. Should fix: key notes 1 and 2 repeat each other; note 5 must match S2

- Exact text: note 1 "For acids and bases completely split into ions, the only reaction is H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l) ..."; note 2 "About −57 kJ/mol for acids and bases completely split into ions (same net reaction)".
- Replacement note 1: "Enthalpy of neutralisation: the enthalpy change per mole of water formed. Exothermic, so ΔH is negative: about −57 kJ/mol for a strong acid with a base fully split into ions (only H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l) happens; the other ions are spectators)." Keep q = mcΔT and the moles note (add "rounded to the data's significant figures"); the example note ends "ΔH = −54 kJ/mol". Four notes instead of five.

### M1. Minor: say the millilitre-to-litre step (scene 8)

- Exact text: "Moles equals concentration times volume in litres. For the acid, one point zero zero times zero point zero five zero zero gives"
- Replacement: "Moles equals concentration times volume in litres, and fifty millilitres is zero point zero five zero zero litres. For the acid, ..." with a grey tag "50.0 mL = 0.0500 L".

### M2. Minor: scene 12 has four text items

- Visual text: "Condition tag: "q is positive; the sign goes on ΔH"". The narration already says it. Remove the tag.

### M3. Minor: "foam cup" against "polystyrene cup"

- Exact text (scene 5): "mix the solutions in a lidded foam cup, called a **calorimeter**." Attempt 1 says "polystyrene cup".
- Replacement: "mix the solutions in a lidded polystyrene foam cup, called a **calorimeter**."

### M4. Minor: attempt 1 narration reads the assumptions aloud

- Exact text (scene 13): "Assume a density of one point zero zero grams per millilitre and a specific heat capacity of four point one eight joules per gram per degree. Calculate the molar enthalpy of neutralisation."
- Replacement: "Use the density and specific heat capacity on screen, and calculate the molar enthalpy of neutralisation." The full worded question, including both assumptions, stays on the card. Saves about 90 characters to pay for S1, S2 and M1.

### M5. Minor: earlier lines stay bright in scenes 11 and 14

- Repair: the m and ΔT lines dim as each next line writes, so the current line, the kept n chip and the givens (or the question card) are the bright items.
