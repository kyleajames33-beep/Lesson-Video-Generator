## P01: reversibility and disturbance practical

- **Wrong syllabus outcome codes (line 7).** The design lists Working Scientifically outcomes as `CH12-2` to `CH12-6`. The 2017 Chemistry syllabus codes these `CH11/12-2` to `CH11/12-6`. The two quoted content dotpoints and their ACSCH codes match the [official syllabus](https://westwyalon-h.schools.nsw.gov.au/content/dam/doe/sws/schools/w/westwyalon-h/localcontent/chemistrystage6syllabus2017.pdf).
- **Open tube shown as closed (scene 4, line 53).** The uncapped iron solution tube gets a solid boundary and the label "closed: matter stays in" because its ions stay in water. Water vapour can leave an open tube, so this apparatus does not meet the stated closed-system definition. [Open and closed system definitions](https://chem.libretexts.org/Courses/University_of_Wisconsin_Oshkosh/Chem_370%3A_Physical_Chemistry_1_-_Thermodynamics_%28Gutow%29/01%3A_Thermodynamics/1.01%3A_Thermodynamic_Variables_and_Equations_of_State).
- **Acid hazard depends on an undecided recipe (scenes 9 and 11, lines 78 and 88).** The screen says the cobalt solution contains acid and specifically concentrated hydrochloric acid. Author note (c), line 24, also permits a high-chloride salt solution. The displayed hazard is not true for both permitted preparations. The risk table needs to describe the actual solution used.

## P03: 48-well solubility practical

- **Syllabus quotation altered (line 7).** The design turns the three example bullet lines into a semicolon-separated sentence. The 2017 Chemistry syllabus has the main dotpoint followed by separate examples for potassium chloride and silver nitrate, potassium iodide and lead nitrate, and sodium sulfate and barium nitrate. The quotation is not word for word as required. The content code `CH12-13` is correct. [Official syllabus, page 51](https://westwyalon-h.schools.nsw.gov.au/content/dam/doe/sws/schools/w/westwyalon-h/localcontent/chemistrystage6syllabus2017.pdf).
- **Calcium sulfate outcome contradicts the stated concentrations (expected matrix, scene 5 and attempt 2, lines 14, 55 to 58, 100 to 108).** Three equal drops each of 0.1 mol/L calcium nitrate and sodium sulfate give about 0.05 mol/L of each ion. Their ion product is 0.0025, about 50 times a tabulated calcium sulfate solubility product of 4.9 × 10⁻⁵. At equilibrium, roughly 0.043 mol/L calcium sulfate precipitates under the simple model. The design calls this only a faint cloud and treats no precipitate as a plausible matched result. [Solubility constants](https://chem.libretexts.org/Ancillary_Materials/Reference/Reference_Tables/Equilibrium_Constants/E3._Solubility_Constants_for_Compounds_at_25C).
- **Lead chloride prediction is too weak (expected matrix and scene 5, lines 14 and 58).** The same equal-drop stocks give approximately 0.05 mol/L lead and chloride, so `Q = [Pb²⁺][Cl⁻]² = 1.25 × 10⁻⁴`, above `Ksp = 1.7 × 10⁻⁵`. The screen predicts "faint, or none at this concentration". A 0.1 mol/L lead nitrate and chloride demonstration produces white lead chloride. [Royal Society of Chemistry demonstration](https://edu.rsc.org/experiments/silver-and-lead-halides/1770.article); [solubility constant](https://chem.libretexts.org/Courses/University_of_California_Davis/UCD_Chem_002B/UCD_Chem_2B/Text/Unit_III%3A_Chemical_Equilibria/17%3A_Solubility_and_Complex-Ion_Equilibria/17.3%3A_Common-Ion_Effect_in_Solubility_Equilibria).
- **Drop-count visual does not show a concentration change (scene 4, lines 51 to 53).** It compares fewer and more equal-ratio drops of the same two 0.1 mol/L stocks, with a clear well turning cloudy. The mixed concentrations and ion product stay the same when both drop counts rise in the same ratio. Only the total amount and possibly visibility change.
- **The excess-ion test gives a false either-or rule (scene 8, lines 70 to 73).** The screen says "at most one test goes cloudy" after adding iodide to one portion of liquid above lead iodide and lead nitrate to another. A saturated supernatant contains both dissolved lead and iodide ions. Adding either common ion can produce more solid, so both tests can cloud even when neither starting reagent was in stoichiometric excess. The same issue affects the categorical claim in feedback 1 (lines 95 to 98) that added chloride cannot produce more silver chloride if chloride was in excess. [Common-ion effect](https://chem.libretexts.org/Courses/University_of_California_Davis/UCD_Chem_002B/UCD_Chem_2B/Text/Unit_III%3A_Chemical_Equilibria/17%3A_Solubility_and_Complex-Ion_Equilibria/17.3%3A_Common-Ion_Effect_in_Solubility_Equilibria).
- **YouTube metadata is absent.** There is no title or description block to check against the required syllabus quotation.

## B11: roan cattle frequencies and SNP evidence

- **Heterozygote called a carrier (lines 59 and 61).** The CT genotype is labelled a "carrier" without any recessive or disease variant being identified. CT establishes heterozygosity; carrier has a narrower genetic meaning. [NHGRI carrier](https://www.genome.gov/genetics-glossary/Carrier) and [heterozygous](https://www.genome.gov/genetics-glossary/Heterozygous) definitions.
- **Sampling outcome stated as certain (line 84).** The narration says a sample of 200 cattle means "you'll stay close to twenty". A larger random sample makes a close result more likely; it does not guarantee one.
- **Syllabus quotation altered (line 12).** The main 2017 Biology dotpoint ends "for example:" and has separate bullets for "examining frequency data" and "analysing single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP)". The design joins them with semicolons. The text is therefore not the required word-for-word quotation. The `BIO12-12` outcome code is correct. [Official syllabus, page 49](https://educationstandards.nsw.edu.au/wps/wcm/connect/657d2611-c201-49ce-a18e-ef0f786a5de0/biology-stage-6-syllabus-2017.pdf?MOD=AJPERES).

## B12: sequencing and profiling for inheritance

- **The hook visual draws a stronger conclusion than its evidence (scene 1, line 41).** The drawing shows only the mother and boy, connects some matching bands, then changes their relationship line to a tick. Shared maternal bands alone do not establish that he is her son. The 1985 investigation also used three undisputed siblings to reconstruct paternal bands. [University of Leicester case account](https://le.ac.uk/-/media/uol/docs/microsites/dna-fingerprinting/genetics-booklet.pdf).
- **A sequencing method is described as universal (scene 3, lines 49 to 51).** "Sequencing never" reads a chromosome whole, then the narration says each base kind is tagged with its own coloured dye and reads are assembled from overlapping broken copies. This is a fluorescent short-read workflow, not a property of every sequencing method. Nanopore sequencing, for example, identifies bases from changes in electrical current without dyes. [NHGRI sequencing fact sheet](https://www.genome.gov/about-genomics/fact-sheets/DNA-Sequencing-Fact-Sheet).
- **Profiling is defined too narrowly (scenes 2, 9a, 9b and 11d, lines 44, 79 to 86 and 109).** The narration says profiling reads no bases, "only measures repeat lengths", cannot see a particular base and "can't tell you about genes". These statements fit the STR length method depicted here, but DNA profiles can also use SNPs or sequenced STRs. They are false as general statements about DNA profiling. [NIST forensic SNP information](https://strbase-archive.nist.gov/SNP.htm); [NIST on sequence-based profiles](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2018/07/nist-builds-statistical-foundation-next-generation-forensic-dna-profiling).
- **YouTube metadata is absent.** There is no title or description block to check. The cited 2017 Biology parent dotpoint, `BIO12-12`, `ACSBL064`, `ACSBL085` and the "DNA sequencing and profiling (ACSBL086)" example in line 12 match the [official syllabus, page 50](https://educationstandards.nsw.edu.au/wps/wcm/connect/657d2611-c201-49ce-a18e-ef0f786a5de0/biology-stage-6-syllabus-2017.pdf?MOD=AJPERES).
