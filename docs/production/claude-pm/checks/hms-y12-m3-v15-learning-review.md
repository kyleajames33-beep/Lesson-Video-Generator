## Findings

1. **Should fix, the overdiagnosis pile does not fit the funnel's numbers.** Before the arithmetic, "a few dots drop into the amber chute 'true, never harmful'". The arithmetic then gives 9 true finds in the green chute and about 50 false alarms in the grey chute, framed "59 positives, 9 true". A student counting the piles sees 9 + a few + 50 and asks where the amber dots came from; or reads overdiagnosis as a third kind of positive outside the 59. Overdiagnosed people are true positives. Fix the picture: the 9 true finds drop first, then 2 or 3 of them slide sideways from the green chute into the amber chute, so the piles read "helped", "true, never harmful" and "false alarm" and still sum to 59. The frame becomes "59 positives: 9 true, some of those never harmful". No narration change is needed; if a spoken clause is wanted: "and some of those nine would never have been harmed." Students should see overdiagnosis as a subset of correct results, which is what makes it "invisible from the inside".

2. **Note, 8a's marking is hard to apply.** The card asks for three things for each of two technologies (six elements) for 4 marks, and the marking line "1 mark each, combined to 2 for watch including who first" is unclear. State it plainly: for each technology, 1 mark for gain with risk, 1 mark for the group that benefits first. The feedback already covers all six.

3. **Note, scene 4 at about 900 characters carries the most new ideas in the video.** Screening gains, overdiagnosis, false positives with arithmetic, lead time and incidental findings. Split at "Take a thousand people" into 4a (gains, overdiagnosis) and 4b (the funnel arithmetic and what follows); no narration change.

4. **Note, the hook's "who pays?" tags are never filled.** The good and bad columns are answered implicitly by scenes 2 to 4. Fill the hook's columns silently at the end of scene 4 ("good: a real rhythm problem caught"; "bad: false alarm, anxiety, tests"; "pays: the person, then Medicare") so the hook closes.
