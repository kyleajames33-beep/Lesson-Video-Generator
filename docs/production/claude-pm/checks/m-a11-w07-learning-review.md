**Verdict: blocker** (1 blocker, 1 should fix, 0 optional).

One button, one snack is a strong anchor for "exactly one output", and A1 and B1 sharing chips teaches early that repeated outputs are allowed. The reversal shows that direction matters. The vertical line test is justified (inputs sit on vertical lines), and the horizontal-line misconception is crossed. The attempts and their answers are correct.

## Findings

1. **Blocker, scene 3 (water teaches that an input may have no output).** Narration: "So the reverse is not a function, even though the original was. Direction matters. And water has no arrow pointing to it at all. That's fine too, because nothing says every output must be used." Visual: by then the ovals have swapped and relabelled "inputs: snacks", and water glows with the tag "no arrow in: allowed". On screen, water is now an input with no output, labelled as allowed. That contradicts the definition the scene has just given (each element of the first set has exactly one output), and it is the main idea of the lesson. Fix: make the water point while the arrows still run from buttons to snacks, and treat it as a second failure after the reversal. Replacement narration from "so this relation is not a function": "...so this relation is not a **function**. Fix B2, so it only gives the muesli bar. Now notice water. No button gives it, and that's fine, because nothing says every output must be used. Now turn the arrows round, from snacks to buttons. Chips points to A1 and to B1, two outputs for one input. And water points nowhere, an input with no output. So the reverse is not a function, even though the original **was**. Direction matters." Visual: the grey tag "no arrow in: allowed" appears while water is in the outputs oval; after the swap, water gets a red tag "input, no output: not allowed". The student should understand that every input needs exactly one output, while outputs may be unused or shared.

2. **Should fix, attempt 1 and feedback 1 (root ruling 6: trim).** Feedback 1 is about 32 s of speech plus four mini diagrams, beyond the 30 s limit. Part (d), x² + y² = 9, is scene 5's own example, so it tests recall, not application. Drop (d). Card: "Which of the following relations are functions? Give a reason for each. (a) {(1, 2), (2, 3), (3, 2)} (b) {(2, 1), (2, 5), (4, 0)} (c) y = x² − 1". Attempt narration: "Decide which of the three relations on the card are functions, and give a reason for each." Feedback: delete "D is a circle, and x equals zero gives y equals three and minus three, so it's not a **function**." and its mini diagram. That leaves about 25 s and keeps the crossed slip on (a).
