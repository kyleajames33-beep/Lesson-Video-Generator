Main question traced: *Amines and amides both contain nitrogen. An amine's solution turns red litmus blue and its smell vanishes with lemon juice, but an amide leaves litmus unchanged. What does the C=O beside an amide's nitrogen change, and how does an organic base differ from an organic acid?* Useful outcome: given an amine or amide, the student can classify and name it, explain its boiling point against an alcohol, acid or isomer, write the amine's equation with water and with HCl, explain why an amine is a weak base and an amide is close to neutral, and set a carboxylic acid against an amine by proton behaviour, litmus, the hydrogen carbonate test and the salt they make.

## Scope and focus

One question, held together by the nitrogen's lone pair: free in an amine (a base), shared in an amide (barely a base), with the acid against base comparison as the payoff. That is a fair single learner question. The video is full (two families named and classified, two sets of boiling points, base equilibrium, salts, amide basicity, the comparison) and sits at the 9.5 minute ceiling, but each idea has its own scene and the boundary is right (no Kb, no aniline, no resonance structures, no hydrolysis, no polyamides). The fixes below add explanation, so the cut plan in finding 9 matters.

No double teaching. c09's shared-charge idea is recalled in one sentence in scene 9 with a grey thumbnail, not re-taught; c09's litmus and fizz results are one sentence and grey chips in scene 10; c10's condensation is "just like esterification"; Module 6's ammonia equilibrium is a grey chip. Amines and amides are first taught here.

## Strengths to keep

- **The hook is a real contrast with a payoff.** Fish, lemon and litmus set up the amine as a base; ethanamide "has a nitrogen too" but leaves litmus unchanged. Scene 8 closes the lemon trick and scene 9 closes the amide.
- **Classification by counting carbon groups on N, with propan-2-amine as the trap** and the alcohol rule shown and crossed.
- **Fair comparisons throughout.** Every boiling point pair is matched for electron count, and the narration says so ("same number of electrons"), which uses c03's dispersion rule.
- **Electronegativity gives the cause** for amines boiling below alcohols, with the smaller δ+ drawn.
- **The salt beat explains the smell**: ions attract strongly, so the salt barely evaporates, shown with the vapour check.
- **The lone pair grab and pair share motions** make the base and not-base cases visible side by side in scene 9.
- **Scene 10 says positively that room temperature gives a salt, not an amide**, with the crossed chip.
- **Both attempts are unseen transfer** (C₄H₁₁N isomers; propanoic acid, propan-1-amine, propanamide), with one crossed mistake each and brief feedback.
