Main question traced: *One species, two very different gene pools: what changes the alleles of a population, and which change matters most?* Useful outcome: given a population and what happened to it, the student can name which process changed its allele frequencies (mutation, gene flow, drift, or selection by contrast), calculate a frequency as copies over total copies, and judge which process matters most from size, isolation and timescale.

## Strengths to keep

- **One real case carries all three processes.** The Florida panther gives a bottleneck and drift (harmful alleles common), mutation too slow to help, and gene flow restoring variation, with a measured outcome. The hook's question ("What had gone wrong ... and how did eight newcomers help fix it?") is answered in scenes 4 and 6. The project manager's decision to keep it is sound.
- **Causes come before names.** The scoop takes a random handful before "genetic drift"; the basin narrows before "bottleneck"; the females arrive and breed before "gene flow" becomes a process name.
- **Drift is shown, not asserted.** Seeded replicate lines at N = 20 and N = 500 let a student predict the wandering and see it; the 0.02 run losing most new alleles makes "mutation is slow" a visible consequence of drift.
- **The judgement is built for the verb.** Scene 7 and both feedbacks end with a verdict by context, which "evaluate" needs. The selection contrast is one sentence and is the distinction HSC questions test.
- **The attempts are genuine transfer.** 9a gives the missing "survival unrelated to this gene" condition, so drift is the justified answer and the copies-not-individuals trap (4/10) is live; 9c is a new species and a plan, not the panther.
