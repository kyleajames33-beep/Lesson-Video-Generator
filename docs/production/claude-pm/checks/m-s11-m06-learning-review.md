## Blocker

**B1. Attempt 2 is answered before it is asked, and its picture slot shows the answer during the countdown.** Scene 7 uses the same two cars as A2 (car A $18 000 at 9 L/100 km, car B $22 000 at 6) and shows car B's bar finishing below car A's, with the narration "by year five it has caught up". A2 then asks "Which is cheaper over five years, and by how much?", and its picture slot is "scene 7's two unlabelled bars (small)", so the answer to (b) is on screen during the countdown. The student only has to compute the amount, and the comparison decision, which is the point of the question, has been given away. Fix (keeps scene 7 and its surprise): change A2's distance to 10 000 km a year, so the result reverses. Car A: 10 000 × 9 ÷ 100 = 900 L, × 2.25 = $2025 a year, 18 000 + 5 × 2025 = $28 125. Car B: 600 L, $1350 a year, 22 000 + 5 × 1350 = $28 750. Car A is cheaper by $625. Replace the picture slot with two blank purchase blocks. Feedback: "At ten thousand kilometres, car B's fuel saving is only six seventy-five a year, so it never catches up. Car A is cheaper by six hundred and twenty-five dollars. Picking car B because it was cheaper in the video is the slip." This also links back to scene 5 ("a cost per kilometre only means something next to the distance it assumes") and tests understanding instead of recall. Update Numbers to verify.
