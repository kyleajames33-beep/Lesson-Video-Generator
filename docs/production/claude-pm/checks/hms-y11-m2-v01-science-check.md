# hms-y11-m2-v01 science check (skeleton, joints and joint actions)

Revision 2 design checked 24 November 2026 (source-only: script, on-screen text in the visual lines, practice, key notes, memory aids and the facts section; no build, audio or playback). Independent science and fact check; the design was not edited.

## Confirmed
- Bone counts (scene 3): 206 bones; axial about 80, appendicular about 126; 7 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar vertebrae; 12 pairs of ribs. Standard anatomy.
- 23 intervertebral discs (scene 4): correct (no disc between atlas and axis). The site's "twenty-four" is rightly corrected.
- Five functions, red marrow, mineral storage, Wolff's law and bone loss in spaceflight (scene 2): correct.
- Joint classes and the mobility-stability trade-off (4, 5): correct. The glenohumeral joint is the most mobile and the most frequently dislocated major joint; "the one that dislocates most often" is acceptable (see note 3).
- Synovial structures (6): articular cartilage avascular and slow to heal; the synovial membrane secretes fluid that lubricates and nourishes cartilage; ligaments bone to bone; tendons muscle to bone; bursae where tendons cross bone. Correct.
- Six synovial shapes with examples (7), including the Revision 2 wrist split (gliding between carpals; condyloid radiocarpal, four ways, no rotation; saddle at the thumb's carpometacarpal joint). Correct.
- Movement terms (8) and the squat (9): flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, rotation, pronation, supination, dorsiflexion, plantar flexion, circumduction; squat down hip and knee flex, ankle dorsiflexes; up all three reverse. Correct.
- 11a/11b (soccer kick): hip flexes, knee extends, ankle held plantar flexed. Correct for an instep kick in the forward swing. 11c/11d: correct and matches the site's marking.
- Memory aids: both assumptions sound as stated in the design.
- Plates and people: no figures; plates named for what they show.

## Findings
1. **Blocker (scene 1 narration and on-screen "a few degrees"; echoed in scene 7's "your knee barely does").** "Sitting down ... Now hold your thigh still and turn only your shin. You'll get a little at most." Sitting puts the knee near 90 degrees of flexion, where the tibia rotates a lot: Zarins and others (1983, *Am J Sports Med*) measured about 45 degrees external and 25 degrees internal passive rotation between 30 and 90 degrees of flexion. Rotation is small only near full extension. Students doing the demonstration as written will feel substantial shin rotation, so the hook teaches the opposite of what they observe. Corrected hook: "Sitting down, lift one foot and turn your whole leg so your toes point in, then out. Now straighten that knee, hold your thigh still, and try to turn only your shin. You'll get almost nothing." On screen: "knee straight: almost none". Scene 7 close: "and your straight knee barely does because it's a hinge, which only allows a little rotation when bent." The "a little, only when bent" chip already supports this.
2. **Should fix (scene 6 narration and heading).** "Every synovial joint has the same six parts." Bursae are not present at every synovial joint. Corrected: "A typical synovial joint has six parts." On-screen heading: "synovial joint: typical parts".
3. **Note (scene 5).** Optional precision: "the major joint that dislocates most often" (finger joints dislocate often too).
4. **Note (scene 8).** "Pronation turns the palm down" assumes the elbow is bent to 90 degrees; from the anatomical position it turns the palm backward. Acceptable at this level; the demonstration should bend the elbow.
5. **Note (11b).** In the forward swing the knee briefly keeps flexing before it extends; an answer of "flexes, then extends" at the knee should not be marked wrong.

## Verdict
Fail until fixed. 1 blocker, 1 should fix, 3 notes.
