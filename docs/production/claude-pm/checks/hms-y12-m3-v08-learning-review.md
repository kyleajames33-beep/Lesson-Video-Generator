## Findings

1. **Should fix, 8a case 2 has a second defensible answer.** The card asks for "the dimension of access that failed" in case 2, and the feedback accepts only appropriateness ("passes the first four gates every time"). But the stem never says the man has a regular GP he can reach; a student who reasons "he keeps going to emergency because no GP is available or affordable for ongoing care" names availability or affordability and proposes the same fix. The marking should accept availability or affordability if the student links it to the missing ongoing care. Add one clause to 8b: "You could also argue availability, if he has no regular GP, and the fix is the same." (about 3 s; 8b is about 27 s now). Students should be rewarded for applying the gates, not for finding the intended one.

2. **Note (science check, currency), "cost rarely blocks a GP".** After the November 2025 extension of bulk-billing incentives, GP out-of-pocket cost is falling as a barrier, but national patient experience surveys still show a minority delaying GP visits because of cost. "Cost blocks fewer people from a GP or a hospital bed" or "cost seldom blocks" is safer. The design rightly avoids narrating bulk-billing trends (site correction 29).

3. **Note, the access model now exists in two forms across the module.** v03 used the six Year 11 gates (by group); v08 regroups them into five dimensions and says "the five-gate strip is the Year 12 form". Later videos should use the five only, with the regrouping shown once here. See the module summary.

4. **Note, scene 3 density.** Six lamps, two coverage tags, a seam, two crossed cards and a gloss pair share one scene. The lamps carry it; hold a beat after "that's the finding" with all six lamps lit before the brochure and complaint cards write.
