## Main question and the useful outcome

Main question (as stated): "When a strong acid or base question dilutes a solution, mixes two, or hides a 2 in a formula, which method fits, and how do you check the answer?"

One main question: yes. All four items answer it, and the three-row check strip ("what's in there?", "in what volume?", "does it make sense?") runs through the whole video. Useful outcome: a student can find the pH and [OH⁻] of a dilute strong acid, treat "made up to" as the final volume, convert pH values to concentrations before mixing, count two hydroxides for Ba(OH)₂, divide the excess by the total volume, and check the side of 7.

## Findings

### S1. Should fix: Q4 repeats C07's worked example 3

- Exact text (5a): "Twenty five millilitres of zero point zero eight zero zero moles per litre barium hydroxide is mixed with thirty millilitres of zero point one zero zero moles per litre hydrochloric acid".
- Problem: C07 scene 11 used "zero point zero eight zero zero moles per litre barium hydroxide" with hydrochloric acid, excess hydroxide and the pOH route. The Decisions table lists the C07 case as "40.0 mL HCl with 60.0 mL Ba(OH)₂ (excess base)" without its concentration, so the "None repeats a C07 case" claim is not accurate. A student who has just watched C07 recognises the same solution and outcome, and the item tests recall rather than transfer.
- Student question: "Isn't this the one from the last video?"
- Repair: change only the barium hydroxide's volume and concentration. The HCl data, the trap, the mirror property and three significant figures in the excess are all kept (values verified in the science check):
  - Card: "40.0 mL of 0.0500 mol/L barium hydroxide, Ba(OH)₂, is mixed with 30.0 mL of 0.100 mol/L hydrochloric acid at 25 °C. Assume the volumes add."
  - 5a: "Question four. Forty millilitres of zero point zero five zero zero moles per litre barium hydroxide is mixed with thirty millilitres of zero point one zero zero moles per litre hydrochloric acid, at twenty five degrees. Assume the volumes add. Calculate the pH of the resulting solution. Pause here to answer."
  - 5b: replace "two times zero point zero eight zero zero times zero point zero two five zero litres" with "two times zero point zero five zero zero times zero point zero four zero zero litres" (the rest is unchanged: 0.00400 mol OH⁻, 0.00300 mol H₃O⁺, excess 0.00100 mol). Line: "n(OH⁻) = 2 × 0.0500 × 0.0400 = 0.00400 mol".
  - 5c: "That spreads through seventy millilitres, so the hydroxide is zero point zero one four three moles per litre. Hydroxide is what's left, so go through the pOH: one point eight four five. Then the pH is fourteen minus that, **twelve point one five five**. Check: base in excess, so the pH should be above seven, and it is." Lines: "V(total) = 40.0 + 30.0 = 70.0 mL = 0.0700 L"; "[OH⁻] = 0.00100 ÷ 0.0700 = 0.0143 mol/L"; "pOH = −log(0.014286) = 1.845"; "pH = 14.00 − 1.845 = 12.155". Cue "seventy millilitres".
  - 5d: replace "and you get one point seven four zero" with "and you get one point eight four five". Ghost line "n(OH⁻) = 0.0500 × 0.0400 = 0.00200 mol"; chip "excess H₃O⁺: pH 1.845"; `PHTrack` markers at 1.845 (crossed) and 12.155.
  - Update the Decisions table row 4, "Changes from the pre-design plan" (mirror 1.845 against 12.155), Precision, the practice model 4, "Numbers to verify" and the YouTube description (no number there, so no change).
- Understanding enabled: the student applies "count the hydroxides" to a solution they have not seen worked, which is the purpose of a practice video.
