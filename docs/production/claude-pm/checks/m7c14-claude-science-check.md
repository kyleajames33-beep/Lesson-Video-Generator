## Facts confirmed (no change)

- **Monomers and repeat units.** n CH₂=CH₂ → [–CH₂–CH₂–]ₙ; n CH₂=CHCl → [–CH₂–CHCl–]ₙ; [–CH₂–CH(C₆H₅)–]ₙ ← CH₂=CHC₆H₅ (styrene, phenylethene, ethenylbenzene, C₈H₈); n CF₂=CF₂ → [–CF₂–CF₂–]ₙ; CH₂=CCl₂ (1,1-dichloroethene, C₂H₂Cl₂) → [–CH₂–CCl₂–]ₙ; [–CH₂–CH(CH₃)–]ₙ ← propene, C₃H₆. Each repeat unit has its monomer's formula. The names poly(chloroethene) and poly(tetrafluoroethene) are right.
- **Bond counts.** With the C=C restored, each backbone carbon has four bonds in styrene, propene and every other monomer used. Adding H gives propane (C₃H₈), which has no C=C and cannot add-polymerise. The site's "replace open bonds with H" is wrong (site error 1).
- **Mechanism wording.** The second (weaker) bond of the C=C breaking and each carbon gaining a new single bond to the next monomer is the accepted HSC description. Nothing else forms (initiator end groups are a negligible fraction and are added, not lost). Each "every second carbon" claim (Cl in PVC, ring in PS) is right for head-to-tail chains.
- **Why a solid, and thermoplastics.** Long chains give a large total of dispersion attraction along their contact. Heating lets chains move and slide without breaking covalent bonds, and cooling restores the forces. "Thermoplastic" is defined correctly. The grey chip on cross-linked thermosets is right.
- **LDPE and HDPE.** LDPE density 0.910 to 0.940 g/cm³ and HDPE 0.941 to 0.97 g/cm³ (ASTM D883), so "about 0.91 to 0.94" and "about 0.94 to 0.97" are right. LDPE melts at about 105 to 115 °C ("about a hundred and ten degrees") and HDPE at about 125 to 137 °C ("nearer a hundred and thirty degrees"). The cause chain (branches, poorer packing, less contact, weaker dispersion, lower density and melting, softer) is the standard HSC answer. Uses (LDPE squeeze bottles and films; HDPE crates, pipes, milk bottles, chopping boards) are right. "Made under different conditions" is right (author note a: about 1000 to 3000 atm and 150 to 300 °C with a radical initiator for LDPE, low pressure with Ziegler–Natta or Phillips catalysts for HDPE). Lightweight singlet shopping bags are mostly HDPE, so tagging the bag only "polyethylene" is right.
- **PVC.** The C–Cl bond is polar (electronegativity 3.16 against 2.55), so dipole–dipole attraction adds to dispersion. Unplasticised PVC is rigid and used for pipes. Plasticisers (often phthalate or other esters) separate the chains, and flexible PVC is used for cable insulation. Correct.
- **Polystyrene.** The bulky phenyl group prevents close packing. Atactic PS is amorphous, so solid PS is clear, rigid and fairly brittle (Tg about 100 °C). Expanded PS is a light insulating foam used for hot-drink cups. Correct.
- **PTFE.** C–F (about 485 kJ/mol average) is among the strongest single bonds to carbon, against C–H about 413 kJ/mol (standard average bond enthalpy tables, for example Housecroft and Constable, or the values on site L21). The F sheath shields the backbone, so very few reagents attack it (molten alkali metals and fluorine do). C–F bonds are strongly polar (3.98 against 2.55), but the symmetric sheath leaves little net dipole and fluorine's low polarisability gives very weak dispersion forces with other substances, so surface energy is very low (water contact angle about 110°) and little sticks. Non-stick pans and plumber's (thread-seal) tape are right. Service to about 260 °C and melting at about 327 °C are right (finding 2 is about the register only). "Very few chemicals" and "almost nothing sticks" are hedged correctly.
- **Attempts.** A1(a) [–CH₂–CCl₂–]ₙ (poly(vinylidene chloride)) and A1(b) propene are right, and the crossed "CH₃–CH₂–CH₃" is the real trap. A2: HDPE chopping boards and LDPE squeeze bottles are real uses. The feedback (straight chains pack, more contact, stronger dispersion, stiff; branches, weaker, softer; no bonds between chains, so heat lets chains slide and the plastic remoulds) is a full-mark answer. Finding 1 fixes the last sentence. Every condition the answers need is on each card.
- **Memory aids.** "Open the double bond, add brackets. Nothing lost." is true for addition polymers. The spaghetti chip correctly treats contact as the variable, because LDPE and HDPE share a repeat unit. "Structure → forces → property → use" is a fair summary.
- **Author notes.** (b) PTFE's helical, highly crystalline chains and atactic amorphous PS are right. (e) Polypropylene melting at about 160 to 170 °C (isotactic) is right. (f) Plunkett, DuPont, April 1938, is right. (g) C–Cl about 328 to 339 kJ/mol across tables is right.
- **Handoff.** "Polymers that do give something off, usually water" is correctly hedged (HCl and methanol are other possibilities).
