## Key notes (suggested wording)

1. FSH (from the pituitary) makes a follicle grow, and a surge of LH makes it release its egg. Progesterone returning in the blood turns FSH and LH down. This is negative feedback: the response opposes the change. In pregnancy the corpus luteum (kept going by hCG) and then the placenta keep progesterone high, so no new follicle grows and no egg is released.
2. Progesterone helps keep the uterus muscle calm. Late in pregnancy the placenta makes more oestrogen, which makes the muscle react more strongly to oxytocin.
3. At birth, the baby's head stretches the cervix, nerve signals reach the brain, and the pituitary releases oxytocin, so the muscle contracts harder and stretches the cervix more. This is positive feedback: the response adds to the change. It ends when the baby is born and the stretch stops.
4. Prolactin makes the breast produce milk, and oxytocin squeezes the milk out. Both are released when the baby suckles. High progesterone and oestrogen from the placenta hold milk production back in pregnancy.
5. Negative feedback opposes a change (here it turns the process down). Positive feedback adds to a change (here it pushes the process further). Neither word means good or bad.

Aid (large): *Oxytocin squeezes (uterus and breast). Prolactin is pro-lactation. Stretch, oxytocin, squeeze, more stretch.* Rule: "Does the result oppose the change or add to it?"
