## Main question and the useful outcome

Main question (as stated): "Shake orange bromine water with two clear liquids: in one tube the colour vanishes, in the other it only seems to. How do you run the bromine water test fairly and safely, and what does the colour change really tell you about a hydrocarbon?"

One main question: yes. The new idea is the practical skill (read both layers against a control); the chemistry is recalled from c04a and c04b. Useful outcome: a student can plan a matched test with a control, describe and explain both results with equations, refuse to call a moved colour a positive result, describe the light demonstration and its evidence, and state what the test can and cannot show.

## Trace from the entry knowledge (student starting Year 12 Module 7, after C04b)

The trace follows the cyclohexane tube from the hook to scene 5, then the cyclohexene tube, then the light demonstration.

1. Hook: "the orange has moved into a layer on top" with a dashed "decolourised?" tag is a real surprise for students who have only seen "orange → colourless". It does reveal the trap in the hook; acceptable, because the heading "Gone, or just moved?" makes it the lesson's question.
2. What the colour means: a short, explicit callback ("You've seen the chemistry in the last two videos"), then the reason the pair is fair. Good.
3. Fair test: six procedural steps in one 513-character scene (the longest). Each has a reason, and card lines dim, so reading load is fine; listening load is heavy (M2). "a third tube with one millilitre of plain water instead, as a control, so you can see what dilution alone does" leaves the student asking, "Why would dilution matter? Water can't make it colourless." The answer arrives only partly in scene 5 (S1).
4. Cyclohexene: the colour goes "within seconds", the model builds the product, "nothing is left to give colour". Good use of the medium (layer lens, engine3d model, stage strip).
5. Cyclohexane: the trap is explained with a cause (non-polar bromine dissolves better in non-polar cyclohexane) and shown in the layer lens. Then "Put it beside the control, and you can see the bromine is still there." A student asks, "How does the control show that? The control is orange all the way down; this tube is orange only on top." The comparison the control makes is not spoken (S1).
6. Light demonstration: a callback ("as in the last video"), plus new evidence (litmus) and the ring equation. Where does "bromine dissolved in cyclohexane" come from? Scene 5 just made it: the orange top layer is bromine dissolved in cyclohexane. Naming that link would join the two scenes and remove neat bromine from the demonstration (S2; science check S2). The litmus evidence also needs a comparison (science check S1).
7. Can and cannot: "Alkynes ... not which" repeats c04b scene 9 nearly word for word; the ring point and "other kinds of compounds" are new (M3). "No colour change means no multiple bond" is absolute (science check M1).
8. Variables and VRA: applied to a yes-or-no test; "accuracy means getting the right answer, which you check with known samples" is clear and correct. Good. The validity line "not from dilution" depends on S1 being fixed.
9. Risk: real classes, controls tied to the set-up, six rows dim one by one, 3 s reading hold, returns in the notes. Acceptable; S2 removes the neat-bromine row.
10. Memory aid: "Gone, not moved" maps onto the two plates with a condition tag. Good, and it supports A1.
11 to 14. Attempts and feedback: see the checklist.
15, 16. Decision rule and notes: clear and useful.

## Findings

No blockers.

### S1. Should fix: the control's job is never spoken as a comparison (scenes 3 and 5)

- Exact text, scene 3: "Set up a third tube with one millilitre of plain **water** instead, as a control, so you can see what dilution alone does." Scene 5: "Put it beside the control, and you can see the bromine is still there. No reaction."
- Student question: "Why would dilution matter? And how does an all-orange control tube show the bromine is still in my cyclohexane tube?"
- Problem: the control is the planning idea this practical adds, but its reasoning is left for the student to supply. Its real use: the control has been diluted by the same volume and stays clearly orange, so dilution cannot explain the pale water layer in the cyclohexane tube; the bromine has gone somewhere, and the orange top layer shows where.
- Replacement, scene 3: "Set up a third tube with one millilitre of plain **water** instead, as a control: it's diluted by the same amount, but nothing can react with it." Replacement, scene 5: "Now look at the control. It's been diluted just as much, and it's still clearly orange. So dilution can't explain the pale water: the bromine has moved into the top layer, and it's still there. No reaction." Visual: on "still clearly orange" the pen bracket links the control to the cyclohexane tube's pale lower layer ("same dilution, much paler"), then to the top layer ("so it moved here").
- Understanding enabled: the student can explain what a control rules out, which is what HSC planning questions ask.

### S2. Should fix: connect scene 6's solution to scene 5's top layer

- Exact text: "In a teacher demonstration, bromine dissolved in cyclohexane stays orange in the dark."
- Student question: "Where does bromine dissolved in cyclohexane come from?"
- Replacement: "In a teacher demonstration, take that orange top layer: it's bromine dissolved in cyclohexane. In the dark, it stays orange." Visual: the scene 5 tube's top layer is drawn off by a coded pipette tilt into a clean tube with the label "bromine in cyclohexane". This also removes neat bromine from the demonstration (science check S2), so the risk table loses its H330 row and the lesson gains a neat payoff: the trap tube becomes the demonstration sample.
- Understanding enabled: the student sees one continuous experiment (the extracted bromine is unreacted, and only light makes it react), which strengthens the "moved, not used up" idea.

### M1. Minor: the litmus needs a comparison strip

- Follow the science check's S1: a second damp blue litmus strip over the tube kept in the dark. Visual only, no extra narration needed beyond "while a strip over the dark tube stays blue" if that observation is confirmed.

### M2. Minor: scene 3 listening load

- Scene 3 carries volumes, the control, stoppering, ten seconds of shaking, the tile and the light. Move "away from sunlight, because bright light can start a slow reaction with an alkane" to the start of scene 5 ("We did this in normal room light, because bright light can start a slow reaction with an alkane.") only if the voiced preview shows the scene feels rushed. Otherwise keep.

### M3. Minor: scene 7 repeats c04b scene 9

- Exact text: "Alkynes decolourise bromine water too, though usually **more slowly**, so the test alone can't tell you which."
- Replacement: "As the last video showed, alkynes decolourise it too, usually **more slowly**, so the test alone can't tell you which." Keep the new ring and "other compounds" points as they are.

### M4. Minor: A1 feedback should say why the top layer is X

- Exact text: "The orange top layer means the bromine **dissolved** in X instead of reacting".
- Replacement: "X floats on the water, so the top layer is X. It's orange, so the bromine **dissolved** in X instead of reacting". About 35 characters; feedback stays near 26 s.

### M5. Minor: A2 transfer is narrow

- A2 is scene 6 with heptane in place of cyclohexane. It is a fair HSC-style item and tests the equation, so keep it; note that g01 carries the wider evaluation. No change required.

### M6. Minor: "formulas" or "formulae"

- Narration says "using structural formulas"; the A2 card says "using structural formulae". Both are Australian usage; make the card match the narration (or the reverse) so the spoken and printed questions are identical.

## Retained strengths

"Gone, or just moved?" as the lesson's question; the layer lens showing bromine drift into the top layer; the cyclohexene model building 1,2-dibromocyclohexane; "nothing is left to give colour"; the ring point (C₆H₁₂ fits CnH2n without a C=C) used again in A1 with new compounds; "a quick test: strong evidence, not proof"; accuracy for a yes-or-no test as "the right answer with known samples"; real hazard classes and halogenated waste; brief feedback with one crossed mistake each; a memory aid that a student can use on a new tube.
