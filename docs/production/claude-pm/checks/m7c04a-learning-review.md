## Main question and the useful outcome

Main question (as stated): "Hydrogen can add across the double bonds in vegetable oil and turn it solid. What else can add across a C=C bond, under what conditions, and what product does each make?"

One main question: yes. Four reagents follow one pattern, and the memory aid and conditions card make that pattern the answer. Useful outcome: a student can explain why an alkene adds and an alkane cannot, write the four alkene additions with structural formulas and conditions on the arrow, name the haloalkane and alcohol products, recognise when an unsymmetrical alkene gives two possible products, and reject a substitution written as an addition.

## Trace from the entry knowledge (c01 to c03, Module 5, Year 11)

Entry: c03 (trigonal planar C=C carbons; tetrahedral single-bonded carbons; "the second bond sits above and below the line between the carbons, and it's weaker than the first"; saturated and unsaturated), c01 (names, locants, di-), c02 ("-ol" means an O–H group; but-1-ene and cyclobutane are both C₄H₈; "later you'll meet a simple test that tells these two apart"), Module 5 (catalysts; pressure and gas equilibria), Year 11 (balancing). The trace follows ethene through scenes 2 to 7, then propene (scene 8 and A1) and C₄H₈ (A2).

1. Hook: oil to margarine is a real change of state with a reason, and the coded segment shows H docking on a C=C before any words name it. The "C=C + ?" board sets the question. Good.
2. Why alkenes add: the saturated alkane against the exposed, weaker second bond, with the bond opener splitting into two stubs, is causal and builds on c03 exactly. Good.
3. Addition: the one ball-and-stick model (flat to tetrahedral as Br₂ adds) is the dotpoint's "construct models" and a beat only video can show. "Picture the reagent splitting in two" uses "reagent" aloud with no meaning; the vocabulary plan puts the meaning on screen only (M1).
4. Hydrogenation: correct, and the crossed ghost "Ni" sliding onto the arrow makes "not used up, so on the arrow" visible. "Hydrogen barely reacts on its own, so we write the conditions on the arrow" joins two causes (why conditions are needed; why they go on the arrow) with one "so" (M2).
5. Halogenation: naming rule in one line, applied, then the bromine water cross-fade in place. The observation is explained by the bromine being used up. "That's the test for a double bond" is stated absolutely (M3).
6. Hydrogen halides: "two different pieces" sets up scene 8; "It needs no number, because both carbons in ethane are alike" is a good reason with the ghost "1-". Good.
7. Hydration: the conditions build one chip at a time, and the 2 → 1 gas-molecule pulse turns "high pressure" into a reason (Module 5). Good. Student question: "Module 5 said heating an exothermic equilibrium lowers the yield. Why 300 degrees?" Not answered (M4, optional). The arrow ends with four chips plus a tag and a card row (M5).
8. Two possible products: the two-way docker with a ghost copy, then but-2-ene's "=" stamp, is the best use of motion in the video. Student question: "So which one do I write in the exam?" The video says both are possible but never says what to write (S1).
9. Check the equation: atom count and a crossed substitution line. Good, and it prepares A1's crossed mistake.
10. Memory aid: introduced after all four reagents; one pattern plus conditions. Good.
11, 12. A1 and feedback 1: the question gives the reagent and "room temperature"; feedback docks one Cl on each carbon, names it, and crosses the substitution. About 24 s. Good. See science M3 for the crossed equation's caption.
13, 14. A2 and feedback 2: good evidence chain (C=C, so not a ring; symmetric, so one alcohol). The premise "only one alcohol is formed" is chemically satisfied by but-1-ene as well (acid-catalysed hydration gives almost only butan-2-ol), so the crossed answer is defensible for a student who has done the site's Markovnikov enrichment (S2; science B1). 2-methylpropene was not drawn in c02; the feedback introduces it, which is fine because the answer does not depend on it.
15, 16. Handoff and key notes: the handoff asks the two questions c04b answers. Notes are useful; note 3's "not HCl" has no reason (M6).

## Findings

### S1. Should fix: scene 8 leaves students unsure what to write when two products are possible

- Exact text: "So there are two possible products: one-bromopropane and two-bromopropane."
- Student question: "Which one do I put in my answer?"
- Replacement: "So there are two possible products: one-bromopropane and two-bromopropane. In an answer, show both." (+25 characters.) Visual: on "show both" the bracket "two possible products" pulses once.
- Key note 4: add "(write both)" after "two possible products".
- Understanding enabled: students write an answer that is correct without Markovnikov's rule, which the design rightly leaves out.

### S2. Should fix (with science B1): attempt 2's premise does not rule out but-1-ene in real chemistry

- Exact text (card): "When X reacts with steam in the presence of a phosphoric acid catalyst at high temperature and pressure, only one alcohol is formed." Narration: "only one alcohol forms." Feedback crosses "X = but-1-ene".
- Student question (from a student who did the site's Markovnikov enrichment): "But-1-ene gives butan-2-ol and almost no butan-1-ol, so only one alcohol forms. Why is that wrong?"
- Replacement (card): "... only one alcohol product is possible." Narration: "... only one alcohol product is possible." (+14 characters.) Keep feedback 2 as written: its "two possible alcohols" now answers the question's own word.
- Understanding enabled: the attempt tests the video's idea (identical or different ends give one or two possible products) and cannot be answered "correctly" in two ways.

### M1. Minor: "reagent" is spoken before it is explained

- Exact text (scene 3): "Picture the reagent splitting in two,"
- Replacement: "Picture the reagent, the molecule being added, splitting in two," (+27 characters.)

### M2. Minor: scene 4 runs two causes together

- Exact text: "Hydrogen barely reacts on its own, so we write the conditions on the arrow: a **nickel** catalyst, at about one hundred and fifty degrees."
- Replacement: "Hydrogen barely reacts on its own, so it needs a **nickel** catalyst and heat, about one hundred and fifty degrees, and we write those conditions on the arrow." (+21 characters; the next sentence then gives why the catalyst goes on the arrow.)

### M3. Minor: "the test for a double bond" is absolute

- Exact text (scene 5): "That's the test for a double bond, which you'll carry out in the practical video."
- Replacement: "That makes it a quick test for a double bond, which you'll carry out in the practical video." (+11 characters.) Alkynes also decolourise bromine water; c04b and p02 own the limits.

### M4. Minor (optional): no reason for the high temperature

- Student question as in the trace. Optional replacement in scene 7: "about three hundred degrees for a fast enough rate, and **high pressure**." (+26 characters.) If not narrated, add to author note f: "Hydration is exothermic; 300 °C is a rate and yield compromise (Module 5)."

### M5. Minor: scenes 4 and 7 end over the clutter limit

- Scene 4: dim the "no C=C left: saturated" tag and the hydrogenation chip before the conditions card row fills. Scene 7: during "Why the pressure?" dim the "steam", "H₃PO₄ catalyst" and "about 300 °C" chips and keep "high pressure" bright; fill the card row after the "2 → 1" tag dims.

### M6. Minor: key note 3's "not HCl" has no reason

- Replacement: "(steam, H₃PO₄ catalyst, about 300 °C, high pressure; the catalyst is an acid, not HCl, which would add to the alkene itself)".

### M7. Minor: feedback 2's mid-scene is crowded

- Remove each unsymmetrical alkene's two small alcohols once its "two" beat ends, keeping only a small "2" badge beside it, so the but-2-ene "=" stamp lands on a clear board.

### M8. Minor: YouTube title over 100 characters

- Current: 110 characters. Replacement (93): "Alkene Addition Reactions: Hydrogen, Halogens, Hydrogen Halides and Water | Year 12 Chemistry".

### M9. Minor: typo in scene 9's visual

- "returns in condensed form, On "one"" should read "returns in condensed form. On "one"".

## Retained strengths

The margarine hook tied to a real change of state; the bond opener and reagent splitter that make "one piece on each carbon" visible before it is named; the single ball-and-stick model turning flat to tetrahedral; the crossed Ni ghost; the bromine water cross-fade in place; the 2 → 1 gas pulse that gives pressure a reason; the two-way docker with the "=" stamp; the atom count; a site error rebuilt as a fair final attempt; Markovnikov's rule kept out while both possible products are named.
