## The blocker

**v10 scene 4, "Carbohydrate is faster, and it's the only fuel the anaerobic systems can use."** One video earlier, v09 taught that the ATP-PCr system's fuel is phosphocreatine, and that cell is on screen in v10's own table. A student who takes the sentence literally will write that ATP-PCr runs on carbohydrate and lose the syllabus's fuel-source criterion. The site's line is narrower ("the only fuel usable anaerobically", in a table comparing fat with carbohydrate). Fix: "it's the only food fuel that can be used without oxygen." v11's key note 1 ("the only anaerobic fuel"), which students copy, needs the same wording (should fix in v11).

## Module-level findings

1. **"Unseen" practice items whose answers the video has just said (should fix, eleven designs).** The same pattern as Module 1, more often. An attempt is answered by a sentence in the body in v01 (11a, scene 9's "everything reverses"), v02 (11a's landing, named in scene 6), v03 (12a's sprinter, scene 5), v04 (11a, the tracked oxygen journey), v05 (12c, scene 9's heat and dehydration), v06 (11c, scene 7's glucose lanes), v07 (11a, scene 7 and the memory card), v08 (11a, scene 3's "sloppy passes"), v11 (10c, scene 7's B-vitamin clause), v18 (9c, scene 6) and v22 (8a, scenes 3 and 5). Several further items are the site's WE b1 verbatim with the answer taught in the body (v04 11c, v05 12a, v06 11a); keeping the site's question is reasonable, but they should not be labelled unseen. Each review gives a replacement case that tests the same idea in a new context, or a one-clause cut in the body that restores the transfer. Designs v09 (second half), v10, v12 to v16, v19 and v21 already set genuinely unseen items and are the model.

2. **One mechanism taught four times: heat, sweat and plasma volume (should fix in v05 and v14).** Sweat lowers plasma volume, stroke volume falls, heart rate rises: v05 scene 9 and 12c, v08 scene 8, v11 scene 8, and v14 9c (which the design calls unworked). Recall it in one line after v05 and use v05 12c and v14 9c for cases the module never practises (a blood donor on link two; response against adaptation).

3. **Double teaching between v06, v10 and v11 (should fix in v11).** Fat at about 37 kJ against 17, glycogen as the small store, protein for repair, and the micronutrient list (iron, calcium with vitamin D, B vitamins, sodium and potassium in sweat) are stated as new in v06, again in v10 (fuels) and a third time in v11. v11 should bring v06's ledger in already filled, say "you met these in digestion", and narrate only what is new (which system each fuel feeds, how each micronutrient limits it, per-kilogram needs).

4. **Contradictions between videos (should fix).**
   - **Breathing's trigger:** v04 scene 9 says carbon dioxide is "the main trigger"; v14 scene 3 says the first rise comes from the brain within seconds, with carbon dioxide later. Align v04 and add a bridge in v14.
   - **Two thresholds:** v12 9b says "the anaerobic threshold of about eighty-five"; v14 teaches a lactate threshold at about 50 to 60 per cent (untrained) and 80 or more (trained). Drop "anaerobic threshold" from v12.
   - **"Per cent of maximum" changes meaning without notice:** v10 (85 per cent, effort), v11 (65 per cent crossover, effort), v12 and v13 (85 per cent and 70 to 85 per cent, maximum heart rate), v14 (stroke volume plateau and lactate threshold, effort). Say which maximum every time ("of maximum heart rate" or "of maximum effort"); one word each.
   - **Continuous skills:** v17 says a swimming stroke is "never" taught in parts and v18 says "A continuous skill stays whole"; the site says "largely", and swimmers use part drills. Soften both to "mostly whole, with part drills rejoined quickly".
   - **v13's memory card against its own progression:** "Raise one dial, and name the one you lowered" against week-by-week rises with nothing lowered. Scope the card to a single session.

5. **Terms used in feedback before they are taught (should fix).** v13 9d marks "specificity", which v12 deliberately left unnamed; add the word in v13 scene 6. v12 9b's "anaerobic threshold" (finding 4). v02's "isotonic" is never glossed (note).

6. **Small science-to-learning slips that a beginner will copy (should fix).** v02: hamstrings called "the antagonist" one scene after they were taught to extend the hip; "the resistance wins" against "eccentric produces the most force". v05: "link two through more blood volume" contradicts the chain's own per-litre model. v07: "gap to gap" for myelin beside the synapse's gap. v19: "it's surprising" offered as a sign of a real finding (the site pairs it with suspicion of convenient claims). v17: the gross-skill consequence in 8b does not follow from scene 2. v20: 9b crosses a mistake the item cannot test. Each review gives the wording.

7. **Recurring cases.** The vertical or basketball jump appears in v01 11a, v02 11a and v08 scene 4; netball carries v08, v10, v13, v17, v19 and v21. Netball as the module's running sport is a deliberate and useful anchor; the jump should not return in later attempts (note).

## Does the sequence build?

Yes. The order runs from structure to whole movement (skeleton, muscles, biomechanics, heart and lungs, efficiency, fuel and hormones, nerves, all seven systems with first aid: v01 to v08), then energy, fuel, training and responses (v09 to v15), then learning skills (v16 to v19), then psychology and communities (v20 to v22), and closes Year 11 in one sentence before Year 12's national health data. All twenty-two joins match: each opening reproduces the previous handoff frame and each heading takes the previous Next chip's words (checked by script), including m1-v18 to m2-v01 and m2-v22 to y12-m3-v01. Recurring models are introduced once and grow: the segment linkage (v01) carries v02, v03 and v08; the delivery chain (v05) returns in v10 and v11; the cardiac output strip (v05) is worked in v14; the six-criteria table (v09) is completed in v10; the stage track (v16) returns in v18 and v20; the research cycle and quality trio from m1-v07 are recalled, not re-taught, in v15 and v19. Apart from findings 2 and 3, earlier ideas are recalled in a clause.

**Against the focus area.** Every owned dotpoint and sub-point is taught on screen or in narration, and each design records what it left to the site page under root's condition. Two deliberate pairings (v08: systems together with first aid; v15: investigation with the testing debate) stay inside the cap. Gaps worth noting rather than fixing: the "demonstrate" half of d6 (v08) and the research task of v19 are left for students to do at school, which is appropriate; d7's "different groups" in v15 is argued for four groups in one dense scene.

## Checks across all twenty-two

- **Causes before reliance:** met; every scene names what acts and why the change follows, with the slips in findings 4 to 6.
- **Attention and the visual medium:** every video has a model that moves with the explanation (linkage with live angles and muscle-band lengths, lever beam, stability triangle, tracked oxygen dot, gradient pair, delivery chain with a cost meter, glucose band, command loop, limit gauges, energy table and share curves, fuel pipes, work-to-rest dial, FITT dials, trace stack, research cycle, stage track, continua, retention curves, evidence ladder, motivation grid, if-then card, supply board). No static-slideshow risk in the written plans; motion still needs judging at playback speed.
- **Response opportunity:** all 44 cards keep the full demand on screen through the 10 s countdown and after, all end "Pause here to answer.", and no picture slot shows an answer.
- **Feedback shape:** every feedback gives the answer, one or two reasons and exactly one crossed classic mistake, and none re-teaches the lesson. Lengths by the authors' counts run 20 to 32 s; at or over 30 s: v01 11d (32), v02 11d (31), v08 11d (31), v20 9d (30). Trims are in each review. v05 12b models only four of five marks (should fix).
- **Reading load:** heavy by syllabus design in v01, v02, v04, v06, v07, v18 (vocabulary dotpoints); each term is glossed on its part and the key notes select five lines. The densest narrated scenes are v06 scene 5 (about sixteen gland and hormone names; should fix), v07 scene 2, v14 scene 3, v15 scene 7 and v18 scene 5 (notes).
- **Memory aids:** one framed card per video from the site's own lines, placed after the explanation; all are scientifically sound for the cases taught, with v13's scope problem and v19's "surprising" heuristic as the exceptions.
- **People, culture, sensitive topics:** nobody is drawn; cases use objects, places, coded linkages and quote cards. Low energy availability, identity loss after injury and body image are handled generally, with help-seeking and no weight or diet advice.
- **Style and length:** 0 em dashes in all twenty-two files (searched by script). Authors' totals with holds run 527 to 569 s; v01 (568), v03 (569), v06 (568) and v09 (569) have no headroom, so any added speech there needs a matching cut.
