## Learner trace

Main question: why does water restore a wilted leaf while a salty surface removes water from cells? Entry is v09's bidirectional osmosis, v10's measured bag change and v03's wall/vacuole comparison. A student should first compare effective outside/inside solute concentrations, predict net water movement, then use the cell's structure to predict the outcome.

The three prefixes are defined alongside a reference cell before they are used. Each large cell case is then placed in a six-case grid, making the structural contrast available without switching to unrelated examples. Distilled-water red cells establish swelling and possible lysis; the isotonic case retains water movement rather than freezing it. The plant cases add the wall and vacuole, show pressure limiting further uptake, and distinguish flaccidity from the more extreme membrane withdrawal of plasmolysis. The transparent gap wash answers what occupies the space outside the shrunken protoplast.

The eleven terms are supported by plain meanings and visible changes. The grid is useful because it collects already explained outcomes rather than asking students to read six unseen conclusions. The healthy-slug decision is retained: only the coded skin-cell lens shrinks, and no animal-distress action is required. The accepted hook does not need a new example or an extra ethical preface.

## Findings

1. **Should fix:** Scene 8 begins with a turgid cell, cross-fades it to flaccid and removes the pressure arrows while the counters show equal water movement throughout. The cell cannot lose volume while its net water movement is zero. For the simplest repair, start on the already-equilibrated flaccid crop and show equal counters; the previous hypotonic case remains in the grid for comparison. If retaining the turgid-to-flaccid transition, show transient net water loss first and equal counters only at the final state. This is a visual substitution with no extra narration or scene.

2. **Should fix, shared with science:** Scenes 6 and 10 and the description say the wall changes only the result, never the direction, but scene 7 correctly says wall-supported pressure stops further net water entry even with a hypotonic exterior. A learner can infer that the concentration arrow continues indefinitely despite the pressure explanation. Scope the three-step rule to the initial osmotic tendency, then keep the pressure-limited final state explicit: water initially enters the hypotonic plant cell; internal pressure rises until net entry stops. Replace the categorical wall/arrow claims and scope the grid captions rather than adding a water-potential topic. Define turgor as pressure of the cell contents against the wall, with the wall resisting; do not label turgor itself merely as the wall pushing back. This clarifies the existing pressure beat and replaces wording/labels within it.

## Practice, continuity and scope

The learner must infer the solution names and explain the hypertonic result; a diagram of an observed crenated cell is not an answer leak. The second card gives the species, strong salt treatment, withdrawal of the membrane and continued viability before transfer to distilled water. Those conditions support reversal rather than requiring the learner to assume damaged cells recover. Structure labels name the wall and membrane without naming the tested condition. The memory aid is introduced after the six cases. It must remain a rule for net water movement and the non-penetrating-solute comparison, with pressure limiting the final state; it is not a rule that every water molecule travels one way or every solute is excluded.
