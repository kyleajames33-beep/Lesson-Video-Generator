## Verification and recalculation

The ion equations Mg2+ + 2OH- -> Mg(OH)2 and Ba2+ + SO4^2- -> BaSO4 balance atoms and charge. White Mg(OH)2, pale-blue Cu(OH)2, Fe(OH)2 green then browning, calcium brick-red and copper blue-green agree with RSC school tests. Flame tests identify solution ions through species produced in a hot flame; do not depict unchanged aqueous ions emitting their solution colour. Neutral atoms are the relevant atomic-emission link to AAS; ionic and molecular emitters can also contribute to actual flame colours. The ACS article's search abstract supports the species distinction; the full page returned 403, so it is not treated as a fully inspected spectrum reference.

Arithmetic rechecked: equal drops of 0.1 mol/L MgSO4, 2 mol/L HNO3 and 0.1 mol/L AgNO3 give total sulfate and silver about 0.0333 mol/L, acid about 0.667 mol/L. Using the stated Ka2 approximation gives free sulfate about 4.9e-4 mol/L and Q about 5.4e-7, below the quoted Ag2SO4 Ksp 1.2e-5. Without acid Q=1.25e-4. This supports the acidified clear silver result at the stated concentrations. For equal volumes of 0.1 mol/L Ca2+ and 1 mol/L H2SO4, the stated sulfate estimate gives Q about 4.5e-4, above 4.93e-5. With 1 mol/L NaOH, Q=0.05(0.5)^2=0.0125, above 5.02e-6. Those approximate equilibrium calculations do not guarantee a visible precipitate at a specified time; a teacher trial remains necessary. The tap-water example 0.05 x 5e-4=2.5e-5 is far above AgCl Ksp. A2's CaCl2 identity, chloride dissolution in dilute ammonia and Ag+ + Cl- -> AgCl are consistent with the givens.

Risk review: separate silver and heavy-metal waste, splash protection, fumes and a hot loop are appropriate controls. Hazard classifications for a solid are not automatically those of a dilute solution. The draft clearly distinguishes them in places, but exact product/concentration SDS and local school assessment remain unverified. Public NSW safety guidance does not establish a special permission to use student lead. No claim that every stated H code has been independently confirmed is made.

### 2. should fix: 9

Text: "Each test should detect only the ion it's meant to."

AgNO3 detects several anions; NaOH precipitates several cations. Specificity belongs to the controlled sequence and interpretation, not each reagent alone.

Repair: Use the length-neutral validity repair in the learning review.

Repair: Screen: "low-ion rinse water". No added narration.

Text: "rules out calcium"

No visible emission is a detection result and does not alone prove absence. The later caveat does not repair the absolute inference at the deciding step.

Repair: Show the calcium positive control and use the qualified result in learning finding 3.
