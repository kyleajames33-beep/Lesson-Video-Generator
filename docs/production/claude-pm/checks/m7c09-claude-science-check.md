## Facts confirmed (no change)

- **Boiling points (CRC values).** Ethanoic acid 117.9 °C; propan-1-ol 97.2 °C; methanoic acid 100.8 °C; propanoic acid 141.2 °C; butanoic acid 163.8 °C (163.5 to 163.8 in tables); 3-methylbutanoic acid 176.5 °C; hexan-1-ol 157.6 °C. Spoken roundings ("one hundred and eighteen", "ninety seven", "about one hundred", "one hundred and forty one", "about one hundred and sixty four", "one hundred and seventy seven", "one hundred and fifty seven") and the 21 °C gap (20.7) are right.
- **Molar masses (IUPAC atomic weights, by script).** C₂H₄O₂ 60.05; C₃H₈O 60.10; C₅H₁₀O₂ 102.13; C₆H₁₄O 102.18 (design 102.17, rounding only). Electron counts 32, 34, 56, 58 are right; in both pairs the acid has slightly fewer electrons, so dispersion cannot explain the acid's higher boiling point.
- **Carboxyl group (scene 2).** C=O and O–H on one carbon; two non-equivalent oxygens; one O–H; both C=O and O–H polar covalent; the C–C(=O)–O region planar. Correct.
- **Naming (scene 3, attempt 1).** "-oic acid" from the alkane stem; the carboxyl carbon is C1 and counted; no locant for a monocarboxylic acid ("always" is correct within the course, which has no dicarboxylic acids); (CH₃)₂CHCOOH is 2-methylpropanoic acid; CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₂COOH is 3-methylbutanoic acid (C1 COOH, C2 CH₂, C3 CH, C4 CH₃), C₅H₁₀O₂ (H 3 + 1 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 10). The crossed name "2-methylpropanoic acid" is exactly what a student gets by dropping the carboxyl carbon and numbering the rest, so it is a fair distractor, and it does name a different, real compound.
- **Dimers (scene 4).** Each molecule donates one and accepts one hydrogen bond, two per pair, closing an eight-membered ring (C, O, H, O, C, O, H, O). Liquid and vapour ethanoic acid contain substantial cyclic dimer. The design's wording (pairs held by two hydrogen bonds, plus dipole–dipole from C=O, so stronger attractions overall) is the standard HSC answer and avoids the site's contradiction. Correct.
- **Solubility (scene 5).** Methanoic to butanoic acid miscible with water at 25 °C (PubChem: butyric acid "miscible"); pentanoic acid about 2.4 g per 100 mL; hexanoic acid about 1.0 g per 100 mL. "Up to butanoic" and "solubility falls" are right; "barely" for hexanoic is fair.
- **Ionisation (scene 6).** CH₃COOH + H₂O ⇌ CH₃COO⁻ + H₃O⁺ balances (C 2/2, H 6/6, O 3/3, charge 0/0). Ka 1.8 × 10⁻⁵; at 0.10 mol/L [H₃O⁺] = 1.34 × 10⁻³ mol/L, pH 2.87 ("about two point nine"), 1.3% ionised; "about one in eighty" matches chem-m6-c05's rounding (1 in 79 from pH 2.9). "Weak means partly ionised, not dilute" is correct.
- **Conjugate base argument (scene 7).** Carboxylate C–O bonds equal (about 1.26 Å, between C=O about 1.21 Å and C–O about 1.34 Å); alkoxide charge on one O. Ethanol pKa about 15.9, propan-1-ol about 16.1 ("about sixteen"); gap about 11 units, a factor of about 10¹¹ in Ka; "each unit is a factor of ten" is right for Ka.
- **Litmus (scene 8).** A 0.1 mol/L ethanoic acid solution (pH 2.9) turns blue litmus red; an alcohol solution leaves it blue. Correct (finding 5 adjusts the cause wording).
- **Reactions (scenes 9 and 10, attempt 2), every equation balanced by atom count.** CH₃COOH + NaOH → CH₃COONa + H₂O (Na 1/1, C 2/2, H 5/5, O 3/3). 2CH₃COOH + Mg → (CH₃COO)₂Mg + H₂ (C 4/4, H 8/8, O 4/4, Mg 1/1; Mg(CH₃COO)₂ is the more common print form, both acceptable). CH₃COOH + NaHCO₃ → CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂ (Na 1/1, C 3/3, H 5/5, O 5/5). CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH + NaHCO₃ → CH₃CH₂CH₂COONa + H₂O + CO₂ (C 5/5, H 9/9, O 5/5, Na 1/1). Salt names "-oate": sodium ethanoate, magnesium ethanoate, sodium butanoate. Correct.
- **Hydrogen carbonate test logic (scene 10).** Ethanoic acid pKa 4.76 against carbonic acid apparent pKa₁ 6.35 ("6.4"), so the proton transfer to HCO₃⁻ is favoured; H₂CO₃ decomposes to H₂O + CO₂. Alcohols (pKa about 16) do not react. "In this course, if an organic compound fizzes with sodium hydrogen carbonate, it's a carboxylic acid" is correctly hedged (finding on site error 8 below).
- **Memory aids.** "Similar size: carboxylic acid > alcohol > aldehyde, ketone > alkane" holds for the matched-size comparisons taught (for example, propanoic acid 141 °C, butan-1-ol 118 °C, butanal 75 °C, pentane 36 °C, all about 72 to 74 g/mol). "Spread the charge, and the proton leaves" is a fair summary of the carboxylate against alkoxide comparison; with finding 3 the lesson already says the acid ionises only partly, so "leaves" does not imply full ionisation. Keep, subject to the learning review.
- **Absolute words.** "always carbon one" (correct in scope); "only the other carries a hydrogen" (correct); "Only about one molecule in eighty" (correct, hedged); "nothing happens" (correct observation for an alcohol with NaHCO₃); "snatch the proton straight back" (figurative, acceptable); "In this course ... it's a carboxylic acid" (hedged). Findings 9 covers "not acidic".
- **Author notes.** (c) solubility, (d) acid data, (e) bond lengths, (f) left-out content (sodium metal gives H₂ with both acids and alcohols; sodium carbonate equation 2RCOOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2RCOONa + H₂O + CO₂ balances), (g) hazards, (h) salt naming and (i) rates are correct.
