## Facts confirmed (no change)

- **Selective toxicity (scene 2).** "Harming the pathogen much more than the host" is the standard definition. Correct.
- **Penicillin (scene 3).** Beta-lactams bind penicillin-binding proteins (transpeptidases) and block peptidoglycan cross-linking; in growing bacteria, autolysins continue to open the wall while new cross-links are not made, and the weakened wall fails under osmotic pressure, so the cell lyses. "Penicillin stops a bacterium building its cell wall", "a growing bacterium can't strengthen its wall, water rushes in, and the cell bursts" and "Your cells have no wall" are correct simplifications. The wall as "a tough mesh that stops the cell swelling and bursting" is correct.
- **Ribosome-targeting antibiotics (scene 3).** Tetracyclines bind the 30S subunit of the bacterial 70S ribosome; human cytoplasmic ribosomes are 80S. "Shaped differently from ours, so the bacterium can't make proteins" is correct. The generic `m5x-ribosome-v1.png` can stand for a bacterial ribosome when tagged "bacterial ribosome (70S)" (Doubt in Assets): a two-subunit outline is correct for both. Mitochondrial ribosome side effects are correctly kept out.
- **Bactericidal and bacteriostatic (scene 3).** Penicillins are bactericidal; tetracyclines are mainly bacteriostatic, and host defences clear the rest. Correct.
- **Why antibiotics don't affect viruses (scene 4).** A virus is nucleic acid in a protein coat with no wall and no ribosomes, and is made by host machinery. Correct. Antibiotics act on the body's normal bacteria and select resistance (finding 4 is a wording point).
- **Oseltamivir (hook, scene 5, scene 6).** Neuraminidase cleaves sialic acid so new virions are released; oseltamivir carboxylate inhibits it, so virions stay bound to the cell surface and to each other. Correct, including the aligned plate's clumped particles. Effect size: Cochrane (Jefferson et al., 2014, CD008965) 16.8 hours shorter time to first alleviation of symptoms in adults (95% CI 8.4 to 25.1); Dobson et al. (*Lancet* 385:1729, 2015) about 25 hours in confirmed influenza. "Can shorten the illness by about a day" is a fair, hedged reading (for Codex to open). Benefit is shown in trials started within 36 to 48 hours of onset; guidelines advise starting within 48 hours. Why early: the drug stops new particles leaving cells; it does not destroy particles or infected cells already present, and viral replication peaks in the first one to two days. Correct.
- **Other antivirals (scene 5).** Aciclovir (activated by herpesvirus thymidine kinase; blocks the viral DNA polymerase), remdesivir (SARS-CoV-2 RNA polymerase), enfuvirtide (HIV fusion) and maraviroc (blocks the host CCR5 co-receptor). The grey tag "HIV entry blockers" covers both entry drugs; the narration "stop it getting into a cell" is correct.
- **"Far fewer antivirals than antibiotics" (scene 6).** De Clercq and Li (2016): 90 approved antiviral drugs in 13 groups for 9 diseases; antibiotics number in the hundreds. Correct as a rough comparison (for Codex to open).
- **Resistance and plasmid conjugation (scene 7).** Resistance is selected from pre-existing or newly arising variants; resistance genes on plasmids pass by conjugation through a pilus and joining point, including between species (for example *bla* and *mcr-1* plasmids among Enterobacterales). "The few that already carry resistance" is fairly hedged; "never the person" is correct. The conjugation plate brief (single-stranded copy crossing at the joining point) is correct.
- **HIV (scene 8, attempt 2).** About 10¹⁰ virions a day in untreated infection (Perelson et al., *Science* 271:1582, 1996); reverse transcriptase error rate about 3 × 10⁻⁵ per base per cycle; AZT monotherapy from 1987 led to resistance; combination therapy from 1996 (finding 1 corrects the steps); treated HIV is controlled, not cured, and life expectancy with early treatment approaches normal. "Billions of times a day" and "under control for decades" are correct.
- **Using drugs well (scene 9).** "Take them exactly as prescribed, and never share leftovers" matches current Australian advice (Australian Commission on Safety and Quality in Health Care; Therapeutic Guidelines), and avoids the contested "always finish the course" claim (Llewelyn et al., *BMJ* 358:j3418, 2017). Routine use in healthy food animals selects resistance; reserve ("last-line") antibiotics are standard stewardship. Correct (for Codex to open).
- **GRAM resistance figure (scene 10, key note 5) rechecked.** The GRAM study (Antimicrobial Resistance Collaborators, *Lancet*, published 16 September 2024) estimates 1.14 million deaths attributable to bacterial antimicrobial resistance in 2021 and 4.71 million associated, with a forecast of 1.91 million attributable deaths in 2050 and about 39 million cumulative attributable deaths from 2025 to 2050. The 2021 figure is slightly below 2019's (about 1.27 million in Murray et al. 2022; the GRAM series gives about 1.2 million), which the authors attribute to the COVID-19 period. "In 2021, resistant bacteria directly caused about one point one million deaths worldwide" and the chip "2021: about 1.14 million deaths directly caused by resistant bacteria" are correct and dated; "directly caused" is the study's "attributable" (deaths that would not have occurred had the infection been drug-susceptible). I found no newer global estimate in this search; Codex to confirm before voicing. (searched) University of Oxford news, 17 Sep 2024 https://www.ox.ac.uk/news/2024-09-17-antibiotic-resistance-has-claimed-least-one-million-lives-each-year-1990 ; Wellcome press release https://wellcome.org/insights/articles/new-forecasts-reveal-39-million-deaths-will-be-directly-attributable-bacterial-antimicrobial ; IHME https://healthdata.org/node/12106
- **Attempt 1.** The model answer rewards selective toxicity, the missing wall target and timing; correct. The crossed mistake "the virus is resistant to penicillin" is genuinely wrong (there is no target to resist).
- **Attempt 2 arithmetic.** 10¹⁰ ÷ 10⁵ = 10⁵ resistant particles a day; (10⁻⁵)³ = 10⁻¹⁵ per particle; 10¹⁰ × 10⁻¹⁵ = 10⁻⁵ a day, one every 10⁵ days (about 274 years). "About one in a million billion", "fewer than one a century" and "about 270 years" are correct. The author note on simplifications is correct and properly kept out of learner copy.
- **Memory aids.** "Antivirals target virus-only steps" is correct with the stated assumption (steps or enzymes the host lacks or makes in a clearly different form). "Not a cell means antibiotics are useless" is correct as recalled. The widened selection aid is correct with finding 5's author note.
- **Absolute words.** "do nothing to the flu virus" (true of the virus); "miss the virus completely" (true); "has nothing in the virus to hit" (true); "Each one targets a step that only the virus uses" (true for the step; maraviroc targets a host receptor, but entry by HIV is the virus's step); "only one virus, or a few related ones" (hedged; acceptable); "never the person" (true); "must be taken early" (true for the shown benefit); "never share leftovers" (advice, correct). Findings 2 to 4 cover the rest.
