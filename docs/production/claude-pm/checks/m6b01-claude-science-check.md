## Facts confirmed (no change)

- **Fleming's 1945 Nobel lecture (11 December 1945).** He said that "the ignorant man may easily underdose himself and by exposing his microbes to non-lethal quantities of the drug make them resistant." The paraphrase in scene 1 is fair. "The scientist who discovered penicillin" is correct: he found its antibacterial action in 1928 and shared the 1945 prize with Chain and Florey. Source: https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/1945/fleming/questions-and-answers/
- **The Lederbergs' 1952 replica plating.** Joshua and Esther Lederberg, J Bacteriol 63(3):399-406, March 1952. They used a velveteen pad, streptomycin, and *E. coli* K-12. The work showed that resistant mutants were present before exposure. Correct. Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14927572 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Replica_plating
- **Penicillin and the staph resistance account.** Resistance became common in hospitals within a few years (59% by 1948 in London). The mechanism is penicillinase, often on plasmids. The scene 9 plasmid tag is correct; learning review finding 4 moves it to scene 8. The scenes 6 to 8 single-allele model is acceptable as a teaching model for the penicillin course itself, but not as the attempt scenario (finding 1).
- **Bacteria pass a mutation to both daughter cells.** This is correct as spoken. Scene 4 sets the mutation as already present in one cell, both strands of its chromosome are copied, and each daughter gets a copy. A change that first arises during replication sits on one daughter strand and becomes fixed in one lineage after the next division. The design keeps this as an author note, which is the right call.
- **Animal germ line.** Only mutations in cells that make eggs or sperm are inherited. "In animals" plus the tag "plants differ" is correct.
- **Gene flow can add an allele that is new to a local population.** Immigration can bring in alleles the population did not have, while mutation remains the original source. Correct (site corrections 2 and 3 are right). Source: https://bio.libretexts.org/Workbench/Modern_Genetics/11:_Population_genetics/11.03:_Mutation_and_Migration
- **Random with respect to need.** Luria and Delbrück (1943) and the Lederbergs (1952) support it. The author note about stress-induced (SOS) mutagenesis is correctly kept out of narration.
- **Many mutations have no visible effect** (non-coding sites, synonymous changes). Correct. "Many" rather than "most" is spoken.
- **Attempt 1 arithmetic.** 10^10 x 10^-8 = 100. Correct. Large abscess infections can reach about 10^9 to 10^10 cells. The scenario is plausible, and with the rifampicin change the frequency is realistic too (order 10^-7 to 10^-8).
- **"Over many generations, which for bacteria can take only days."** Correct (doubling time about 30 min in vitro, slower in a host).
- **Site corrections 1 to 4** in the design are scientifically right.
- **Em dashes:** none in the design.
