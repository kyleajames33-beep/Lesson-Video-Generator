## Key notes (suggested wording)

1. Artificial insemination: semen from a chosen male is collected, divided into many doses, usually frozen, and placed in a female's reproductive tract when she can conceive. It spreads one male's alleles widely, at a lower cost than keeping or moving the male. A hormone implant can time the cycle so a group of cows come on heat together.
2. Artificial pollination: the breeder removes the anthers of the seed parent, puts pollen from the chosen parent on its stigma and covers the flowers. Over several generations she keeps the plants with the wanted traits.
3. Copying the winner (cloning, for example potato tubers or banana cuttings) gives identical plants quickly. They share the same weaknesses, as the Cavendish banana shows.
4. Using a few parents on many farms narrows the gene pool, the variety of alleles in a population, so one disease or harmful allele can spread. Frozen semen from other bulls, heritage breeds and outcrossing (breeding with unrelated individuals) help protect variety.
5. Evaluate means make a judgement using criteria, the things you judge it by. Use gain (with evidence, such as comparing with similar farms that do not use it), variety, cost and welfare (for animals). Say which way you lean, give reasons from each criterion, weigh them, and say when the answer would change.

Aid (large): *Pick few parents: gain speed, risk variety.* Tag: "few parents, used widely. Speed means faster improvement than leaving mating to chance." Checklist: gain, variety, cost, welfare.
