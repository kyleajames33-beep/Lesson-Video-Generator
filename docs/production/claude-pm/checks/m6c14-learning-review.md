## Main question and the useful outcome

Main question (as stated): "Hydrochloric acid and ethanoic acid at the same concentration, titrated with the same base, give two very different curves. What can you read from a titration curve (strong or weak, the equivalence point, how much acid, even its Ka), and what does a conductivity graph show?"

One main question: yes in substance (what does a reaction profile tell you about an acid?), but the wording joins two questions with "and what does a conductivity graph show?" (M8). The route and the dotpoint put both profiles here; scenes 8 and 9 answer the same question with another measurement, so no split is needed. Useful outcome: a student can read strength from the start and the buffer region, find the equivalence point in the jump and use its volume, read pKa at half the equivalence volume and convert it to Ka, recognise the strong acid/weak base profile, and explain why the HCl conductivity falls then rises while ethanoic acid's rises with a bend.

## Trace from the entry knowledge (student starting Year 12 Module 6)

Entry: C05, C07, C08, C10 to C13 and Year 11 conduction. The trace follows the two 0.100 mol/L curves from the hook to feedback 2.

1. Hook: two curves drawing together is a strong contrast. The narration says "Two flasks" while the plates are pH-meter beakers (M4).
2. Axes and starting pH: causal ("fully ionised"; "about one in a hundred has ionised"), with the same-concentration condition on the chip. The HCl start is reported as 1.00 although [H₃O⁺] = 0.100 mol/L has three significant figures, so the C07 rule gives 1.000 (S3).
3. Buffer region: the ratio bar filling while the curve levels is the best visual in the video. Good.
4. Half-equivalence: the caliper folds a bracket labelled "equivalence volume" before scene 5 has found the equivalence point; the label is marked "faint, explained in scene 5". Student question: "Halfway to what? How do I know equivalence is at twenty five?" The method the video later teaches (find equivalence, then halve) runs in the opposite order here (S1).
5. Equivalence point: "not where the curve crosses seven" is false for the HCl curve on screen, which does cross 7 at equivalence (science S1). A student comparing the two curves will notice.
6. Equivalence pH and the jump: "because its buffer held the pH up" ties the shorter jump to scene 3. Both jump spans repeat C13 scenes 10 and 11 without a callback (M6). At the end, two labels, two brackets, two tags and a crossed chip are bright (M1).
7. Strong acid with weak base: the level stretch near 9 explained by the ammonia buffer is good. The x label changes to "volume of NH₃ added" while the two dimmed NaOH curves stay on the same axes, so those curves are now read against the wrong axis (M3).
8, 9. Conductivity: causal throughout (fast hydronium replaced by slower sodium; few ions at first for ethanoic acid). The ion tally sized by conductivity is a good visual model. Good.
10. Memory aid: "At half-equivalence, pH = pKa", with "Find equivalence in the middle of the jump, halve the volume" spoken. Good.
11 to 14. Attempts and feedback: see below.
15, 16. Decision rule and key notes: four notes, useful. Key note 3's "pH is 7" needs "at 25 °C" (science M1).

## Findings

### S1. Should fix: scene 4 halves an equivalence volume the student has not been given

- Exact text: "Halfway to equivalence, at twelve point five zero millilitres, half the ethanoic acid has become ethanoate." Visual: bracket "equivalence volume" (faint, explained in scene 5).
- Student question: "Where did twenty five come from? The video tells me to find equivalence first."
- Replacement: "The moles say each flask reaches equivalence at twenty five millilitres. So halfway, at twelve point five zero millilitres, ..." with the bracket labelled "equivalence: 25.00 mL, from the moles". Scene 5 then opens "On the curve, the equivalence point sits ..." and says "as the moles said", and its concentration line becomes "So for an unknown acid, that volume gives the concentration: here, zero point one zero zero moles per litre."
- Understanding enabled: the order matches the method (equivalence volume first, then half), and scene 5 shows why the curve matters when the concentration is unknown.

### S2. Should fix: feedback 1's strength evidence depends on part (b)

- Exact text: "It's weak: it starts at pH two point seven nine, not one, and it has a buffer region before the jump."
- Problem: "not one" is true only for a 0.100 mol/L strong acid; the student learns the concentration only in part (b). Scene 2 itself made the comparison conditional ("at the same concentration").
- Replacement: "It's weak: there's a buffer region before the jump, and the pH at equivalence is above seven." Chip "weak: buffer region; equivalence pH > 7"; after the c line, a grey tag "a strong acid at 0.100 mol/L would start at pH 1.000, not 2.79".
- Understanding enabled: two features that need no concentration, and the starting-pH check shown in its proper place.

### S3. Should fix: report the HCl start as 1.000 (scene 2)

- Exact text: "Hydrochloric acid starts at pH one point zero zero"; label "1.00"; author note b "three significant figures in c, two decimal places".
- Problem: the owner rule (C07) gives pH as many decimal places as [H₃O⁺] has significant figures: 0.100 mol/L gives 1.000. Beside 2.87 (two decimal places, from a two-figure Ka), the two labels then show the rule working.
- Replacement: "one point zero zero zero"; label "1.000" with a grey tag "[H₃O⁺] = 0.100 mol/L: 3 s.f. → 3 d.p.", rewriting to "[H₃O⁺] from Ka: 2 s.f. → 2 d.p." on 2.87; author notes and tables updated. The spoken "pH one" in scene 7 and "not one" are approximate and can stay.

### M1. Minor: scene 6 end state

- Dim the "buffer: shorter jump" and "at these concentrations" tags as the crossed chip writes, and dim the crossed chip when its sentence ends; the two labels and two brackets are colour-paired.

### M2. Minor: direct curve labels

- Each curve gets a label at its right end in its own colour ("HCl", "CH₃COOH", later "HCl + NH₃"), dimming with its curve, so the comparison does not rest on colour alone.

### M3. Minor: scene 7 axis

- Label "volume of 0.100 mol/L base added (mL)" with a small key "pale blue, teal: NaOH | amber: NH₃".

### M4. Minor: hook says "flasks"; the plates show beakers

- Replacement: "Two beakers, each with ...".

### M5. Minor: units in the concentration lines (scenes 5, 12, 14)

- Replacement: "c(acid) = (0.100 mol/L × 0.02500 L) ÷ 0.02500 L = 0.100 mol/L", and the same form in both feedbacks.

### M6. Minor: call back C13 for the jump spans (scene 6)

- Replacement: "And as in the indicator video, the jumps differ: ..."; the new point (the buffer as the reason) stays.

### M7. Minor: YouTube prerequisites

- Add "titration calculations" (C12).

### M8. Minor: main question wording

- Replacement: "... give two very different pH curves and two different conductivity graphs. What can those profiles tell you about an acid: strong or weak, where equivalence is, how much acid there is, and even its Ka?"
