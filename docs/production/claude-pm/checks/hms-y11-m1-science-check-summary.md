## Blockers in full

### 1. v07, 10b feedback (survey run twice)
Current: "It's reliability. ... The classic mistake is calling it validity, but the questions still ask about the right thing." The post-exam week is an uncontrolled condition affecting responses "for a reason unrelated to what health means to them"; by scene 7's own definition and by NESA science usage (controlled variables bear on validity) a validity answer is defensible, so the feedback marks a reasonable answer wrong.
Corrected 10b: "Mainly reliability: run twice, it didn't give the same result. You could also argue validity, because the post-exam week added a condition unrelated to what health means to them. Either way, the repair is to run both at comparable times, with the same instructions. The classic mistake is calling it credibility, but nothing about the source changed." Update the model to accept validity with that reasoning and cross "credibility".

### 2. v12, scene 8 narration (intention-behaviour gap)
Current: "Even then, almost everyone who intends to sleep more or move more doesn't." Rhodes and de Bruijn 2013 (*Br J Health Psychol* 18:296 to 309) found 36 per cent unsuccessful intenders against 42 per cent successful, so about 46 per cent of intenders did not act: close to half, not almost everyone.
Corrected: "Even then, many people who intend to sleep more or move more don't. For exercise, studies suggest close to half of intenders don't follow through. That distance is the intention-behaviour gap, and a plan should expect it." (No-figure option: "Even then, many people who intend to sleep more or move more don't.") Keep consistent with hms-y11-m2-v21's "close to half".

## Should fix (one line each; full wording in the per-video files)
- v01 scene 6: the social and emotional wellbeing model has seven domains (body; mind and emotions; family and kinship; community; culture; Country; spirituality and ancestors); split the merged label and say "spirituality and ancestors".
- v03 scene 4 and key note 3: morbidity is also measured by surveys; "only" and "never appear" become "usually" and "often don't appear".
- v04 scene 6: "community-controlled health services ... work so well" becomes "are often more trusted and better used".
- v04 scene 8: "the most effective way" becomes "often one of the most effective ways".
- v05 scene 4 and key note 2: clustered effects "compound", not "multiply".
- v08 scene 8: the same "multiply" fix; "work because communities run them" becomes "are trusted because communities run them".
- v10 scene 5: "the skill young people use least" and "explain most of that" are unsupported; becomes "many young people delay seeking help, or never seek it. Five common barriers ...".
- v11 scene 3: the cited source says "most common factor", not "strongest single source".
- v16 scene 6: "free youth mental health centres" becomes "free or low-cost".
- v17 scene 2 and key note 2: the MDGs already had an environment goal (MDG 7); say "gave the environment far more weight, and added inequality".
- v17 scene 4: diarrhoea is "prevented by safe water and sanitation, not by clinics alone".
- v17 scene 5: replace the cost comparison with the Gakidou 2010 finding (education explains about half the fall in child deaths since 1970) or "can save more".
- v17 scene 8 (pre-voicing gate): the Monash and SDSN reports support climate and inequality, not Indigenous health outcomes; use "Independent reviews have found Australia off track on climate and inequality, and national reports show several health targets for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people are not on track."
