The 60 chips are a finite realised set: conditional on those exact positions, choosing one of the 60 gives a discrete empirical distribution, not exactly f(x)=x/8. Introduce the latter as the idealised model of a uniformly chosen point on the disc, or as the distribution before independent uniformly placed chip positions are realised; keep the 15-per-ring counts labelled as this illustrative sample, not a general guarantee. The design's author assumption equating an arbitrary fixed sample with exact area proportions is invalid. For continuous X no exact distance is more probable than another: describe a mode as a peak of density or most likely *small neighbourhood*, especially at the endpoint, rather than a most likely exact value. A density's value at a single point can be changed without changing its distribution; use the limiting shape for the endpoint mode. A flat uniform density has multiple modes, rather than no peak at all.
