# B13 learning review: what collaborative population-genetics data can show

Scope: independent AI source review of `../lessons/bio-m5-b13.md` (Revision 1, 18 Oct). Read as a student starting Year 12 who has watched B7, B9, B11 and B12 and knows Year 11 natural selection. References: `docs/production/student-learning-review.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `../site-extracts/bio-m5-l18.md`, `../site-corrections.md`, `programme/syllabus/bio-2017-y12.json` (B5-IQ5-03 to -06). Not learner evidence. No stills, preview or audio inspected (none exist yet). Motion is not judged; human listening pending. U+2014 searched in the design: none.

Main question traced: *What can data pooled from huge numbers of people (or animals) show that one family or one hospital can't, and how far can we trust what it shows?* The useful outcome: the student can read a trend in pooled data, propose a relationship, name a rival explanation, and say what the data cannot tell them.

**Summary.** No blocker. The lesson has one question and one repeated move ("pattern, then rival explanation, then limit"), applied to three contexts; that is a real structure, not a list. Strengths to keep: the small-against-large simulation shows *why* scale matters instead of asserting it; the sickle map is framed as a puzzle against Year 11 selection before the explanation; the malaria layer sliding over the allele map is a genuine video advantage; scene 6 blocks "the maps match, so it is proven" with both a coincidence argument and a concrete rival (founders on an island); the devil trays make "diversity" visible before the term; both attempts are unseen, carry all data on the card, and have brief feedback with one crossed mistake. Four should-fix items below, chiefly a causal gap in scene 8 and a missing mechanism in scene 5.

## Learner trace

1. **Hook.** Tube chips fill to 500,000; one chip opens to a DNA strip; a five-symbol pedigree and a 200-chip block sit beside the huge grid. The question is clear. Number load (2006, 500,000, 40 to 69, 850,000) is high but each number has its own cue and tag. Pass.
2. **Scene 2.** "Collaborative project" named after the pooling picture; "inference" glossed. The simulation explains why chance swamps small effects. Pass with finding 6.
3. **Scene 3.** Expected falling line beside a map where the allele is common: a genuine puzzle from Year 11 knowledge. Pass.
4. **Scene 4.** Three cards, each with what it cannot show, joining into one conclusion. Recall clauses only (B7, B9); no re-teaching. Pass with finding 5.
5. **Scene 5.** Overlay and genotype bars; the term after the cause. Fails the "why?" test at the carrier protection step (finding 2) and overstates the match (finding 3).
6. **Scene 6.** Association against cause (one B11 clause), direct study card, founder boat. Strong. Pass with finding 7.
7. **Scene 7.** Devil trays before "genetic diversity"; crash line; a disease passes along both trays; pairing cards; the "guarantees survival" chip crossed. The causal chain (fewer alleles, fewer chances some resist) is explicit. Pass.
8. **Scene 8.** Bars, nested circles and a tree are well chosen, but the inference skips its cause and the scene carries two ideas (finding 1).
9. **Scene 9.** Improves / does not remove table plus one concrete bias (healthy volunteers). Pass.
10. **Scene 10.** Two lines, each after its explanation; both reconstructable. Pass.
11. **Attempt 1 (11a).** Six rows, two demands, scatter without trend line or highlighted point. Everything needed is on the card through and after the 10 s countdown, which starts after "Pause here to answer.". Answer not given away. Feedback 11b (385 characters, about 29 s): answer, reason, one crossed mistake. At the top of the 15 to 30 s band; acceptable. Pass.
12. **Attempt 2 (11c).** Both values, ten markers, two demands on the card; island and mainland chips. Feedback 11d about 26 s; one crossed mistake. Pass after finding 4.
13. **Scene 12 and notes.** Decision rule echoes the repeated move; handoff to G03 names its question. Notes: five items, separate from new reasoning, quiet hold. Pass with finding 9.

## Designer's doubts (decisions)

- (a) Scene 8 crowding: resolved by finding 1 (reference genome kept as one sentence; donor detail moved to screen).
- (b) Map source: Piel et al. 2010 bands are usable (science check finding 3); keep the coded outline.
- (c) "Subset" wording: changed (finding 1; science check finding 6).
- (d) Heterozygote advantage at this level: keep, with the one-clause mechanism (finding 2).
- (e) Attempt 1 generic variant: keep generic. A named real variant would need sourced values and would add facts students might memorise; labelled example data is better for an unseen reasoning task.
