## Findings

1. **Blocker:** The open-beaker crystal drop and rising plume are treated as isolated diffusion because the water started still and at room temperature. Those conditions do not remove currents introduced by the crystal’s entry, dissolution and density differences. [Original 2025 experimental analysis, DOI 10.1021/acs.jchemed.5c00262](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jchemed.5c00262) explicitly identifies this problem. Its measured diffusion scale is about 1.5×10⁻⁹m²/s. The one-dimensional RMS distance in 20 min is about 1.9 mm, not a large fraction of a school beaker. The same painted spreading stage also means 20 min in scene 1 and 6 min in scene 3, whose ruler readings are invented from illustration. This is learning blocker 1. Root adopted the source-backed agar repair: a single 1.0 cm cube immersed in permanganate solution 5 min, removed, blotted and cut to inspect penetration, taking the single-size subset of [Nuffield’s original published protocol, printed p210](https://assets.ctfassets.net/pc40tpn1u6ef/file-14740/f38818f1a3c97330263cbd2f04968f57/9288-Rev_Nuff_Biol_Text_2_living_things_3.pdf). Exact same-duration scene replacements, character deltas, named cuts and three new plate specifications are in `agar-repair-and-assets.json`. The open-beaker stages are superseded and must not be painted. The three agar plates illustrate partial penetration, not an observed five-minute result; the source specifies neither rim depth nor a guaranteed clear core. The later art task can proceed with this explicit illustrative status; no physical experiment is claimed or required to complete this review/art brief. No surface-area/volume comparison or calculation is added.

2. **Should fix:** Scene 5’s “barely get through in the time of the demo”, scene 6’s “The sugar stays inside ... water keeps moving in”, and the universal sign rule in scene 9 are stronger than the source evidence. Common Visking admits small sugars, and a 30 min sucrose-retention rate is not established for unspecified tubing, concentration, area and temperature. Identify/pilot the actual tubing and interval; keep any faster-water explanation conditional on that setup. Say “During this observation, the difference drives net water inward”, not indefinite retention/flow. Positive/negative percentage change means net mass gain/loss; it indicates water gain/loss only when solute exchange/leaks/retained surface water are negligible or accounted for. Keep +16% arithmetic, but replace the sign chips with “mass gain/mass loss” and explain the water-dominant condition in the same sentence. This is one permeability-and-measurement repair, not a new thermodynamics lesson. [Direct glucose/Visking practical](https://practicalbiology.org/exchange-of-materials/digestion-and-absorption/evaluating-visking-tubing-as-a-model-for-a-gut.html), [manufacturer MWCO](https://www.medicellmembranes.co.uk/tubing1.html) and [manufacturer permeability guidance](https://www.serva.de/enDE/Catalog/311_Sample_Preparation_Dialysis_212_0.html) support the correction but do not give a universal school-demo rate.

Do not silently remove observations or select the most convenient results. This is learning should-fix 1, a shorter replacement at the existing mean cue. Repeated valid trials reduce random scatter; they do not remove systematic blotting/calibration error.

## Numerical, method and safety checks

17.4−15.0=+2.4g; (2.4/15.0)×100=+16%. Control 14.9−14.8=+0.1g; percentage=0.675675...%, about 0.68%. Potato 4.40−5.00=−0.60g; percentage=−12%. The wrong final-mass denominator gives −13.636...%, correctly shown as −13.6%. Displayed decimal places do not themselves establish instrument uncertainty; record balance resolution/bounds in the actual practical. The small control difference should not be treated as more precise than the balance permits. No repeat dataset was provided, so no mean can yet be independently recomputed. All mass data are explicitly illustrative, not observations.

The 2.4 g gain in 30 min is not numerically impossible, but cannot be quantitatively validated without solution strength, actual tubing, surface area and hydraulic permeability. Keep it as a constructed arithmetic example, not a predicted standard result. A half-filled bag should show a modest increase consistent with the illustrative volume, not an arbitrary doubling/taut inflation; the painting is not a volumetric measurement. Adjust initial slackness or final plumping rather than silently changing the given masses.

Soaking/softening, sound knots, a leak check, rinsing off external sucrose and identical blotting are appropriate preparation. The published Nuffield tubing method explicitly leak-tests knots before adding sugar. A water-filled blank checks the no-solute comparison. The level-rise method needs a sealed joint, a filled continuous liquid path and vertical clamped tube. For the same added volume Δh=ΔV/(πr²), so a narrower tube makes the rise larger; half the radius gives four times the rise. No numerical level data are supplied. Do not imply water rises indefinitely: back pressure and changing concentrations eventually oppose further rise, without needing a new learner scene.

The source-backed agar repair uses the source’s 1.0 cm size and 5 min interval; the book does not specify permanganate concentration or rim depth. Teacher preparation must use the current school chemical procedure and a pilot. No 5 min painted rim is to become a fabricated measured depth. Rulers/readouts must use actual observations or be clearly schematic. No people, faces or hands are required; preparation and cutting can be offscreen while pupils time, observe and measure.

Prepared human-cell slides remain the series choice; this practical needs no human tissue. The public human-tissue restriction and staff-only-current-policy limitation are documented in `work/brief147/official-safety.json`. Glass/breakage and slips controls are appropriate. Dilute solution and solid oxidiser hazards must not be conflated; the stock is kept apart from the sucrose activity. Do not copy older drain-disposal or medicinal statements as permission for this current practical.
