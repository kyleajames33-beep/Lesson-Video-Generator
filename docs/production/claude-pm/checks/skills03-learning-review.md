## Main question and useful outcome

Main question: *When a question asks you to compare, contrast or distinguish, what makes the answer actually compare?* There is one question, and it is carried throughout. Useful outcome: a student who knows the content sketches a feature-by-case table, then writes one paired sentence per row with "both" or "whereas", and gives differences only for contrast and distinguish. The owner's brief ("students know the content but can't write the answer") is served directly. The worked pair (snapdragons against roan cattle) is well chosen: B10 taught both, and the two-description weak answer is exactly the 8/12 fault.

## Trace from entry knowledge

1. Hook: the 12-tile grid with eight flags, then two squiggle blocks sliding into paired columns, shows the problem and the fix in one move. Good.
2. Three verbs: the meanings are clear and the tip is labelled as HSCScience advice, not glossary. The zero for contrast and distinguish is not scoped to the paper set in speech (S3).
3. and 4. Question and reading: the routine runs on the card. The true-breeding step skips why the offspring are heterozygous (S4).
5. Pair table: this is the best teaching move in the video. The table makes "same feature, both sides" visible before any sentence exists. Row 2's wording clashes with roan (S5).
6. Table to sentence: each pen arrow carries a row into a sentence, and the comparison word rings as it is spoken. The closing line implies one mark per sentence (S1).
7. Comparison words: the mismatched "whereas" sentence is a sharp, memorable contrast. It teaches that the word cannot rescue unmatched features. The memory aid is earned by scenes 5 to 7.
8. Weak against strong: convincing. The weak answer is correct biology, so pairing is the only difference. 1 of 3 (root decision) is labelled illustrative, and the lost-marks tag is specific.
9. Guidelines: both examples are accurate (see the science check), but the conclusion turns them into a rule that the first example contradicts (S1). The scene stacks four visual items in about 37 s (M1).
10. to 13. Drills: each practises the taught skill. Drill 1 trains both halves of compare (a surprising similarity, the equal titre, and two differences); drill 2 trains "differences only, still in pairs". The full card stays visible during "Pause here to answer." and the 10 s countdown, and nothing answer-bearing appears. Feedback 1 is too long and its screen rows are dense (S2).
14. and 15. Decision rule and notes: separate from new reasoning, with a quiet copy hold. Good.

## Findings

No blockers.

**S1 (should fix). Scenes 9 and 6: an example turned into a rule.**
Quoted (scene 9): "So more marks usually means **more paired rows**, not longer descriptions."
Quoted (scene 6): "Three rows, three sentences, three marks."
Problem: the same scene has just said that a three-mark NESA sample answer was two sentences (Biology 2025 Q23). The table example (Biology 2024 Q21(b)) is about all against most of the requested features, not about adding rows. A student will ask "So is it one mark per row or not?"
Replacement (scene 9): "So the marks followed **complete pairing** of the features asked about, not longer descriptions." Visual: the returning pair table has every row paired and ticked beside a loose list that stays unticked; no fourth and fifth rows.
Replacement (scene 6): "Three paired sentences: in our marking guide, three marks."
Understanding enabled: pairing every requested feature is what scores, not counting sentences.

**S2 (should fix). Scene 11 (feedback 1): length and screen load.**
Quoted: "Compare, with two constraints: the data, and acid strength. Similarity: both need the same **twenty millilitres** of sodium hydroxide, because they have the same amount of acid, ..." (482 characters, about 36 s). The first screen row is about 190 characters.
Problem: over the owner's 15 to 30 s feedback band, and three long rows under the three-row table exceed the screen limit.
Replacement: "Two constraints: the data, and acid strength. Similarity: both need **twenty millilitres** of sodium hydroxide: same moles of acid, and the weak acid keeps ionising as base is added. ..." ending "The habit to drop: one acid, then the other." Shorten the screen rows (for example row 1: "Similarity: both need 20.0 mL of NaOH: same concentration, both monoprotic, so the same moles of acid. The weak acid keeps ionising as OH- reacts."), and dim each table row once its line has used it.

**S3 (should fix). Scene 2: zero count not scoped.**
Quoted: "Contrast and distinguish didn't appear as commands in the last five years of papers, but compare did, seven times."
Problem: the pack's zero applies only to the 10 Chemistry and Biology Section II papers, "not a claim about all NESA exams". A student may hear "never asked, skip them", and drill 2 then asks distinguish.
Replacement: "In the last five years of Chemistry and Biology written questions, compare appeared seven times. Contrast and distinguish didn't, but they're still glossary words, so know them." Count tag: "Chemistry and Biology, Section II, 2021 to 2025".

**S4 (should fix). Scene 4: skipped cause.**
Quoted: "**True-breeding** tells you each parent has two identical alleles, so every offspring is heterozygous."
Problem: homozygous parents give heterozygous offspring only because the two parents carry different alleles. A student will ask "why heterozygous, if both parents are homozygous?"
Replacement: "**True-breeding** tells you each parent has two identical alleles. The parents differ, so every offspring gets one of each: heterozygous."

**S5 (should fix). Scene 5, row 2: wording clashes with co-dominance.**
Quoted: "in both, they look like **neither parent**" and the cells "like neither parent".
Problem: a roan calf shows red hairs and white hairs, and B10's aid says "co shows both". A student will say a roan looks like both parents.
Replacement: "in both, they look different from **both parents**", with cells "different from both parents" (the "≠ red, ≠ white" marks stay).

**M1 (minor). Scene 9 visual density.**
The sample-answer frame, the all/most table and bar, the returning pair table, loose chips and a footer tag share about 37 s. Show one at a time: the frame collapses to its tag before the bar draws, and the bar clears before the pair table returns.

**M2 (minor). Scene 13 (feedback 2) about 33 s.**
Trim to about 30 s ("Distinguish means differences: two marks, two paired differences." and "whereas sister chromatids are identical").

**M3 (minor). Placement and open decisions.**
Root has decided placement (after C12 in Chemistry) and the weak-answer mark (1 of 3). Record both as decided in the header, playlists line and open decisions 3 and 4.

## Retained strengths

The pair table turns "compare" into a visible planning step. The mismatched "whereas" sentence teaches the matched-feature check better than a rule would. Weak against strong uses correct science, so pairing is the only difference. Drill 1's equal titre gives students a genuine similarity to find. Every question carries the HSCScience tag, and every mark tally is labelled illustrative.

## Verdict
