## Findings

### S1. Should fix (blocker in the learning review): the scrubber is described as not involving proton transfer

- Exact text (scene 7): "Sulfur dioxide has no hydrogen to donate, so this isn't a proton transfer." Also the scene 7 tag "no proton moves", key note 4 "SO₂ (an acidic oxide, no proton transfer)", Stops after "explaining why SO₂ reacts with a base without a proton transfer", the teaching check "Why the scrubber reaction is not a proton transfer", site correction 9's note, and the YouTube description "how a scrubber removes sulfur dioxide without any proton transfer".
- Problem: in a wet slurry SO₂ first forms sulfurous acid (as scene 6 teaches), and the hydroxide neutralises its hydrogen ions.
- Replacement (scene 7): "Sulfur dioxide has no hydrogen of its own, so it can't donate a proton. It's an **acidic oxide**: a non-metal oxide that makes an acid in water and reacts with bases to make a salt. In the slurry's water it first makes **sulfurous acid**, and the hydroxide neutralises that." Key note 4: "SO₂ is an acidic oxide (no H of its own; in water it makes H₂SO₃)." Description: "how a scrubber removes sulfur dioxide, an acidic oxide,". Remove the C01 CaO + SO₃ callback chip from scene 7 (that reaction is dry). Add the wet mechanism to author note (d).

### M1. Minor (no change to learner copy): iron oxidation state in mine drainage

- Scene 8 shows "amber Fe³⁺ tokens" on "dissolved iron ions". Pyrite oxidation releases Fe²⁺ first, which oxidises to Fe³⁺ in air (the orange colour is iron(III) hydroxide and oxyhydroxide). The narration says only "iron ions", and author note (f) records the Fe²⁺ to Fe³⁺ step, so the Fe³⁺ tokens are an acceptable simplification that sets up Fe(OH)₃. No change.

### M2. Minor (no change): the aluminium threshold scale

- "Below about pH five and a half" is a water-scale value; NSW DPI uses pHCa (about 0.5 to 0.8 lower). Author note (c) already says so. No learner change.

### M3. Minor (no change): no state on Ca(OH)₂ in the scrubber equation

- "Ca(OH)₂ + SO₂(g) → CaSO₃(s) + H₂O(l)" deliberately omits the state because the reagent is a slurry (author note). Acceptable; "(s)" would also be accepted in HSC marking. No change.
