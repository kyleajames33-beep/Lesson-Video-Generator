## Findings

1. **Should fix, both halves of 8a are the body's own examples.** Scene 2 uses "low iron found by a blood test" as reason 1, and scene 3 says "iron should follow a blood test"; 8a's runner has a blood test showing low iron. Scene 3 says "Once intake is adequate, more protein doesn't build more muscle"; 8a's footballer "eats a varied diet with adequate protein" and wants a powder "to get bigger". Both answers are body sentences. Replace with cases that test the same rules from a new side, for example A: "a 16-year-old vegetarian rower who has had no blood test wants an iron supplement because a teammate takes one" (test first, food sources, excess iron harms; a guess is not a deficiency) and B: "a 15-year-old who trains straight after school, then spends an hour on a bus and can't eat a meal until 8 pm, asks about a protein drink" (a genuine practical-constraint reason; convenience, not magic; food first once home). Demand and marks unchanged. Students should decide whether each case meets one of the three reasons.

2. **Should fix, the hook's ranking for a seventeen-year-old omits the adolescent caution on performance supplements.** Scene 6 answers the hook ("Caffeine and creatine probably rank above protein powder and a multivitamin") for a 17-year-old team-sport athlete. Scene 4 cautions teenagers about caffeine but says nothing about creatine for under-18s, and sports bodies generally advise junior athletes to avoid performance supplements beyond food and medically indicated use. Without that condition, a student learns that a 17-year-old should start creatine. Fix (about 4 s, from reserve): after "it depends on the athlete", add "And for anyone under eighteen, most sports bodies advise food first, with performance supplements only on medical or dietitian advice", and add a grey "under 18: advice first" condition chip beside the three. For the science check: confirm the current Australian guidance wording for junior athletes and creatine.

3. **Should fix, on-screen tags name internal video numbers.** The board shows grey tags "v14" (scene 2), "v08" (scene 3, twice) and "strict liability (v22)" (scene 5). Students never see file ids; the brief says "earlier" (and "later" for v22). Use "earlier" and "later".

4. **Note, the protein line is now said a third time.** Year 11 (hms-y11-m2-v11: "The training is the signal to grow, and protein is the material"), v14 scene 2 and v16 scene 3 all say it. Here it belongs to the dotpoint, so keep it, but present it as recall ("As you've seen, training is the stimulus and protein the material") and let the v14 review's fix take it out of v14.

5. **Note, 8d is about 30 s.** Trim "with a batch-tested product, and medical advice" to "with medical advice and a batch-tested product" only if the audio runs over; the line on screen carries both.
