## Notes (no change required to record)

- **Second sperm:** the tube releases "the sperm cells" (plural) and one joins the egg. The visual lets the other fade. Students may ask what it does. The Claude decision to leave double fertilisation out is acceptable, because "one sperm cell joins the egg" is true. Keep the fade quiet and do not add narration. If the Codex check prefers it, a clause is enough: "A second sperm cell does a different job that you do not need yet."
- **Sperm in the grain:** many pollen grains hold a generative cell that divides to make the sperm cells as the tube grows. "Each pollen grain carries sperm cells" is the usual school simplification. Record it for the Codex check.
- **Typical flower versus strawberry:** the hook uses a strawberry, scene 2 a generic flower, scene 6 a pea pod and a strawberry seed. Say "typical flower" and "pea" aloud (findings 2 and 6) so the switch is announced.
- **Scene 9 food store:** potato tubers and bulbs also store food. The line "Seeds also have a coat and a food store, and they can travel far" is true but does not separate seeds from runners and tubers. Use "Seeds can travel far, so the plant can reach new places." (-10 words). Also replace the closing fragments, "Fast and alike close by. Varied and far away with seeds.", with "Runners are fast and keep the plant alike, close by. Seeds make varied plants, and they can travel far."
- **Hook:** "Not yet." resolves the question straight away. Consider letting the board hold on the question mark for two seconds, then "Not yet. The seed hasn't started."
- **Aid:** "Land, tube, join, seed" leaves out the embryo and the fruit. The key notes carry them. Fine. "STAMEN has MEN in it" is a harmless anchor; it should not be read as "carpel is the only female part" (it is not claimed).
- **Fruit hedge:** botanically the ovary of any flowering plant becomes a fruit. "In many plants" is cautious for learner copy and matches the site. Keep.
- **Gendered aid:** "men" is used as a pun for "male". No issue.

## Key notes (suggested wording)

1. Pollination is pollen moving from an anther to a stigma (by wind, water or animals). Fertilisation is a sperm cell joining an egg cell in an ovule to make a zygote. Pollination is not fertilisation.
2. The pollen grain grows a pollen tube down the style to an ovule. The tube carries the sperm cells to the egg.
3. The zygote develops into an embryo. The ovule becomes a seed (embryo, food store and coat). In many plants the ovary becomes a fruit.
4. Vegetative propagation (runners, bulbs, tubers, cuttings) is asexual: a piece of the parent grows into a new plant, so the offspring are usually clones.
5. Seeds come from sexual reproduction, so they vary and can travel. Clones are fast and alike, but share the same weaknesses.

Aid (large): *Land, tube, join, seed.*
