## Findings

1. **Should fix, 11a is the journey the video has just walked.** "Explain how oxygen moves from the alveoli to a working muscle cell" is the tracked dot of scenes 2, 6 and 8, so a student can recite it. Ask for the return trip instead: "Explain how carbon dioxide produced in a working muscle cell reaches the air you breathe out. (4 marks)" Model: diffusion from the muscle (higher partial pressure) into the blood; carried in veins to the vena cava and right atrium; pumped by the right ventricle through the pulmonary artery to the lungs; diffusion into the alveoli, then breathed out. Crossed mistake: "it leaves through the pulmonary vein" or "the muscle pumps it out". Students should show they can run both circuits and both gradients the other way.

2. **Should fix, scene 6's pathway skips two valves that 11d then requires.** Scene 6 says "passes the tricuspid valve into the right ventricle, and is pumped through the pulmonary artery" and "into the left ventricle, and out through the aorta". 11d asks students to "name every structure in order, including the valves" and lists the pulmonary and aortic valves. Add "through the pulmonary valve" and "through the aortic valve" to scene 6 (about 3 s), so the taught pathway is the marked pathway.

3. **Should fix, scene 9's breathing trigger disagrees with v14.** Scene 9 says "Sensors in the blood detect rising carbon dioxide ... Carbon dioxide, not low oxygen, is the main trigger." v14 scene 3 says breathing rises "within seconds, because the brain signals the breathing muscles at the same moment as the working muscles", with carbon dioxide pushing it further later. A student who watches both will ask which is right. Reword scene 9: "Both rise within seconds, because the brain and sensors in moving muscles and joints drive them together. Then rising carbon dioxide, not low oxygen, pushes them further." The hook's answer (one shared signal) still holds.

4. **Note, scene 8 never says the muscle's partial pressure.** "The blood arrives at about a hundred, the muscle is using oxygen up" leaves the low side of the gradient unspoken. Add "and the muscle's is about forty, lower in exercise". Show "mmHg" on the readouts.

5. **Note, scene 2's "a difference in oxygen that never runs out".** Say "that stays steep, because breathing and blood flow keep refreshing it"; "never runs out" invites "so why do I get puffed?".
