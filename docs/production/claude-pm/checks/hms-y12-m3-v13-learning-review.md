## Findings

1. **Should fix, five overlapping checklists in one video.** Students meet four tests of whom to believe (scene 3), six questions before deciding (4), eight misleading patterns plus three service checks (5), and five gates in the sixty-second method (6): twenty-six items, several repeated in different words ("who benefits?" is test 1 and gate 1; "how big, absolutely?" is question 2 and gate 4; "fits the evidence?" is test 3 and close to gate 3). The memory card and handoff then use the five gates. Students may try to learn four lists instead of one method. Show the overlap instead of a new list: in scene 6, as each gate labels, a thin line links it to its source card from 3 to 5 (gate 1 to test 1, gate 2 to question 1 and the authority-words stamp, gate 3 to test 3, gate 4 to question 2, gate 5 to question 4), with a grey tag "you've met these". Narration at the start of 6: "Now put it together. Every gate is something you've just used." (about 3 s). Key note 5 then becomes the one list to keep. Students should leave with one method that collects the earlier tests, which v14 onwards can recall.

2. **Note, 8c's "forty per cent is relative".** "Boosts performance by 40%" is a percentage change with no baseline, not a relative risk in the scene 4 sense. Say "Forty per cent of what? There's no starting point, no population and no study." which uses the taught idea without stretching the term.

3. **Note, 8a is identification of patterns named minutes earlier.** The three claims map one to one onto stamps 2, 4 and 5. That is fine for a 3-mark "identify", and the classic mistake ("they're lies") adds the useful idea; 8c carries the transfer.

4. **Note, key notes 3 to 5 are lists of lists.** Note 4 abbreviates the patterns ("proxy", "lab only", "hype"), which students have not heard as words in the narration. Use the narrated names, or drop note 3 and keep notes 4 and 5, which are the parts exams reward.
