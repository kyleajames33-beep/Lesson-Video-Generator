## Findings

1. **Should fix, 11a is scene 3's own example.** Scene 3 says "Sloppy passes late in a game mean the nervous system", and 11a asks about a footballer whose passes go astray late in a match. Keep 11a and change scene 3's nervous example to one that does not preview it, for example "A gymnast whose landings wobble late in a routine, while she still has power, points to the nervous system." Students should then apply "how it fails names what failed" to the footballer themselves.

2. **Note, 11d is about 31 s.** Trim "Use the three situations as evidence: TOTAPS and RICER, cooling and fluids, DRSABCD for a collapse" to "Use the three situations as evidence." The green plan on screen lists them.

3. **Note, the vertical jump is the module's most repeated case.** It is v01's 11a, v02's 11a (a rebound jump) and v08's scene 4. Scene 4 adds phases, so it earns its place here, but later videos should not reuse it.

4. **Note, scene 9's "A collapse at the end of hard effort is never just tiredness."** As a first aid rule (treat it as serious) it is right; some collapses after a finish line are benign, so the science check should confirm the wording is acceptable as a safety rule rather than a medical claim.
