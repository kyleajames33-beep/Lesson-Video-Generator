# Brief 98: error-only check

Revision 2 designs, checked 22 October 2026.

## chem-m6-p01

1. **Scene 3: the pigment is stored behind an internal membrane too.**
   - Old: "The heat damages the cells' thin outer membranes, which hold the pigment in, so the pigment leaks out faster."
   - Corrected: "Heat damages the membranes in the cabbage cells, so the pigment leaks out faster."
   - Anthocyanins are stored in vacuoles. Their enclosing membrane is the tonoplast, inside the cell. The design's author note (e) already identifies the vacuoles correctly. Remove "outer" from the cause chain and entry-knowledge description too. If the cell lens depicts the pigment's storage location, show an internal pigment compartment before the membranes break. [Primary microscopy study: Chanoca et al., 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4815043/).

2. **Safety facts check: the hydrochloric-acid respiratory-irritation limit has no 25% upper boundary.**
   - Old: "Skin Irrit. 2, Eye Irrit. 2 and STOT SE 3 from 10 to under 25 percent."
   - Corrected: "Skin Irrit. 2 and Eye Irrit. 2 apply from 10 to under 25 percent. STOT SE 3 applies at 10 percent and above."
   - This changes the author reference statement, not the classification of the 0.10 mol/L reagent. [ECHA: hydrochloric acid, current Annex VI entry](https://echa.europa.eu/harmonised-classification-and-labelling-previous-consultations/-/substance-rev/81502/term).

## bio-m6-p01

1. **Scene 2 and key note 1: a colony count is not necessarily an individual-cell count.**
   - Old narration: "So every colony marks one cell that survived and kept dividing."
   - Corrected: "Each colony grew from a surviving cell or small clump that kept dividing."
   - Old visual: "one colony = one cell that could still divide".
   - Corrected visual: "one colony = one surviving cell or small clump".
   - Old key note: "A colony grows from one cell that could still divide, so a colony count measures survival."
   - Corrected key note: "A colony grows from a surviving cell or small clump, so colony counts estimate survival."
   - The author reference-facts paragraph already notes that a budding pair or clump counts once, but the narration, gloss and notes turn the single-cell case into an exact rule. Keep the same correction in the definition and boundary summaries. The survival arithmetic is unchanged. The CAN1 protocol cited by this design explicitly disrupts clumps and checks this under a microscope before counting cells. [Primary CAN1 protocol, procedure step 2](https://bio-protocol.org/pdf/Bio-protocol1272.pdf).

2. **Hook: only mutants that survived can be inside the colonies that grew.**
   - Old: "If some cells did mutate, where would they be? Not in the empty spaces. They'd be hiding inside the colonies that grew."
   - Corrected: "If some cells mutated and survived, where would they be? They'd be hiding inside the colonies that grew."
   - A mutation can prevent growth or kill a cell. The design's own Memory aid check correctly states that lethal mutations leave no colony. The hook needs the survival condition before assigning the mutants to visible colonies. [Primary CAN1 protocol: survival and mutant colony counts are measured separately](https://bio-protocol.org/pdf/Bio-protocol1272.pdf).

3. **Safety author reference: the Australian Standard does not supply the claimed RG1 organism list.**
   - Old: "Codex to confirm S. cerevisiae in its risk group 1 examples".
   - Corrected: "Science ASSIST lists S. cerevisiae among RG1 organisms used in Australian schools, applying the AS/NZS 2243.3:2022 classification. The Standard does not list RG1 organisms."
   - The classification conclusion can remain. Its source attribution needs correcting: ASTA explicitly distinguishes its school examples from the Standard's classification. [ASTA/Science ASSIST, Guidelines for best practice for microbiology in Australian schools, Edition 2, page 10](https://asta.edu.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Microbiology-Guidelines-Edition-2.pdf).
