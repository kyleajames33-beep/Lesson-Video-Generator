Main question: how does a student cover all demands and build a supported judgement? From s01, s02, s04 and s06, demand ticks, a one-line plan, worked PEEL paragraph and weak-versus-strong answer create useful new synthesis. Building paragraph 3 first is defensible if the answer remains numbered in final order. Drill 1 clearly requests a plan; drill 2 isolates judgement rather than a list of pros and cons.

## Findings

1. **Blocker. Scenes 5 to 9 and model P2/P4.** The card says 95% must be immune and two doses protect 97%, but the answer equates 95% vaccinated with 95% immune. The threshold conclusion does not follow. Give a target for two-dose vaccination coverage and keep the answer about coverage, or give actual immune proportions and use those consistently. Do not teach a numerical contradiction as model use of evidence.
2. **Blocker. Scene 9 and opening/closing verdicts.** Nothing establishes that immunity from 310 cases "probably also helped" the regional decline or that the program was the "main reason". Population size and other causes are absent; cases already fell 310 to 180 before the program. Repair: "The program may have contributed: coverage rose and cases fell after it began, but cases were already falling and these data cannot separate its effect from other changes." Point at 2016 to 2018 before the later values. Keep outbreak immunity as a possibility in author notes unless its significance is quantified.
3. **Should fix. Scene 3.** Seven-year table, repeated dual-axis graph, background sentences, demands and answer area compete during a short attempt. Remove the redundant graph at first reading, drop the unneeded droplet sentence as the author suggests, and explicitly allow pause for the one-minute plan. Keep every demand and value during the countdown.
4. **Should fix. Scenes 1 and 15, notes 3 to 4.** "For every long answer", judgement first and "each with a number" turn advice for a quantitative assess task into a universal rule. Repair the board: "If a judgement is requested, plan it first. Select relevant evidence for each paragraph, including numbers when the stimulus provides them." Keep PEEL a scaffold, not a compulsory four-sentence marking rule.
5. **Should fix. Scene 1.** The audit counts reports mentioning unused OR copied stimulus, but narration claims all flagged unused stimulus alone. Say "Every report flagged answers that ignored the stimulus or copied it without explaining it." Preserve the denominator: recurrence in feedback reports, not the fraction of students.

## Checks

Table values are consistent. Coverage 89 to 95 is 6 percentage points; 180 to 6 is a fall of 174 cases. Drill 1, 6.0 to 2.5 per 1,000 patient-days, is a 58.3% fall, so fewer than half is correct. The concurrent cleaning change prevents attribution to training alone. Drills retain complete demands, brief feedback and one crossed habit. The soap conclusion weighs the facts given. Its thumbnail can stay out until art acceptance is recorded. No playback or student-learning pass is implied.

Counts: **2 blockers, 3 should fix, 0 optional**.
