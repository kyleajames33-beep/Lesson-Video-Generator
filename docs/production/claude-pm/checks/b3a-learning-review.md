## Key notes (suggested wording)

1. Fertilisation (a sperm joins an egg to make a zygote) usually happens in the oviduct. The zygote divides by cleavage as it moves toward the uterus and becomes a blastocyst, which implants in the uterine lining about a week after fertilisation. Fertilisation and implantation are different events, and a pregnancy begins at implantation.
2. After the egg is released, the corpus luteum makes progesterone, which keeps the uterine lining thick and ready. With no pregnancy the corpus luteum breaks down about two weeks after ovulation and the lining is shed.
3. After implantation the embryo's outer cells make hCG. hCG acts on the corpus luteum, so it keeps making progesterone. Progesterone acts on the lining.
4. Over the first two to three months after fertilisation, the placenta takes over making progesterone. The placenta passes oxygen and nutrients from the mother's blood to the baby and carbon dioxide and wastes back, without the bloods mixing.
5. A sperm has a tail to swim, mitochondria for energy and an acrosome with enzymes to get into the egg. The egg is large because it carries a food store for the early embryo.

Aid (large): *Progesterone is pro-gestation. hCG is the embryo's message to the corpus luteum.* Sequence rail: meet, divide, attach, signal, hand over.
