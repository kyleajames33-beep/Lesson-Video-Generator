## Facts confirmed (no change)

- **Insulin since the 1980s.** The US FDA approved Humulin (recombinant human insulin from *E. coli*) on 28 October 1982. "Since the nineteen-eighties, bacteria have made human insulin" is correct. The on-screen tag "since 1982" is correct.
- **First bacterial insulin.** The A- and B-chain genes were chemically synthesised (Crea et al., PNAS 1978). Each was cloned separately in pBR322, fused to β-galactosidase and expressed in *E. coli*. The chains were then cleaved off, purified and joined (Goeddel et al. 1979). The result was announced in September 1978. "In the late nineteen-seventies, scientists built DNA for insulin's two chains from scratch" is correct. "Made the two chains separately, and chemists purified them and joined them into insulin" is correct. Site corrections 1 and 6 are confirmed. (Searched: PMC382885.)
- **Most Australian cotton is Bt.** The OGTR fact sheet (December 2025) says over 99.5 per cent of Australian cotton is GM. Bollgard 3 (insect-resistant) varieties have been reported at about 90 to over 95 per cent of the crop since 2016 to 2018. "Most Australian cotton now makes its own insect toxin" is correct as worded. This is time-sensitive, so recheck near recording. (Searched: https://www.ogtr.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-12/genetically_modified_cotton_in_australia_0.pdf ; https://www.graincentral.com/cropping/bollgard-3-dominates-cotton-plantings-video/ ; https://www.queenslandcountrylife.com.au/story/4621659/bollgard-3-popular-in-the-field/ .) It is also consistent with b10 and b13.
- **Introns.** Most human protein-coding genes contain introns, which are removed by spliceosomes in the nucleus. Bacteria lack spliceosomes and cannot remove eukaryotic spliceosomal introns (some bacteria carry self-splicing group I and II introns, which is not relevant here). The insulin gene *INS* has two introns. "Bacteria can't do that cutting" is correct.
- **Intron-free DNA.** cDNA is made from mature mRNA by reverse transcriptase. Coding DNA can also be synthesised chemically. Correct. Reverse transcriptase appears on screen only.
- **Restriction enzymes' natural role.** They are part of restriction-modification systems: they cut phage DNA, and the host's own sites are protected by methylation. "Bacteria make these enzymes to chop up the DNA of invading viruses" is correct.
- **Sticky ends.** Many type II enzymes cut the two strands at staggered positions, leaving single-stranded overhangs (EcoRI leaves 4-base 5' overhangs). Others cut blunt. "Many of them" is correct. "The same enzyme, or one that leaves matching ends" is correct (for example BamHI and BglII leave compatible ends). Site correction 2 is confirmed.
- **Ligase.** Paired overhangs are held only by hydrogen bonds until DNA ligase forms phosphodiester bonds in each backbone. Correct.
- **Recombinant DNA and the marker gene.** Cloning plasmids carry an antibiotic-resistance gene (ampicillin resistance, *bla*). Correct.
- **Low transformation fraction.** Chemically competent cells take up a plasmid at well under 1 per cent of cells. "Only a small fraction" is correct.
- **Selection and screening.** Antibiotic selection keeps cells with any plasmid. Self-ligated vectors also confer resistance, so colonies are screened by insertional inactivation of a second marker (blue-white *lacZ*, or tetracycline resistance), colony PCR, digests or sequencing. Correct. Site correction 3 is confirmed. 10a's model answer is correct.
- **Promoter and shared code.** Bacterial RNA polymerase needs a promoter it recognises. The nuclear genetic code is shared by bacteria and humans; it is nearly universal, with variants in mitochondria and some microbes that do not apply here. Correct.
- **Fermenters.** Insulin is produced in large industrial fermenters, in *E. coli* or yeast. "Huge tanks of engineered microbes make it" is correct.
- **Agrobacterium, disarmed vectors, Bollgard event 531.** Disarmed Ti vectors lack the tumour-inducing genes. MON531 was made by *Agrobacterium*-mediated transformation of Coker 312, with an *nptII* selectable marker. Plants were regenerated from transformed cells. "That's how Bt cotton was first made" is correct (Bollgard and Australia's Ingard, 1996). The on-screen label "transformed cells selected with a marker, grown into plants" is correct.
- **Every cell and the seeds.** A plant regenerated from one transformed cell carries the insert in all its cells, including the germ line, so the insert is inherited through seed. Correct.
- **Attempt 1.** Chymosin is the main milk-clotting enzyme of calf rennet. Recombinant chymosin was produced in *E. coli* K-12 from a prochymosin coding sequence. The question supplies an intron-free copy and an ampicillin marker. The model answer's chain is correct. Expression needs a promoter, which is marked as extension.
- **Attempt 2.** The scenario is constructed and consistent with the biology. "Bacteria cannot remove introns, so ... the protein is faulty" is correct (intron codons add wrong amino acids, or an early stop codon ends the chain). The crossed mistake "bacteria use a different genetic code" is a correct target.
- **Absolute words.** "Bacteria don't take up plasmids easily" is correct. "Only bacteria carrying a plasmid ... grow" is correct when the host lacks resistance. "Every cell of those plants" is correct. "Bacteria can't do that cutting" is correct for spliceosomal introns. "Only reshuffles" is wrong (finding 1). "Only at one particular short sequence" applies to type II enzymes (finding 6).
- **Memory aids.** "Cut, paste, put in, pick" is correct as the core order, with its limits stated. "Same enzyme, matching sticky ends" is correct, with the compatible-ends note on screen. The note about blunt cutters is author copy only.

A web search found no NESA past-paper item with this wording (searched). Chymosin cloning appears in other boards' banks in general form only. These items are original.
- **YouTube metadata.** The dotpoint is quoted verbatim. "how Agrobacterium carried a Bt gene into cotton" is correct. The prerequisites and next link match the design.
