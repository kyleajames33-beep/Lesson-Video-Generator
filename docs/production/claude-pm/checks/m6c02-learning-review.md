## Main question and the useful outcome

Main question (as stated): "Given an acid and the substance it reacts with, how do you name everything, predict the products, and write the balanced equation?"

One main question: acceptable, with a caution. It covers two dotpoints (nomenclature and properties; acid reaction products). Naming is a step in the prediction (attempt 1 needs "nitric acid" to become HNO₃ and "nitrate" to name the salt), and the decision rule in scene 18 starts with "Name the acid", so the two halves serve one task. The risk is scene 5 (properties), which is a list that does not feed the prediction; see S3. Useful outcome: given a named acid and partner, the student writes the salt from charges, adds the right extra product, and balances.

## Trace from the entry knowledge

Entry: C01 (proton donor and acceptor; ammonia and carbonates as acceptors), Year 11 ionic formulas, polyatomic ions, balancing, states, acid reactions, activity series, oxidation as electron loss.

1. Hook: one acid, three partners, three visible results (fizz, fizz, warm). Good attention. It promises "name every substance here, predict the products of each reaction, and balance the equations". See S1: two of the three are never balanced on screen.
2. Binary acids: template blocks coloured, examples build. Clear.
3. Oxoacids: the cause ("named after the ion inside them", H⁺ tokens joining the ion, number of H equals the ion's charge) is shown, not just stated. The crossed "hydrosulfuric" chip with H₂S lighting is a good contrast. Reading load is heavy (six rows, three items each); see M2.
4. Bases: ionic naming is Year 11; ammonia and carbonates link back to C01 in one clause. Good.
5. Properties: a list of sensory properties with a safety caveat and a pointer to strength. No cause. See S3.
6. Salt rule: clear, and the charge tally demonstration is useful before the patterns. The four-acid strip plus builder plus tally at once is a lot; see M2.
7. Hydroxide: "a hydroxide ion plus a proton is a water molecule" is a clear cause, shown as a hop. Mg(OH)₂ + 2HCl is a well-chosen real antacid case.
8. Ammonia: the "no water" contrast with a crossed "+ 2H₂O" chip is the strongest moment in the video; it follows directly from scene 7's cause.
9. Carbonate: two protons onto carbonate, carbonic acid splitting, the CO₂ bubble rising: the cause of the fizz is visible. One visual mismatch: see S2.
10. Metal: correctly tagged as electron transfer, not proton transfer. Aluminium shows the harder balance. Good.
11. Above hydrogen: the activity bar and the copper beaker with no bubbles are good evidence. The "why" is a ranking restatement ("below hydrogen, so nothing happens"); acceptable because the activity series is Year 11 and electrochemistry is not Module 6.
12. Gas tests: clear and visual; apparatus only, no hands.
13. Memory aid: three short, checked aids, with condition tags for ammonia and metals.
14 to 17. Attempts and feedback: see below.
18, 19. Decision rule and notes: separate from new reasoning, quiet copy hold.

## Findings

### S1. Should fix: the hook promises equations that never appear

- Exact text (scene 1): "By the end of this video, you'll be able to name every substance here, predict the products of each reaction, and balance the equations."
- Problem: the video balances Mg(OH)₂ + 2HCl, CaCO₃ + 2HCl and 2Al + 6HCl. It never writes NaOH + HCl, and Mg + HCl appears only as the crossed "Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + 2H". A student who waits for the hook's beakers to be resolved does not see two of them.
- Repair (screen only, no extra narration): in scene 7, after the Mg(OH)₂ line, write small "hook: NaOH(aq) + HCl(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l)" beside the third hook beaker thumbnail. In scene 11, under the crossed chip, write the correct line "Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl₂(aq) + H₂(g)" beside the magnesium beaker.
- Visual text to replace (scene 11): "On "H two" a chip "Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + 2H" fades in and is crossed (caption "hydrogen gas is H₂")." Replacement: "On "H two" a chip "Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + 2H" fades in and is crossed; under it "Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl₂(aq) + H₂(g)" writes."
- Understanding enabled: every hook case closes, and the student sees the correct H₂ form beside the crossed one.

### S2. Should fix: vinegar clip with a hydrochloric acid equation (scene 9)

- Exact visual text: "the clip `m6-clip-baking-soda-vinegar-before-v1.png` to `-fizz-v1.png` plays small, and a second strip line writes small: "NaHCO₃(s) + HCl(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g)"."
- Student question: "Is vinegar hydrochloric acid?" The picture and equation name different acids.
- Replacement: "the clip `m6-clip-baking-soda-vinegar-before-v1.png` to `-fizz-v1.png` plays small with the tag "baking soda + vinegar: same pattern", and a second line writes small: "NaHCO₃(s) + HCl(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g)"."
- Understanding enabled: the clip shows the pattern; the equation shows the HCl case; neither is mistaken for the other.

### S3. Should fix: properties scene is a list without a cause

- Exact text (scene 5): "These names come with family properties. Acids taste sour, like lemon juice and vinegar, and they turn blue litmus **red**."
- Problem: the student hears five properties with no link to anything they can use. The author's open decision anticipates this.
- Repair: give one plain cause, then keep the litmus and reactions. Replacement for the first sentence: "These families share properties because acids give away protons and bases take them."
- Understanding enabled: the properties hang on the C01 idea, and the student sees why "react with carbonates" and "neutralise" belong in the same list.

### M1. Minor: "Pattern one ... Pattern two ... Pattern three" with ammonia in between

- Exact text (scene 8): "Now a base with **no hydroxide**."
- Students may ask whether ammonia is "pattern one" or a fourth pattern. Replacement: "Now pattern one with a base that has **no hydroxide**."

### M2. Minor: reading load in scenes 3 and 6

- Scene 3 visual: "Two columns build". Build one row on its cue and dim earlier rows to grey once the next row starts; keep only the current row bright.
- Scene 6 visual: "a strip writes: "HCl → chloride, Cl⁻ | H₂SO₄ → sulfate, SO₄²⁻ | HNO₃ → nitrate, NO₃⁻ | H₃PO₄ → phosphate, PO₄³⁻"". Let the strip shrink before the CaCl₂ demonstration starts, so the tally is the only bright item on "charges".

### M3. Minor: A1 states for nitric acid

- The question names "nitric acid" without "dilute" or a state; the answer writes HNO₃(aq). Fine for HSC. Optional: "dilute nitric acid" in the question for parallelism with A2.
