# B5b learning review: meiosis (how a cell halves its chromosome number and makes gametes that differ)

**Verdict: pass after blockers.** Two blockers, thirteen should-fix items and several notes. The design has real strengths: a concrete hook with one question; the b05a model cell reused with the same colours and centromere discs; causes before names for most terms (why gametes must be haploid, why homologues pair, what swaps before "crossing over" is named, what separates before "anaphase I"); "pair" always a homologous pair and the two copies always "sister chromatids"; "set" used only as "one of each kind" (scene 7 does this well); allele beads that make "new combinations, not new alleles" visible; a four-cell result whose letters I recomputed and which are right for both line-ups and for the no-crossing-over contrast; an unseen two-item attempt with every condition on screen and a countdown after the last word; and feedback that names wrong chains. The blockers are both vocabulary collisions that make a correct reading of the lesson's own words wrong. First, the count tracker gives a chromosome with one strand "one chromatid" in the last column, but b05a (4b) taught that a separated chromatid "is called a chromosome" and the same tracker row says "none to count" for the single-strand chromosomes in column 1. Second, scene 3 says "Alleles... are shown by letters: A and B on the long plum chromosome, a and b on its teal partner", which a student hears as A and B being two versions of one gene, so every later combination ("A b", "a B") is unreadable. The should-fix items are mostly density (scenes 5, 6 and 9), "single chromosomes" colliding with "single thread", the cause for a second division, an unqualified "different", a visual/narration mismatch in scene 13, an attempt stem that names the process, a missing wrong chain in item one, and the memory aid's "the copies".

## Memory aid check

**"Meiosis I splits the pairs. Meiosis II splits the copies."** Learner wording is "homologous pairs" and "sister chromatids", which keeps "pair" for the b05a meaning. It supports a new case (attempt item two: pairs lined up means meiosis I; the end of that division halves the number). It is true when a normal diploid cell goes through both divisions. It is false at "the copies" once crossing over has happened, so the gloss should be "the two halves of each doubled chromosome" (finding 12). It does not say what each division produces (haploid cells with doubled chromosomes after I) and says nothing about chromatid number; that stays with the tracker (blocker 1). Place it after both divisions (scene 18) and in the notes, as the design does. The design's Decisions text says it is given in scene 14; scene 14's narration does not contain it, so correct the text.

**"Mitosis keeps the number. Meiosis halves it."** True when the parent is diploid and "halves" means from two sets to one. The design uses "same chromosomes as the parent" against "half the number" in 14, which is safe for a haploid parent. Add "usually different" (finding 7). It must not be read as "half the chromosomes chosen at random": scenes 7 and 11 say one of each kind. Keep.

**Not used on purpose.** "Cross, Independently, Randomly" belongs to b08. Right call. IPMAT recalled once on the stage strip.

## Key notes (suggested wording, about 210 words, hold 75 s)

1. Meiosis turns one diploid cell (two sets) into four haploid cells (one set each). The DNA is copied once, then the cell divides twice.
2. Prophase I: homologous chromosomes pair up, and crossing over swaps matching pieces between non-sister chromatids. This makes new combinations of alleles, not new alleles. Only mutation makes new alleles.
3. Metaphase I: homologous pairs line up in the middle, each pair facing either way, independently of the other pairs (independent assortment). This also makes new combinations of alleles, not new alleles.
4. Anaphase I: homologous partners separate and the centromeres do not split. Each cell now has half the chromosome number (one set), and each chromosome still has two chromatids.
5. Meiosis II: each chromosome lines up on its own, the centromeres split and the sister chromatids separate. The result is four haploid cells, each chromosome a single strand.
6. Human cell: 46 chromosomes; after copying 46 chromosomes and 92 chromatids; 23 chromosomes in each of two cells; 23 in each of four cells. Fertilisation joins two gametes: 23 + 23 = 46.
7. With n homologous pairs, each facing either way, assortment alone gives 2^n combinations of whole chromosomes in a gamete (crossing over ignored; human 2^23, more than 8 million). Gametes from one person almost never match, but it is possible.
8. Mitosis: one division, two cells, same number, identical (barring errors). Meiosis: two divisions, four cells, half the number, usually different.
9. Meiosis I splits the homologous pairs. Meiosis II splits the sister chromatids.
