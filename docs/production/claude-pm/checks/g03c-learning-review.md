## Main question and useful outcome

*In an equilibrium calculation you have not seen, where is the trap, and what do you do first so it cannot catch you?* Afterwards a student should be able to look at an unseen question and name the first move for each trap: divide by the volume, measured value into the E row, coefficients in the change row only, solids out of K, flip or square K for a rewritten equation, perfect square or tiny K (with the 5 per cent check), convert back at the end, and reuse K at the same temperature. The video is a practice set (type 3 in the playbook), so it applies taught methods and teaches nothing new; one idea is new in practice: a mixture whose product starts above its equilibrium value runs backwards, and E minus I shows it.

## Trace from entry knowledge (Revision 2 order)

- **Hook.** "Trap" is defined in use ("a small trap early on: moles where K needs concentrations..."). The checklist gives every later scene a landmark to tick. The method strip repeats the owner order (E row first, then E - I, then the coefficients). Earns attention: the owner's framing (these questions stump students) is honest and specific.
- **Q1, iron two oxide (now first).** Every step is a callback: solids out (C5), divide first (C6a), x with a 1 : 1 change (C6b), a linear equation solved by multiplying both sides by the bottom (C6b), convert back with the 1 : 1 ratio and the molar mass (Year 11). The ghost ruler (dropping x would take 50 per cent) answers "why not just drop x?" before a student asks. The classic mistake (5.6 g, volume forgotten) is concrete and crossed with an arrow to its cause. A good warm-up: three traps, no new idea.
- **Q2, methanol (now second).** The first concrete case traced in full. Start 0.100, 0.200, 0.200 mol/L; measured 0.160 into the E row; E - I = -0.0400; the coefficients give +0.0400 and +0.0800; E row 0.140, 0.280, 0.160; K = 14.6. At each step the student can see where the number came from: the pen arrow, the "E - I" bracket, the coefficient chips. The sentence "You did not have to guess that: the subtraction gave you the sign" is the causal payoff of the owner's method. The mistake scene crosses the most likely slip (measured value written as the change) on a ghost table beside the real one, a useful contrast.
- **Q3, nitrosyl chloride.** Reversed K, then a 2 : 2 : 1 table, the tiny-K drop on 2x, a cube root and the 5 per cent check on the amount actually taken off (2x, not x). The "only just" pass at 4 per cent, plus the exact-value tag, models honest use of a rule of thumb.
- **Q4a and Q4b.** A type 1 question whose K is carried as a teal card into part b. The perfect square is hidden as (0.250 - x)(0.250 - x) and the narration says why it is a square (equal starts). The final trap (x is the change, not the answer) is the right last lesson for a practice set.
- **Decision board and key notes.** A recap of what was just done, not new reading; each row has its question badge. The notes are five short items with a copy hold, separate from the new reasoning.
