## Facts and numbers confirmed (no change)

- **C. perfringens (Q1 card, 2c, Numbers).** (opened) CDC, *C. perfringens* signs and symptoms: symptoms "usually happens within 6 to 24 hours after eating contaminated food", "can start suddenly", and "usually last about 1 day". Vomiting and fever are not listed as typical symptoms. (opened) CDC *About*: diarrhoea and stomach cramps; outbreaks most often involve "foods cooked in large batches and held at unsafe temperatures"; poultry, meat and gravy are named. The CDC pages I opened do not state the person-to-person point, though it is standard (the illness is a toxin-mediated infection after eating large numbers of vegetative cells). Codex should confirm it from the NSW Health factsheet before it goes in any learner copy. The design never says it aloud, which is fine.
- **Timing.** Monday 6 pm to Tuesday 2 to 6 am is 8 to 12 h. Tuesday 12.30 pm to Wednesday 2 to 6 am is 13.5 to 17.5 h. "About eight to eighteen hours" covers both, and both lie inside 6 to 24 h. One extra check: Monday 6 pm to Wednesday 2 to 6 am is 32 to 36 h, outside the window. The Wednesday cluster therefore cannot be late cases from the pasta, which strengthens the second-meal reading. The 4-hour blocks from Monday 6 pm put 2 to 6 am on a block boundary, as drawn.
- **Leftover chicken explains both clusters.** This is plausible and is the classic *C. perfringens* pattern. Chicken cooked in bulk and cooled slowly lets spores germinate and multiply. The same batch, held overnight and served cold in sandwiches, can carry more cells, which fits the higher attack rate (16 of 28 against 18 of 60). The salad-roll eaters also ate Monday's pasta without falling ill, so the comparison is fair. It reads as a natural part of the case, which answers author note "Camp Wollemi".
- **Q1 arithmetic.** 60 − 18 = 42 = 28 + 14. 16 ÷ 28 × 100 = 57.1 per cent, about 57 per cent. 0 ÷ 14 = 0 per cent. 18 + 16 = 34, which matches the site. Crossed chip: 16 ÷ 60 × 100 = 26.7 per cent, about 27 per cent. All correct.
- **Q2 arithmetic.** 4800 ÷ 400 000 × 1000 = 12.0. 840 ÷ 420 000 × 1000 = 2.0. 50 ÷ 2000 × 100 = 2.5 per cent. 6 ÷ 2000 × 100 = 0.3 per cent. Crossed chip: 50 ÷ 400 000 × 100 = 0.0125 per cent, and 2.5 ÷ 0.0125 = 200 (equal to 400 000 ÷ 2000). Incidence fell 83 per cent and prevalence 88 per cent. All correct.
- **Q2 science.** "Bite mostly at night" is correct (b12 check finding 5). Insecticide-treated nets reduce uncomplicated *P. falciparum* episodes by about 45 per cent compared with no nets (Cochrane, Pryce et al. 2018, CD000363, as confirmed in the b13 check). Prompt ACT treatment shortens infection and cuts gametocyte carriage, so it reduces onward transmission (WHO *Guidelines for malaria*, for Codex to open). "Could cause" matches the demand. The author's note on asymptomatic infections is right and belongs in author notes only.
- **Q3 cholera.** (opened) WHO cholera fact sheet: "Symptoms appear 12 hours to 5 days after infection"; "Most people infected with V. cholerae do not develop symptoms" and they "can spread the bacteria through their faeces for 1–10 days"; "Most people with the disease have mild or moderate symptoms"; transmission is by "consuming food or water contaminated with the bacterium". The card's "Most infected people have mild or no symptoms, but they still pass the bacteria in their faeces for up to 10 days" is a fair combination of the two WHO statements (author doubt d). It is correct.
- **WHO on quarantine.** The current fact sheet I opened **does not mention quarantine**. The Numbers line "WHO advises that measures such as quarantine ... are not effective" is not supported by the current page. It is author-note only and nothing spoken depends on it. Codex should either find the source (older WHO fact sheets and GTFCC guidance said travel and trade restrictions and quarantine are not needed for cholera control) or delete it. The model's reasoning does not need WHO's authority: the route and the carriers carry the justification.
- **Q3 graph.** Weeks 1 to 10: 4, 9, 15, 24, 33, 38, 22, 9, 3, 1. After A (week 3) cases rise 15 to 38. After B (week 6) they fall to 22 in week 7 and 1 by week 10. A 5-day quarantine matches the 5-day maximum incubation. The demand says "followed by", which correctly avoids claiming that A had no effect.
- **Chlorination and sanitation against household quarantine.** This is correct for a waterborne outbreak. Safe water, sanitation and hygiene are WHO's long-term control for cholera, and household quarantine leaves the shared water untouched. The model hedges correctly ("weaker choice for this outbreak, not that it never helps").
- **Model answers a marker would credit.** Q1(a) and (b), Q2(a) to (c) (with finding 1) and Q3(a) and (b) (with finding 3) would all earn full credit. Q1(b) at 4 marks is fair: judgement, curve and timing, the expected propagated pattern and the attack-rate comparison are four separate points (author doubt b).
- **Absolute words.** "Always divide by the people who could have caught it" is fine as a habit. "every household got treated bed nets" is a stated condition of the case. "Never found" is finding 3.

## Exam style

All three items are HSC Section II style: calculate (2 marks) plus evaluate with data (4 marks); calculate, calculate and explain (2 + 2 + 3); identify from a graph (1) plus justify (3). The verbs match the models: "evaluate" ends with a judgement, and "justify" gives reasons for a choice over an alternative.
