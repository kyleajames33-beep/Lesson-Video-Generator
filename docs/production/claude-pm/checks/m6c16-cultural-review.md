# m6c16 cultural review (chem-m6-c16, scene 8)

Reviewer: independent Claude reviewer (did not author the design). Date: 21 Oct 2026.
Scope: scene 8 of `lessons/chem-m6-c16.md` (the Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander application), key note 5, the pituri plate and the YouTube line, under the project manager's decision of 21 Oct. This is a desk review of written sources by an AI reviewer. It is not a cultural review by an Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander person and does not replace one. The handling follows `chem-m5-c12.md`: direct sources, credit to communities, no recipe, and no human figures or ceremonial items.

## Project manager decision (given)

- Do not use pituri: it is a nicotine-containing plant, which ties Aboriginal knowledge to drug use, and it raises cultural protocol concerns. Drop the pituri plate.
- If the dotpoint requires an Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander acid-base example, use the site's example if it is well documented and genuinely acid-base. Otherwise use a well-documented, non-drug, respectfully framed example with a cited source, with no plate of any person, kept short.
- If no suitable example can be documented, state the chemistry principle and note the gap for the owner. Invent nothing.

## 1. The dotpoint

Ledger `C6-IQ3-05` (NSW Chemistry Stage 6, 2017, Module 6, p53): "explore acid/base analysis techniques that are applied:" with "in industries", "by Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples", "using digital probes and instruments".

The three items are listed contexts, without "for example" (contrast `C6-IQ3-06`, "for example: soft drink, wine, juice, medicine", and the Module 5 cycad dotpoint, "for example: toxins in cycad fruit"). **The dotpoint does require the Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander application to be addressed.** It does not name a specific practice.

## 3. Outcome: principle stated, gap recorded

No suitable example could be documented, so scene 8 (Revision 2) states the documented framing and the chemistry principle, with no specific practice:

> Acid-base analysis isn't only done with glassware. The syllabus also asks about acid-base techniques used by Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples. Their chemical knowledge has been built by observing, testing and passing on the results over many generations, and it's still held today. It belongs to the communities who hold it. The cycad leaching in Module 5 is one documented example, though its chemistry is solubility. Where a practice uses acid-base chemistry, it rests on the idea from this module: a base takes protons from an acid, and that changes how a substance behaves.

Visual: tags, the existing `m5x-cycad-leaching-v1.png` thumbnail (crushed kernels in a woven bag in a stream; no people) and a generic proton-hop strip ("acid-H⁺ + base ⇌ acid⁻ + base-H⁺"). No map, no place names, no plant, no new plate and no person. Key note 5 is removed and the pituri plate is dropped from the plate list.

## 4. Sources for what the scene does say

- "built by observing, testing and passing on the results over many generations": `chem-m5-c12.md`, sourced to Asmussen (2011, Technical Reports of the Australian Museum Online 23(10)) and Beck (1992, Economic Botany 46). The same wording was used in that video's scene 7.
- "still held today": `chem-m5-c12.md` (Nungalinya College account, 2024; Beck's fieldwork at Donydji).
- "It belongs to the communities who hold it": `chem-m5-c12.md` scene 2 wording, and the site's ICIP statement (L19 card 6).
- "the cycad leaching ... its chemistry is solubility": `chem-m5-c12.md`; site new correction 15.
- "a base takes protons from an acid": Brønsted-Lowry (C06). This is a general principle, attributed to no community.

## 6. For the owner to confirm

1. **Principle-level treatment.** Accept scene 8 as written for release, knowing that it gives no specific Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander acid-base practice.
2. **Seek a community-sourced example.** For a later revision, ask an Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander educator or organisation (for example the NSW AECG, or a local Aboriginal education officer) for an acid-base practice that the community is happy to have taught, with its preferred wording and attribution.
3. **Wording review.** Ideally, an Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander reviewer reads scene 8 before voicing, as for `chem-m5-c12.md`.
4. **Site lesson 19 card 6** still presents pituri and the unsourced ash and colour-change claims. That is outside this review (the site is read-only here), but the owner may wish to align the site with the video.
5. **Do not restore pituri** without the owner's explicit OK.
