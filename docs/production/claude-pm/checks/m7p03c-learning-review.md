## Main question and the useful outcome

Main question (as stated): "Your spirit burner will catch only about half the energy of each alcohol. So how do you measure the enthalpy of combustion of several alcohols, compare them reliably anyway, and explain where the rest of the energy goes?"

Useful outcome: a student can set up and justify the spirit-burner method, calculate ΔHc with water in q and fuel in n, explain why the comparison is reliable and valid for ranking while each value is inaccurate, trace each error to q or n and to ΔH, match each improvement to its cause, and write the risk rows.

One main question: yes. It follows the syllabus verb ("measure and reliably compare") exactly, and the "about half, and that's fine" framing turns the expected low value into the reason for the lesson.

## Trace from the entry knowledge (c07, m6-c03, m6-p02, m5-p01, Year 11)

Entry: combustion equations, data values, "about half" and the burner plates (c07); q = mcΔT, ΔH = −q ÷ n, the C03 staging (m6-c03); the error chain, "not every one", extrapolation to mixing, validity, reliability and accuracy (m6-p02); variables and risk (m5-p01); n = m ÷ M. ## Findings

### S1. Should fix: the method and the data disagree about when to stop heating

- Exact text (scene 3): "Light it, stir, and heat until the water has risen **twenty degrees**. Cap the burner to put it out, keep stirring, and record the **highest** temperature." Visual: on "highest" "the readout climbs a little more and stops at '42.4 °C'". Every trial in scene 6 has a rise of exactly 20.0 °C.
- Student question: "If I cap it at a twenty degree rise and it keeps climbing, my rise is more than twenty. Which number is the controlled rise?"
- Replacement (scene 3): "Light it, stir, and heat until the water is almost **twenty degrees** warmer. Cap the burner to put it out, keep stirring, and record the **highest** temperature." (+3 characters.) Visual: the flame is capped at "41.9 °C" and the readout climbs to "42.4 °C". Step 4: "heat until almost a 20 °C rise; stir". Key note 1: "Cap just before a 20 °C rise; ΔT = highest − starting temperature."
- Understanding enabled: the student sees that the controlled rise is measured from the highest reading, and why the cap goes on slightly early.

### S2. Should fix: "the losses are similar" sits beside visibly drifting percentages

- Exact text (scene 11): "For ranking the alcohols, mostly yes: only the alcohol changes, so the losses are similar each time, and the order holds."
- Student question: "Scene 6 showed 53, 50, 49 and 45%. So the losses aren't the same. Is the ranking still valid?"
- Replacement (scene 11): "For ranking the alcohols, mostly yes: only the alcohol changes, so the losses are similar. The percentages drift down a little, but that drift is far smaller than the gaps between alcohols, so the order holds." (about +85 characters.) Visual: on "drift" the four percentage tags from scene 6 return small beside the validity row and dim.
- Understanding enabled: the student can defend validity for ranking with the evidence on screen, instead of a claim the data seem to contradict.

### M1. Minor: "and it's useful" is never cashed in

- Exact text (scene 1): "That's normal for this practical, and it's useful."
- Replacement: "That's normal for this practical." (−17 characters.) The next sentence already says the comparison can still be made.

### M2. Minor: crowded end states in scenes 6 and 8

- Scene 6: write the four percentage tags in one small grey column at the right of the graph (one item), not beside each pair.
- Scene 8: dim the accuracy header and the "systematic" tag when the amber row writes; end on the three ΔH boxes and the amber row.

### M3. Minor: scene 4's reason says "the losses"

- Exact text (scene 4): "Why the same rise? Heat escapes faster the hotter the water gets above the room, so equal rises keep the **losses** similar."
- Replacement (same as science M1): "Why the same rise? Hotter water loses heat faster, so equal rises keep the heat **lost** from the water similar." (about +5 characters; cue "lost".) See science M1 for the reason: the larger loss, hot gas passing the can, is controlled by the flame distance and the shield, which are on the controlled list already.
