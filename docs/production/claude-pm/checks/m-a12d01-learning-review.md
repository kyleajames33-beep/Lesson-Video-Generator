Main question: how do horizontal scaling, sliding and vertical multiplication change an exponential's derivative? The useful outcome is explaining and applying f′(x) = ka e^(ax+b), rather than merely copying a rule.

## Learner trace

Entry knowledge agrees with the route: e^x differentiates to itself, chain rule, constant multiple rule and exponential graphs (ciac4383bd, ci00fa8fac, cif6a6cb7d, ci98f43d14). Scene 2 briefly recalls tangent slope; it does not require a first-principles derivation. At (1,e), the tangent triangle has rise e and run 1. Squashing its run to 1/2 at the corresponding point (1/2,e) gives slope 2e. Moving to (0,1) then gives slope 2. The mirror gives a negative run and slope, not a negative exponential value. Sliding transports the same tangent without tilting it. Multiplying heights by three multiplies rises and slopes by three. The proof then recalls the chain rule and keeps u = ax+b visible. Both worked examples and attempts follow from that chain.

The hook is a useful prediction: e exceeds 2 at x=0, but the gradient-to-height factors are 1 and 2. The two attempts have the full functions, evaluation points and explanation demands, with no answer on the countdown card. Ten seconds is an invitation to pause, not enough to write a three-mark answer unaided; the existing pause instruction handles that. Feedback is about 23 and 26 seconds. The key notes are separate from new reasoning. Motion is planned to explain the mathematics; actual reading load, graph clipping, cue timing and motion require playback review.
