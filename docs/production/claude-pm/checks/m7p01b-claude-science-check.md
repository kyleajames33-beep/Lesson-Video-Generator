- **Well-plate pattern (asset brief 2b).** Row 1 column 4 (D); row 2 columns 3, 4 (C, D); row 3 columns 1, 3, 4, 7 (A, C, D, G); row 4 all. This matches the dataset.
- **Tracing logic (scene 7).** After R1, C and D are both pink and were partners, so only the start row separates them. "A and G join them, partners of C and D" is correct.
- **A1.** Starter P: Q (R1), then R and T (R2), giving P, Q, R, T. Starter Q: the same. Starter R: S, then P and U, so S and U would be pink (contradicts). Starter T: U, then Q and S (contradicts). S or U: clear, so not the starter. The answer, P or Q, is correct, and so is the (b) method change. The 12b narration matches each case. "Order on the list isn't evidence" is a fair crossed mistake.
- **A2.** The model answer is correct: it names the mechanism differences (every contact transmits, no immunity, no latent delay, uneven mixing) and keeps the valid use. The crossed mistake is fair.
- **pH values.** 0.1 mol/L Na₂CO₃: [OH⁻] = √(2.1 × 10⁻⁴ × 0.1) ≈ 4.6 × 10⁻³, pH about 11.7 (the design says 11.6; fine). 0.0125 mol/L: [OH⁻] ≈ 1.6 × 10⁻³, pH about 11.2 (correct). Both are well inside phenolphthalein's pink range and below its high-pH fading. Three drops per well hold the cup's concentration. One drop of indicator dilutes a well by about a quarter, which makes no difference at pH 11.
- **Controls.** The negative control (unmixed water stays clear: no contamination or basic water) and the positive control (base turns pink: the indicator works) are correctly reasoned. Repeating with a new starter tests reliability. Correct.
- **Limits (scene 10).** Every listed difference is correct: transmission probability, multiplication against dilution (a false negative), no latent or infectious delay, no recovery or immunity, and scheduled equal contacts. The validity verdict is correct and not absolute.
- **Indirect contact (scene 9).** Fluorescent powder on a fomite, seen under a UV-A torch, is a standard hand-hygiene demonstration. "Vectors are harder to model safely, so use secondary data" is fair. Never shining UV-A into eyes is correct advice.
- **Disposal and spills.** Drop quantities of indicator in dilute carbonate can go to the sink with water, or to waste, as the school procedure says. Correct, and properly deferred to the teacher.
- **Memory aid.** "The last test shows who; the saved samples show when; the contacts show how." Its assumptions are correctly stated in the design, including that "how" means the likely link in real tracing.
- **Absolute words.** "every contact passes it on" and "everyone meets exactly one person" describe the model and are correct. "Any cup ... turns pink" is finding 6. "Each infected person passes it on" is finding 1. No others need changing.

## Exam style

A1 is "identify ... justify" plus "propose ONE change" (3 + 1 marks) on a small pairs table. A2 is "assess" (3 marks) of a student's conclusion. Both are HSC Section II style, original, and tagged "HSC-style question, written for HSCScience". Every condition stays on screen. The models reward eliminating starters with evidence, separating P from Q with timed samples, and judging validity by mechanism. Feedback is about 26 s and 29 s, inside the brief-answer rule.
