## Verification and recalculation

NHMRC's lead fact sheet endorsed in 2025 gives 0.005 mg/L, equal to 5 micrograms/L or 5e-6 g/L. The hook is dimensionally sound. The old 0.01 mg/L value is obsolete for the current Australian guideline.

Beer-Lambert A=epsilon*l*c is dimensionless when epsilon is L mol^-1 cm^-1, l is cm and c is mol/L. 0.480/(120 x 1.00)=0.00400 mol/L. A straight calibration requires the same species, wavelength, path length, blank treatment and a tested linear range. A colourimeter's broad filter makes its effective calibration different from a tabulated monochromatic epsilon. Hydrated Cu(II)'s broad near-infrared band around 800 nm and the ammonia complex's orange-region band around 600 nm are reasonable qualitative teaching landmarks. Exact epsilon values 12 and 50 and the fourfold comparison were not independently confirmed from a fully inspected primary spectrum here; do not promote those author-only estimates as measured constants. Excess ammonia must be matched between sample and standards.

Least squares independently recomputed from all five lead points: slope 0.1618, intercept 0.0002. The endpoint gradient 0.162 is correct. 0.113/0.162=0.697531, times 250.0/25.00=10 gives 6.97531, reported 6.98 mg/L. The fitted line gives 0.697157 and 6.97157 mg/L, so the author's claim that this is the same at two significant figures is correct, not identical at three. Copper A1 slope 0.0902 and zero intercept give 0.226/0.0902=2.50554; factor 100.0/10.00=10 gives 25.0554 mg/L, 25.1 or graph-read about 25.

The lamp's hollow cathode contains the analyte element; Agilent's handbook supports Pb 217.0/283.3 nm and Cu 324.7 nm. Atomisation produces free atoms; ground-state absorption is different from flame emission. Exact detection limits depend on method and matrix; the university instrument comparison puts lead flame detection around 30 micrograms/L and furnace near 0.09 micrograms/L, illustrating why a furnace distinction matters. Multi-element lamps exist, so "one lamp means one element" is a single-element-lamp simplification, not a universal cost rule.

Flint: the EPA residence report records 397 micrograms/L on 3 March 2015, but does not prove that this single reading triggered federal action or identify its analytical method. Virginia Tech/EPA's published later study used ICP-MS. The site cannot infer AAS from the number. Log the unsupported method attribution; do not claim every Flint result used ICP-MS.

Independent machine arithmetic for 52 records is saved in `../assets/brief117-numerical-audit.json`, generated by `scripts/brief117-numbers.py`.

Primary-literature follow-up: the ACS copper-ammonia colorimetry experiment reports a calibration at 600 nm with a method-specific slope of 42.7 L mol^-1 cm^-1, supporting a value of the order of 50 and stronger absorption than hydrated copper. A primary Sensors study places the aqueous copper band near 800 nm. Neither establishes one universal exact epsilon for all reagent conditions; retain the measured calibration rather than treating the author-note constants as certified instrument values.
