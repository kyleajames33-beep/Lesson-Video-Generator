## Main question and the useful outcome

Main question (as stated): "Baking soda calms an acid, and it also calms a base. How can one ion act as a base in one reaction and an acid in another, and what are the "conjugate" partners that every acid-base reaction makes?"

One main question: yes, framed as "follow the proton both ways". Conjugate pairs (2 to 5) are the tool, amphiprotic species (6 to 8) the answer to the hook, and water (9) the same idea applied to water itself, which the route's stop names ("water's role"). Useful outcome: a student can mark the acid, base and both conjugate pairs in an unfamiliar equation, write a conjugate acid or base, write the dissolving equation and both water equations for either syllabus salt, and say what neutral means.

## Trace from the entry knowledge (student starting Year 12 Module 6)

Entry: C01 follow the proton, NH₃ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻ with "only some", baking soda making water basic; C02 ion names and carbonic acid breaking down; C05 strong and weak arrows, acetate taking protons back; Year 11 spectator ions. The trace follows the baking-soda hook.

1. Hook: fizz in acid, no fizz in hydroxide, both "calmed". A good puzzle with a visible contrast. The screen ends with two labels, two pH-bar tags, a heading and two chips (S4).
2. Run backwards: the right move from a known equation. The closing "That's why only some ammonia reacts at any moment" claims more than the sentence before it shows: a reaction that can reverse explains the double arrow, but how much reacts needs the "pull" idea from scene 5 (M1).
3. Conjugate pairs: plain meanings, one-proton and one-charge rule with the H counts lit. Good.
4. Worked example: proton followed, one pair at a time. Task line, strip, two "±H⁺" tags and two pair labels are bright together (M4). The H₂SO₄ trap is well placed.
5. Conjugate strength: **a student will ask "is HCl strong because chloride doesn't pull, or does chloride not pull because HCl is strong?"** The narration says "Hydrogen chloride is a strong acid, so its conjugate base ... has almost no pull ... That's why the reaction goes all the way", which is circular: strong, so no pull, so it goes all the way, which is what strong means (S1). "never takes it back" also contradicts C05's "almost never" (science check M1). The see-saw tag repeats the see-saw (M4).
6. Amphiprotic: both reactions with their observable outcomes (fizz from H₂CO₃ breaking down, no gas from carbonate). The amphibian comparison maps one idea to one idea. "Its useful ion" is vague (M2).
7. Proton chain: a clear spotting method, applied only to chains that exist in water (project manager decision 7). Eight small "−H⁺ →" and "← +H⁺" labels, two end labels, a tag and a crossed chip are planned; the crossed chip "a negative ion can't give a proton" is never narrated, so the student reads a misconception and its repair in silence (S3).
8. Syllabus salt: dissolving equation, then one water equation per role, both ⇌. Good structure. The screen adds a "spectator" tag, two "one step" tags pointing at a chain no longer on screen, a callback tag and a "mildly basic" tag (S5). "the base reaction wins slightly, so it's mildly basic" gives no reason; C01 already told students baking soda makes water basic, so link to that instead of leaving a bare claim (S5).
9. Water: the middle of its own chain, the conductivity evidence and the sea-of-water zoom are strong. **"always in equal numbers" is stated without its cause** (each transfer makes one of each), and the student's entry idea "neutral = pH 7" is left hanging (S2). "two water molecules in a billion are ionised" is ambiguous (science check S1). Strip, two pair labels, beaker label and two tags are bright together (S2).
10. Memory aid: three short aids, each checked; the small sub-line makes a fifth text item (M3).
11 to 14. Attempts and feedback: see below.
15 and 16. Decision rule and notes: separate from new reasoning; five useful notes.

## Findings

### S1. Should fix: scene 5 explains the arrows in a circle

- Exact text: "Hydrogen chloride is a strong acid, so its conjugate base, chloride, has almost **no pull** on a proton and never takes it back. That's why the reaction goes all the way, with a single arrow. Acetic acid is weak, and its conjugate base, acetate, has a **real pull**, so it keeps taking protons back"
- Student question: "Which causes which?"
- Replacement: "Pairs also explain the arrows from the strength video. Look at the conjugate bases. Chloride, from hydrogen chloride, has almost **no pull** on a proton, so it almost never takes one back. That's why the reaction goes all the way, with a single arrow. Acetate, from acetic acid, has a **real pull**, so it keeps taking protons back from hydronium, and you get the double arrow. So the stronger the acid, the **weaker** its conjugate base."
- Visual: remove the "strong acid ↔ very weak conjugate base" tag (the see-saw says it); the pull meters dim as the see-saw writes.
- Understanding enabled: the conjugate base's pull is the cause, the arrow is the result, and the inverse rule is the summary.

### S2. Should fix: give the cause of "equal numbers" and connect neutral to pH 7 (scene 9)

- Exact text: "only about **two water molecules in a billion** are ionised at any moment, and always in **equal numbers**. Equal amounts of hydronium and hydroxide is what **neutral** means."
- Student questions: "Why equal?" and "I learnt neutral is pH 7. Which is it?"
- Replacement: "only about **two water molecules in a billion** have given a proton away at any moment. And because each transfer makes one of each, they're always in **equal numbers**. Equal amounts of hydronium and hydroxide is what **neutral** means, and the next video links that to pH seven."
- Visual: the products pulse together as each transfer forms them; pair brackets dim when the beaker appears; the beaker label dims at its sentence end; the "2 in a billion" tag dims on "equal numbers". Bright at the end: the strip and the neutral tag.
- Understanding enabled: equal amounts follow from the mechanism, and the new definition is joined to the old one without teaching Kw here (project manager decision 1).

### S3. Should fix: scene 7 shows an unnarrated misconception and repeats step labels eight times

- Visual text: "−H⁺ →" and "← +H⁺" on every step of both chains; chip "a negative ion can't give a proton", crossed, caption "HPO₄²⁻ still has one to give".
- Repair: key arrows on the first step only; the end labels dim on "middle"; narrate the chip: "Even **hydrogen phosphate**, with its two minus charge, still has a proton to give." (about 75 characters, useful given the 5,500 floor).
- Understanding enabled: the misconception is heard and seen at the same moment, and the chain reads as species, not labels.

### S4. Should fix: the hook ends with seven text items

- Visual text: two beaker labels, pH-bar tags "less acidic" and "less basic", heading "One powder, two jobs?", chips "base here?" and "acid here?".
- Repair: the pH bars carry arrows only; the beaker labels dim on "acid". Bright: the heading and the two chips.

### S5. Should fix: scene 8 crowds the screen and leaves "mildly basic" without a reason

- Visual text: task line, line 1 with "spectator", line 2 with "acid: one step right in the chain", line 3 with "base: one step left" and "met in the models video", "NaHCO₃(aq): mildly basic".
- Problems: up to seven bright items; the "one step" tags point at a chain that has left the screen; "the base reaction wins slightly, so it's mildly basic" asserts a result the video cannot explain yet.
- Repair: a small mini chain "H₂CO₃ ⇄ HCO₃⁻ ⇄ CO₃²⁻" at the top right, whose steps light amber and violet as lines 2 and 3 write, in place of both step tags; remove the callback tag; the task line and line 1 dim on "acid"; the mini chain dims on "mildly basic". Narration: "In baking soda solution the base reaction goes slightly further, which is why baking soda makes water **mildly basic**, as the models video said." The reason it goes further stays out (Ka and Kb are chem-m6-c10; salt hydrolysis is C13 or C14, decision 2).

### M1. Minor: scene 2's closing sentence overclaims

- Exact text: "That's why only some ammonia reacts at any moment."
- Replacement: "The proton keeps swapping both ways, which is why this equation has the double arrow."

### M2. Minor: "Its useful ion" (scene 6)

- Replacement: "The ion that does the work is **hydrogen carbonate**".

### M3. Minor: scene 10's small sub-line is a fifth text item

- Repair: remove "acid − H⁺ → conjugate base; base + H⁺ → conjugate acid (charge changes by 1)"; the narration says it and key note 1 holds it.

### M4. Minor: scenes 4 and 5 hold one item too many

- Scene 4: the task line dims once the strip writes; fold "−H⁺" and "+H⁺" into the bracket labels. Scene 5: see S1.

### M5. Minor: feedback 1 has tags as well as colours

- Repair: drop the "acid" and "base" tags on the two role lines (amber and violet carry them, as in scene 8); keep the thumbnail chain, which shows the two-step mistake.
