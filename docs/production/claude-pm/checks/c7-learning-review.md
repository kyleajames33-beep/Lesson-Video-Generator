## Key notes (suggested wording)

1. Equilibrium yield is how much of the reactants has become product once the mixture has settled. The rate decides how soon the mixture gets there. A plant needs both, because it makes product in a limited time.
2. If the forward reaction gives out heat, cooling raises the equilibrium yield but lowers both rates, because fewer collisions have enough energy. Industry uses a moderate temperature and a catalyst. Haber: about 400 to 450 degrees Celsius with iron.
3. A catalyst makes the mixture settle sooner but does not change the settled mixture. By making a moderate temperature fast enough, it lets the plant avoid a much hotter reactor that would lower the yield.
4. Higher pressure raises the yield when there are fewer gas particles on the product side, and it raises the rate by crowding the particles. Cost and safety limit it. Haber: about 150 to 250 atmospheres, not 1000.
5. Removing the product (condensing ammonia) shifts the mixture forward, and recycling the unused reactants raises the overall conversion (Haber: above 95 percent).
6. To justify a condition, give the effect on the yield with its reason, the effect on the rate with its reason, and what limits it.

Aid (large): *Haber is a compromise: yield asks for cold, speed asks for hot.* Second line: *Justify with three things: yield, rate, limit.* Tag: "forward reaction gives out heat and is too slow when cold. If it takes in heat, heat helps both." (Six bullets are kept here; merge 5 into 4 if the hold must stay near 70 seconds, or keep six with a 90 second hold.)
