## Findings

1. **Blocker, 8a session 1 is marked HIIT, but its ratio breaks the video's own HIIT rule, and v04 calls the same session aerobic intervals.** Scene 3 teaches HIIT as efforts of "thirty seconds to four minutes, with only one to one or one to two" (rest equal to or twice the work), and the key note copies "HIIT: 30 s to 4 min, 1:1 to 1:2". 8a session 1 is 6 × 4 min with 2 min jogging, which is 2:1 (rest half the work), and the card demands a justification "with reference to the work-to-rest ratio". A student who applies the taught rule cannot reach "HIIT" from the ratio; the feedback then says "about two to one" as if it fitted. Worse, v04 scene 3 and 8a define aerobic interval training as "five four-minute efforts near the threshold" with 2 to 3 min recovery, so the same session is a different method one video later. Fix: replace session 1 with a clean HIIT case, "8 × 60 s hard, 60 to 90 s jogging between" (1:1 to 1:1.5), and feedback line 1 "1 HIIT (about 1:1, incomplete rest)"; say "about one to one, so recovery stays incomplete". Students should be able to name every method from the ratio the video taught, and v04's aerobic intervals stay distinct (see the v04 review).

2. **Should fix, 8c's "beaten off the first step in the second half" points to fatigue, but the marking accepts only peak-power methods.** "In the second half" tells a careful student that the guard is fine early and fades late. That is a repeated-sprint problem, which scene 3 says HIIT trains (incomplete recovery, aerobic and anaerobic power together). The feedback recommends SIT with full rest and crosses "more running to fix the second half", so a student who proposes short-rest repeated sprints for late-game fade has applied the video correctly and loses marks. Fix, either: remove "in the second half" from the stem and narration ("and is slow off the first step"), or add to the marking "repeated-sprint HIIT (for example 6 × 6 s with 20 to 30 s rest) accepted for late-game fade, if justified". The first is cleaner. Students should be able to read the profile and get one defensible answer from the video's own rules.

3. **Note, scene 2 recalls more Year 11 than one sentence.** The duration bands (ATP-PC to about 10 s, glycolysis 10 s to 2 min, aerobic beyond) are Year 11 content, restated here with a draining PCr gauge and hydrogen ion dots. This is defensible because the training rule needs them on screen, and each is framed as a rule for training; keep it, but add the grey "earlier" tag to the ruler as well as to the table and dial.

4. **Note, SIT's tag "trains: peak power, ATP-PC".** SIT efforts run to 30 s, which the scene 2 ruler places in glycolysis. For the science check: say "peak power and repeated maximal sprints" and drop "ATP-PC" from the tag, or limit the ATP-PC tag to efforts under about 10 s.

5. **Note, place the spring on the tendon, not the bone.** On the painted calf plate the Achilles tendon is the narrow white band at the lower left where the muscle bellies converge; the pale vertical strip at the right is bone. Write the build instruction as "the teal spring on the tendon band at the plate's lower left" so the spring lands on the structure that stores the energy.
