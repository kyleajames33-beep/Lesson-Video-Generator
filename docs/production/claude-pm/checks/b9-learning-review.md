## Main question and useful outcome

*Two tall pea plants have a short offspring: how does that happen, how do we predict it, and how can a family tree show whether a trait is dominant or recessive?* Afterwards a student should be able to explain dominance with the protein reason, build a 2 x 2 square and read 1 : 2 : 1 and 3 : 1 as chances per offspring, use a test cross, read pedigree symbols, recognise the two decisive autosomal patterns with named individuals, and turn a pedigree into a probability.

## Designer's open questions, decided

- **Ten new terms:** accepted. They are the dotpoint's core vocabulary, each glossed in a short clause at first use, and after L1 they are spread across six scenes (dominant and recessive in 2; homozygous and heterozygous in 3; Punnett square in 4; test cross in 6; pedigree and autosome in 7; carrier in 8). Genotype and phenotype are recalled from B7, not new.
- **Pedigrees as "patterns" not proofs:** decided as follows. The narration teaches two kinds of evidence: *clues* (every generation, affected parent; skipping a generation) that suggest a mode, and *giveaways* (unaffected parents with an affected child; affected parents with an unaffected child) that decide it under the stated assumptions (one gene, full expression, no new mutation; the on-screen tag). Scene 9 now says "clues"; scene 8's and 10's giveaways are genuinely decisive (checked by brute force in the science check), so their wording stays. No scene claims P2 alone proves dominance.
- **"Individual 1 in generation two" in speech:** keep; it is how HSC answers cite individuals, and the symbol rings on its cue.
- **Huntington's (late onset):** keep, with the existing assumption tag; the bank's trap uses it.
- **Dihybrid crosses:** owner note: no route entry; flag to the board if wanted.

## Retained successful moves

The protein reason for dominance instead of a metaphor; chips flying from gamete discs into boxes with plum and teal origin; the seed replay and the 42 per cent bar; the inset trio carrying the dominant giveaway; the model answer that names II-3, II-4 and III-1.
