## Blocker

**B1. Scene 6's "cut that doesn't help" is false: both pieces of that diagonal cut have known heights.** The narration says a line from the front-left corner to the back-right corner "splits the house block into two triangles, but neither one has a height you know". With the block's corners at front-left (0, 0), front-right (20, 0), back-right (20, 30) and back-left (0, 40):
- Triangle 1 (front-left, front-right, back-right) has a right angle at the front-right corner, which is marked on the plan. Its legs are 20 m and 30 m, so its area is ½ × 20 × 30 = 300 m².
- Triangle 2 (front-left, back-right, back-left) has the 40 m left side as a base, and the back-right corner is 20 m from that side, measured at right angles (the block's width). Its area is ½ × 40 × 20 = 400 m².
- 300 + 400 = 700 m², the same answer again.
A student who looks at the plan can see these heights, and the lesson then tells them a false statement about the very skill it teaches. Fix: rewrite scene 6 so the diagonal cut becomes a third check, not a bad example. Narration, for example: "What about a diagonal cut, corner to corner? It still works, as long as you use perpendicular heights. This triangle has a right angle at the front, so its area is half of twenty times thirty, three hundred. This one has the forty metre side as its base, and its height is the block's width, twenty, measured at right angles. That's four hundred. Seven hundred again. The slip is using a slanted edge, like the twenty-two point three six metre back fence, as if it were a height." Keep the crossed "½ × 22.36 × 40" with the caption "a slanted edge isn't a height". Change the scene's hand tag from "no known height" to "find the perpendicular height", and Decisions' "A cut that creates a piece with no known perpendicular height is shown and crossed" to match. Key note 2 ("Cut at right angles so every piece has a known base and height") stays as advice.

## Should fix

Scene 5 never gives the apex's position, so the two slanted sides' legs (80 m and 40 m) appear from nowhere.** The narration says the slanted sides "come out at ninety-one point seven nine and sixty point two one" and the Visual places the apex "40 m in from the left" without a label being specified. A student cannot see where 80 (that is, 120 − 40) and 40 come from. Fix: label "40 m" from the left corner to the foot of the dashed height on the plan, and say: "The apex is forty metres in from the left, so the right-hand piece is a hundred and twenty minus forty, eighty metres wide." Show the two Pythagoras rows one at a time on their cues.
