## Reactions and conditions

- HX adds to an alkene at room temperature without a catalyst (Q2a): standard HSC treatment (c04a).
- Alkane chlorination needs UV light; no reaction in the dark (Q2b, Q3): Chemguide ("There is no reaction in the dark"; UV or sunlight).
- "In the dark ... so this isn't substitution" (Q3): correct for the given conditions, and the single organic product with no HCl named is consistent.
- Chlorine adding to an alkyne in two steps at room temperature in the dark (Q3): correct as stated chemistry. In practice, chlorine and ethyne are usually reacted with an iron(III) chloride catalyst or in a solvent, and gas-phase mixtures with light are hazardous; the stem states the result as a given, which is acceptable for an HSC-style item (M2, author note only).
- "Bromine water shows unsaturation: a double or a triple bond" (Q4): correct.

## Absolute claims

- "Adding twice needs a **triple** bond" (4b), with key note 4 "2X₂ means a triple bond" and model answer 3 "which needs a triple bond": false in general (two C=C also add 2X₂; buta-1,3-diene is C₄H₆). The answer stays right because of the positions in 4c. S1.
- Key note 4 "the formula decides: alkene CnH2n, alkyne CnH2n−2": needs "open chain, one multiple bond" (dienes, cycloalkenes). S2.
- "Each carbon in a C=C has one fewer H" (key note 1): true relative to the same carbon in the saturated chain; acceptable.
- "A double bond can open only once" (4b): true.
- "the double bond is named in the ending, so it gets first claim on the low numbers" (2d): true under IUPAC rules for hydrocarbons.
- No other absolute claims found.

## Findings

### S1. Should fix: "Adding twice needs a triple bond" (4b, key note 4, model answer 3)

- Exact text (4b): "A double bond can open only once, so it would add one chlorine molecule. Adding twice needs a **triple** bond."
- Evidence: buta-1,3-diene (C₄H₆) adds two Cl₂ (to 1,2,3,4-tetrachlorobutane); the design's own Numbers to verify lists it. The positions, not the count, prove the triple bond.
- Replacement (4b): "A double bond can open only once, so it would add one chlorine molecule. Adding twice needs two extra bonds: a **triple** bond, or two double bonds."
- Replacement (4c): "So both additions happened across the same pair of carbons. That needs a triple bond, between carbon **one** and carbon two."
- Key note 4 and model answer 3: as in learning S1.

### S2. Should fix: key note 4's formula rule needs its condition

- Exact text: "Bromine water shows unsaturation; the formula decides: alkene CnH2n, alkyne CnH2n−2."
- Replacement: "Bromine water shows unsaturation; for an open-chain hydrocarbon with one multiple bond, the formula decides: alkene CnH2n, alkyne CnH2n−2."

### M1. Minor: Q4's "rapidly" and "quickly"

- Alkynes add bromine much more slowly than alkenes (Fahey review figures, about 10² to 5 × 10⁴ times slower in acetic acid). Replace with "at room temperature" in both card and narration (learning S3).

### M2. Minor: author note for Q3's conditions

- Add to Review notes (a): "Chlorine and alkynes are normally reacted in a solvent or with an iron(III) chloride catalyst; gas-phase mixtures can react violently in light. The stem states the result as a given ('reacts with excess chlorine ... in the dark'), which is acceptable for an HSC-style item; do not show apparatus."

### M3. Minor: dotpoint codes in the description

- Match the ledger: "... including: (ACSCH127) alkanes, alkenes, alkynes, alcohols (primary, secondary and tertiary), aldehydes and ketones, carboxylic acids, amines and amides, halogenated organic compounds" and "... including: (ACSCH035) chain isomers, position isomers, functional group isomers". Root to confirm against the c03 precedent, which keeps the code after the stem.

### M4. Minor: author note on Q1's drawn geometry

- Hex-2-ene and 4-methylpent-2-ene each have E and Z forms; a zigzag skeletal drawing shows one of them. Add to Review notes: "Draw both as the E (trans) zigzag. HSC names do not require E/Z; the answer box names without a stereodescriptor." No learner change.
