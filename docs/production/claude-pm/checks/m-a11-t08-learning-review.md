**Verdict: should-fix** (0 blockers, 1 should fix, 2 optional).

The string round the cake tin makes π a count: half way round the rim the string has been laid three point one four times, and "the circumference is two pi r, so it holds two pi radius-lengths, on any circle" gives the reason. The radian is then defined as "how many radius-lengths of arc it cuts off", with S11-11's corrected wording, and a second circle shows the ratio does not change with size. 360° = 2π follows from the full turn's arc in one line, and both conversion factors follow from 180° = π. "The number in degrees is always the bigger one" is a useful self-check that the crossed slip uses. Scene 2's pizza fractions are a fact "whatever unit you use", which sets up t09 without teaching l = rθ. 2. **Optional, scene 3 ("a radian has no units").** Every spoken value in the video then says "radians", which can sound like a contradiction. Reword: "Since it's a length divided by a length, a radian measure is a pure number. We often say 'radians' to be clear, but the number doesn't depend on centimetres or metres." The tag "no units" can stay.
