## Notes (no change required to record)

1. **Units of K.** Omitting the unit is the HSC convention and follows C5. The substitution lines show bare numbers while the table headings show mol/L, and for PCl5 and N2O4 the unit would not cancel. Add a quiet tag at the first substitution, "K: no unit written", so the question is answered where it is asked. No spoken sentence needed.
2. **C5 dependency.** Scene 4 reads 0.053 against the C5 band scale and scene 6b calls the recovered tube "the same settled tube". C5's own blocker (the "real mix" wording against the 2, 2, 14 picture) must be resolved first. If C5 ends with "above about a thousand, almost all products; below about a thousandth, almost all reactants; in between, measurable amounts of both", the finding 10 sentence is right. If C5 changes the thresholds, change this sentence to match.
3. **Wrong answers not named.** "I used the 0.0300 as x" gives 0.18 and "I used moles" gives 0.0106. Both are plausible. The first is the more likely and is what the line "NO2 rose by 0.0300" in 10a is there to prevent. Leave as is, or add one clause: "If you got about 0.18, the 0.0300 is the change in N O two itself, not the amount of N two O four that reacted." (+22 words, a cut candidate if the preview runs long.)
4. **Site numbers.** The video replaces the site's SO2, H2 + I2 (K = 54.3, start 0.100) and PCl5 (K = 65.0 for formation) numbers on purpose, to stay with the C2 flask and the C4b tube. A student comparing screens will see different values and the same method. The PCl5 value is the reverse direction of the site's example (1 / 65.0 is 0.0154, against the video's 0.053) and the site correction list does not include this. Record it with the owner. The "illustrative values" chip is the right on-screen answer; keep it.
5. **The checks are partly circular.** Both the phosphorus sum (0.12 + 0.080 = 0.20) and 0.0350 + 0.0300 / 2 = 0.0500 follow from how the change row was built, so they confirm the subtraction, not the coefficient logic. That is fine for a first table; the real check for the K-given case is the back-substitution in 6b, which is independent. Say so once: "This checks the subtraction" in scene 4.
6. **"Multiply across" (6b).** Plain wording is "multiply both sides by 0.45 minus x". Same words, no ambiguity about what is multiplied. (+3 words.)
7. **Catalyst video reference (scene 5).** K was named in C5, not in the catalyst video, so "the hot tube from the catalyst video, where K is 49" reads as if the catalyst video gave K. Finding 8's wording ("from the last two videos") fixes it.
8. **Numbers spoken aloud.** "0.0300 squared is 0.000900, over 0.0350" (10a) and "0.2401 over 0.0001" are hard to hear. On screen they are fine. If the voiced preview stumbles, the TTS can say "nine ten-thousandths" for 0.000900 or read the numbers as written; check in the preview.

With the repairs (attempt hold 100 s, notes hold 70 s, a 10 s hold in 6b, doubling cut) the design lands near 9.2 minutes. If the preview exceeds 10 minutes, the cut order in the design stands.
10. **Colour realism.** "Chlorine is yellow-green, so its colour tells us how much" is a teaching device (the PCl5 flask at 250 C also has colour). The hook says it is one measured value, which is enough. Codex to confirm and the p02 video owns the real method.
11. **N2O4 K.** A K of about 0.026 for N2O4 ⇌ 2NO2 is plausible near 40 C, well above the room temperature value (about 0.005). Because the attempt gives no temperature the value cannot be contradicted by a table. Codex to confirm as illustrative.
12. **Vessel tint.** The attempt vignette shows a pale gas and a brown tint. A tint implies NO2 is already present at the start; make the starting vessel pale and let the tint appear only in the feedback, so the picture agrees with "start: no NO2".

## Memory aid check

"Only the change row follows the coefficients." It is correct for all three cases under its stated assumptions (one balanced equation, concentrations in mol/L, the net reaction running forward from the left). Change rows: 1 : 1 : 1 (ex 1), 1 : 1 : 2 (ex 2, -0.35, -0.35, +0.70), 1 : 2 (attempt, -0.0150, +0.0300). It prevents the two errors the feedback names (0.018 and 0.045). It helps a new case: a Haber-type start of N2 and H2 gives -x, -3x, +2x with an unrelated initial row. Limits to keep in the record: (a) the equilibrium row is not "any numbers" because K fixes it (finding 1); (b) cells can look like the ratio by coincidence when two reactants start equal; (c) if the net reaction runs in reverse, the signs flip and the sizes still follow the coefficients; (d) it should not be applied to the equilibrium row to check the answer. "Work in concentrations, not moles" (scene 2, key note 1) is carried by the conversion, and its trap is tested by item one. "Flip the equation, flip K. Multiply the equation, power K." holds for the lesson's tube (1/49 and 49^2) at one temperature and for the equation as written; if the doubling is cut (finding 7), keep the second sentence in the notes only, with the conditions. Neither aid is presented as the explanation.

## Key notes (suggested wording)

1. An ICE table has a column for each substance and three rows: Initial, Change and Equilibrium. Work in concentrations: concentration (mol/L) = moles divided by litres.
2. The change row follows the coefficients of the balanced equation. A substance used up is minus and a substance made is plus. The initial and equilibrium rows need not follow the coefficients. Equilibrium = initial + change.
3. To find K: write the expression, fill the table from the start and the one measured value, substitute the equilibrium values (never the initial ones), and check the subtraction.
4. To find a concentration from K: call the change x, write the change row by the coefficients, substitute and solve. The square root shortcut works only when both sides are perfect squares, for example H2 and I2 starting equal. Other starts need a method from a later video. Keep the positive root, and check that no concentration comes out negative.
5. K belongs to the equation as written, at one temperature. Reverse the equation: K becomes 1 / K. Multiply every coefficient by n: K goes to the power n (halving gives the square root).

Aid (large): *Only the change row follows the coefficients.* Second line: *Flip the equation, flip K. Multiply the equation, power K.* Tag: "used up is minus, made is plus; same temperature, equation as written."
