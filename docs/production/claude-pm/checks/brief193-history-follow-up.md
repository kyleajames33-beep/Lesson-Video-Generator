# Brief193 primary history follow-up

7 November 2026. Supplements the v20 source review. No lesson changes.

- SLAC's institutional timeline places the electron-proton experiments in 1967 and the constituent result in 1968. Keep the late-1960s description rather than treating publication and first running as one date. [SLAC timeline](https://www6.slac.stanford.edu/about/our-story/history/slac-timeline), [Fermilab historical account](https://www.fnal.gov/pub/science/inquiring/matter/ww_discoveries/index.html).
- Muon discovery in 1936 is supported by CERN's timeline. Other accounts give the 1937 publication date. Do not flag the design's 1936 as an error. [CERN timeline](https://drupal-timeline.web.cern.ch/anderson-and-neddermeyer-discover-muon), [Anderson's Caltech account](https://calteches.library.caltech.edu/558/2/Anderson.pdf).
- Pion discovery in 1947 agrees with Powell's autobiographical account. The generated chamber plate is illustrative apparatus, not the historical observation. [Powell account, Bristol archive](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/physics/media/histories/12-powell.pdf).
- Gell-Mann and Zweig independently proposed the quark model in 1964. The site's single attribution is logged as an addition to its existing historical clause. [CERN account](https://home.cern/who-invented-quarks/).
- Reines and Cowan's 1956 reactor detection is supported by their contemporary report. [Nature report](https://www.nature.com/articles/178446a0).
- Anderson's positron discovery is 1932. Distinguish discovery from the later detailed publication and Nobel award. The direction argument requires the known slowing through the lead plate; a curve alone does not fix charge without direction and magnetic-field direction. [APS historical account](https://www.aps.org/apsnews/2004/08/discovery-positron-1932).
