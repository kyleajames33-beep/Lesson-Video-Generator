## bio-m6-b03: point mutations

1. **Scene 1 and YouTube description, "nothing happens at all".** The GAG to GAA example retains glutamate, but a synonymous change is not proof that nothing else changes. Synonymous substitutions can change RNA splicing and protein production. **Correct text:** "Change a different base in that same codon, and the amino acid stays the same." In the description, replace "nothing at all" with "the same amino acid". Source: [CFTR synonymous substitution with altered splicing, primary study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2860476/).
2. **Scenes 3 and 8, "A substitution changes just one codon" and "A substitution changes one codon".** This is true for a substitution within an ordinary coding codon, but a base substitution can alter a splice signal or start codon and change much more of the protein. The blanket rule also conflicts with the lesson's point that position matters. **Correct text:** "A substitution within a coding codon changes that codon; read the new codon to see whether it specifies the same amino acid, a different one or a stop." Source: [primary functional study of synonymous CFTR substitutions that alter splicing](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3440543/).
3. **Scene 5, "The third kind is the most drastic substitution."** A nonsense substitution can be severe, but its effect depends on its position and the gene; it is not always more drastic than a missense substitution. **Correct text:** "A third kind of substitution creates an early stop." Source: [primary study showing that the position of a nonsense mutation can predict differing severity](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7437896/).

## bio-m6-b04: chromosomal mutations

1. **Scene 9 comparison board, effect row "one protein" for point mutations.** B03 has already shown a silent point mutation that leaves the amino acid sequence unchanged, so the row incorrectly implies every point mutation changes a protein. **Correct text:** "one gene; protein may stay the same or change". Source: [primary study of synonymous CFTR substitutions and their varied effects](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3440543/).
2. **Scene 10 narration and visual, "A gene that's flipped still reads normally" and "a flipped gene still reads from its own start".** This is too absolute. An inversion can break a gene or separate it from regulatory DNA, changing its expression even without a loss of chromosome material. **Correct text:** "An intact gene inside the flipped segment can still be read from its own start. A break or changed control region can affect it." Source: [primary study of a human inversion that disrupted a gene and changed expression](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4591017/).
