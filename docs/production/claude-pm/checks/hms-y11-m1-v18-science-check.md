# hms-y11-m1-v18 science check (localising the SDGs)

Revision 2 design checked 24 November 2026 (source-only: the Revision 2 section, script, practice, key notes and the design's facts section; no build, audio or playback). Independent science and fact check; the design was not edited.

## Confirmed
- "the United Nations itself points out that most of the targets can't be reached without local government" (scene 2): UN-Habitat states that "the majority of the SDGs' targets cannot be reached without proper engagement and coordination with Local and Regional Governments" (UN-Habitat written input to CEPA 2023); the OECD (*A Territorial Approach to the Sustainable Development Goals*, 2020) puts it at at least 105 of 169 targets. The spoken claim is correct.
- Localisation definition (scene 2, key note 1): consistent with UN-Habitat ("transforming the SDGs into reality at local level", coherent with national frameworks and local priorities).
- "some Australian councils publish their own review" (scene 2): City of Melbourne Voluntary Local Review (2022), Brisbane City Council VLR (2023, with UN-Habitat) and the Hunter Region VLR (2024). Correct.
- Council functions (scene 2, key note 2): typical; "food and water safety" is fair as a general list, because many regional NSW councils are local water utilities and councils inspect food premises.
- Impact staircase applied (scene 6, Revision 2) and the "5 of 5" readout (root fix): consistent.
- SDG-washing (scene 3, 9a/9b): a recognised term; definition fair.
- Practice 9a/9b and 9c/9d: correct; no reasonable alternative crossed.
- No Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander content.

## Findings
1. **Note (facts section only).** The design's facts list says "around 65% of the 169 targets cannot be achieved without local and regional government involvement". The UN inter-agency figure of at least 65 per cent describes targets "linked to the work and mandates of local and regional governments"; the "cannot be reached without" figure is the OECD's at least 105 of 169 (about 62 per cent) or UN-Habitat's "the majority". Correct the source note; the narration ("most of the targets") stays true.

Pass. 0 blockers, 0 should fix, 1 note.
