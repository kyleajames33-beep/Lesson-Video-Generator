## Findings

1. **Should fix: flame emission is assigned to metal ions.** Scene 6 says "Heat lifts electrons in the metal ions". Common flame-test emission arises from atoms, with some molecular emitters, rather than simply the electron structure of the aqueous ion. Replace the clause with: "Heat excites electrons in the sample's atoms; as they fall back, they emit light characteristic of the element." [RSC explanation](https://edu.rsc.org/infographics/how-to-carry-out-flame-tests/4024075.article).

2. **Should fix: silver is said to form a hydroxide solid.** Scene 9 says "Silver and lead would make hydroxide solids too". Silver gives Ag2O under these aqueous conditions. Replace with "Silver and lead would form solids with hydroxide too". The silver reaction is 2Ag+ + 2OH- -> Ag2O(s) + H2O. [UC Riverside research](https://mrzgroup.ucr.edu/sites/default/files/2019-02/2013_cf_ag2o.pdf).

3. **Should fix: a numerical author note uses formal sulfuric acid concentration as free sulfate.** Note (a) gives Q = 0.05 x 0.5 = 0.025 after equal mixing of 0.1 mol/L Ca2+ and 1 mol/L H2SO4. Much sulfate remains HSO4(-), so 0.5 mol/L is not free SO4(2-). The concentrated mixture can still precipitate CaSO4. Replace H2SO4 with 1 mol/L Na2SO4 in that numerical example, making the stated simple Q calculation valid. Author-only correction; no narration addition. [Official Chemistry acid-equilibrium syllabus](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/noindex/2025-03/chemistry-stage6-syllabus-word.docx).

4. **Should fix: PbCl2 is on the NESA data sheet.** The author note/Numbers to verify say otherwise. The official 2023 sheet, PDF page 41 (sheet page 1), visually inspected, prints 1.70e-5. Correct its attribution to NESA. [Official paper and data sheet](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/noindex/2025-05/2023-hsc-chemistry_20240318.pdf).

5. **Should fix: no chloride precipitate does not exclude dilute lead.** See learning finding 1 and its exact replacement. Barium remains the correct answer because of the pale-green flame. Count this once across reviews.

The hidden-condition and mixture findings are counted in the learning review, not again here.
