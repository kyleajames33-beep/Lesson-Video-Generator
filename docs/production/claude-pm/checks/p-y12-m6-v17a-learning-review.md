## Should fix

**V17A-RON, scene 11 and attempt 2, "At switch on, before it turns, the ammeter reads three amps."** A student copying this method cannot do it: a hobby motor reaches speed in a fraction of a second, and a digital multimeter updates a few times a second, so the switch-on current is a flick the meter will not catch. It also brings back the "literal instant" wording that v17's review flagged (the coil's inductance from v12 makes the current rise over a short time; v17 now says "just after the switch closes, before the coil has turned much"). The workable bench method is to hold the rotor still and read a steady current. Replacement scene 11 narration (530 characters, about the same as now): "Last, the current. Let's run our motor on six volts. First, hold the flywheel still with the wooden block and switch on for a moment. The ammeter reads three amps. The coil isn't turning, so there's no back emf, and its resistance is six over three, two ohms. Now let it go. As it speeds up, the current falls to zero point eight zero amps. From last time, back emf is V minus I R, six minus zero point eight zero times two, which is four point four **volts**. Press the block on again, and the current climbs back towards three amps." Keep "Only for a moment, or the coil **heats**." if length allows; otherwise move "only for a moment" to the screen tag. Re-cue the visual (block presses first, then withdraws on "let it go"). Attempt 2 card: "When the shaft is held still, the current is 2.4 A." and its narration "With the shaft held still, the current is two point four amps."; feedback 2: "With the shaft held still, the coil isn't turning, so there's no back emf ..."; key note 5: "Held still: R = V ÷ I. Running: ε_back = V − IR." Students should understand that R is measured with the coil stopped because only then is the back emf zero, and that this is a reading they can actually take.

**V17A-PAIR, scenes 4 and 6, the same pair 0.78 and 0.76.** Scene 4 says "half the difference" of +0.78 and −0.76 is 0.77; scene 6 uses 0.78 and 0.76 again and halves their difference to get ±0.01. A student hears "half the difference" of the same two numbers give 0.77 and then 0.01. Scene 4 also never says why the drift cancels (the cause chain item 3 is only in the visual). Change the reversal pair to +0.79 g and −0.75 g (drift +0.02 g; average of sizes still 0.77 g) and say the reason. Replacement (from "Then reverse the current."): "Then reverse the current. The reading should drop below zero by the same amount. Say it reads plus zero point seven nine, then minus zero point seven five. A drift in the zero adds the same small amount to both, so average their sizes. That's zero point seven seven, and the drift **cancels**." Update the reading-pair strip, the drift tag ("zero drift: +0.02 g (illustrative values)"), the algebra "(0.79 + 0.75) ÷ 2 = 0.77 g" and the Numbers tables. Students should understand that a constant offset makes one reading too big and the other too small by the same amount.

**V17A-RHEO, scene 10, "Turn the rheostat to raise the current, and the coil picks up speed more quickly."** Students have just learned in v17 that a running motor's steady current is set by its load, not by the supply. The visual shows the ammeter stepping from 1.0 to 2.0 A while the coil is already spinning, which a careful v17 student will see as a contradiction (turning the rheostat down makes it run faster at about the same steady current). The torque comparison only works from rest. Replacement: "Turn the rheostat down and start it again from rest. More current flows, and the coil picks up speed more quickly. More current means more torque, because torque equals n I A B sine theta." Visual: two starts from rest with starting readings 1.0 A and 2.0 A (illustrative), matching the existing two spin-up curves; no steady step on the running ammeter. Students should connect larger current with larger torque without contradicting back emf.

**V17A-AID, scene 12 memory aid "Push the wire, and the magnet gets pushed back."** Scene 11 shows v17's recall tag "No spin, no push back." where "push back" means the back emf; about forty seconds later "pushed back" means the third-law reaction. Two meanings of the same phrase in consecutive scenes invites a student to link the balance reading to back emf. It also blurs who pushes whom (the field, or the wire?). Replacement aid (scene 12, key-notes card and Memory aid check): "So here's the idea that makes the balance work. The magnet pushes the wire, and the wire pushes the **magnet**." Card: amber "The magnet pushes the wire," navy "and the wire pushes the magnet." with "Δm × g = F" under it. Assumptions as already checked (wire fixed, equal and opposite; the palm rule gives the direction). Students should be able to name both members of the force pair.

**V17A-MIRROR, scene 9 visual, "the motor plate mirrors left to right".** Mirroring the plate flips the whole model (coil, split ring, brushes and terminals change sides), so a student sees the motor flip rather than two magnets change places, while the narration says "Swap the magnets". The changed part should be the only thing that moves (v18 uses exactly this rule for its ring swap). Keep the plate unmirrored; show the swap on the plate with coded outline tags "N" and "S" over the two magnets that slide past each other and trade places (or ask Codex for the magnets as separate layers in a later plate pass), and keep the schematic's N and S swap. Students should see that only the field was reversed.

**V17A-VALID, scenes 3 and 6, validity and reliability never named.** The site's lesson 07 "Assess 6 marks" on this exact rig is built on the two criteria ("The commonest failure is to use validity and reliability as synonyms for good"), and HSC practical questions ask for them. The video teaches both ideas but never attaches the words, and it omits the site's specific threat to validity (the wire sagging or twisting, so θ changes). Scene 3, replace "and the wire square to the magnet, so the angle stays at ninety **degrees**." with "and the wire clamped square to the magnet, so it can't sag or twist, and the angle stays at ninety **degrees**. That's what makes it a valid test." Scene 6, replace "Bigger readings are more **trustworthy**." with "Close repeats make the results reliable, and bigger readings help **most**." (re-cue the hand tag). Optional screen tags "valid: only I changes" (3) and "reliable: repeats agree" (6). Students should be able to answer "assess the validity and reliability" with the right idea under each word.

**V17A-GAP, scene 8 visual and the cradle brief.** The top view turns the magnet to φ = 40° with the wire fixed. With a 5.0 cm pole-face length the wire only leaves through the field region's ends (the design's geometry) if the gap between the magnets is at least L tan φ: 1.8 cm at 20°, 4.2 cm at 40°. A typical school cradle gap is narrower, and the Codex cradle brief does not set it, so the top view risks showing the wire passing through a magnet, which a student will notice. Either draw the top view (and brief the plate) with a gap of at least 4.5 cm, matching scene 1's cradle, or stop the turn at 20° and 30° (gap at least 2.9 cm) with the readouts recalculated. Leave the physics of the claim to the open decision 3 check.

## Optional

**V17A-FB1, scene 14 length and reason.** 411 characters is about 31 s plus a 1 s hold, just over the 15 to 30 s rule (the design's "about 25 s" understates it), and it never says why Δm × g is the force on the wire. Replacement (426 characters, offset by the trims below or by dropping the hold): "The balance gives the force on the magnet, which equals the force on the wire. Zero point four nine grams is zero point zero zero zero four nine kilograms, times nine point eight, about zero point zero zero four eight newtons. So B equals F over I l, zero point zero zero four eight over two point five times zero point zero four zero, about zero point zero four eight **tesla**. Leaving grams unconverted gives forty-eight tesla, far too **big**." If length is tight, keep the current text and only add the first sentence in place of "First, the force."

**V17A-READ, scene 2 screen load.** The end-on inset, palm-rule axes, paired arrows with bracket, two-line algebra, two recall chips and the "F ∝ I?" tag all build in one 35 s scene beside the bench. Fade the axes once the paired arrows are up, and fade the inset before "Change grams to kilograms", so the algebra is the only new item while it is spoken. Similarly in scene 7, fade the Δm column once the F column is filled.

**V17A-SAME, scene 11 "This small motor runs on six volts."** The opening, scene 11 and the handoff use the same plate as v17's 12 V motor, and scene 17 calls it "where we left the motor". The replacement text in V17A-RON ("Let's run our motor on six volts") ties it to v17's motor; its 2.0 Ω coil matches v17's 2.0 Ω, so the numbers stay consistent.

**V17A-ROCK, scene 10, "so the current in the coil never reverses. The coil rocks back and forth".** The reason for rocking (past face on, the torque turns it back) is in the cause chain but not the narration. v16 taught it and the recall card helps, so this is optional: "... never reverses. Past face on, the torque turns it back, so the coil rocks back and forth and stops face on."

**V17A-DEPTH, scene 3, "the same wire at the same depth in the gap, so the length in the field stays the same".** Depth changes which part of the field the wire sits in (B), not its length in the field. Use "the same wire through the same stretch of the gap, so the length in the field stays the same".

**V17A-HOW, scene 8, "the whole wire has to stay inside one wide, uniform field".** A student will ask how. One screen tag is enough, for example "e.g. a short wire turned inside a large solenoid's field"; no extra narration.
