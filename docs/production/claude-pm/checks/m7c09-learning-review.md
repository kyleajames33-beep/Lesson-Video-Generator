Main question traced: *Ethanoic acid and propan-1-ol have the same molar mass, and both contain an O–H group. Yet the acid boils 21 degrees higher, turns blue litmus red and fizzes with baking soda, while the alcohol does none of these. What does the carboxyl group change, and why does it make the molecule an acid?* Useful outcome: given a carboxylic acid, the student can draw and name it (COOH carbon as C1), explain its higher boiling point against an alcohol of similar size, explain why it is a weak acid while an alcohol is not acidic, and identify it with the hydrogen carbonate test and its equation.

## Scope and focus

One question with two halves (boiling point and acidity), both answered from the same group. That is a fair single learner question, because the hook contrasts one pair of molecules on both counts. The boundary is right: the amine comparison waits for c11 (amines are not yet taught), phenol, inductive effects and calculations are out. The hook's fair comparison (propan-1-ol at equal mass instead of the site's ethanol) is a real improvement. No double teaching: c05's hydrogen bonding, c08b's carbonyl, c08a's oxidation and Module 6's weak acids, pKa and acid reactions are recalled in one sentence or a grey chip each, and the shared-charge reason is new here (Module 6 C06 and C10 teach only that a weak acid's conjugate base pulls protons back, not why).

## Strengths to keep

- **The hook is a genuine contrast.** Same mass, both O–H, yet three different observations. Every later scene answers part of it.
- **"Only one O–H" is said positively** with the crossed "two O–H groups" chip, which heads off the site's Common Error.
- **Naming is compact and causal.** "The carboxyl carbon is always carbon one, so there's no number" gives the reason for the missing locant.
- **The hydrogen carbonate test is explained, not just stated.** "Ethanoic acid is a stronger acid than carbonic acid, so it passes a proton to hydrogen carbonate. The carbonic acid that forms breaks up into water and carbon dioxide."
- **Aligned plate pairs** (litmus, beaker) show each observation as a before and after on the same object.
- **Both attempts are genuine transfer** to new molecules (3-methylbutanoic acid with hexan-1-ol; butanoic acid with butan-1-ol), with one crossed mistake each.
