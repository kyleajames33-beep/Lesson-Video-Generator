# hms-y11-m2-v16 science check (learners and stages of skill acquisition)

Revision 2 design checked 24 November 2026 (source-only: script, on-screen text, practice, key notes, memory aids and facts section; no build or playback). Independent check; the design was not edited.

## Confirmed
- Six learner characteristics (syllabus); ability as stable and largely genetic, skill as learned (Schmidt and Lee, *Motor Control and Learning*); negative transfer between similar serves (2). Correct.
- Fitts and Posner's three stages; limited attention; dual-task probes of automaticity; stage is skill specific (3, 4). Correct.
- Feedback frequency by stage (guidance hypothesis); external focus benefits learners at all levels and experts, internal focus disrupts automatic skills (Wulf, *Int Rev Sport Exerc Psychol* 2013); "aim for the back of the rim" (5). Correct, and root's softened memory aid matches the evidence.
- Temporary performance drop after a deliberate technique change in elite athletes (Carson and Collins 2011) (6). Correct.
- 8a/8b (associative swimmer) and 8c/8d (kayakers): correct.
- Memory aids sound with stated assumptions. No people; plates named correctly.

## Findings
1. **Note (scene 4).** "An associative learner knows it was wrong, but not why": associative learners progressively detect and correct some of their own errors. Acceptable as the site's probe; "often knows it was wrong, but not always why" is more exact.
2. **Note (scene 4).** "A player who's great at training and poor in matches is often associative, not lacking confidence": a heuristic. "can be associative" avoids overclaiming.
