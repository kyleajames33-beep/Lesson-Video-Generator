## Findings

### 1. blocker: 4b and question 3 method givens

Text: "the lowest concentration it can reliably measure"

The explanation teaches detection limit as reliable quantification. A student would approve any numerical result merely because it is above an LOD. That undermines the evidence-evaluation objective.

Repair: Change Q3's given and narration to "validated quantification limit: 0.001 mg/L" and explain "the lowest concentration this method can measure with the stated reliability". Keep the ratio 0.005/0.001=5. The numerical case and duration can stay the same.

### 2. should fix: 2e

Text: "below the diluted result"

6.2 mg/L is compared with 7.1 mg/L for the original sample, not the diluted result 1.42 mg/L. The student must distinguish those samples to apply the main rule.

Repair: Replace with "below the dilution-corrected result" or "below the original concentration found after dilution". No extra scene.

### 3. should fix: 4c

Text: "The school could flush the bubblers each morning"

The worked evaluation moves from inadequate evidence to a practical safety recommendation without verifying a flushing method or having qualified management assess exposure. A student needs to separate an evidence conclusion from a validated remedy.

Repair: Finish with "The school needs further sampling and advice from its water authority to find and control the lead source." Keep the first-draw/flushed comparison; do not present two minutes or morning flushing as a proven universal fix.
