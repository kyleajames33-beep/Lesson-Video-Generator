## Main question and the useful outcome

Main question (as stated): "Butane is a gas and candle wax is a solid, yet both are made of the same kind of molecule. How does carbon's bonding set a hydrocarbon's shape, and how do size and shape set its boiling point and solubility?"

Useful outcome: a student can state the shape and angle at any carbon by counting bonding directions; build alkane, alkene and alkyne members up to C8 and check them against a general formula; tell a series from a set of isomers; explain chain length, branching and equal-size comparisons with intramolecular and intermolecular bonding; and explain why hexane floats on water but mixes with heptane.

One main question: only just. The question has two halves joined by "and", and as written the shapes half (scenes 2 to 4) does not feed the properties half (scenes 8 to 12): the hook is answered entirely by chain length and dispersion forces, and the branching argument uses whole-molecule shape (long against compact), not the local tetrahedral or planar geometry. The design's own justification ("the contact argument for branching needs 3D shape") is not said anywhere in the narration. This is fixable with one link sentence (S2) rather than a split.

## Findings

### S1. Should fix: the homologous series definition excludes alkanes

- Exact text (scene 5): "A family like that is called a homologous series: the same functional group, with members that differ by C H two." Scene 6: "Alkanes have no functional group. Members of a series share a group, so they **react** alike,"
- Student question: "If a series needs the same functional group and alkanes have none, why are alkanes a series? And what do they share that makes them react alike?"
- Replacement, scene 5: "A family like that is called a homologous series: members built the same way, with the same functional group if they have one, each differing by C H two." (+40 characters.)
- Replacement, scene 6: "Alkanes have no functional group, just single bonds. Members of a series share the same bonding, so they **react** alike," (+28 characters.)
- Screen: scene 5 chip "homologous series: same functional group (if any), each member + CH₂". Key note 2: "Homologous series: same functional group (alkanes: single bonds only), each member differs by CH₂."
- Understanding enabled: the definition covers all three series the video builds, and "react alike" has a stated reason for alkanes as well.

### S2. Should fix: the shapes half is never connected to the properties half

- Exact text (scene 10): "A straight chain can lie alongside its neighbours, like strands of uncooked spaghetti."
- Student question: "Why did we learn 109.5 degrees? Nothing about boiling point used it."
- Replacement: "A straight chain, zigzagging at each tetrahedral carbon, can lie alongside its neighbours, like strands of uncooked spaghetti." (+40 characters.) Visual: on "zigzagging" the two pentane models briefly show one small tetrahedron outline each (scene 2's outline, faint), then fade before the contact shading.
- Understanding enabled: the main question's chain closes (bonding sets local shape, local shape sets the chain's overall shape, overall shape sets contact), which the handoff then states.

### S3. Should fix: feedback 2 omits the step that makes contact the deciding variable

- Exact text: "Between molecules, both have only dispersion forces."
- Student question: "Hexane's chain is longer than 2,3-dimethylbutane's main chain, so doesn't it just have more electrons?" Scene 10 taught that equal electrons is why shape decides; feedback 2 drops it, and an HSC answer without it is incomplete.
- Replacement: "Both have the same electrons, and only dispersion forces between molecules." (+23 characters.) Move the "same molecular formula C₆H₁₄" underline to "same electrons".
- Paired trim (same scene): "Saying the branched molecule has weaker covalent bonds is **wrong**: boiling separates molecules." becomes "Saying the branched one has weaker covalent bonds is **wrong**: boiling separates molecules." (−5). Feedback 2 becomes 398 characters, about 30 s.
- Understanding enabled: the student controls for size before arguing from shape, which transfers to any isomer comparison.

### S4. Should fix: no time headroom; fund the fixes with these trims

S1 to S3 add about 131 characters (about 10 s), which would take the video to about 9.7 minutes. Apply these trims, none of which removes a cause:

- T1, scene 10: delete "It's why two-methylpropane boils below butane in **camping gas**." (−61). Keep the silent stove callback with c02's two chips and the "explained" tick; the scene's explanation already pays off c02's promise. Move the "camping gas" cue to "lower boiling point".

- T4, scene 7: "Don't confuse a series with isomers from the last video." becomes "Don't confuse a series with isomers." (−20; the c02 thumbnail stays.)
