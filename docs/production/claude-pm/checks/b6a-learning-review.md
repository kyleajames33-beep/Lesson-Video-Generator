## Answers to the designer's open doubts

**(1) "Bacteria" versus "prokaryotes".** The narration is right to say "bacteria" for coiling by proteins and for the absence of histones; the nucleus and the plasmid claims are true of both groups, so "prokaryote" is right there. The lesson breaks its own rule in one place only: the copied table puts "no histones" in the Prokaryote column. Fix that cell (should fix 2) and add one spoken clause in scene 2 that bacteria are the prokaryotes used as the example. Do not mention archaea in the learner copy: the omission does not teach a wrong idea now the table is fixed, and an archaea clause would add a term with no use later in this lesson. Keep archaea as the author note it is. In the exam, the student writes what the question says; the 10b frame can use "bacterial DNA" and the student adapts the word.

**(2) Griffith and Avery.** Leaving them out is right: they answer a different question (how does DNA carry heredity, and how do bacteria take it up), and they do not help a student compare the forms of DNA. Check what the site treats as essential and what is therefore missing: (a) nothing needed to answer the six-mark compare or the five-mark histones explanation; (b) the plasmid-as-vector idea (key idea 7, Activity 2), whose three properties (small, separate, self-copying) scene 5 already teaches, needs a one-line link in the notes (finding 11), not a scene; (c) the history has no home in the ledger or route. Raise (c) with the owner as a gap for the genetic technology or DNA-as-genetic-material lesson; B11 to B13 stay uncited here because the wording is not used.

**(3) Scenes 7 and 9 density.** Both are too dense for one beat. Split 7 into why-and-how then names (finding 3); trim 9 to about 68 words and one idea per beat, cutting the 16,000 figure (finding 6). Scene 8 needs the same treatment because it carries the adjustable-winding idea and a division recall (findings 4 and 5). Do not cut nucleosome or chromatin to screen tags: both are in the site's key terms and the 5-mark answer. Re-count the narration after these edits: net about +30 words for the splits and clauses, about -40 from scene 9 and the scene 14 trim, so the runtime stays about 8.3 minutes.

**(4) Five answer parts.** It is seven answers, not five. The 10 second countdown is only a cue and the student pauses, so time is not the issue; the screen and the naming-heavy item 1 are. Trim to four demands, show the items in sequence, and make item 1 ask for evidence from the drawing (finding 10). The owner's rule is respected: no longer hold is requested.
