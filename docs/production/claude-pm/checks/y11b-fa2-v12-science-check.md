1. **Blocker:** Scene 6 defines osmotic pressure as "the push of water moving in by osmosis" and conflates it with root pressure. Osmotic pressure is the pressure difference needed to oppose net osmosis; root pressure is a possible hydrostatic outcome of ion accumulation and water entry. Separate those terms in narration, diagram and notes. If more time is needed, cut the cut-flower example in scene 1 and its callback in scene 5.
2. **Should fix:** Scene 1 says tension is the opposite of pressure. It is negative pressure relative to a reference, not the absence or opposite of pressure. Replace that phrase with "under negative pressure, or tension".
3. **Should fix:** Scene 9 gives a universal one-metre capillarity limit and says root pressure disappears when transpiration is fast. Capillary rise varies with tube radius and wetting; root pressure varies with plant and conditions. Use root decision 15's qualitative fallback and avoid a universal one-metre figure. This is shorter.
4. **Should fix:** The petroleum-jelly answer assumes coating lower leaf surfaces seals every stoma and stops all transpiration; stomata may also occur elsewhere and cuticular loss continues. Specify a plant with sealed stomata on both surfaces and predict a substantial reduction rather than zero.
5. **Should fix:** The dendrometer observation supports a water-status explanation but does not independently establish its cause, and transpiration is not always fastest by day. Say it is consistent with greater daytime water loss; the trunk can shrink because living tissues lose water. Replace the absolute diagnosis.
6. **Should fix:** The linked-bead animation suggests one literal molecular chain from roots to leaf and a single bubble stops the whole plant. Describe continuous water columns within interconnected conduits, and keep the pit detour; molecules rearrange. Screen-only clarification.

[Osmosis physical analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10457415/) distinguishes osmolarity-driven water flow and pressure. [Xylem pressure experiments](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC59486/) measure tension; [stem-diameter measurements](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10525319/) support the proposed daily relationship without proving a specific cause from a single observation. The L09 claim that blocked transpiration necessarily stops root water uptake is too strong; distinguish active mineral uptake from subsequent delivery to leaves.
