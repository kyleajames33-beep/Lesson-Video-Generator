## Main question and useful outcome

Main question: *What does each of identify, describe and explain ask you to write, and how do you know you've actually explained?* Useful outcome: a student can tell how much a verb demands and can check that an "explain" answer gives causes, not a restated result. The three-rung ladder on one stimulus is an excellent design. The content is held still, so the only thing that changes between answers is the verb. The Chemistry ladder in scene 9 gives the same point in one image for the other subject.

## Trace from entry knowledge

1. Hook: the frequency chart is accurate and motivating, and "what happens, not why" sets up the whole video.
2. Glossary ladder: clear and brief, with an attribution tag. One gloss sits inside the attributed sentence (M2).
3. Card: the reciprocal cross is new against B10, so students apply rather than recall. Good. The spoken signpost for Chemistry viewers is in place.
4. Identify: "a name, that's it" is shown with a ghost paragraph overflowing. Good.
5. Describe: shown with arrows from the table values. But the narration says "**So** every daughter had her father's eye colour", then immediately says "notice what's missing: there's no because" and later teaches "so" as an explain marker (B1, S1).
6. and 7. Explain: the cause chain is built on the X and Y chips with visible movement (the mother's X to sons, the father's X to daughters). This is a real use of the medium. A student can see where each X comes from.
8. Weak against strong: the weak answer contains "**because** the trait is sex-linked", yet the scene calls it a description. That is the right judgement, but the narration never says that a "because" alone does not make an explanation (B1).
9. Chemistry ladder: good, compact recall.
10. Because test: "does it say what causes what?" is a good working test. The memory aid "Explain says why: because, so" teaches a surface cue that all three weak answers in this video already pass (B1).
11. to 14. Drills: both are explain, the verb that most needs practice. The full card stays visible during "Pause here to answer." and the 10 s countdown. The condition "Assuming no copying errors" is on the card. Nothing answer-bearing appears. Drill 1's spoken feedback gives only the rates route; it does not say that a linked Le Chatelier answer is also full value (M3).
15. and 16. Decision rule and notes: clear, with a quiet hold.

## Designer's flagged question: is 1 of 3 too generous for the weak "sex-linked" answer?

Yes, as currently written. "Because the trait is sex-linked" restates the stem ("the gene for eye colour is on the X chromosome"), and the first half restates part (b). A marker would usually not credit repeating the stem. But 0 of 3 makes the example less realistic and less useful. The better fix is to give the weak answer one genuine but incomplete point, the kind a real student writes, so that 1 of 3 is earned and the two missing marks are exactly the inheritance routes. Doing this also helps B1, because the weak answer will then contain both "because" and a causal-sounding clause while still not explaining. See S2.

## Findings

**B1 (blocker). The memory aid and the "because or so" framing conflict with the video's own examples.**
Quoted (scene 10 and notes): "Describe says what. Explain says why: because, so."
Problem: the weak answer in scene 8 ("because the trait is sex-linked"), the drill 1 crossed habit ("because gas comes out of the drink") and the drill 2 crossed habit ("because each has one old strand and one new strand") all contain "because". A student applying the aid as a surface check would pass all three. The take-home cue therefore teaches the opposite of the examples. The design's author note admits it is a "check on form", but the narration never says so.
Replacement for the memory aid (scene 10, the notes screen and the design's Decisions): "Describe says what. Explain says how one thing causes the next."
Add to scene 8 narration, after "It **described** when the question said explain.": "Notice it even says because. A because isn't enough: it has to say how."
Replacement for scene 15 narration: "**Explain**, give the cause and the effect, linked with because or so" → "**Explain**, show how each cause leads to its effect, step by step".
Replacement for key note 2: "Link each cause to its effect with 'because' or 'so'." → "Show how each cause leads to its effect; a 'because' alone is not enough."

**S1 (should fix). Scene 5 narration uses "so" inside the describe answer.**
Quoted: "So every daughter had her father's eye colour, and every son had his mother's."
Problem: this sentence comes right before "there's no because. Describe doesn't ask why", and in a video that teaches "so" as an explain marker. The model answer in the questions section also uses "So daughters matched...".
Replacement (narration): "In other words, every daughter had her father's eye colour, and every son had his mother's."
Replacement (model answer (b) point 2): "All 48 male offspring had white eyes. Daughters matched their father's phenotype and sons their mother's."

**S2 (should fix). Scene 8 weak answer and tally.**
Quoted: "All the males had white eyes and all the females had red eyes, because the trait is sex-linked." / "In our marking guide it earns **one** of three, for linking the trait to the sex chromosomes."
Problem: the credited idea is already given in the stem, so 1 mark is generous.
Replacement (weak answer, on screen and spoken): "All the males had white eyes and all the females had red eyes, because the trait is sex-linked and males have only one X."
Replacement (tally sentence): "In our marking guide it earns **one** of three, for saying males have only one X."
Replacement (lost-marks tag): "-2: never says where each X comes from, for sons or daughters". The "where does each X come from?" slot stays. Replace the "why does one allele show in a male?" slot with "why are daughters red?".

**M1 (minor). Hook claim wording.**
Quoted: "one key word came up in the written section more than any other"
Problem: the count covers NESA glossary headwords only; non-glossary commands such as *determine* and *state* are excluded. "Key word" is NESA's term, so the spoken line is defensible, but the screen tag should say so.
Replacement (grey tag): "NESA glossary key words only; Section II, 2021 to 2025, counted by question part"

**M2 (minor). Scene 2 gloss inside an attributed sentence.**
Quoted: "**Describe** means give the features or characteristics: what it's like, or what happened."
Problem: "what it's like, or what happened" is HSCScience's gloss, spoken inside "Here's what NESA's glossary says".
Replacement: "**Describe** means give the features or characteristics. In plain words: what it's like, or what happened."

**M3 (minor). Drill 1 feedback leaves out the other valid route in speech.**
Quoted: "That just describes the bubbles."
Problem: there is no constraint here (scene 6 taught that any correct reasoning counts). A student who wrote a linked Le Chatelier answer hears only the rates route, and the on-screen tag is unspoken.
Replacement: "That just describes them. A linked Le Chatelier answer works too."

**M4 (minor). Feedback length (scene 14 about 35 s).**
Quoted: "That describes the result. It doesn't say why the sequences match."
Replacement: "That says how they're built, not why the sequences match."

**M5 (minor). Key note 1 attribution scope.**
Quoted: "Identify: recognise and name (a name for each mark; no reasons needed). ... (NESA glossary, paraphrased.)"
Problem: the parenthetical "a name for each mark" is HSCScience advice, but it sits inside the attributed note.
Replacement: "Identify: recognise and name. Describe: give the features or characteristics. Outline: the main features in general terms (NESA glossary, paraphrased). Tip: identify needs a name per mark, no reasons; describe uses values from any data."

## Retained strengths

One stimulus with three verbs. Visible X chromosome movement for the cause chain. Cause boxes and arrows drawn over the answer lines. Chemistry recall in scene 9. Both drills are explain at explain's common mark values. Assumptions sit on the card. The counting caveat is shown on screen.
