## Main question and the useful outcome

Main question (as stated): "A weak acid question gives you a Ka, a measured pH, or an ion that can go either way. Which method fits those data, and how do you check the answer?"

One main question: yes. "The data choose the method" is a clear organising idea, and the decision board closes it. Useful outcome: a student can use the shortcut and justify it, recognise a failed check and keep the x, work backwards from a pH to Ka and pKa, and decide which way an amphiprotic ion mainly reacts by comparing Ka with the Kb from its conjugate acid.

## Findings

### S1. Should fix: Q4 never says why the Kb comes from the acid one proton up

- Exact text (5b): "Accepting, it becomes carbonic acid, so its K b is K w over carbonic acid's K a". Exact text (5d): "The classic mistake is finding K b from the ion's own K a. For dihydrogen phosphate, that gives one point six times ten to the minus seven, and the wrong answer, basic. For an ion's K b, use the K a one proton up."
- Student question: "Why carbonic acid's K a, and not the ion's own?"
- Problem: C10 taught "Ka × Kb = Kw ... only for a conjugate pair". Q4's trap is exactly that condition, but neither scene names it, and 5d crosses the mistake with no reason, only a rule. The 5d caption "that's the Kb of HPO₄²⁻" is never spoken.
- Replacement (5b): "Accepting, it becomes carbonic acid, its conjugate acid, so its K b is K w over carbonic acid's K a: **two point three times ten to the minus eight**." The bracket "conjugate pair" (C10 style) joins H₂CO₃ and HCO₃⁻ in the mini chain on "conjugate acid".
- Replacement (5d): "The classic mistake is finding K b from the ion's own K a. That gives one point six times ten to the minus seven, the K b of hydrogen phosphate, and the wrong answer, basic. K a times K b is K w only for a conjugate pair, so for an ion's K b, use the K a one proton up." Cue "hydrogen phosphate": the caption "that's the Kb of HPO₄²⁻" writes. Cue "conjugate pair": the chain brackets H₃PO₄ and H₂PO₄⁻.
- Understanding enabled: the student can rebuild the aid from C10's condition, and can therefore apply it to a new ion such as HPO₄²⁻.

### S2. Should fix: Q4's working area is over the clutter limit, and 5c shows unspoken pKb values

- Exact text (5c visual): "A small `PKRuler` thumbnail shows the two pairs of markers (HCO₃⁻: pKa 10.33 and pKb 7.63; H₂PO₄⁻: pKa 7.20 and pKb 11.85; screen only, no narration)". Also 5b and 5c: each fork arrow carries a full equation, plus a constant chip, the centre ion, a mini chain, a Kb line, a ratio tag and the answer boxes.
- Problem: in 5b about six bright text items are on screen at once; 5c adds a thumbnail fork, the `PKRuler` with four unspoken numbers, and a grey C06 tag. pKb is not taught in C10 (only pKa is), so the thumbnail introduces an untaught term with no narration.
- Repair: cut the `PKRuler` thumbnail from 5c. On each fork, write the full equation on its cue, then reduce it to a short label once the constant chip pins ("donates → CO₃²⁻ + H₃O⁺"; "accepts → H₂CO₃ + OH⁻"); the full equations stay in the answer box (see science S1). The Kb line dims as soon as its chip pins (as designed). Keep the grey C06 tag, but bring it in only after the ratio tag fades.
- Understanding enabled: the student watches two constants and one thicker arrow, which is the whole comparison.

### S3. Should fix: 3b states the reason for the failed check backwards

- Exact text: "Dropping x would take away about a quarter of the acid, so the shortcut doesn't hold."
- Student question: "Dropping x takes acid away? I thought dropping x meant pretending none ionised."
- Problem: dropping x keeps the acid at c. The error is that about a quarter of it has really ionised, so c − x is far from c.
- Replacement: "About a quarter of the acid ionises, so c minus x is nowhere near c, and the shortcut doesn't hold." (+14 characters.)
- Understanding enabled: the student links the 5% check to the term it protects (c − x ≈ c).

### M1. Minor: Q2's narration orders the demands differently from the card

- Exact text (3a): "Calculate the pH of a zero point zero two zero zero moles per litre solution. Show why the simplifying assumption can't be used."
- Replacement: "Show that the simplifying assumption can't be used for a zero point zero two zero zero moles per litre solution. Then calculate its pH." (This matches card parts (a) and (b), and the "Show that" verb.)

### M2. Minor: Q3's sense check repeats the stem

- Exact text (4c visual): tag "Ka < 1, pKa > 0: weak ✓".
- Problem: the stem already says "weak monoprotic acid", so the check confirms nothing.
- Replacement tag: "pKa 4.19: between methanoic acid (3.74) and ethanoic acid (4.74) ✓" (the C10 values), with the two C10 markers on the `PKRuler` thumbnail. The check then tests a realistic size, not a label.

### M3. Minor: length after the fixes

(the design's reserve trim 1; the `PHTrack` carries it); (2) 4b's last sentence, "Nothing here is approximated, so there's no five percent check." (reserve trim 2; the grey tag "measured, not approximated: no 5% check" carries it); (3) 5c's "by tens of thousands" (reserve trim 3; move the tag "Ka ≈ 45 000 × Kb" to the cue "K a wins").
- Result: 5,702 characters, about 571 s (9.5 minutes), recounted by script. If the voiced take runs over, trim the hook's last sentence before cutting any reason.

### M4. Minor: the quadratic assumes Mathematics Advanced

- Exact text (Entry knowledge): "Mathematics: the quadratic formula (Year 11 Mathematics Advanced) or a calculator's equation solver."
- Problem: some HSC Chemistry students take Mathematics Standard, where the quadratic formula is not taught, and not every approved calculator has a solver.
- Repair: keep the worked quadratic (see the science check judgement). Replace the entry-knowledge text with: "Mathematics: the quadratic formula (Mathematics Advanced). For students without it, the formula line on screen shows every substituted value, and the substitution check confirms the root." No narration change is needed: "The quadratic formula, or your calculator's solver" stays.
