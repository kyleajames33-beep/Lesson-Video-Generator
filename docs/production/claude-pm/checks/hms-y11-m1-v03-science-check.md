## Findings
1. **Should fix (scene 4 narration and key note 3).** "An illness only gets counted when someone goes to a service, is diagnosed, and is recorded. If you never walk in the door, you never appear in the data." Population surveys (for example the ABS National Health Survey and the National Study of Mental Health and Wellbeing) also measure morbidity by self-report and by survey instruments, without anyone presenting. A teacher would flag "only" and "never". Corrected narration: "But morbidity is harder to measure, because most illness data come from services, so an illness usually gets counted only when someone goes to a service, is diagnosed, and is recorded. Surveys fill some of the gap, but if you never walk in the door, you often don't appear in the data." Key note 3: "Morbidity: illness among the living; mostly counted only if people present." (Scene 9's "people who can't reach a service can look healthier than they are" stays correct.)
2. **Note (scene 3).** "Mortality is the most reliable measure we have" is the standard textbook claim; it is reliable for counts, though cause-of-death coding is less exact. Acceptable at this level.
3. **Note (11a/11b).** A student who expresses prevalence as a rate (2,400 per 100,000, or 2.4 per cent) has not answered wrongly; the model asks only for identification, so no change, but the marker note should accept it.

## Verdict
Pass with 1 should fix. 0 blockers, 1 should fix, 2 notes.
