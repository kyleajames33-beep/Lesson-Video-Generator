The boat is a well-chosen anchor: five gauge readings give "the range of the data" a literal meaning, so "between" and "beyond" are things the student can point to, and the minus-twelve-litre readout gives the lesson its question in the first 20 s. The probe that changes colour as it leaves the band, and the gauge needle stuck on "E" while the readout goes negative, make the two key ideas visible rather than stated. "Not automatically wrong, just less reliable" is said, and the two-part limitation card is a copyable exam habit with a crossed "It might not be accurate." ## Findings

1. **Should fix, scenes 3 and 4 (valid range and reliability blur).** Scene 3 says six hours is extrapolation and less reliable. Scene 4 then says the model "only makes sense from zero to seven and a half hours" and writes "valid range: 0 ≤ h ≤ 7.5" in green, the colour that scene 2 used for "fairly safe". A student will ask: "So is six hours fine now, or not?" The design never separates "makes sense" from "is checked by data". Fix. Narration, after the valid-range sentence: "Valid doesn't mean reliable, though. From four to seven and a half hours the answers still make sense, but they're extrapolation, so take care." Visual: split the strip under the h-axis, green from 0 to 4 with the tag "data: fairly safe" and amber hatching from 4 to 7.5 with the tag "makes sense, but extrapolation"; keep the label "valid range: 0 ≤ h ≤ 7.5" spanning both. Students should be able to say that a valid range rules out nonsense, while the data range says how much to trust an answer. About 7 s; scene 4's last sentence (the reserve trim) can go if needed.

2. **Should fix, scene 5 (the worked example has no data to interpolate between).** Interpolation was defined in scene 2 as "predicting between the values you already have", but the savings example has no values: the student has $350 and a job that has not started, so week 10 is a future prediction, and calling it "interpolation, inside the twenty-six weeks" quietly changes the meaning to "inside the planned time". A careful student will notice the two uses disagree, and an exam marker expects the data meaning. Fix with no number changes: make the data real. Narration: "Your bank balance was checked every four weeks of a twenty-six-week holiday job, and on the last day, and the readings fit S equals three hundred and fifty plus forty w." Then "At ten weeks ... That's interpolation, between the readings at eight and twelve weeks, so it's fairly safe." Task strip: "S = 350 + 40w fits your savings S ($), checked every 4 weeks and at week 26 of a 26-week holiday job. Estimate S at 10 weeks and predict it at 52 weeks. Comment on the reliability of each." Plot: ink points at w = 0, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 26 (350, 510, 670, 830, 990, 1150, 1310, 1390, checked in `suggested.py`) inside the shaded band. The limitation slots stay as written ("the job ends after 26 weeks"). Students should understand interpolation and extrapolation as statements about where the data are. About 4 s.

3. **Optional, scene 3 or 4 (when the intercept makes sense).** The strand never says that the intercept is itself a prediction at input zero, and is only meaningful when zero is a real case. Here it is (the 60 L reading at 0 hours is real data). One sentence after "the boat has stopped, and the model hasn't" would do it: "The same goes for the start. Sixty litres is a real reading at zero hours. But if a model is built from one to four hours, its value at zero hours is extrapolation too." No new picture (the probe can slide to h = 0 inside the band, tag "start: a real reading"). About 7 s. Do not use the jet ski, which would answer part of A1.
