Main question traced: *how do you collect, record and present data on how common a characteristic is, and how sure can you be about what it shows?* Useful outcome: the student can plan an ethical, anonymous class survey with definitions first, record a tally and frequencies with a stated total, pool classes, draw a correct column graph, and write a cautious comparison with a limitation.

## What works (keep)

- The hook ("nine of twenty-eight ... Not yet") sets the real question and the three chips (how collected, how big, how judged) organise the whole video.
- Every method step carries its reason: agreement and anonymity (no one traced or pressured), definitions before counting with an "unsure" box (different judgements change what the numbers mean), test the definitions on practice diagrams, two counters (catch mistakes), state which total, add counts not percentages.
- Scene 6's "But which total?" is a strong predict-then-show beat, and it sets up the attempt's crossed mistake (18 / 42).
- The graph scene shows the axis-from-zero rule with a misleading inset, a good use of the medium.
- The attempt is the site's task, with every number on the card and no totals column; feedback (358 characters, about 27 s) gives the answer, the key reason and one crossed mistake.
- Dropping the Tuskegee hook and keeping the ethics as practice is right for the scale of a class tally.
- No human figures; slips and tallies move by themselves.

## Can a student run the practical from the video?

- **Materials:** slips, box, mirror, tally sheet, pencils, bead bag. Clear.
- **Steps with reasons:** yes (scenes 3 to 9). One practical gap: one hand mirror does not show your own earlobe well (finding 2).
- **What to record:** tally table with category, tally, count, frequency and the stated total; class table and pooled row; column graph. Clear.

## Is the earlobe line diagram clear enough?

Partly. A lone curve meeting a vertical line is abstract: at video scale a student may not see which part of the curve is the earlobe, and they must apply the card to a real ear in a mirror. The coded-line approach is right under the owner's no-people rule; it needs a little more ear and a label (finding 1).
