## Findings

### 1. Blocker: the population model replaces allele frequency with carrier and field proportions

Scene 3: "their frequency is the share of plants carrying them". Visual: "gene pool: all the rice plants in the region". Scene 10: "a crop's genes spread only as far as it's planted". These also recur in Decisions, boundaries, notes, memory verification and metadata.

A gene pool consists of alleles, not plants. Allele frequency counts copies at a specified locus. A heterozygous carrier contributes one insertion copy and one non-insertion copy; a homozygote contributes two insertion copies. Neither carrier proportion nor field proportion is generally that frequency. Natural selection also operates in crops, and wild frequencies also change through drift and gene flow. Farmer choice is useful here without replacing those mechanisms.

Use learning-review finding 1's shorter scene 3, field-area meter and Golden Rice-specific memory repair. Change its selfing clause to "Rice mostly pollinates itself, so pollen rarely passes these genes to other plants". Selfing reduces successful outcrossing, not necessarily how far a pollen grain travels. Retain a possible pollen transfer rather than making every escaping dot disappear. Remove the wild-versus-crop claim that selection and seed choice are exclusive alternatives. Apply the same repairs to all repeated summaries and the description's claim that seed choices "set the allele frequency".

The [OECD rice biology consensus, 2021 document published in the 2022 volume](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/safety-assessment-of-transgenic-organisms-in-the-environment-volume-9_e49bd2e8-en/full-report/component-11.html) confirms predominantly selfing rice, successful pollen-mediated flow and variation among conditions. Its transgenic-to-cultivated-rice examples are usually below 1%, with exceptions; a universal below-1% ceiling would be wrong. The [USDA rice assessment](https://www.aphis.usda.gov/brs/aphisdocs2/06_23401p_com.pdf) gives the usual below-1% qualification. This finding is the same underlying blocker as learning finding 1, not a second blocker.

### 7. Should fix: the gene-drive exception is presented as an inevitable property of every drive

Scene 9: "a gene drive spreads itself after a small release"; the mini-grid fills automatically. The [2021 original SPECIES experiments](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-23531-z) demonstrate threshold-dependent gene drive. Drive design, fitness costs, resistance and population conditions matter; biased inheritance alone does not guarantee invasion from any small release.

Replace the sentence with: "Some gene drives can spread after a small release, so context also matters at the decision to release." This is shorter than the original. Label the mini-grid "possible spread, not every drive" and show a conditional schematic, not an observed environmental result. Change the notes and author claims of correctness consistently. The [National Academies phased-testing framework](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/23405/chapter/7) also makes public engagement and governance relevant during testing and after release, not exclusively before it. The design can keep the exception without teaching inevitability. Its claim that no drive has ever been released is not needed in learner copy and has not been certified by a complete 2026 global release-register search.
