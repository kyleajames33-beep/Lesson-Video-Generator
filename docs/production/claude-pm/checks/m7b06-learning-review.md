Main question traced: *A splinter pushes bacteria through your skin. What does your body do in the first hours, before any defence built for that pathogen is ready, and how do those first defences hold the bacteria back?* Useful outcome: given a breach, the student can sequence barrier, inflammation, phagocyte arrival and digestion, and dendritic cell alert; link each sign of inflammation to its vessel change; and explain a changed case (a leak test, a cell that cannot leave the blood).

## Strengths to keep

- **One case changes in place.** The same close-up under the splinter tip goes from early to inflamed to contained, with each region revealed at its cue (vessel widens, fluid appears, mast cell empties, neutrophil squeezes out). Students can point at what changed and compare it with the earlier state. This is the visual model that route change 6 relies on.
- **Causes before names.** Scene 5 says what histamine does to the vessel and why each change follows; scene 6 ties each sign to that cause and then gives the purpose, so the crossed chip "inflammation = just symptoms" is answered with a mechanism.
- **Phagocytosis as four aligned plates.** Bind, wrap, seal, digest, one plate per step on the existing alignment master, with "sealed in a bubble" before the lysosomes explaining why the cell does not digest itself.
- **The hook earns attention.** A starfish larva with no antibodies still gathers cells around a thorn overnight; the before and after pair shows the surprise rather than telling it.
- **Both attempts are real transfer.** The dye leak test makes students separate widening from leaking; the adhesion defect makes them use "stick, squeeze out" to explain three linked observations. Each feedback is brief with one crossed mistake.
- **The memory aid is earned.** "Block, alarm, eat, alert" comes after all four stages and maps one verb to each rung of the strip, which is what g03's sequencing question needs.
- **Handoff wording matches b07's question.**

## Plates: are all the new plates needed?

The design says thirteen; the brief has fourteen (finding 9). Assessed one by one:

- **Dermis triplet (early, inflamed, contained)** (scenes 4 to 7, 9, 10): essential. This is the model: every narrated physical change appears in place.
- **Three phagocytosis partners (wrap, after-v2, digest)** (scene 8): essential. I inspected the existing `m7-macrophage-engulf-after-v1.png` against the before plate: the cell outline, nucleus and rod all shift, so it cannot crossfade. The wrap plate is the step students most often skip ("wrap" against "absorb").
- **Dendritic cell and lymph node** (scenes 10 to 12, and b07): essential. Neither exists, and a coded lymph node would be a clip-art icon.
- **Skin splinter pair (normal, inflamed)** (hook, 4, 6): needed. The inflamed partner is where "you can see it" happens: the micro causes become the redness and swelling a student sees on a real splinter. Second cut, as the design says.
- **Airway cilia and mucus** (scene 3): useful. It shows how mucus traps and cilia move it, which answers the marker comment the design cites ("how each barrier stops entry"). Third cut.
- **Starfish larva pair** (hook): useful but the most optional. The before and after carries the surprise. First cut, as the design says.
- **Wound bacterium**: not used on this board. Keep it in this brief only so that b07 has it in time (b07 uses it in six scenes), and label it as a b07 plate.

The design's cut order is right.
