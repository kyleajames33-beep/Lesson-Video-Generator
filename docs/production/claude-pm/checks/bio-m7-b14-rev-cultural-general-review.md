## What scene 10 is meant to teach

Ring vaccination (scene 7's smallpox idea) used against Ebola in 2015, and why a randomised trial is far stronger proof than one drop in cases (scene 5's Snow problem). Who pays: consent, and the waiting rings carried the risk.

## Strengths to keep

- **It answers the video's hardest question.** Scene 2 asks "could something else explain the change?", and scene 5 shows the weak answer (one drop that might have happened anyway). Scene 10 shows the strong answer: chance decides which rings wait, so the groups are alike. This is the best possible replacement for the video's spine, not just a safe one.
- **Reuse is purposeful.** The scene 7 `CaseRing` returns, so the student sees the same idea used again, and scene 5's falling line returns as a grey thumbnail for the contrast.
- **The spoken claim is precise.** "No one vaccinated straight away caught Ebola" matches the trial's primary comparison (vaccinated people in immediate rings), not the looser "no one in the immediate rings".
- **No people**: rings and dots only. The spiked virus plate is rightly not used for Ebola.
- **No community content remains** in scene 10, the key note, the visual link or the description (searched). The handoff to b15 still names "Aboriginal protocols", which is the next video's title, not community content.

## Science check: the Ebola ring vaccination trial

| Fact as used | Verdict and confidence |
| --- | --- |
| Trial in Guinea, 2015, during the West African epidemic (2014 to 2016) | Confirmed, high confidence. "Ebola ça Suffit!", randomisation from 1 April 2015 in Basse-Guinée. A later, non-randomised part also enrolled in Sierra Leone; the "Guinea" label is right for the randomised comparison. |
| Ring = contacts and contacts of contacts of each new case (screen tag) | Confirmed, high confidence. |
| A new vaccine, rVSV-ZEBOV (screen tag) | Confirmed, high confidence. Later licensed as Ervebo (EMA and US FDA, late 2019). |
| Rings chosen at random for immediate vaccination or vaccination 21 days later ("three weeks later") | Confirmed, high confidence (cluster randomisation, 21-day delay). |
| From day 10, 0 cases among those vaccinated straight away against 16 in the waiting rings (screen "0 against 16 cases from day 10") | Confirmed, high confidence for the 0 and the 16, which appear in both the interim (*Lancet* 2015; 386: 857 to 866) and final (*Lancet* 2017; 389: 505 to 518) reports. The 16 were among eligible people in delayed rings. Day 10 is counted from randomisation, not from vaccination (finding 1's label). |
| Vaccine efficacy 100%, 95% CI 68.9 to 100 (final; Numbers only) | Fairly confident (the interim interval was 74.7 to 100). Not in learner copy; Codex to confirm. |
| Each participant gave informed consent | Confirmed in principle (written informed consent was required); Codex to confirm the consent wording, as briefed. |
| After the interim analysis in July 2015, randomisation stopped and all rings were offered immediate vaccination (screen tag) | Confirmed, high confidence (late July 2015, on the data and safety monitoring board's advice; children were included from August 2015). |
| Epidemic size about 28,600 cases and 11,300 deaths (Numbers only) | Confirmed, high confidence (WHO). |
| Exact cluster and participant counts, the all-eligible effectiveness estimate | **Not confirmed**: I cannot recall these with confidence. None is in learner copy; flag for Codex only if a note quotes them. |
