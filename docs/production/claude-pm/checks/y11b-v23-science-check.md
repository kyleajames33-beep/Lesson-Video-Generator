[Human Okazaki fragment experiments](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3825817/) document coordinated polymerase δ, FEN1 and ligase I action; do not import the bacterial polymerase I primer-removal story as a universal human mechanism. [Human polymerase fidelity work](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3581418/) shows proofreading improves fidelity while errors still occur and later repair contributes. For the simplified labelled single fork, both new strands extend 5′ to 3′; the leading grows with the fork and the lagging fragments away from it. This does not imply that replication of an entire linear human chromosome is one fork starting at one end. A two-round semiconservative model gives 2 of 4 molecules containing an original strand; 4 of 8 individual strands are new in round two. Six billion diploid base pairs and around eight hours are illustrative, varying by cell and S phase. A video model is not a completed student practical investigation.

The ~1000 bases/s figure lacks organism and per-fork scope and is not universally human; log a source/organism requirement, not a claim that all forks have one exact speed. "Toward the fork" is understandable when the fork moves, but "in the same direction as the fork" is clearer.
