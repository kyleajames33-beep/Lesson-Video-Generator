# hms-y12-m4-v21 science check (injury management, rehabilitation and return to play)

Revision 2 design checked 24 November 2026 (source-only: script, on-screen text in the visual lines, practice, key notes, memory aids and facts section; no build or playback; plates checked by name and description). Independent check; the design was not edited. Line numbers refer to `lessons/hms-y12-m4-v21.md` as of this check.

## Confirmed
- **RICER and No HARM (flagged, line 53).** Sports Medicine Australia's current soft tissue injury fact sheet (2025 PDF and web page) still teaches RICER for 48 to 72 hours, "to limit bleeding and damage", ice for 20 minutes every two hours, never directly on skin, a compression bandage, elevation and referral, and No HARM (no heat, alcohol, running or massage). The syllabus and site teach RICER, and hms-y11-m2-v08 already taught it. Keeping RICER is correct. Newer frameworks (PEACE and LOVE: Dubois and Esculier, *Br J Sports Med* 2020) drop ice, warn against anti-inflammatories early and stress early pain-guided loading; the evidence that ice reduces swelling or speeds healing in humans is weak (Bleakley and others, *Am J Sports Med* 2004), while it does ease pain. See finding 1 for wording that stays correct under both.
- Hard tissue (2): do not move unnecessarily, support as found, call for help. Correct.
- Rehabilitation sequence (3), keeping the rest of the body fit, cold early and heat later. Correct.
- Return to play on criteria (4): pain, range, measured strength, skill under fatigue, psychological readiness, clearance; re-injury risk highest soon after return; graded return (Ardern and others, 2016 consensus on return to sport, *Br J Sports Med* 2016). Correct.
- Policy (5) and shared responsibility (6). Correct.
- 8a/8b: RICER with reasons and the No HARM mechanism. Correct against SMA.
- 8c/8d (flagged): loss of consciousness means suspected concussion; a doctor decides; parents' consent is not clearance; school policy and duty of care. Correct against the Australian Concussion Guidelines for Youth and Community Sport (2024). But see finding 2.
- Memory aids: "Criteria, not a calendar" sound. "Cold early, heat later" sound with its stated assumption. No people.

## Findings
1. **Should fix (line 53, scene 2; line 58, scene 3; line 88, 8b).** Current: "Ice, wrapped, for about twenty minutes at a time: it narrows blood vessels, limiting bleeding, swelling and pain." The pain effect is well supported; limiting bleeding and swelling is the traditional rationale (SMA) but weakly supported in human trials, and newer guidance questions prolonged icing. Corrected, still RICER and not wrong under either view: "Ice, wrapped, for about twenty minutes at a time: it narrows blood vessels, which eases pain and may help limit swelling." Scene 3: "Cold early, mainly for pain." 8b is unaffected (it gives no ice mechanism). Add the grey tag root proposed, which this check finds warranted because the omission would let students think icing is the whole of early care: "newer guidance also stresses protecting the injury, then early pain-free movement". No other learner copy change.
2. **Should fix (line 98, 8d; line 124, marking).** The basketballer is fifteen and the note is for Friday, six days after the injury. The 2024 national youth and community guideline requires medical clearance and a minimum of 21 days before return to competition (with at least 14 days symptom-free before contact training), so Friday is wrong even with a doctor's note; the feedback as written implies a doctor could clear her for Friday. Corrected 8d: "Being knocked out means a suspected concussion, so a doctor decides, not the player or her parents. A parents' note is consent, not clearance, and under the national guideline she can't play for at least three weeks anyway. The school sets and enforces the policy: medical clearance, then a graded return. The classic mistake is accepting the parents' note as clearance." Marking line 4: "no play or contact training without medical clearance, a graded return, and no competition within the national minimum (21 days for youth sport)". Line on screen 4 likewise.
3. **Note (line 63).** "strength gaps predict re-injury": asymmetry is associated with re-injury but predicts it poorly in individuals. "are linked to re-injury" would be exact.
4. **Note (line 93, 8c).** A loss of consciousness is itself a red flag needing urgent medical assessment at the time (ambulance or emergency department). Not part of the question asked; a silent tag "knocked out: urgent medical check at the time" would model it.

## Verdict
Pass with fixes. 0 blockers, 2 should fix, 2 notes. RICER flag: keep RICER, reword the ice mechanism and add the grey tag. Concussion flag: wording correct, but 8d needs the minimum stand-down.
