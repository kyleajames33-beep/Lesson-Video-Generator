# hms-y11-m1-v04 science check (social justice principles)

Revision 2 design checked 24 November 2026 (source-only: the Revision 2 section, script, practice, key notes and the design's facts section; no build, audio or playback). Independent science and fact check; the design was not edited.

## Confirmed
- Equality and equity (scene 2, key note 1): correct.
- Inequity as avoidable, unnecessary and unfair (scene 3, key note 2, memory aid): matches Whitehead (1990/1992, "The concepts and principles of equity and health", WHO Regional Office for Europe). Correct.
- Six access barriers (scene 4): a sound checklist, correctly "at least six".
- Rights (scene 7): the WHO Constitution preamble says "The enjoyment of the highest attainable standard of health is one of the fundamental rights of every human being without distinction of race, religion, political belief, economic or social condition." The shortened quote on the card does not change the meaning. Fair.
- Practice 10a/10b: access through a language and literacy barrier is correct; the model already accepts equity with an explanation. 10c/10d: correct.
- Cultural content: community-controlled services described generally; no community named. Compliant.

## Findings
1. **Should fix (scene 6).** "That's why community-controlled health services, run by the communities they serve, work so well." The evidence supports better access, trust and culturally safe care (for example Panaretto and others 2014, MJA, "Aboriginal community controlled health services: leading the way in primary care"; Campbell and others 2017, Aust NZ J Public Health, review of ACCHS contribution), but "work so well" is an unqualified effectiveness claim a teacher would query. Corrected: "That's why community-controlled health services, run by the communities they serve, are often more trusted and better used."
2. **Should fix (scene 8).** "So equity isn't just fair. It's the most effective way to lift a whole population." The mechanism in the scene supports a larger gain per unit of effort where avoidable ill health is greatest, not that equity is always the single most effective approach. Corrected: "So equity isn't just fair. It's often one of the most effective ways to lift a whole population." (The 10d feedback already avoids the superlative.)
3. **Note (scene 4).** "Physical, whether the building works for people with disability" is narrower than physical access (which also covers transport and service design), but fine as an example.

## Verdict
Pass with 2 should fix. 0 blockers, 2 should fix, 1 note.
