## Student learning trace (entry knowledge: B3a part 1, hCG, progesterone, placenta, and Year 11 hormone basics)

- Question: three questions in the hook (why no new egg for nine months, why does labour only get stronger, how is milk made and moved). The video answers them in that order and the "two loops, then two jobs" chip is the map.
- Negative feedback is taught by cause: progesterone travels to the pituitary and the pituitary releases less FSH and LH, so no egg is released. The thermostat is a second picture with the same layout, so the analogy is a comparison, not decoration. The same brake is then reused for pregnancy and for the tablet in the attempt, so the student meets one pattern three times.
- Positive feedback is taught by tracing a loop one stage at a time (stretch, nerve signal, pituitary, oxytocin, contraction, more stretch) with the loop redrawn as a circle that grows lap by lap. Oestrogen is explained as raising how strongly the muscle responds to oxytocin, shown with receptors, not just named.
- Misconception control: "negative is bad, positive is good" (crossed thumbs, should-fix 5), "she isn't pregnant so she ovulates" (item 1), "prolactin because it is the milk hormone" (item 2), "painful" and "protects the pregnancy" (item 3). The attempt tests all of them.
- Application: item 1 transfers the brake to a tablet (new source, same sensing), item 2 separates making milk from moving milk, item 3 corrects a sentence and asks the student to classify both loops with reasons. Feedback uses the same pictures.
- Memory aids: "oxytocin squeezes" and "prolactin is pro-lactation" sit after the explanation, and the loop-icon strip rebuilds the positive loop in four words. The silent "who does what" strip keeps four hormones separate, with the same colours used throughout the video.
- Reading load: fine in explanation scenes. The two-card explain answer builds one sentence at a time. Key notes are the heaviest page (should-fix 9).
