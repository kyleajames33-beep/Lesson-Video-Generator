## Main question and useful outcome

*Why don't all crosses give 3 to 1, and how do you tell which pattern you're looking at?* Afterwards a student should be able to explain why X-linked recessive traits show in males with one copy, build X-linked crosses with sons and daughters read separately ("of all children" against "of sons"), read the X-linked pedigree pattern and rule it out with an affected daughter of an unaffected father, tell complete, incomplete and co-dominance apart by the heterozygote, and explain and predict ABO crosses.

## Designer's open questions, decided

- **Split fallback:** one video. B9 already taught the square, ratios and pedigree method, so each pattern needs only its new idea; the "why not 3 : 1?" question ties them; runtime about 9.6 minutes. The B10a/B10b fallback is kept in the design only as a record.
- **"Small i" TTS:** keep the words "small i". The letter and the pronoun sound the same ("eye"), and "small" carries the case, so nothing depends on letter case. The doubled form is removed (L3). Check at preview.
- **Roan as co-dominance:** keep. The site, HSC convention and the bank use it, and the hair-level argument (each hair fully red or fully white) is the correct reason; the "incomplete at coat level" view stays an author note.
- **X-linked dominant:** not taught here (route stop rule); flag for G02.

## Retained successful moves

The empty "white-eyed females 0" column; daughter and son bands on the square; the two brackets for "all children" against "sons"; the hair zoom beside the petal wash; enzyme-per-allele antigen marks; the A x B surprise square.

## Verdict

Pass for recording after Revision 2. Repeat on the exact voiced preview, with attention to scene 4 (507 characters) and the pronunciation of "small i" and "haemophilia".
