Main question traced: *Banana and pineapple flavourings often get their smell from esters, and each ester is made from a carboxylic acid and an alcohol. How do the two halves join and how is the ester named, why does the reaction never quite finish, and how do you push the yield up and get the ester out?* Useful outcome: given an acid and an alcohol, the student can write the esterification equation with ⇌ and conditions, name the ester and work back from an ester to both reactants, explain an ester's low boiling point, explain why the yield stops short and which changes raise it (and which only speed it up), and outline how the ester is separated and checked.

## Scope and focus

One question, with four connected parts that follow the life of one reaction (join, name, yield, isolate). That is reasonable at 9 minutes. The flow chart (scene 10) is the one part outside the main question; it is short and uses only taught steps, so it does not crowd the lesson, but it should be signposted (finding 9). Boundary is right: the practical procedure and risk table go to p05, saponification and fats to c12, and K calculations stay in author notes. Rate and yield are given separate scenes, which is the right answer to the pack's main trap. No double teaching: reflux (c06), oxidation (c08a), hydrogen bonding (c05), the "-oate" ending and fizz test (c09), Le Chatelier (M5 C03) and catalysts (M5 C04b) are each recalled in a sentence or grey chip.

## Strengths to keep

- **The condensation join shows where the water comes from**, on the two real reactant structures, before the word "condensation" is given its meaning.
- **"Ethyl butanoate and butyl ethanoate have the same formula, but they're different molecules"** turns the site's pineapple error into the naming point, with the cut line making the swapped halves visible.
- **The cut rule** ("Find the C double bond O, and cut at the single-bonded oxygen next to it. The side with the C double bond O came from the acid") is concrete and tested at once in attempt 1.
- **The equal-mass boiling point strip** (77, 138, 164 °C at 88 g/mol) isolates the cause, as c09 did.
- **The yield bar with a grey ghost** makes the shift from two thirds to nine tenths visible and comparable.
- **Hazards are said once, plainly**, with the reason (corrosive, flammable, so no flame).
- **Both attempts are HSC-shaped and transfer** to new esters, with one crossed mistake each.
