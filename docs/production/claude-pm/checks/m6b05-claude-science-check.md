## Facts confirmed (no change)

- **Jolie's own account.** Angelina Jolie, "My Medical Choice", The New York Times, 14 May 2013 (https://www.nytimes.com/2013/05/14/opinion/my-medical-choice.html): "I carry a 'faulty' gene, BRCA1, which sharply increases my risk of developing breast cancer and ovarian cancer" and "My doctors estimated that I had an 87 percent risk of breast cancer and a 50 percent risk of ovarian cancer, although the risk is different in the case of each woman." The hook's "her doctors estimated her breast cancer risk at eighty-seven per cent" matches her wording and attributes the figure correctly. She does not say "inherited", but a *BRCA1* variant found by a blood test is germ-line, and she wrote of her mother's death from cancer at 56; "She'd inherited it" is a sound inference. The video gives no general carrier risk, which is right (site correction 1). Reprints carrying the same quotations: https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/national-international/angelina-jolie-i-had-double-mastectomy-to-prevent-breast-cancer/2079364/
- **"A raised risk, not a certainty".** *BRCA1* breast cancer penetrance is high but incomplete: cumulative risk about 72 per cent to age 80 (Kuchenbaecker et al., JAMA 2017;317:2402). The tag is cut in Revision 2 for screen load; the "her doctors' estimate" caption keeps the point.
- **Somatic patch by mitosis; earlier means bigger.** A somatic mutation is carried by the descendants of the mutated cell only (a clone); sun-exposed normal skin carries many such clones (Martincorena et al., Science 2015;348:880). An earlier embryonic mutation gives a larger mosaic patch. Correct as a tendency.
- **Cancer needs several mutations in division-control genes.** Correct (oncogenes and tumour suppressor genes accumulating in one lineage).
- **UV and "most skin cancers".** Cancer Council Australia: about 95 per cent of melanomas and 99 per cent of non-melanoma skin cancers in Australia are caused by UV exposure, cancer.org.au. Correct.
- **Germ-line route.** A mutation in a spermatogonium can be carried by many sperm; a zygote from such a sperm has it in every cell, including its own germ line. The parent's body is unaffected; a heterozygous offspring may show nothing if the allele is recessive. Correct.
- **Coding fraction.** Protein-coding sequence is about 1 to 1.5 per cent of the human genome (International Human Genome Sequencing Consortium, Nature 2004;431:931). "About one to two per cent": correct.
- **"Once written off as junk".** Ohno coined "junk DNA" (1972); the term was later debated (ENCODE 2012 and its critics). Correct as history.
- **Promoter mutations.** Promoter mutations reduce transcription with a normal coding sequence (for example *HBB* -28 A>G, beta-plus thalassaemia). The simplified "polymerase binds less well" is fine at this level; the author note on transcription factors is right.
- **Regulatory sequences far from the gene.** Enhancers act from thousands to about a million bases away and set timing and tissue. Correct.
- **Lactase persistence.** The -13910 C>T variant (rs4988235), about 14 kb upstream of *LCT* in an intron of *MCM6*, is associated with persistence in Europeans and acts as an enhancer; the lactase protein is unchanged; other variants in the same region underlie persistence in some African and Middle Eastern populations, so "In many of them" is right. Enattah et al., Nat Genet 2002;30:233; Olds and Sibley, Hum Mol Genet 2003;12:2333; Tishkoff et al., Nat Genet 2007;39:31. "Most mammals stop making lactase after weaning": correct.
- **Introns and the cut signal.** Changes deep in an intron are usually neutral; changes at the splice donor or acceptor sites cause exon skipping or intron retention (several beta-thalassaemia alleles, such as IVS1-1 G>A). Correct.
- **"No known job".** Estimates of the constrained (functional) fraction of the human genome are roughly 8 to 15 per cent (Rands et al., PLoS Genet 2014;10:e1004525). "Changes there are often neutral": suitably hedged.
- **Fly somatic eye patch (13a).** X-irradiating *Drosophila* larvae produces clones of white facets in the adult eye, because eye imaginal disc cells are still dividing; this is the basis of the white/white+ eye-spot somatic mutation and recombination test, in which X-rays are a standard positive control (Vogel and Nivard, Mutagenesis 1993;8:57). The author note is right that a single mutation shows only where no working *white* allele remains (a male's single X, or a heterozygous female); the card names no gene or sex and no demand depends on it. A mutation in a sperm-producing cell leaves fly B's body unchanged, and only offspring from sperm descended from that cell carry it ("some offspring"). Correct.
- **Attempt 2 codons.** By script: CUU Leu, CUC Leu (silent); the Revision 2 distractor rows CCU Pro and UUU Phe are also computed. The promoter's tenfold drop is a given, not a claim about a real gene. Correct.
- **Memory aid "Somatic stays with you. Germ line can go to your kids."** Correct for animals reproducing sexually. Its two limits are handled: plants (a mutated shoot can flower; somatic mutations reach seeds and cuttings) are spoken in scene 12, and "can" carries the one in two chance. The site correction 6 wording is right.
- **Memory aid "Non-coding doesn't mean unimportant."** Correct: promoters, enhancers and splice signals are non-coding. Its limit (some non-coding DNA has no known job) is said in scene 9 before the aid.
- **Site corrections 1 to 9** in the design are scientifically right (87 per cent is her own estimate; the patent ruling covered isolated natural DNA; the testing study was a US insured population; an inherited allele can still be lost by drift or selection; no universal coding against non-coding ranking; a testing rise is not evidence of heritability; the plant exception; BRCA1 is not a UV skin-cancer gene and inheritance is by chance; the plant caveat on "disappears when the individual dies").
- **Em dashes:** none in the design.
