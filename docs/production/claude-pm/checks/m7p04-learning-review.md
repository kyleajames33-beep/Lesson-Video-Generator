Main question: what evidence identifies the product after dichromate turns green? From c08a and c08b, trace propan-1-ol through prompt distillation to propanal, then compare its mirror and brick-red results with propanone and the acid. The water blank tests warming alone; the flask versus distillate comparison teaches validity.

Keep the real apparatus, vapour return, one-product-per-scene sequence, test-pattern table and short transfer attempts. The tertiary comparison correctly explains why it has four carbons. Prior reaction content is recalled rather than re-taught.

## Findings

1. **Should fix. Scene 9.** "Run each test on a reference sample, such as glucose" can imply glucose is the acid reference. Show glucose only beside Tollens' and Benedict's, known dilute acid beside litmus, and water as the negative control. Replace the unconditional inference with "A positive glucose control shows those aldehyde-test reagents work." A negative test is not proof of absence under every sample condition.
2. **Should fix. Scene 11.** Nine long risk rows and hazard codes cannot be read at a 2 s final hold while earlier rows dim. The notes only repeat a short summary. Keep one hazard-harm-control row bright during speech and offer a quiet optional full-table pause afterwards. Students must understand the control, not merely spot its icon.
3. **Should fix. Scenes 8 to 10.** "All three turned dichromate green" confuses reacting starting alcohols with products being identified, and "organic product only" falsely teaches purity after simple distillation. Use "The starting alcohols reduced dichromate; the product tests distinguish the outcomes" and "distillate: product and water, separated from the non-volatile reagents".
