Main question: how does hard water change the agents' action, and how does a fair test show it? From c12, students can follow calcium binding two stearate ions, scum forming and less surfactant remaining. Matched tubes, controls and a second observable make this practical useful beyond the earlier micelle lesson.

## Findings

1. **Blocker. Scenes 5 to 8 and 17.** The design promises clear liquid, unchanged SDS froth and a long-lived emulsion in extremely hard water after adding very little pure SDS. The primary-source check establishes calcium dodecyl sulfate precipitation as a real room-temperature limitation. The final SDS concentration is about 0.85 mM, not 1%. Students obtaining precipitate would be taught to reject their observation. Pilot the exact agent, dilution, temperature and tubes before fixing the illustrative results and plate reuse. If another formulation or hardness is needed, test it first. Do not invent replacement observations.
2. **Should fix. Scene 8.** "An emulsion: micelles holding tiny oil droplets apart" conflates a visible emulsion droplet with a micelle. Repair: "Surfactant particles coat tiny oil droplets, with tails in the oil and heads in the water. The coating helps keep the droplets apart." Show coated droplets merging when too little surfactant remains. Do not call each large droplet a micelle.
3. **Should fix. Scene 7.** "Tests cleaning itself" overclaims an oil-separation timer. It measures emulsion stability in a tube, not oil removed from fabric. Say "tests how well the mixture keeps oil dispersed" and label the dependent variable accordingly. Keep the useful point that froth alone is not cleaning performance.
4. **Should fix. A1 and feedback.** Unknown Y is inferred to contain calcium or magnesium, then feedback declares calcium stearate. Accept either corresponding ionic equation and state which ion is used, or specify calcium in the question. The lesson should not penalise the other hardness ion it taught.

## Evaluation and practice

Means 6.0, 0.6, 6.4 and 6.1 cm and ranges 0.4, 0.3, 0.3, 0.3 cm are correct. Matching shaking and 30 s reading times is sensible. Adding calcium chloride also changes chloride and ionic strength, so "only calcium differs" needs repair. All four agent-water combinations and two blanks are necessary. A2 tests unequal dose and uncharacterised formulations; repeats alone cannot fix them. Investigate an outlier and report the reason, rather than discarding it because it disagrees. Give the detailed risk table a real reading pause if required as student notes. The reused plates were inspected but owner acceptance was not established from the brief107 hand-in.

Counts: **1 blocker, 3 should fix, 0 optional**.
