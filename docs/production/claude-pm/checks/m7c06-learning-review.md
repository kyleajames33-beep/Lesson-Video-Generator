## Main question and the useful outcome

Main question (as stated): "The ethanol in this bottle could have started as ethene from crude oil, as a haloalkane in a flask, or as sugar fermented by yeast. How can we make ethanol, and how do the conditions and the separation help us judge each route?"

Useful outcome: a student can write the equation and conditions for each route, explain each condition (pressure, catalyst, temperature; aqueous hydroxide and reflux; warmth, air excluded, gas vented), say how the ethanol is separated in each case, and weigh the routes on feedstock, energy, rate and purity.

One main question: yes. The route board, the three judging points set in the hook ("start", "conditions", "separation") and the scene 10 comparison hold the three routes together. No split recommended.

## Findings

### S1. Should fix: scenes 2 and 3 re-teach c04a's equilibrium arrow and pressure reason

- Exact text (scene 2): "This reaction is **reversible**: ethanol can break back down into ethene and steam. So, from Module five, it's written with an **equilibrium** arrow, two half arrows, not a single one." Scene 3: "At three hundred degrees, all three substances are gases. Two gas molecules make **one**, so high pressure shifts the equilibrium towards ethanol. The catalyst ..."
- Student question: "We did this last time. Is there something new here?" c04a scene 7 already said it, with ⇌ and (g) state symbols on screen.
- Replacement, scene 2's last two sentences: "Remember, it's reversible, so it's an **equilibrium**, written with two half arrows. Two gas molecules become **one**, so high pressure pushes it towards ethanol." (−22 characters.)
- Replacement, scene 3's opening: "Two more conditions matter for judging this route. The catalyst ..." (−92 characters; the rest of scene 3 unchanged.)
- Visual: move the "2 gas → 1 gas" merging dots and chip into scene 2 on "one", with a small grey "from the addition video" chip; scene 3 opens on the catalyst pulse. Keep the "(g) + (g) ⇌ (g)" state symbols on the strip.
- Design text: in the scope line, replace "adds what that video left out: the equilibrium arrow, the recycle loop and the separation" with "adds what that video left out: the catalyst's role, the temperature compromise, the recycle loop and the separation".
- Understanding enabled: hydration time goes to the judging ideas c04a did not teach.
- Note for root (outside this file): c04a key note 3 writes hydration with "→" although c04a scene 7 draws ⇌; c06's key note 1 uses ⇌. Consider aligning c04a key note 3.

### S2. Should fix: replace A1's premise (see science B1)

- Exact text (scene 12 and question card): "A student mixes glucose solution with yeast in a conical flask and keeps it at 35 °C, but leaves the flask open to the air. After two days, very little ethanol has formed. Explain this result, ..."
- Student question (from a student who has seen open-topped brewing, or tries it): "My open flask still made alcohol. Why?" Brewer's yeast ferments even with oxygen present at these sugar levels, and an open flask quickly fills with its own CO₂.
- Replacement question card: "A student keeps two flasks of glucose solution and yeast at 35 °C. Flask A is fitted with a bung and delivery tube. Air is bubbled through flask B the whole time. After two days, flask B contains less ethanol than flask A. Explain this difference, and write a balanced equation for the fermentation of glucose."
- Replacement narration (scene 12): "Your turn. A student keeps two flasks of glucose solution and yeast at thirty five degrees. Flask A has a bung and delivery tube. Air is bubbled through flask B the whole time. After two days, flask B holds less ethanol than flask A. Explain this difference, and write a balanced equation for the fermentation of glucose. Pause here to answer." (343 characters, +62.)
- Replacement feedback 1, first three sentences: "Air was bubbled through flask B, so its yeast had **oxygen**. With oxygen, yeast can respire aerobically, breaking some sugar down fully to carbon dioxide and water, so less ethanol forms. The temperature was the same in both." (Feedback 394 characters, about 29.6 s.) Underline "Air is bubbled through flask B", "less ethanol" and "at 35 °C" on their cues; chip "O₂ in → some sugar respired → CO₂ + H₂O, less ethanol". The crossed "yeast as a reactant" stays.
- Understanding enabled: the student explains a controlled comparison that would really occur, using the hedged oxygen cause.

### S3. Should fix: "fast" straight after "only a few percent reacts"

- Exact text (scene 4): "It's fast and continuous, but the ethene comes from **crude oil**."
- Student question: "If only a few percent reacts each time, how is it fast?"
- Replacement: "The plant runs nonstop, so it's fast and continuous, but the ethene comes from **crude oil** or natural gas." (+42 characters; the feedstock change is science M1.) Feedstock tag unchanged ("non-renewable feedstock").
- Understanding enabled: "fast" is tied to continuous throughput, not to one pass.

### S4. Should fix: feedback 2 does not say why the mixture is heated

- Exact text (scene 15): "Reflux lets you heat it without losing the volatile liquids: the condenser returns their vapours."
- Student question: "Why heat it at all?" A2 asks the student to "explain why the mixture is heated under reflux", and scene 6 taught both halves.
- Replacement: "Heating speeds it up, and reflux stops the volatile liquids escaping: the condenser returns their vapours." (+9 characters; feedback about 28.9 s.) Tag "heat: faster; condenser: no vapour lost".
