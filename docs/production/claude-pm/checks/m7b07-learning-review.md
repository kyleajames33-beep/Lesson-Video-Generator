Main question traced: *Your body can make antibodies that fit a bacterium it has never met. How does it find the right B cells, and how do the antibodies they make target that bacterium?* Useful outcome: the student can explain that fitting B cells exist before infection, that the antigen selects them, that they clone and become plasma cells releasing antibodies of the same binding shape, and how those antibodies help remove the pathogen; and can use this to explain a serum clumping result and reject a "design" claim.

## Strengths to keep

- **The hook sets up the right puzzle.** "So how does it design them in time?" then Burnet's answer, "it doesn't design anything", makes selection the surprise that the rest of the video earns.
- **One B cell followed from library to clone.** The painted B cell becomes the bare partner with coded triangle-tip receptors in place, is selected, divides (aligned dividing plate), and its clone keeps the triangle tips into the plasma cells' antibodies. Students can track "same shape" visually from receptor to antibody.
- **"Receptor = antibody held in the membrane" (scene 4)** is what makes "same binding shape" in scene 8 make sense. Good addition beyond the site.
- **Why a first response takes days is a cause, not a fact.** "A few rare cells have to multiply into thousands before there are enough to matter", backed by the clone tree and a day axis, and then tested in 13c.
- **Antibody actions as three panels with mechanisms.** Block (covering the attachment threads from b03), clump (two arms), coat (phagocyte receptors for the stem, linked to b06). The crossed "antibodies kill bacteria" is answered by what they actually do.
- **The shape model is labelled as a model** at each first use, and scene 3 shows three kinds of antigen on one bacterium.
- **Both attempts are genuine transfer**, each feedback brief with one crossed mistake.
