# hms-y11-m2-v09 science check (ATP-PCr and glycolytic systems)

Revision 2 design checked 24 November 2026 (source-only: script, on-screen text, practice, key notes, memory aids and facts section; no build or playback). Independent check; the design was not edited.

## Confirmed
- ATP as the immediate fuel; about 2 s stored at maximal effort; resynthesis by three systems (2). Correct.
- PCr + ADP → ATP + creatine; 1 ATP per PCr; maximal intensity; about 50 per cent back in 30 s and most in 2 to 3 min, resynthesised aerobically; fatigue as PCr runs low (3, 4). Correct (Harris and others 1976, fast half-time about 21 to 22 s; Bogdanis and others 1995, about 65 per cent at 1.5 min after a 30 s sprint).
- Duration "about ten to twelve seconds" and 95 to 100 per cent: the usual HSC convention; laboratory data put PCr predominance nearer 6 to 10 s. Acceptable as stated.
- Glycolysis: glucose and glycogen; 2 ATP per glucose; high intensity; hydrogen ion accumulation as the taught fatigue cause; recovery 30 to 60 min for by-products, up to a day or more for glycogen (5). Correct as the school model.
- Lactate as a fuel, cleared within about an hour, faster with active recovery; delayed soreness from fibre damage after eccentric work (7). Correct.
- Shift, not a switch (8). Correct; glycolysis is active from the first seconds.
- 11a/11b (wrestler, 30 s): glycolytic, hydrogen ions; correct. 11c/11d (repeat sprints): mechanism correct (see finding 3 for numbers).
- Memory aids sound. No people; plates named correctly.

## Findings
1. **Blocker (scene 6 narration).** "The hundred is over before glycolysis has done much." Muscle biopsy studies show glycolysis supplies roughly 40 to 50 per cent of anaerobic ATP in a 6 to 10 s maximal sprint (Gaitanos and others, *J Appl Physiol* 1993, 75:712 to 719: muscle lactate rose markedly within the first 6 s sprint). The design's own scene 8 ("glycolysis, which was running all along ... even a hundred raises blood lactate") and facts section say the same. What the 100 m lacks is time for hydrogen ions to accumulate. Corrected: "The hundred is over before hydrogen ions have had time to build up much."
2. **Should fix (scene 5 narration and on-screen "no oxygen needed" tag line).** "Breaking it down is called glycolysis, and its early steps don't need oxygen." Glycolysis as a whole needs no oxygen; "early steps" implies its later steps do. Corrected: "Breaking it down is called glycolysis, and it doesn't need oxygen."
3. **Should fix (11d on-screen lines).** "3 min: PCr mostly restored (about 98%)" and "15 s: under a third restored (about 29%)" are model outputs shown as data. After repeated maximal sprints PCr recovery is slower than the model: about 85 per cent at 6 min after a 30 s sprint (Bogdanis and others, *J Physiol* 1995, 482:467 to 480). Corrected lines: "1 3 min: PCr mostly restored" and "2 15 s: well under half restored". Spoken "Fifteen seconds restores under a third" can stay as "Fifteen seconds restores well under half" to match the model answer.
4. **Note (scene 5, "leads from about ten seconds to two minutes").** HSC convention; measured contributions show aerobic and anaerobic supply equal at about 60 to 75 s of maximal work (Gastin, *Sports Med* 2001, 31:725 to 741). Acceptable as the syllabus convention; consistent with v10.
5. **Note (scene 6, "glycolysis supplies most of the energy" in a 400 m).** Measured 400 m contributions are about 40 per cent aerobic (Spencer and Gastin 2001; Duffield and others 2005), so glycolysis gives the largest share but not a clear majority. "the largest share of the energy" is exact.
6. **Note (scene 4, "nothing has built up").** Inorganic phosphate also builds up and contributes to sprint fatigue (facts section already records it as an author note). Acceptable.

## Verdict
Fail until fixed. 1 blocker, 2 should fix, 3 notes.
