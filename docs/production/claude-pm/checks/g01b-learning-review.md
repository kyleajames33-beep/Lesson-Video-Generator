## Main question and what a student should be able to do

Main question: "When a question hands you a sequence or a cell you have never seen, what do you check first so the trace comes out right?" Afterwards a student starting Year 12 should, for a new case, ask which strand, which end and what is being counted, and then carry the trace through. The three-row checklist is a good, reconstructable anchor, and each question exercises at least one row.

## Route position and entry knowledge

Playlist order: b06c, p01, **g01**, b07, b08. So B8 (linkage and recombination frequency) comes after this video. Traced each question from its entry knowledge:

- **Q1 (copying error).** Fully supported by B4 scene 6a/6b, which shows the same G-opposite-T mismatch and says "one new copy has a changed base pair and the other is normal". Followable. Note: Q1 is close to a recall of B4's own case, so it is a warm-up rather than a transfer item. Acceptable as Q1.
- **Q2 (coding strand written 3' to 5').** Supported by B6b (coding versus template, "same letters, in the same direction", the "which strand" trap) and B6c (chart, AUG, stop codons). One sentence conflates the mRNA's 5' end with the strand's 5' end (should fix 1).
- **Q3 (koala counts and DNA mass).** Chromosome and chromatid counts are B5a/B5b. DNA mass per cell is new, but B5b scene 9 already said "a gamete cannot keep doubled chromosomes, because it would carry twice the DNA of one set", and 4b states the rule before using it. Followable.
- **Q4 (sweet pea, one crossover).** Part (a) is supported by B5b (non-sister chromatids swap matching pieces; one crossover per pair drawn between two genes). Part (b) is not: B5b held back linkage and recombination frequency, and never says that crossovers fall at different places in different cells, so some cells have none between two given genes. The walkthrough supplies that idea for the first time in 5d. During the protected attempt a student has no basis for the second half of the answer (should fix 2).

## Difficulty and HSC standard

Rises as claimed: Q1 one trace, Q2 three stacked checks, Q3 two quantities through three stages, Q4 a chromatid picture plus a reasoned limit. Q2 to Q4 sit at HSC band 5 to 6 short-answer standard. Q1 is easy but a fair warm-up.

## Walkthroughs: one stage at a time, one crossed mistake each

Each question has three walkthrough scenes with one stage each (copy 1, copy 2; strand/end, chart/stop; rules and S phase, divisions; chromatids, cells, limit). Each ends with exactly one spoken crossed-out classic mistake (2, reading from the left, 3.5 pg after meiosis I, all four new). The extra unspoken crossed chips in 3d and 4d are secondary and small; acceptable, but do not let them land at the same moment as the spoken cross. No walkthrough re-teaches a core: each rule is one clause with a grey tag.

## Memory aids

- "mRNA is a copy of the coding strand, built on the template strand." Accurate; recalled exactly where it decides Q2.
- "Centromeres count the chromosomes; chromatids count the DNA." (new) Accurate and useful: it is the exact move Q3 needs and it generalises to mitosis questions. Its condition tag on the notes screen is narrower than necessary (minor 4).
- "Meiosis I splits the pairs. Meiosis II splits the copies." Accurate; used after both divisions are shown.

## Reading load

Manageable. Heaviest moments: Q2 (15 tiles plus the full 64-cell chart; the chart is needed) and Q4's card (two plates, chromosome stimulus, key strip, three givens, two demands). The fix to Q4 below adds one given line; to compensate, drop the "assume no mutation" line (mutation is irrelevant to a single meiosis question at this level) so the card stays at three givens.

## Findings

### Should fix

1. **Scene 3b, which end.** Quoted: "**Which end?** The mRNA is read from its 5' end, and here the 5' end is on the **right**. So read the strand from right to left: **A U G**, U G G, A A A, U A A, G G C."
   Problem: "the 5' end is on the right" refers to the coding strand, but the sentence is about the mRNA's end. A student asks "the mRNA isn't on screen yet, so whose 5' end is on the right?" It also says "read the strand" and then speaks U, which is not on the DNA strand.
   Replace with: "**Which end?** The mRNA runs the same way as the coding strand, so start at the strand's 5' end, on the **right**. Read right to left, T as U: **A U G**, U G G, A A A, U A A, G G C."
   Enables: students link the direction rule to B6b's "same letters, in the same direction" instead of a memorised "read from the right".

2. **Q4 part (b) needs a condition that has not been taught (5a card and narration, 5d).** Quoted (5a): "Then explain why, across this plant's pollen, new combinations are much **less common than half**." Quoted (5d): "And in many cells, no crossover lands **between these two genes**, so all four keep the original combinations."
   Problem: B5b did not teach that crossover position varies between cells (linkage is B8, which comes after G01). During the attempt a student can reach only half the answer. The route's stop rule asks for model limits after a protected attempt, so the condition must be on the card.
   Replace 5a sentence with: "Then, if most anther cells have no crossover between these genes, explain why new combinations are much **less common than half**." Card: replace the given "assume no mutation" with "most other anther cells: no crossover between these two genes"; demand (b) becomes "(b) Using the givens, why are new combinations much less than half?".
   Replace 5d sentence with: "And as the card says, most cells have no crossover **between these two genes**, so all four keep the original combinations."
   Enables: students combine "only two of four change" with the stated condition, and B8 still owns frequencies and the reason crossovers vary.

### Minor

3. **Scene 2b.** Quoted: "A T and a G don't fit as a proper pair, so this molecule has a **mismatch**." A G·T pair does form (wobble), so "don't fit" invites a sharp student's objection. Replace with: "A T and a G aren't partners, so they pair badly, and this molecule has a **mismatch**."
4. **Notes condition tag.** Quoted: "after copying, before the chromatids separate". The aid also holds before copying and after separation (each single chromosome is one chromatid's worth of DNA); the tag implies it fails there. Replace with: "a single, unpaired chromosome counts as one chromatid".
5. **Hook.** Quoted: "a sequence written backwards". A strand written 3' to 5' is not backwards; the mistake is reading it from the wrong end. Replace with: "a sequence written from its 3' end".
6. **Notes, crossover line.** Quoted: "One crossover changes two of the four chromatids: one meiosis gives two original and two new combinations." Add the location condition: "One crossover between two genes changes two of the four chromatids: two original and two new combinations."
7. **Scene 3b visual.** "The strand then flips in place (a smooth half-turn...)" A literal half-turn turns letters upside down. Prefer: the tiles slide and re-order right to left into the mRNA row, keeping the "5'" tag attached to the A end.
8. **Q3 answer lines.** The givens say "about 7.0 picograms"; keep "about" on the result lines ("about 3.5 pg") because X-bearing and Y-bearing sperm differ slightly (see the science check).

## What works (keep)

The three-row checklist and its decision board; the "each strand is its own template" family-tree line in 2c; showing the mismatch as crooked rather than as a changed pair; the count table as the one retained result in Q3, with the unspoken 4 x 3.5 = 14.0 check; the outer two rods tagged "not touched" in 5b; organism plates that identify each new context without decoration standing in for a diagram.
