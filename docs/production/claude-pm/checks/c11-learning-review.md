## Main question and useful outcome

*When an ionic solid dissolves, what pulls its ions out, and why do some salts make the water hot while others make it cold?* Afterwards a student should be able to: describe hydration with ion-dipole attraction, write NaCl(s) → Na+(aq) + Cl-(aq), explain a temperature change with both energy steps, say why an endothermic salt can still dissolve, explain saturation as equal rates (with tracer evidence), and predict the effect of adding solid, adding water and heating.

## Trace from entry knowledge

- **Hook to hydration (1 to 4).** Year 11 lattice and polar water are recalled in one sentence each. The corner-ion beat gives a real cause for which ion leaves first. The equation scene recalls C10's "dissociation" in one clause. Followable.
- **Energy (5 to 7).** Step one "costs energy because opposite charges attract", step two "released when water clings": both causes are stated before the names. The hot-pack and cold-pack pair and the NaCl near-cancel make the sign rule concrete. Scene 7 uses G01's two factors in one clause, and stops at "spreading out wins" with no ΔG numbers. Good.
- **Saturation (8a to 10).** The C2 rates-meeting picture is reused correctly: steady dissolving, rising crystallising. The tracer gives evidence, and the added-solid trap uses C5 and C8 in one clause each.
- **Attempts.** A1 (NH4Cl cooling) applies scenes 5 and 6a to an unseen salt. A2 (Revision 1 A3: adding water with solid left) applies Q against K. Every condition stays visible during the countdown and nothing answer-bearing appears.
