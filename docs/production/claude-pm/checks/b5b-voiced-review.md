## Student learning trace (entry knowledge: B5a mitosis, B1 gametes carry one set, B4 DNA copying)

- Main question (hook): "How does a cell halve its chromosome number, and make gametes that differ from each other?" The learner outcome: explain meiosis I versus meiosis II, count chromosomes and chromatids at each stage, and compute 2^n combinations.
- The reason for halving is shown with numbers (46, 92, 184) before the method. Prophase I pairing is drawn as the first difference from mitosis; crossing over is shown with letters so the student can see a new combination without a new allele (apart from SHOULD FIX 1, which leaves "allele" undefined at the point of use).
- The two divisions are tied to their jobs on screen ("centromere stays whole: chromatids not separated" in division one; "centromeres split" in division two), and the tracker with the "joined copies only" convention keeps the counting rule from B5a consistent.
- Combinations are built as a doubling, then 2^n with a real number; the honest chip "whole chromosomes only; crossing over adds more" says what is not included.
- The attempt gives a bounded two-part task with every condition visible and a 10 s countdown; feedback names four wrong chains for Q1 and three for Q2, each with its cause.
- The memory aid ("Meiosis I splits the homologous pairs ... Meiosis II splits the sister chromatids") is a plain contrast placed after the explanation and is applied in Q2 feedback, so it supports a new case.
- Reading load: most scenes show one stage at a time. The exceptions are the crossing-makes scene (SHOULD FIX 2) and the key notes page (SHOULD FIX 8).
