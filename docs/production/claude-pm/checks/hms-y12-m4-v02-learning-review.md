## Findings

1. **Should fix, scene 4 says elite tests must "catch a change smaller than normal day-to-day variation".** A change smaller than day-to-day variation cannot be told apart from noise, and scene 6 then tells students to "judge change against normal variation". A careful student will ask how a test can catch a change that is smaller than its own wobble. The site's point is that elite changes are small, so the test's own error must be smaller still. Fix: "precise enough that its own error is smaller than the small changes that matter at the top level" (same length), and relabel the elite precision cell "error smaller than a worthwhile change"; redraw the small noise band so the change sits outside it. Students should leave with one consistent rule: a change counts only if it is bigger than the test's normal variation.

2. **Should fix, 8a person B's answer is the body's own example.** Scene 5 works a netballer with "a big difference between legs on a single-leg hop", and the block card re-tests that hop. 8a's volleyball blocker returning from an ankle injury is answered with "a single-leg hop, comparing the injured side with the other". The case is new but the test and the reason (left against right) are given. Either keep scene 5's netballer and cut the hop from her profile (replace it with "a slow 20 m sprint" so the limiter is speed and power), or change person B to a decision the body has not shown: "A 15-year-old swimmer wants to know whether her eight-week aerobic block worked" (answer: repeat a timed 400 m or submaximal test with the identical protocol, because the decision is about change, not symmetry). Students should choose the test themselves.

3. **Should fix, scene 3 packs four new tests and a calculation into one scene (about 930 characters).** The student meets the component table, the one-rep max, the Yo-Yo test, the Wingate test and the fatigue index, and the fatigue-index calculation arrives as the last sentence with no settle. The calculation rule in `AGENTS.md` asks for the task, the equation and the givens shown one stage at a time. Fix: split at "The syllabus names two tests for a reason" into 3a (component first, the six rows) and 3b (Yo-Yo, Wingate, then the strip: givens, then "(1000 − 600) ÷ 1000", then "40%", each on its cue with a 1 s settle on the framed answer). No narration change.

4. **Note, the hook asks for three written answers with a one-second hold.** Add 2 s with the three slots lit; the design is about 567 s, so take the time from reserve cuts.

5. **Note, "specificity" means two things in this focus area.** Here it is a property of a test (matches the sport's demands); v06 teaches it as a training principle. Both meanings are fine, but add the word "test" to card 3 ("test specificity: matches the sport's demands") so v06's principle does not seem to be a re-definition.
