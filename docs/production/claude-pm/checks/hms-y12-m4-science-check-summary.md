## Blockers in full
None. Two should-fix items carry the most real-world risk and should be done first: v21's 8d (a concussed 15-year-old's return) and v22's hook (prohibited painkillers in competition).

## Should fix (one line each; full wording in the per-video files)
- v04 scene 2: below about 70 per cent of maximum heart rate the stimulus is "usually too small to keep improving the fitness of someone who already trains", not "too small to force adaptation" (ACSM).
- v04 scene 5: a 2,000 m rower "races well above the threshold, close to maximum aerobic capacity", not "just above the threshold".
- v05 8a/8b/marking: the 14-year-old trains "with a teacher supervising but no spotter"; the model answer must not present unsupervised training for a young teenager as safe (NSCA youth position; 2014 international consensus).
- v06 scene 4a and 8d: drop "however long you go"; the walk at 60 per cent of maximum heart rate "drives little new aerobic adaptation", not none.
- v16 scene 6: join the ranking to the junior caution ("For adults, caffeine and creatine probably rank above ... But our athlete is seventeen ...").
- v16 8c/8d: speak "seventeen-year-old" in the stem; feedback "At seventeen, food first: no caffeine tablet unless a doctor or sports dietitian advises it".
- v20 scene 5: concussion is "a brain injury from a knock to the head or a jolt through the body", not just "from a knock".
- v21 scene 2 (and 3): ice "eases pain and may help limit swelling"; add the grey tag "newer guidance also stresses protecting the injury, then early pain-free movement". RICER stays.
- v21 8d and marking: add that under the 2024 national guideline she cannot return to competition for at least 21 days, so Friday is out whatever the note.
- v22 hook: "a permitted painkiller", not "a strong painkiller" (narcotics and, since 2024, tramadol are prohibited in competition).
- v22 scene 2: cortisone injections "close to competition need an exemption" (all injectable glucocorticoid routes prohibited in competition since 2022).
