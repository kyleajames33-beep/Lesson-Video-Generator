**Verdict: should-fix** (0 blockers, 4 should fix, 3 notes).

The bag puzzle is a genuine surprise that most students get wrong, and "Ask what is resisting the movement" gives them a test they can reuse. Muscle bands whose length readouts are computed from the joint angles make "shortening", "lengthening" and "constant" visible rather than asserted. The fibre traits are explained as one cluster with one cause ("These fibres work with oxygen, so they need capillaries to deliver it, mitochondria to use it, and myoglobin to store it"), which is far better than a two-column list.

## Findings

1. **Should fix, scene 9 calls the hamstrings the antagonist just after scene 3 taught that they extend the hip.** Coming up from a squat the hip extends, so a student will ask how a hip extensor can be the antagonist. Add one clause: "The hamstrings are the antagonist at the knee, though at the hip they help the gluteus maximus." (about 3 s), and make the table row "hamstrings | antagonist at the knee; assist hip extension". Students should learn that a role belongs to a muscle at a joint, which scene 8's "These are roles, not muscles" already sets up.

2. **Should fix, scene 6 and scene 7 seem to contradict each other.** Scene 6: the muscle lengthens "because the resistance wins". Scene 7: "Eccentric work also produces the most force, so you can lower more than you can lift." A student asks how the strongest contraction can be the one that loses. Reword scene 6: "because the muscle lets the resistance win, slowly, while still pulling against it", and scene 7: "A muscle can brake a heavier load than it can lift." Students should picture eccentric work as controlled braking.

3. **Should fix, scene 6 gives away the hardest third of 11a.** Scene 6's eccentric examples are "lowering the curl, or landing from a jump"; 11a then asks about a rebound landing. Drop "or landing from a jump" (or use "lowering yourself into a chair"), so students must apply "ask what is resisting" to the landing themselves.

4. **Should fix, the fibre plate shows the two types in separate zones.** `hms-fibre-cross-section-v1.png` paints dark fibres grouped on the left and pale fibres on the right, while the narration says "Every muscle is a mix" and real muscle is a mosaic. A student may conclude that fast and slow fibres sit in different parts of a muscle. Either add a grey tag on first appearance, "real muscle mixes them like a mosaic", or ask Codex for a v2 with interspersed fibres. The recruitment lighting in scene 5 works either way.

5. **Note, scene 3's hamstring strain has no because.** "They cross two joints, one reason they're so often strained in sprinting" leaves the reason implicit. Add "because a sprint stride stretches them at both ends at once".

6. **Note, "isotonic" is never given a plain meaning.** Students will copy "isotonic concentric" without knowing what the first word adds. One gloss on screen is enough: "isotonic: changes length while moving a load".

7. **Note, the arm overlay is not yet painted** and carries scenes 1, 3, 7, 8 and 12; v03 depends on it too. 11d is about 31 s; trim "Give one similarity, and name events for each" to "Add a similarity and an event for each."
