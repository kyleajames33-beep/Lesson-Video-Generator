## Findings

1. **Should fix, scene 4 offers "it's surprising" as a sign of a real finding.** "B is the real finding. It's specific, it admits its own cost, and it's surprising." Surprise is not evidence (claim D, "visualising works as well as practising", is surprising too), and the design's stop line repeats it as a test. The site pairs the idea with its warning: "Claims that are convenient, round-numbered and flattering to the person repeating them deserve more scrutiny than claims that are awkward." Say: "It's specific, it admits its own cost, and it's inconvenient: it tells coaches the better session looks worse. Convenient, round-numbered claims deserve the most suspicion." Students should leave suspicious of claims that are easy to like, not trusting of claims that are surprising.

2. **Note, 8d uses a fact the card does not give.** The feedback's further question is "does this hold for children, not the adults studied?", but 8c's stem never says the study used adults. Either add "(from a study of adult beginners)" to the stem, or change the model question to "does this hold for children learning butterfly?".
