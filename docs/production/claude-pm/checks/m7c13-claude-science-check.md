## Facts confirmed (no change)

- **Formulas and molar masses (IUPAC atomic weights, by script).** Propyl ethanoate CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₃ and ethyl propanoate CH₃CH₂COOCH₂CH₃ are both C₅H₁₀O₂, 102.13 g/mol, and they are isomers. Propan-1-ol and propan-2-ol C₃H₈O; propanone C₃H₆O; 1- and 2-bromopropane C₃H₇Br; ethanal C₂H₄O. All correct.
- **Equations (each balanced by atom count).** Hydration C₂H₄ + H₂O ⇌ C₂H₅OH. CH₃CH₂OH + 2[O] → CH₃COOH + H₂O. CH₃CH₂OH + [O] → CH₃CHO + H₂O. C₃H₇Br + NaOH → C₃H₇OH + NaBr. CH₃COOH + CH₃CH₂CH₂OH ⇌ CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₃ + H₂O (C 5/5, H 12/12, O 3/3). CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ + [O] → CH₃COCH₃ + H₂O. CH₃CH₂CH₂OH + 2[O] → CH₃CH₂COOH + H₂O. CH₃CH₂COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ CH₃CH₂COOC₂H₅ + H₂O. C₃H₈ + Cl₂ → C₃H₇Cl + HCl. All balance as the design lists.
- **Conditions match the earlier designs.** Hydration (steam, H₃PO₄, about 300 °C, high pressure, about 60 to 70 atm, ⇌) matches c06. Haloalkane substitution (NaOH(aq), heat under reflux, same carbon) matches c06. Oxidation (acidified dichromate; distil for an aldehyde; excess oxidant and reflux for an acid; secondary to ketone; tertiary no reaction) matches c08a. Esterification (a few drops of conc. H₂SO₄, reflux, ⇌) matches c10. Amide ("heat strongly") matches c11 Revision 2. UV substitution gives a mixture (c04b). Industrial hydration at about 300 °C and 6 to 7 MPa over supported H₃PO₄ is right (Ullmann's, "Ethanol"; for Codex to open).
- **Worked example.** The route propyl ethanoate ← ethanoic acid + propan-1-ol; ethanoic acid ← ethanol ← ethene; propan-1-ol ← 1-bromopropane is valid, uses each starting material once, and is the shortest route from these starts (four reactions). The carbon tally (2 + 3) is right. The ester cut at the single-bonded O gives the acid and the alcohol correctly.
- **The flawed plan.** Propane → propan-2-ol in one step does not exist in the course. Propanone → propanoic acid does not exist. A ketone has no H on its carbonyl carbon, so acidified dichromate does not oxidise it. (Strong oxidants that cleave C–C bonds would give ethanoic acid and CO₂, not propanoic acid, so the arrow fails either way.) The detour through chloropropanes (a mixture, including polysubstitution) is right.
- **Attempts.** A1: 2-bromopropane → (NaOH(aq), reflux) propan-2-ol → (K₂Cr₂O₇/H⁺, heat) propanone. Correct, in two steps, and the crossed "propan-1-ol" is a real trap. A2: ethene → ethanol; propan-1-ol → propanoic acid (excess oxidant, reflux); esterify (⇌). This is the only route from these starts. The crossed route really does give propyl ethanoate, an isomer with the same formula. Every condition the answer needs is on each card.
- **Overall yield.** That each step loses product and fewer steps "usually" give a higher overall yield is right (author note f: 0.8 × 0.8 × 0.67 ≈ 0.43).
- **Pear odour.** n-Propyl acetate is widely described as having a pear-like odour (PubChem CID 7997, "pears" in flavour descriptions; for Codex to open). "Smells a lot like pears" is fairly hedged. Author note (h) is right that isopentyl ethanoate is the classic banana and pear-drop ester.
- **Absolute words.** "The only arrows that join two molecules" (true on this map); "No reaction we've met does that" (correctly scoped); "acidified dichromate can't oxidise it" (true); "the O H lands exactly where you need it" (true for primary substitution with aqueous hydroxide); "every synthesis question uses the same four moves" (a method claim, acceptable); "at all" (finding 5).
