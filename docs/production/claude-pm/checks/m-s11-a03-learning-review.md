## Should fix

**SF1. The "backyard pool" is 25 m by 10 m by 1.8 m, which is a public lap pool, not a backyard pool.** It holds 450 kL, about ten times a typical home pool, and the narration ends "which is why pools are filled by a water truck" (a 450 kL pool would need many truckloads). A student who has seen a real backyard pool will not believe the numbers, and the sense check the strand teaches works against the lesson. a09 reuses the same pool ("a backyard pool with a sloping floor", and "Remember the flat pool"), so fix both together. Fix: keep every number and call it "a twenty-five metre lap pool" (or "the school's twenty-five metre pool") in a03 scenes 5 and 6 and a09 scene 5. Replace the water-truck clause with "so filling a pool from a hose takes about two weeks" or drop it. The water-bill line can stay ("at the example price of two dollars seventy a kilolitre").

## Notes

- N1. Scene 2 says a 125 cc scooter "has cylinders that hold a hundred and twenty-five cubic centimetres". Engine size is the volume the pistons sweep, not what the cylinders hold. Say "an engine of a hundred and twenty-five cc, that's cubic centimetres" and keep the cc = cm³ = mL point, which is the useful part.
- N2. "A box you could sit in holds as much as a thousand cartons of milk" is a strong image; the faint grid of 1000 carton icons behind the cube must not compete with the counter. Check in the voiced preview.
