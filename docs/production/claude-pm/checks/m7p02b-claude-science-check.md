## Facts confirmed (no change)

- **Indicator.** *E. coli* is a gut bacterium whose presence signals faecal contamination, so faecal pathogens may be present. Correct. "If detected, immediate corrective action" supports scene 2's "decides safety" column. (finding 1 sources)
- **CFU.** "A cell, or a clump of cells, that grows into one colony." Correct.
- **Spread plating.** 0.1 mL delivered by pipette and spread with a sterile spreader; three plates per dilution. Correct. Pour plating (1 mL in molten agar) is not taught, which is acceptable; no line conflicts with it.
- **Aseptic technique.** Sterile pipettes and spreaders, lids lifted briefly, work beside a Bunsen. Correct as school practice. The tag "rising air carries dust away" is the standard school explanation.
- **Plate handling.** Label the base; two short strips of tape at opposite edges, not sealed all round, so oxygen still diffuses in and anaerobes are not favoured; incubate inverted so condensation does not drip on the agar; do not open after incubation; view through the lid. All match school guidance. (searched, finding 4 sources)
- **"Never opened" rule.** School guidance states lids must not be removed after incubation because spores and grown organisms could spread; full sealing is allowed just before viewing if there is a risk of opening. The design's rule is correct.
- **Controls.** Negative control: sterile diluent, handled the same, expect no growth; growth means the method added microbes. Positive control: a known risk group 1 culture, expect growth, showing the medium and conditions work. Correct. Not naming a species in learner copy is the right call.
- **Countable range reasons.** Above the range, colonies merge and crowding can inhibit growth, so the count runs low; below it, sampling chance is proportionally large. Correct (BAM gives the same reasons).
- **What colonies show.** Colonies show microbes culturable on this medium at this temperature; most environmental heterotrophic plate count bacteria are not pathogens; colony appearance on nutrient agar cannot identify a pathogen; viruses do not grow on agar. Correct.
- **Autoclave.** Steam under pressure at 121 °C (about 103 kPa above atmospheric); cycle time set by the procedure (at least 15 minutes at temperature, longer for loads). Learner copy gives no time, which is right.
- **Risk group 1 and PC1.** AS/NZS 2243.3:2022 is the current edition and classes microorganisms in risk groups 1 to 4, with physical containment levels PC1 to PC4; risk group 1 organisms are unlikely to cause disease and are handled at PC1. "The lowest risk group in the Australian standard" is correct. (finding 2 sources)
- **Ethanol 70 %.** Flash point below 23 °C: Flam. Liq. 2, H225. Correct.
- **Absolute words.** "can't show water is safe", "viruses won't grow on agar at all", "never thirty seven" and "never open" (school rules), "every count is in doubt" (if the negative control grows) are acceptable. "You can't spot a pathogen by looking at a colony" is true on nutrient agar. "work only with risk group one" is finding 2.
- **Memory aid.** "Label the base, tape don't seal, upside down, 25 to 30 °C": each assumption is true as a school rule, and the design states the probability wording ("would favour", not "prevents").
