## Main question and what a student should be able to do

Main question: "When a cross or a family tree is new to you, how do you choose the model, find the probability, and say what the evidence can and cannot prove?" The three first moves (choose the model, write the sure genotypes, combine the chances) are a sound, reusable routine, and each question uses them visibly.

## Entry knowledge trace

- **Q1 (Andalusian chickens).** B10 taught incomplete dominance with snapdragons and the 1 : 2 : 1 heterozygote cross; B9 taught ratios as chances per offspring. Multiplying for "and" is introduced in one sentence with its cause ("each chick is a separate chance"). Followable.
- **Q2 (tortoiseshell).** B10 taught X and Y, "Dad gives his X to daughters and his Y to sons", "of all children" against "of sons", and co-dominance. Followable.
- **Q3 (PKU).** B9 taught carriers, the trio giveaway and per-child chance. Conditional probability is new and is taught here as "cross out the box she can't be"; 4c explains it causally. The update in 4d asserts "That proves she's a carrier" without the cause (should fix 2).
- **Q4 (which pattern).** B10 taught the X-linked recessive pattern and the quick rule-out (affected daughter, unaffected father). B10 did not state "an affected mother has only affected sons"; it gave the parts ("each son's only X comes from his mother", "a female needs two copies"). Scene 5b uses that rule as the lead reason without the link (should fix 3). The rest of Q4 (no giveaway, both fit, rare implies dominant more likely) follows from B9's giveaways.

## Difficulty and HSC standard

Rises as claimed. Q3 (2/3 then the 1/4 update) and Q4 (rule out, both fit, more likely, not proven) are full HSC band 6 items. Q1 is a fair warm-up.

## Walkthroughs and crossed mistakes

One reasoning stage per scene, with the certain genotypes kept as the established result in Q3. One spoken crossed-out mistake per question (blue x blue for only blue; autosomal treatment; 1/2 carrier; "dominant because every generation"). Unspoken second chips are small and acceptable. No core is re-taught.

## Memory aids

- Recalled bank aids ("Incomplete is in-between. Co shows both.", "Dad gives his X...", the giveaways) are each used where they decide the step. Good.
- **"Cross out the box they can't be."** (new) Accurate under its stated condition (parents' genotypes known, boxes equally likely), memorable and causal. Useful; keep, with the notes condition tag.
- **"Separate births: multiply."** (new) Two problems. (1) Chicks hatch; "births" jars in the one question it is taught in. (2) It is true only when the parents' genotypes are known. Q3 is exactly the case where it fails: the chance both children have PKU is 2/3 x 1/4 x 1/4 = 1/24, not 1/6 x 1/6 = 1/36. A student who memorises the bare aid will make that error on the next HSC item (should fix 1).

## Reading load

Manageable per question. The decision board (six rows, 410 characters of narration) is the heaviest screen; it is a summary hold, not new reasoning, so acceptable. Q4's assumption line is long and unclear (minor 6).

## Findings

### Should fix

1. **The "multiply" aid (2c chip, 6 decision board, 7 key notes).** Quoted (key notes): "Separate births: multiply the chances." Quoted (6): "For **separate births**, multiply."
   Problem: wrong word for chicks, and no condition, so it teaches 1/36 for the Q3 family.
   Replace chip with: "Separate offspring, known parents: multiply." Key notes: "Separate offspring, known parents: multiply the chances." Scene 6: "For **separate offspring** of known parents, multiply." Decision-board row: "two offspring, parents known → multiply (Q1)". The 2c narration "each chick is a **separate chance**" can stay.
   Enables: students multiply only when each child's chance is fixed, and see in Q3 why an unknown parent breaks it.

2. **Scene 4d, the update needs its cause.** Quoted: "Now suppose their first child has P K U. That **proves** she's a carrier. So for the second child, the chance is simply **one in four**."
   Problem: likely student question, "why does one child prove it, and why does the chance go up rather than down?" One assertion does not answer it (the author's doubt (b)).
   Replace with: "Now suppose their first child has P K U. The child needed a recessive allele from her, so that **proves** she's a carrier. Now both are carriers, so the second child's chance is **one in four**."
   Enables: students see that new evidence changes what we know about a parent, not the child-by-child chance from known parents.

3. **Scene 5b, the X-linked recessive rule-out skips a link.** Quoted: "Individual 2 in generation one is an affected mother. Under that pattern, she'd pass an affected X to **every son**."
   Problem: B10 never stated this rule; a student asks "why every son?"
   Replace with: "Individual 2 in generation one is an affected mother. Under that pattern, both her X chromosomes carry the allele, so **every son** gets it."
   Enables: students derive the rule from "a female needs two copies" and "a son's X comes from his mother", which transfers to new pedigrees.

### Minor

4. **Scene 5c, "married in".** Quoted: "the two unaffected people who married in, individual 1 in generation one and individual 4 in generation two". I-1 is a founder, not someone who joined the family later, and "married in" is unexplained jargon. Replace with: "the two unaffected partners, individual 1 in generation one and individual 4 in generation two". Mirror in 5d: "two unrelated carriers marrying into one family" can become "two unrelated partners both being carriers".
5. **Scene 3b, tortoiseshell and "Co shows both".** The small bank line labels tortoiseshell as co-dominance. Each skin patch expresses only one allele (X inactivation), so this is a looser fit than ABO. Keep the line if desired (many HSC texts use the example), but pair it with the on-screen wording "both alleles show, in different patches".
6. **Q4 givens.** Quoted: "the trait always shows when its allele pattern is present". "Allele pattern" is unclear. Replace with: "anyone with the genotype for the trait shows it".
7. **Q1 implementation note (2b).** "computed by `cross` with a co-dominance-style map from B10's `CO('blue')`". Use B10's incomplete-dominance map so no code path or debug label can surface "co-dominance" on the incomplete-dominance question.
8. **Multiple alleles.** The route lists B-multiple-alleles for this video and no question uses it. B10 already practises ABO; the author's lightest option (a decision-board row only) is enough. Record on the board that G02 does not add multiple-allele practice.

## What works (keep)

Hiding the type badge during Q1 and Q2 attempts; the cross-out overlay on the Punnett grid for 2/3; the explicit partner certainty "x 1"; the mode-test table with named individuals; the unspoken "decisive future evidence" tag in 5d, which models what would settle the question; limiting Q4's choices to the three taught patterns.
