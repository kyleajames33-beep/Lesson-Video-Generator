# hms-y12-m4-v16 science check (supplements)

Revision 2 design checked 24 November 2026 (source-only: script, on-screen text in the visual lines, practice, key notes, memory aids and facts section; no build or playback; plates checked by name and description). Independent check; the design was not edited. Line numbers refer to `lessons/hms-y12-m4-v16.md` as of this check.

## Confirmed
- Food first and the three reasons (2). Consistent with the AIS Sports Supplement Framework (ausport.gov.au/ais/nutrition/supplements) and sports-nutrition consensus (Maughan and others, IOC consensus statement on dietary supplements, *Br J Sports Med* 2018).
- Micronutrients (3): iron in haemoglobin; higher deficiency risk in adolescent female and endurance athletes; no benefit when replete; excess iron harmful; test before supplementing. Correct (IOC 2018; Sports Dietitians Australia adolescent position statement, *Int J Sport Nutr Exerc Metab* 2014).
- Protein (3): no extra hypertrophy above an adequate intake (Morton and others, *Br J Sports Med* 2018). Correct; consistent with Year 11.
- Caffeine (4): central adenosine-receptor effect, lower perceived exertion, alertness; benefits across endurance, repeated sprints and concentration; anxiety, tachycardia, gut upset, sleep disruption (Guest and others, ISSN position stand, *J Int Soc Sports Nutr* 2021). Correct. Both caffeine and creatine are AIS Group A.
- Creatine (4): raises muscle creatine and phosphocreatine, more maximal work and faster phosphocreatine resynthesis between efforts (Greenhaff and others 1994); little help for long endurance; mass gain mostly water (Kreider and others, ISSN position stand 2017). Correct.
- Contamination, strict liability (World Anti-Doping Code, art. 2.1), batch testing reduces but does not remove risk, placebo (5). Correct.
- **Junior caution (flagged, line 73).** "under eighteen, supplements generally aren't recommended: food first, and performance supplements only on medical or dietitian advice." Consistent with the Sports Dietitians Australia position (nutrient needs of adolescent athletes "should be met by core foods rather than supplements", 2014) and with national sporting bodies applying the AIS framework (for example Athletics Australia's supplement policy, 2025 and 2026: it "does not encourage the use of supplements for athletes under the age of 18 years unless advised" by accredited sports medical professionals, "This includes Group A performance supplements"). The AIS framework pages reached did not state their own under-18 wording (see summary). The sentence is correct and suitably general.
- 8a/8b: correct (test first; flavoured milk as a food-based convenience). Memory aids sound. No people.

## Findings
1. **Should fix (line 73, scene 6; the hook at line 48).** The hook asks for a ranking for "a seventeen-year-old team-sport athlete", and scene 6 answers "Caffeine and creatine probably rank above protein powder and a multivitamin" before the junior caution, which is then said as a general rule. A student can come away ranking creatine first for this 17-year-old. Join the two: "So, the hook. For adults, caffeine and creatine probably rank above protein powder and a multivitamin. But our athlete is seventeen, and under eighteen, supplements generally aren't recommended: food first, and performance supplements only on medical or dietitian advice. And for every one, it depends on the athlete." (Reorder within the existing sentences; length about neutral.)
2. **Should fix (lines 93 and 98, 8c and 8d; line 124, marking).** The card says "A 17-year-old swimmer", but the spoken stem says only "A swimmer", and the feedback's fix is "trial it in training first, with medical advice", which treats a caffeine tablet as a normal option for a 17-year-old, contrary to the caution the video has just given. Spoken stem: "A seventeen-year-old swimmer plans to take ...". 8d: "A poor plan. At seventeen, food first: no caffeine tablet unless a doctor or sports dietitian advises it, and then trialled in training with a batch-tested product." On-screen line 4: "4 judgement: poor; under 18, advice first; trial in training, batch-tested". Marking line 4 matches.
3. **Note (line 63).** "Teenagers are generally advised to limit it" (caffeine) is correct; a common figure is up to about 2.5 to 3 mg per kg per day for adolescents (Health Canada; EFSA 2015), not needed on screen.

## Verdict
Pass with fixes. 0 blockers, 2 should fix, 1 note. The flagged junior wording is confirmed.
