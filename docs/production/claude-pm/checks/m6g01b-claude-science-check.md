## Facts confirmed (no change)

- **Q1 pairing.** Template 3' TAC GGT CTT AAA CCG 5' pairs to mRNA 5' AUG CCA GAA UUU GGC 3': Met, Pro, Glu, Phe, Gly. Mutant 1 (GGT to GGA, template T to A): CCA to CCU, Pro, silent. Mutant 2 (CTT to ATT, C to A): GAA to UAA, stop, chain Met Pro ("stops after two amino acids"). Mutant 3 (AAA to AGA, A to G): UUU to UCU, Ser, missense. Each mutant has exactly one changed base. The wrong method (template read as mRNA, T as U) gives UAC GGU CUU AAA CCG: Tyr, Gly, Leu, Lys, Pro, with no AUG. All by script. Correct.
- **Q2 sequences.** Normal AUG GCU AAC GGA UCU CAG UGG: Met, Ala, Asn, Gly, Ser, Gln, Trp. Mutant AUG GUA ACG GAU CUA CAG UGG: Met, Val, Thr, Asp, Leu, Gln, Trp. The exhaustive search finds **one solution only**: delete base 5 (the C in codon 2), then insert an A after normal base 15 (after codon 5, UCU). So "the first four bases match", "a C that the mutant doesn't [have] ... in codon two" and "just after codon five, where an extra A appears" are each exactly right. No stop codon arises in the shifted stretch. The deletion-only ghost row AUG GUA ACG GAU CUC AGU GG gives Met, Val, Thr, Asp, Leu, Ser, as the design states. Both lines are 21 bases. Correct.
- **Crick et al. 1961.** Crick, Barnett, Brenner and Watts-Tobin, "General nature of the genetic code for proteins", Nature 192:1227-1232 (link above). Using proflavin-induced mutants in the rII B cistron of bacteriophage T4, they found that combining a "plus" with a "minus" mutation restored function when the two were close, and that three pluses or three minuses also did, which supported a code read in threes from a fixed starting point. They did not sequence the gene, so the card's "sequences are illustrative" chip and the narration's "helped show" are right. The new plate of a T4 phage matches the organism.
- **Q3a, pericentric inversion.** Normal A B ● C D E F G; mutated A D C ● B E F G: the segment B ● C D is reversed with its centromere (a pericentric inversion); the same genes stay on the same chromosome; balanced. "Then the normal B, centromere, C, D reads D, C, centromere, B" is right letter by letter. A translocation needs a second, non-homologous chromosome. Correct.
- **Q3b, Turner syndrome.** 45,X (monosomy X) is a change in chromosome number; Turner syndrome affects about 1 in 2,500 newborn girls (MedlinePlus); most 45,X conceptions miscarry. A sex-chromosome nondisjunction in one parent's meiosis can give a gamete with no sex chromosome; fertilised by an X-bearing gamete it gives 44 autosomes and one X. Correct, with findings 1 and 2.
- **Q4a, Belgian Blue myostatin.** The double-muscled phenotype is caused by an 11-base deletion (nt821del11) in the third exon of bovine *MSTN*, which shifts the frame and causes a premature stop, truncating the protein in its bioactive C-terminal region (Grobet et al., Nat Genet 1997;17:71; Kambadur et al., Genome Res 1997;7:910, https://genome.cshlp.org/content/7/9/910 and https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9314496/; McPherron and Lee, PNAS 1997;94:12457). OMIA lists it as p.(D273Rfs*14): the stop is 14 codons into the shifted frame, so "a stop codon soon turns up" is right, and "missing the part that does its job" matches the lost active domain. Myostatin (GDF-8) limits skeletal muscle growth. Inheritance is partly recessive, so the card's "two copies" is needed and present. 11 ÷ 3 = 3 remainder 2. Correct.
- **Q4b, one muscle cell.** A somatic mutation in a cow's muscle cell is not in her germ line, so her calves cannot inherit it. The author note on multinucleate muscle fibres and satellite cells is right; "copied only into that cell's descendants" holds either way. Correct.
- **New memory aid "Align base by base, then read in threes."** Sound. Aligning letter by letter finds an insertion or deletion because codon-by-codon comparison makes every downstream codon look changed; the effect is then judged by reading the mutant in threes from the start codon. Assumptions (unchanged start codon, both sequences written in the same direction) are stated in the design. In a long repeat the exact position of an indel can be ambiguous, but the reading-frame conclusion is not; nothing in the video depends on that case.
- **Recalled aids.** "Substitutions: Silent, Missense, Nonsense", "Multiples of three don't shift the frame", "Non-disjunction: chromosomes fail to dis-join" and "Somatic stays with you. Germline goes to your kids." are each correct where used (the last for a cow, an animal).
- **Site errors 1 to 5** listed in the design are scientifically right (a nonsense substitution can shorten the chain; a frameshift need not change everything downstream; only balanced rearrangements keep the amount of DNA; the plant exception; an early nonsense substitution can be as damaging as a frameshift).
- **Em dashes:** none in the design.
