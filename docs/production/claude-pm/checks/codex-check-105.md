## bio-m7-b11

- **Scenes 2, 5 and 11, key notes and metadata:** The general rules `A drug has to hit something the pathogen has and your cells don't` and `Antivirals target virus-only steps` are too absolute. Maraviroc binds the human CCR5 receptor to prevent certain HIV strains entering cells. Define selective toxicity as harming the pathogen much more than the host. Say `Many antivirals target a viral enzyme or step; some block a host factor the virus needs.` The scene can keep oseltamivir as its virus-enzyme example. The invented virus V in the first attempt explicitly has its own enzyme, so its answer remains valid. Source: [FDA maraviroc label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/022128s011lbl.pdf).
- **Scene 6 narration, scene 10 table and key notes:** `Each one also works on only one virus, or a few related ones` is too absolute. Remdesivir has laboratory activity across several RNA-virus families, though approval and clinical benefit are disease-specific. Correct to `Most antivirals are narrow, although some act against viruses from several families; a drug still needs clinical evidence for each disease.` Source: [original remdesivir profiling study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9950143/).

## bio-m7-b12

- **Decision 18, author-only site note:** The design proposes changing the site's historical Curaçao figure from sterile males to sterile flies of both sexes. USDA's National Agricultural Library explicitly reports sterile males for that figure. Delete the proposed correction and do not add it to the site log. Source: [USDA National Agricultural Library collection introduction](https://www.nal.usda.gov/exhibits/speccoll/exhibits/show/stop-screwworms--selections-fr/introduction).
