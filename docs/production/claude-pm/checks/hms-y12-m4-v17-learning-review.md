## Findings

1. **Should fix, scene 3 re-teaches Year 11's force principles, and scene 6's functional example is Year 11's.** hms-y11-m2-v03 taught summation of forces ("body parts in sequence, largest to smallest ... legs, hips, trunk, then arm in a throw"), absorbing force by spreading it "over a longer time and a larger area. Bending your knees as you land buys time", and the box held close to the spine to cut torque. v17 recalls only levers, then teaches sequencing, absorption and bent-knee landing as new under new names ("force application", "kinetic chain", "force absorption"), and scene 6 uses the box lift as its functional movement. A Year 11 student hears about 45 s of repeat and two names for one idea. Fix: scene 3 opens "In Year 11 you met levers, summation of forces, largest to smallest, and absorbing force over more time and area. Here's what they do to energy cost." Keep the throwing chain and landing trace as recall with a grey "earlier" tag and Year 11's term "summation of forces" beside "kinetic chain", and narrate only the efficiency consequence (an arm-only throw needs more effort for less speed; a stiff landing raises peak force and the cost of the next stride). For scene 6's functional movement, use one Year 11 did not: "carrying the shopping in two bags, one in each hand, instead of one heavy bag on one side" (balanced load, less side-bending tension, less tiring over a long walk). Students should build on Year 11's principles rather than meet them again.

2. **Should fix, 8a is scene 6's first row.** Scene 2 names over-striding as force in the wrong direction ("the foot lands ahead and brakes you, so you pay twice"), scene 3 explains the stiff landing, and scene 6 works "a recreational runner who shortens an over-long stride lands under the hips, so braking drops". 8a's recreational runner who over-strides with a straight knee, answered "Shorten the stride, land closer under the hips", is that row. Replace with an unseen case that needs the same chain, for example: "A recreational rower pulls mostly with the arms, with little push from the legs, and tires within ten minutes. Explain how this technique reduces efficiency and raises injury risk, and recommend a change." (Force application out of sequence: small arm muscles do work the legs could, so more cost for less boat speed; early fatigue degrades technique and loads the lower back and shoulders; recommend leg drive first, then trunk, then arms.) Marks unchanged. Students should run the four-link chain on a movement the video did not analyse.

3. **Should fix, narration and screen name internal video numbers.** Scene 2 says "In v-eight, endurance improved by raising the ceiling" and scene 5 "the drafting tactic from v-thirteen"; the board shows grey tags "v08" and "v13". Students never see file ids; the brief says "earlier". Say "Earlier, endurance improved by raising the ceiling" and "the drafting tactic you met earlier".
