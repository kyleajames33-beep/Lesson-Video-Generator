## Main question and the useful outcome

Main question (as stated): "An acid solution at pH 2.52 is more acidic than one at pH 2.78, but its acid is the weaker one. How do Ka and pKa measure strength whatever the concentration, how do you get Ka from a measured pH, and what do they tell you about strong acids and conjugate bases?"

One main question: yes, with a caution. The question has several clauses, but they all answer the route question "what do Ka and pKa say about acid strength?": the hook pair is resolved by Ka (scenes 3 to 5), carried through pKa (6, 7), the dotpoint's strong and weak split (8) and the conjugate bases of the same two acids (9, 10). Ka × Kb = Kw stays (project manager decision 4); polyprotic acids and amphiprotic direction are out (decision 5). No split needed. Useful outcome: a student can explain why pH cannot rank acids at different concentrations, calculate Ka from the pH of a weak acid of known concentration, convert between Ka and pKa with the right decimal places, rank acids and their conjugate bases, and say how Ka separates strong from weak acids.

## Findings

### S1. Should fix: strong acids get no number of any kind, and scene 2 must not imply ionisation is independent of concentration (project manager decision 6; science check S1 and S2)

- Exact text (scene 8): "Its K a is too large to measure in water, and tables list it as **strong**, or with a negative p K a."; ruler label "pKa below 0"; tag "tables: 'strong', or estimated pKa < 0".
- Student question: "If it can't be measured, how do tables give it a pKa? What is the pKa of HCl?"
- Replacement: "Its K a is too large to measure in water, so tables simply list it as **strong**." One hatched zone "strong: too large to measure in water" with no Ka or pKa numbers; no tag.
- Exact text (scene 2): "The bigger K a is, the further the acid ionises, whatever its concentration."
- Student question: "So diluting an acid doesn't change how much of it ionises?"
- Replacement: "K a doesn't change with concentration, and the bigger it is, the further the acid ionises at the **same concentration**." Key note 1: "Ka measures an acid's tendency to ionise; its value does not depend on concentration (at a given temperature)."
- Understanding enabled: Ka is a property of the acid; percentage ionisation is a property of a solution, which is exactly the hook's point.

### S2. Should fix: say why half a pKa unit is a factor of about three (scene 7)

- Exact text: "H X and ethanoic acid are half a unit apart, which is that factor of about three."
- Student question: "One unit is ten times, so half a unit is five times?"
- Replacement: "H X and ethanoic acid are half a unit apart, and ten to the power of a half is about **three**, not five." Bracket "0.50 → 10⁰·⁵⁰ ≈ 3.2" with a faint crossed "5?".
- Understanding enabled: students read pKa differences on a log scale, which they need for every ranking question.

### S3. Should fix: more than about three bright text items in scenes 1, 3, 4, 6, 8 and 10

- Scene 1: drop the separate pH ruler; a small "more acidic" tag sits beside the 2.52 readout and fades when the bracket appears.
- Scene 3: the strip dims on the first line; the bar comparison and its labels fade when the result shrinks to its chip.
- Scene 4: the strip brightens only for the 1 : 1 pulse; the "work backwards" arrows fade once the HX cell fills.
- Scene 6: the "like pH" tag dims when the first marker drops; each working line shrinks into its marker label.
- Scene 8: the lower "pKa below 0" label and the extra tag go (S1); the thumbnails grey after their sentences.
- Scene 10: the fixed-product bar dims and the Kb line merges into the ranking card.
- Understanding enabled: the task, the current line and one supporting result are what the student reads during each step.

### M1. Minor: the hook calls both acids weak before the video shows it

- Exact text: "Two weak acids, two pH readings."
- Replacement: "Two acids, two pH readings." (ethanoic acid is already known to be weak; scene 3 then shows HX is weak.)

### M2. Minor: say that the backwards table needs no approximation (scene 4)

- Replacement: add "We measured the hydronium, so **nothing is approximated** here." with a grey tag "measured [H₃O⁺]: exact c − x, no 5% check".
- Understanding enabled: separates this method from C08's square-root shortcut, ahead of chem-m6-g02's method choice.

### M3. Minor: frame scene 8's complete ionisation as a Module 5 recall

- Replacement: "As you saw in Module 5, a strong acid ionises completely, so there are essentially **no molecules left** to measure."

### M4. Minor: the fixed-product bar tag uses log algebra

- Exact text: tag "log Ka + log Kb = −14".
- Replacement: "Ka × Kb = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴: a longer Ka bar means a shorter Kb bar".

### M5. Minor: remove the optional sour milk plate (project manager decision 7)

- Replacement: Assets, Codex brief and Open decisions record that no plate is wanted; the attempt 1 card is text only.
