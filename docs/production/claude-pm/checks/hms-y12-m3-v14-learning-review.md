## Findings

1. **Should fix, 8a's card asks for a binary that the answer refuses.** The demand says "classify the challenge as a chosen change or an external pressure", and 8b's answer for case 2 is "both". Scene 2 did teach that some chips belong in both, but a student who follows the card's wording picks one and loses the mark for the "chosen distribution" component. Change the demand to "classify the challenge as a chosen change, an external pressure, or both". 8b is about 30 s already; trim "and support for people most at risk" to "and support for those at risk" to absorb nothing new. Students should see on the card that "both" is allowed.

2. **Should fix, "chronic disease" sits in the outside column as something that "can't be reversed, only prepared for".** v05, v06 and v10 taught that chronic disease incidence responds to prevention (CVD and type 2 diabetes incidence are falling), and this video itself lists "small prevention share" as a chosen change. A student who writes "chronic disease is an external pressure that can't be changed" contradicts the module. Split the chronic disease chip like the workforce chip: "ageing" stays outside; "chronic disease" splits into "more people living longer with it" (outside) and "how much we prevent" (chosen). One spoken clause after the workforce example: "Chronic disease is the same. More people living longer with it comes from outside. How much of it we prevent is a choice." (about 5 s; take it from the reserve cuts). Students should keep prevention as a choice the system can make.

3. **Note, scene 4 density.** Five changes in function, the slider, a gloss and four funnel arrows share one scene. Split at "Two consequences" into 4a (what changed) and 4b (the slider and the funnel); no narration change.

4. **Note, 8d is a good model, but "the next pandemic" needs the "one emerging challenge" link to be explicit.** The card asks for ONE emerging challenge; the feedback names three ("heatwaves, fires and the next pandemic"). Say "for example, the next pandemic" so students see one is enough.
