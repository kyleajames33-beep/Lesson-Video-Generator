Main question traced: *You want a pear-scented ester, but all you have is a gas and a haloalkane. How do you plan a route from the compounds you have to the one you want, and show it as a flow chart?* Useful outcome: given a target and allowed starting materials, the student can count carbons, work backwards to the precursors, pick the start that puts each group on the right carbon, and write a forwards flow chart with every reagent, condition, name and equilibrium arrow, then reject arrows that do not exist.

## Scope and focus

One learner question, one worked example (propyl ethanoate from ethene and 1-bromopropane), one flawed plan and two attempts. The boundary is right: no mechanisms, no Markovnikov by name, no reductions, no yield arithmetic, and the harder drills are left to g03. The four-move method is a fair structure for a flow chart question and each move has its own scene.

Double teaching is the main scope risk. Every reaction here is recalled, which is correct, but two scenes re-teach whole earlier beats rather than recalling them: scene 2 re-narrates c08a's "alcohol is a hub" scene almost item for item (finding 2), and scene 6 re-explains c08a's distil against reflux decision with both apparatus plates and the vapour animation (finding 3). The ester cut (c10 scene 4), the main road (c10 scene 10), the same-carbon swap (c06) and the ketone stop (c08a) are recalled briefly, as they should be.

## Strengths to keep

- **The hook sets a real puzzle.** "Neither one looks anything like the ester" makes planning necessary, and "synthesis" gets a plain meaning at once.
- **Counting carbons first, with a cause.** "These reactions change the functional group, but they don't break the carbon chain" turns a rule into a check, and the 2 + 3 tally with dotted guide lines shows which start becomes which half.
- **The backward hook makes working backwards visible.** Each "?" box resolving into a precursor is the clearest picture of retrosynthesis a student will get, and it reuses c10's ester cut instead of re-teaching it.
- **The two-arm flow chart that joins** is the HSC format c10 only previewed, built one arrow at a time with chips in spoken order.
- **Scene 7's flawed plan** gives two crossed arrows with reasons, not just "wrong".
- **Feedback 2's trap closes the hook.** Oxidising the wrong alcohol makes propyl ethanoate, the pear ester, which shows why the ester cut decides which alcohol is oxidised.
- **Language stays honest.** "Many routes", "usually", "cleaner start" avoid absolute claims.

## Author flags judged

- **"Work backwards to plan; write forwards to answer."** Keep it. It is sound, short and reconstructable, it names the exact exam slip the site flags (planning backwards and then writing the answer backwards), and it helps a new case: any target, any starts. It is a method summary rather than a causal anchor, so give each half its reason once (finding 6, with the forward reason first said in scene 6, finding 3). It does not cover checking; the four cards and scene 7 carry that, so it does not need to.
- **Main road with the side road.** Keep the main road (c10's line). The side road as written is the blocker in finding 1; with "halogen already on the right carbon" it is true and useful.
- **Worked example size.** Four reactions on two arms is right for this video. Dimming completed arrows (finding 3) is a better fix than pre-building the lower arm, because scene 5 earns the lower arm and scene 6 should show it written as a flow chart step.
- **"Retrosynthesis" as a quiet tag only.** Fine.
- **Reduction arrows left out.** Agreed.
