## Findings

1. **Should fix, scene 7 attaches the term "opportunity cost" to the wrong sentence.** The narration says "Cost deferred isn't cost avoided. That's **opportunity cost**, the value of what you give up by choosing something else." A student hears that opportunity cost means a deferred cost, which is a different idea (and the scene 8 memory card then puts "Cost deferred is not cost avoided" first). The on-screen gloss is correct, but the spoken link is wrong. Reorder: "Cost deferred isn't cost avoided. And every choice has an **opportunity cost**, the value of what you give up by choosing something else. Twenty more surgery places now, or a falls program?" Move the gloss to write on the surgery and falls example. Students should be able to define opportunity cost as what is given up, which 9a tests directly.

2. **Note, 9c applies scene 6's chain almost step for step.** Scene 6 works "a subsidy falls behind, providers charge a gap, affordability fails, care is delayed, cost returns" with dental care; 9c is the same chain with psychology sessions, and its picture is the empty chain. The judgement is new, but the reasoning is recall. Acceptable for a 5-mark "assess", which needs a judgement; a harder alternative is a case where the cost shift lands somewhere other than hospitals (for example, a freeze on a prevention grant).

3. **Note, tertiary prevention against treatment.** "Limiting harm once it exists" sounds like treatment, and the next sentences contrast prevention with treatment. Add a short example in the gloss: "tertiary: limit the harm, e.g. rehabilitation after a heart attack". Students then see why it counts as prevention.

4. **Note, scene 4 is the heaviest list.** Five reasons and two honest limits arrive in about 40 s. Each has a reason, which is good; fade cards 1 to 3 to grey as 4 and 5 write.

5. **Note, 9d at about 30 s.** At the limit. Trim "and the gap in outcomes widens" (the equity line already says it) if exact audio runs over.
