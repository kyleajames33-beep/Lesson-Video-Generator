## Main question and the useful outcome

Main question (as stated): "A pH probe reads the same white vinegar as 2.46 neat and 2.96 diluted tenfold, and neither number is the amount of acid in the bottle. How do you prepare, measure and report a household sample so that each number means what you say it means?"

One main question: yes. Every scene serves "which solution is each number measured on, and what does it mean?". Useful outcome: a student can plan a household acidity analysis (pH on the neat sample; amount by titration of a planned dilution), estimate a titre from a label, carry the dilution factor through the calculation, report pH and grams per 100 mL with limits, and judge validity, reliability, accuracy and risk.

## Findings

No blockers.

### S1. Should fix: the Ka check invites "then why not go backwards?", and the hook's "neither one tells you how much" is absolute

- Exact text, scene 1: "Same vinegar, two different pH values, and neither one tells you how much acid is in the bottle." Scene 10: "And they fit together: with K a, zero point six eight moles per litre of ethanoic acid predicts a pH of about two point four six."
- Student question: "If the concentration predicts the pH, can't I run it backwards and get the amount from the pH?" (C16 scene 9 told them "for a weak acid, it doesn't", without the reason in that video; C12's hook gave the reason.)
- Problem: for a single known weak acid, c = [H₃O⁺]² ÷ Ka + [H₃O⁺] does give an estimate (pH 2.46 gives 0.67 mol/L). The reason the titration is still needed is sensitivity and identity: a 0.01 error in pH shifts c by about 5 % (2.45 gives 0.70, 2.47 gives 0.64 mol/L), Ka itself is uncertain, and other acids are not counted. Without this, the Ka check looks like it undermines the video's main claim.
- Replacement, scene 1: "Same vinegar, two different pH values, and neither one **measures** how much acid is in the bottle."
- Replacement, scene 10 (insert after "about **two point four six**."): "Why not run that backwards? A reading just zero point zero one out shifts the answer by about five percent." Visual: on "zero point zero one out" a grey strip writes "pH 2.45 → 0.70 mol/L | 2.46 → 0.67 | 2.47 → 0.64" (grey tag "the titration video", dims).
- Understanding enabled: the student can explain why a pH reading checks a titration result but cannot replace it, which is the C12 and C16 idea applied to their own data.

### S2. Should fix: "pipette less vinegar" is a valid alternative that the video never mentions, and A1's feedback implies dilution is the only correct change

- Exact text, scene 5: "You could use a stronger base, but ... Instead, dilute the vinegar ten times." Feedback 1: "Dilute it ten times first, and the titre drops to about twenty five."
- Student question: "Why not just use a 2.50 or 5.00 mL pipette of neat vinegar?" A student who wrote "use a smaller aliquot" in A1 will think they were wrong. An HSC marker would accept it: 2.50 mL of the 6 % vinegar needs about 25 mL of base.
- Replacement, feedback 1: "Dilute it ten times first, **or pipette a tenth as much**, and the titre drops to about **twenty five**." Screen: the line "titre ≈ 25 mL; multiply the result by 10" becomes "dilute × 10 (or a 2.50 mL aliquot): titre ≈ 25 mL". Model answer in Decisions: add "or a 2.50 mL aliquot".
- Optional for scene 5 (a screen tag only, no narration): a grey chip "or a smaller pipette: also fine; dilution gives many aliquots from one careful transfer" under the scale bar.
- Understanding enabled: the student sees the principle (match the moles in the flask to one burette), not a single procedure.

### M1. Minor: buffer choice differs from C16 without a reason

- Exact text, scene 3: "Calibrate the probe with a pH seven buffer and a pH **two** buffer, so the vinegar's reading falls between them."
- Replacement: "Calibrate the probe with a pH seven buffer and, because vinegar reads below four, a pH **two** buffer, so its reading falls between them." (+25 characters.)

### M2. Minor: "about two percent above the label's four" can be heard as "six percent"

- Exact text, scene 9: "about two percent above the label's four, so it's **close**."
- Replacement: "just over the label's four grams, so it's **close**." The "+2%" bracket stays on screen as "+2 % of the label value".

### M3. Minor: the memory aid says the amount is measured "on a dilution", but C12 titrated neat vinegar

- Exact text: *pH on the sample as it is; amount on a dilution, scaled back up.*
- Problem: a student who watched C12 (10.00 mL neat vinegar with 0.5000 mol/L NaOH) may think C12 was wrong. Dilution is a choice when the titre would not fit.
- Replacement: *pH on the sample as it is; for the amount, dilute if you need to, then scale back up.* Condition tag unchanged.

### M4. Minor: the hook does not connect to P04

- Add a silent grey tag under the two readouts in scene 1: "+0.50: a weak acid on tenfold dilution (dilution practical)". It rewards prior knowledge at no narration cost.

### M5. Minor: the vinegar plate's pale gold liquid

- Scene 2 and scene 12 rely on "clear" (and C16 chose a direct titration because the sample is clear and nearly colourless). White vinegar is colourless. Recommend the open decision's small new plate `m6-white-vinegar-bottle-v1.png` (the same bottle, colourless liquid). If not painted, keep the coded label and avoid "colourless" anywhere in copy.
