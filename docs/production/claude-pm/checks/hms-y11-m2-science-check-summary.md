## Blockers in full

### 1. v01, scene 1 hook (and scene 7's close): the seated knee-rotation demonstration
Current: "Sitting down, lift one foot and turn your whole leg so your toes point in, then out. Now hold your thigh still and turn only your shin. You'll get a little at most." On screen: "a few degrees", "knee: barely". Sitting puts the knee near 90 degrees of flexion, where the tibia rotates substantially (about 45 degrees external and 25 degrees internal between 30 and 90 degrees of flexion; Zarins and others, *Am J Sports Med* 1983). Rotation is small only near full extension. Students doing the demonstration will feel the opposite of what the video claims.
Corrected: "Sitting down, lift one foot and turn your whole leg so your toes point in, then out. Now straighten that knee, hold your thigh still, and try to turn only your shin. You'll get almost nothing." On screen: "knee straight: almost none". Scene 7: "and your straight knee barely does because it's a hinge, which only allows a little rotation when bent."

### 2. v09, scene 6: "The hundred is over before glycolysis has done much."
Glycolysis supplies roughly 40 to 50 per cent of anaerobic ATP in a 6 to 10 s maximal sprint (Gaitanos and others, *J Appl Physiol* 1993: muscle lactate rose markedly within the first 6 s sprint). The design's own scene 8 and facts section say glycolysis runs from the start and supplies about half. What the 100 m lacks is time for hydrogen ions to accumulate.
Corrected: "The hundred is over before hydrogen ions have had time to build up much."

## Should fix (one line each; full wording in the per-video files)
- v01 scene 6: "Every synovial joint has the same six parts" becomes "A typical synovial joint has six parts" (bursae are not at every joint).
- v02 scene 2: "when it contracts, it shortens" becomes "it pulls on the bone, and if it shortens, it draws the bone towards itself" (eccentric and isometric contractions follow).
- v03 scene 4: centre of gravity is "the point where your weight is balanced in every direction", not where "mass is evenly spread".
- v03 scene 10: a well-set wheelchair gives "longer, smoother strokes", not pushes that are "harder each stroke" (PVA 2005 guideline).
- v05 scene 6: drop "resting heart rate climbs from seventy to nearly ninety"; "heart rate is higher at every workload, most of all in exercise" (at rest, extra oxygen extraction covers a 20 per cent haemoglobin fall).
- v05 scene 8: hypertensive heart "fills less easily, and stroke volume can't rise as it should" (resting stroke volume is usually preserved).
- v06 scene 7: food in the stomach "is digested much more slowly", not "stops being digested".
- v06 scene 8: chronic stress disturbs digestion through the gut's movement and blood flow; not "too little blood for too long".
- v07 scene 7: balance corrections start in spinal reflexes and continue through the brain stem and cerebellum, "all before you decide anything"; not "decided in the spinal cord".
- v07 scene 3: "each chemical crossing adds a small delay", not "the slow part of the whole route" (conduction over long axons takes longer than one synapse).
- v08 scene 9 (flagged): "Never put a collapse after hard effort down to tiredness" (most post-finish collapses are benign postural hypotension; Asplund and others 2011), kept as a rule.
- v08 scene 8: "treat confusion in a hot athlete as heat stroke", not "means heat stroke" (low sodium and low glucose also confuse).
- v09 scene 5: glycolysis "doesn't need oxygen", not "its early steps don't".
- v09 11d: remove the model's "about 98%" and "about 29%" from the answer lines; "mostly restored" and "well under half" (Bogdanis 1995: about 85 per cent at 6 min after a 30 s sprint).
- v10 scene 8: a netball match is 60 minutes of play (four 15-minute quarters), not seventy.
- v12 scene 2: "phosphocreatine never fully refills" (about a third refills in 20 s).
- v21 facts section: the Rhodes and de Bruijn figure is 46 per cent, not 48.
- v22 scene 3 and key note 3: say both kinds of cohesion are linked to performance and social cohesion strongly to turning up, rather than task cohesion "more closely" to performance (separate effect sizes not confirmed; see below).

## Facts that could not be confirmed
- No study reporting submaximal heart rate one week after blood donation was retrieved; v05 12c's "about ten beats" rests on calculation from the measured haemoglobin fall (about 8 per cent), and is acceptable as an illustrative stem.
- Carron and others (2002): the separate task and social cohesion effect sizes were not available in the abstracts reached; the overall cohesion-performance relationship was confirmed.
- Romijn and others (1993): the exact fat and carbohydrate shares at 85 per cent of VO₂ max were not retrieved; v11's "almost all" is left as a note, not a finding.
- Gaitanos and others (1993): the percentage split for the first 6 s sprint was taken from secondary summaries (glycolysis roughly 40 to 50 per cent); the direction of blocker 2 does not depend on the exact figure.
- Bogdanis and others (1995): the 3-minute PCr value was not retrieved (1.5 min about 65 per cent, 6 min about 85.5 per cent).
- Plates were checked by name and the designs' descriptions only; no image was viewed.
