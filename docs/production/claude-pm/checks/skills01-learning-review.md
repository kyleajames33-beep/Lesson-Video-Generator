## Main question and useful outcome

Main question: *Before you write a word, what exactly is this question asking for?* There is one question, and it is carried throughout. Useful outcome: a student who knows the content runs a four-part read (verb, marks, content words and givens, constraint), then answers the question that was asked rather than the nearby one. The owner's brief ("students know the content but can't write the answer") is served directly. The worked case is well chosen: a correct Le Chatelier answer that still loses marks because the constraint asked for collision theory. That makes "right science, wrong question" concrete and believable.

## Trace from entry knowledge

1. Hook: the 12-report grid is a strong opener and gives the video authority. The spoken generalisation needs one hedge (S1).
2. to 6. Pulling the card apart: each routine step is shown working on the real card (highlight, ring, underline, bracket) as it is named. The technique is shown, not just told. Good. Scene 5's heuristic needs rewording (S2).
7. and 8. Build: the three points are linked and each tick lands on its line. A Chemistry student can follow, because C3 and C4B gave "the heat-absorbing reaction has the higher activation energy, so heating helps it most". One wording issue sits in the activation-energy sentence (see the science check). A Biology student gets no spoken signpost for about two minutes of collision theory (S3).
9. Weak against strong: convincing. The weak answer is good chemistry, so the lesson lands: correct science that does not follow the constraint loses marks. 1 of 3 for linking the shift to the blue colour is fair and realistic.
10. "One mark, one idea" and its limits: good content, but one cited example is not in the research pack (S4, also in the science check). The scene is visually dense (M2).
11. Routine and aid: *Do what? How much? About what? Using what?* is short, ordered and maps one to one onto the annotation icons. It is usable under exam pressure: four questions, no acronym to decode. Good.
12. to 15. Drills: each practises the taught skill exactly (drill 1 tests the "using the information" constraint; drill 2 tests two verbs plus a constraint). The full card, including the table, ΔH, every demand and the mark value, stays visible during "Pause here to answer." and the 10 s countdown. Nothing answer-bearing appears. Drill 2's deliberate reversal (this time Le Chatelier *is* asked for) is the best teaching move in the video: the constraint decides the reasoning, not the topic.
16. and 17. Decision rule and notes: separate from new reasoning, with a quiet copy hold. Good.

## Designer's flagged questions

- **Hook generalisation (open decision 3).** "Missed what the question actually asked" is a fair umbrella for the bundled row: switching prevention for treatment, covering one part, describing instead of explaining, and omitting a required equation are all failures to answer the question as asked. Two parts go beyond the pack, though. "The student knew the science" is stated as universal, and the pack records recurrence, not student knowledge. "NESA's markers write" is also loose: the feedback is published by NESA. See S1. The YouTube description's "Most lost marks..." is a proportion claim the pack explicitly disclaims (S5).
- **Heuristic "Examiners rarely add information for decoration."** As worded, it risks students forcing every given into the answer. Drill 2 in this same video shows why: ΔH = +57 kJ mol-1 is on the page, but only its sign matters, and a student told "your answer probably needs it" may try to use 57. Reframe it as a question students ask of each given (S2).

## Findings

**S1 (should fix). Scene 1 narration.**
Quoted: "Here's something NESA's markers write about every year. In every HSC Chemistry and Biology marking report from 2020 to 2025, they point out answers that missed what the question actually asked. The student knew the science. They just answered a **nearby question**."
Problem: "the student knew the science" generalises beyond the pack, which counts recurrences only, and the feedback is NESA's, not individual markers'.
Replacement: "Here's something NESA's marking feedback raises every year. In every HSC Chemistry and Biology report from 2020 to 2025, it points out answers that missed what the question actually asked. Often the student knew the science. They just answered a **nearby question**."

**S2 (should fix). Scene 5 narration.**
Quoted: "Examiners rarely add information for decoration. If it's on the page, your answer probably needs it."
Problem: this is an unresearched heuristic that pushes students to force givens in. Drill 2's "+57" is a counterexample within this video.
Replacement: "Givens are usually there for a reason. For each one, ask what it tells you, then decide if your answer needs it."

**S3 (should fix). Scene 7 opening.**
Quoted: "So let's build the answer, in collision theory."
Problem: Biology viewers (half the audience, and s01 opens both playlists) get only a grey tag in scene 2, then about two minutes of collision theory. s02 handles this in speech; s01 should too.
Replacement: "So let's build the answer, in collision theory. If you take Biology, watch how each line uses the constraint."

**S4 (should fix). Scene 10 visual chip.**
Quoted: "a chip 'two balanced equations' with the grey tag 'e.g. a NESA Chemistry 2024 guideline'"
Problem: `what-markers-reward.md` does not contain this example. Chemistry 2024 Q34 appears in the pack only as a causal-link fault and a 4-mark explain. The on-screen attribution to NESA is unsupported. The pack does support Chemistry 2025 Q33 (a balanced equation with states in the top band).
Replacement: "a chip 'a balanced equation with states' with the grey tag 'e.g. a NESA Chemistry 2025 guideline'". Make the same change in the Decisions bullet ("Explain row: Chemistry 2024 Q34's top level needed two balanced equations, not one") and point it to the Calculations section, Chemistry 2025 Q33.

**S5 (should fix). YouTube description.**
Quoted: "Most lost marks in HSC science aren't about not knowing the content. They come from answering a slightly different question."
Problem: this is a proportion claim. `common-faults.md` states that its counts "are recurrences in published feedback, not estimates of the proportion of students who made the error."
Replacement: "NESA's marking feedback flags the same problem every year: answers that respond to a slightly different question from the one asked."

**M1 (minor). Scene 1 visual.**
Quoted: "On 'every' each tile gets a red tick in turn"
Problem: green ticks mean marks earned for the rest of the video, so a red tick reads oddly. "Every" is also spoken twice.
Replacement: "On 'every HSC' each tile gets a red flag mark in turn"

**M2 (minor). Scene 10 visual density.**
Quoted: "a seven-mark box shows seven bullet facts and a band label 'sound, not comprehensive'"
Problem: there are three limit cards, each with its own mini-demonstration, plus a footer, in about 27 s of speech. Show one card at a time and clear the previous card's demo.
Replacement: "a seven-mark box shows seven bullet facts and a band label 'sound, not comprehensive'; earlier cards collapse to their titles"

**M3 (minor). Scene 13 feedback length (about 33 s).**
Quoted: "So: roan parents had red calves and white calves, so each parent must carry **both alleles**. They're heterozygous."
Problem: the owner's band is 15 to 30 s. The series allowance makes this tolerable, but a small trim helps.
Replacement: "So: roan parents had red calves and white calves, so each parent carries **both alleles**: heterozygous."

## Retained strengths

The annotation layer teaches the routine as visible actions. Weak against strong uses correct science, so the constraint is the only difference. Drill 2 reverses the constraint. The mark counter is labelled illustrative. The "one mark, one idea" limits are honest. Every question carries the HSCScience tag.

## Verdict

**Ready to voice after fixes** (S1 to S5, together with the science check's wording fix in scene 8). Repeat this review on the exact voiced preview.
