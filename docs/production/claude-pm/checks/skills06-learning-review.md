Main question traced: how to use graph evidence without losing scale, unit or inference marks. A student with Year 11 axes and independent-variable knowledge can follow the 2010 to 2020 table into the uneven-year trap, correct plot, trend, 2016 interpolation and 2040 extrapolation. The Chemistry drill then changes the graph shape and the Biology drill changes the trend. s05's units and rounding are recalled, not retaught; s07's VRA content is left alone.

## Strengths to keep

The two competing year axes show why equal spacing of unequal intervals distorts a trend. The extension fan makes uncertainty visible. Percentage points are explained by actual subtraction before relative percentage increase is used. Question and drill cards preserve the givens during each countdown, and the brief drill feedback has one crossed habit. The eight-mark worked walkthrough is teaching, so it need not be compressed to end-feedback length.

## Findings

| # | Severity | Scene | Problem | Concrete repair |

| 1 | should fix | 9, decision rule and notes 6 | "Use a line graph when both variables are numbers" is not a sufficient selection rule. Numerical categories and unrelated paired measurements need not be joined, and the line can imply intermediate values that are meaningless. | "Use a line graph for a continuous relationship or change over time, like these years and percentages. Use columns for separate categories, like different antibiotics." Notes should keep the same distinction. |
| 2 | should fix | 6 and drill 1 Decisions | The scripted explanation of resistance can sound like the data prove antibiotic use caused this hospital trend. Separately, the Decisions text says acid is used up although the stem says excess acid; the later shot correctly says marble chips. | Scene 6: "If asked to explain, add a plausible cause: antibiotic use can select resistant bacteria, increasing their share of samples. The graph alone doesn't establish that cause." Replace the acid-used-up tag instruction with "as the marble chips are used up" everywhere. |
| 3 | should fix | 3 and question-card layout | The worked card contains two sentences, ten table values, four demands, marks and a 20 by 15 grid. Treating that as three items does not make it readable. A ten-second countdown is not enough for all eight marks without a clear pause instruction. | Keep the full card through the countdown, but explicitly tell students to pause for all four parts. Use full-size question/grid space during the response opportunity. During feedback retain just the current demand with the table or established graph it needs. Judge the resulting size in the exact preview. |
| 4 | optional | notes | Six dense notes plus two memory lines are demanding in a fifty-second hold. The plotting note is particularly long. | Split into two quiet screens, or keep TAILS on screen with a short expansion and say students may pause. Do not reduce the font to fit the full paragraph. |

Zero blockers, three should-fix findings, one optional.

## Checks against the review questions

Axes, best fit, scatter, interpolation, extrapolation and percentage points have explanations or concrete referents. The hook's report counts refer to reports, not student proportions. All drills are answerable from their own givens. The trend changes at 3 percent, and the worked inference compares an interior year to twenty years beyond the observations. The notes can support transfer after the graph-choice rule is repaired. Each narrated graph change has a matching planned action, but playback and listening remain pending.
