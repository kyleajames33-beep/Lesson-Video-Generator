Main question traced: *What makes a disease infectious, and why does knowing which kind of pathogen causes it decide how it can be treated?* Useful outcome: the student can say whether a disease is infectious, sort an unseen pathogen into one of seven groups by structure (cells or not, many-celled animal or not, nucleus or not, and what a non-cellular agent is made of), give a named disease for each group in animals and plants, and explain why a drug must hit something the pathogen has and the host lacks.

## Strengths to keep

- **The Fleming hook sets up the whole lesson as a puzzle.** "Penicillin does nothing for the flu, for tinea, for malaria or for a tapeworm" makes the four crosses something students will want explained, and scene 9 pays it off with the cause.
- **One sorting tree carries fourteen new terms.** Each term gets one fixed place on screen, so the term load (the heaviest in Module 7 so far) is manageable. The tree grows, gains plant cases, then gains treatment chips: one visual system throughout.
- **The true-scale zoom ladder uses the video medium well.** Rod beside red cell, virus row along the rod, TMV beside the coronavirus: students see size relationships that a textbook table only states.
- **The misfolding row (scene 7) shows a mechanism, not a label.** One misfolded protein converts its neighbour, one at a time, before the bad apple picture is given. The aid comes after the explanation, as the rules require.
- **Scene 9 explains the treatment link causally** ("a drug has to hit something the pathogen has and the host doesn't"), and both attempts are genuine transfer: unseen descriptions with no plates (11a), and a new pair of cattle diseases (11c).

## Plates: are all nine new plates needed?

Yes. Each new plate carries a narrated explanation or a named example the syllabus asks for:

- **Fleming dish:** the hook's visible case (the clear ring), reused in scene 9.
- **TB bacilli:** the only non-flagellate rod, shared with b02.
- **TMV:** the plant virus and a size pair.
- **Viroid:** RNA only, used in scenes 3 and 7 and in 11b.
- **Prion normal form:** essential for the misfolding crossfade.
- **Tuber pair:** shows the change caused by the viroid.
- **Crown gall and root-knot galls:** the only plant bacterium and plant worm examples in scene 8, which answers the dotpoint's "plants and animals".

A grey tag in those plant slots would be a text box in a diagram slot, which AGENTS.md says does not count as a useful visual. If the brief must shrink, the cheapest option is to paint the tuber pair as one side-by-side plate (healthy and spindle), as the house `b3b-wheat-rust-leaf-v1.png` does, and replace the crossfade with a comparison. This saves one plate and loses a little motion. I do not recommend cutting crown gall or root-knot. There is no existing m7 plate that can stand in: `m7-banksia-phytophthora-v1.png` is an oomycete, and `m7-infected-leaf-v1.png` names no disease.
