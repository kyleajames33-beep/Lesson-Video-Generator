## Properties

Acids sour, turn blue litmus red, react with reactive metals and carbonates; bases bitter, slippery, turn red litmus blue; acids and bases neutralise each other: correct as standard descriptions, with the "never taste or touch" safety line. Soap is mildly basic: correct.

## Other claims

- Hydroxide ion + proton gives water; carbonate accepts two protons to form carbonic acid, which decomposes to water and CO₂: correct.
- Metal plus acid is electron transfer (metal atoms give electrons to H⁺, which pair as H₂), not proton transfer: correct.
- Marble is calcium carbonate; stomach acid is mainly hydrochloric acid; magnesium hydroxide is an antacid active; ammonium sulfate fertiliser is made from ammonia and sulfuric acid: correct.
- Activity series placement: Mg, Al, Zn, Fe above hydrogen and react with dilute HCl or H₂SO₄ to give H₂; Cu and Ag below hydrogen and do not react with dilute non-oxidising acids. Correct. The A2 stimulus order K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Sn > Pb > (H) > Cu > Ag > Au is the standard order. Lead is above hydrogen but reacts very slowly (insoluble chloride/sulfate coating); not asked, and the author note covers it.
- Avoiding nitric acid with metals (oxidising acid, gives nitrogen oxides): correct and sensible.
- Gas tests: CO₂ turns limewater milky (insoluble CaCO₃); hydrogen burns with a squeaky pop when a lit (burning) splint is held at the mouth of the tube: correct.
- Site corrections 2 to 6 (Ba(OH)₂ + H₂SO₄ precipitate; low solubility is not weakness for Mg(OH)₂; methyl orange is yellow above about pH 4.4; NH₃ + H₂SO₄ equivalence point is acidic, below bromothymol blue's 6.0 to 7.6 range; indicator equilibrium usually credited to Ostwald): each is correct as stated and none is used in this video.

## Findings

No blocker or should-fix science errors.

### M1. Minor (author note only): IUPAC status of binary acid names

- Exact text (author note a): "the hydro-/-ic and -ic/-ous acid names are the traditional names that IUPAC retains for these acids and that NESA papers use"
- Replacement: "the -ic/-ous oxoacid names are IUPAC-accepted names, and hydro-/-ic names are the standard names for the aqueous solutions that NESA papers use"

### M2. Minor: hydrogen test plate shows no gas source

- Exact text (asset brief 5): "a test tube held mouth-up in a small retort clamp on a stand, with a thin wooden splint resting across the top"
- Problem: with an empty tube the pop has no visible source. Replacement: "a test tube held mouth-up in a small retort clamp, with a few metal granules fizzing in acid at the bottom and a lit splint at its mouth"

### M3. Minor: "a lit splint" wording in scene 12

- Exact text: "Put a lit splint at the mouth of the tube."
- Fine as written. Optional, to separate it from the glowing-splint oxygen test students meet elsewhere: "Hold a burning splint at the mouth of the tube."
