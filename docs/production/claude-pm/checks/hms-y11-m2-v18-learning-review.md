## Findings

1. **Should fix, 9c is scene 6 restated.** Scene 6: frequent, instant, detailed feedback "makes a session look better, and learning worse. The learner stops attending to her own feel ... So fade it: less often, a little later, and ask, how did that feel". 9d is the same three moves. Use the opposite case, which tests scene 7's stage table and guards against over-applying scene 6: "A beginner in her first month of golf gets no comments during sessions. At the end of each week her coach tells her only how many putts she made. Explain why this feedback suits her poorly, and what the coach should do instead. (4 marks)" Model: a cognitive learner can't yet detect her own errors; a weekly count is delayed knowledge of results and says nothing about what to change; she needs prompt augmented feedback on one point of the movement (knowledge of performance); then fade it as she becomes associative. Crossed mistake: "less feedback is always better". Students should show they can match feedback to the stage, not just repeat "fade it".

2. **Note, scene 5 carries six terms in about 60 s.** The author flags it; the switch board with three rows is the right carrier. Judge at playback speed and keep the "it went wide / your shoulders opened early" pair on screen as the anchor.

3. **Note, scene 3 "A continuous skill stays whole".** Soften with v17's fix ("mostly stays whole; any part drill is rejoined quickly") so the two videos agree with each other and with the site.
