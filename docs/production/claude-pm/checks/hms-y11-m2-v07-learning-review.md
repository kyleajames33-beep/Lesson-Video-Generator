## Findings

1. **Should fix, scene 3 uses "gap" for two different things.** Myelin "lets the signal jump from gap to gap", then in the next breath neurons "don't touch" and the signal crosses a synapse. A beginner will merge the two gaps and think myelin speeds signals across synapses, which undermines the reflex logic in scene 7 (fewer synapses, faster). Say "lets the signal jump between bare points along the axon", and keep "gap" for the synapse only. The neuron plate shows the bare points between sheath segments, so a pointer can land on them.

2. **Should fix, 11a is scene 7's sentence and the memory card.** "Explain why a reflex response is faster than a voluntary response" is answered by "A reflex is faster because the route is shorter and crosses fewer synapses, not because its neurons are faster", repeated in scene 10. Make it a transfer: "Explain why a sprinter's reaction to the starting gun is much slower than a knee-jerk reflex, even though both use the same kind of neurons. (3 marks)" Model: the gun must be heard and interpreted by the brain before a decision; the command then runs from the motor cortex down the cord to the muscle; a longer route with more synapses, each a slow chemical step; the knee-jerk stays in the spinal cord. Crossed mistake: "the sprinter's nerves are slower". Students should use the route rule on a voluntary response they know.

3. **Note, scene 2 is dense.** About a dozen terms in about 50 s (cerebrum, motor cortex, cerebellum, brain stem, spinal cord, peripheral, sensory, motor, somatic, autonomic, sympathetic, parasympathetic). The tree grows one branch per cue, which is the right carrier; check it at playback speed and let the two autonomic leaves write without narration if it runs fast.
