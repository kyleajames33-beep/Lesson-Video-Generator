## Comparison of routes (scene 10)

- Hydration: fast, continuous, high temperature and pressure, non-renewable feedstock. Correct. Ethene comes from steam cracking of naphtha (crude oil) and of ethane (mostly from natural gas), roughly 40 to 50% each worldwide; "usually made from crude oil" (scene 1) understates natural gas (M1).
- Fermentation: warm, renewable crop, slow and batch, dilute product that takes energy to distil, land and water use. Correct and balanced.
- Substitution: suits a particular alcohol in the lab; the haloalkane must be made first. Correct.
- "No single winner"; "fermentation: always the greener choice?" said positively. Correct; the site's "near-carbon-neutral" claim is rightly not repeated.

## Findings

### B1. Blocker: the oxygen statement and A1's premise are wrong for brewer's yeast

- Exact text: scene 8 "because with plenty of oxygen, yeast mostly respires aerobically, making carbon dioxide and water, so little ethanol forms"; feedback 1 "With plenty of oxygen, yeast mostly respires aerobically, making carbon dioxide and water, so little ethanol forms"; key note 3 "(with plenty of O₂, yeast mostly respires aerobically)"; scene 8 side chip "plenty of O₂: CO₂ + H₂O, little ethanol"; A1 "leaves the flask open to the air. After two days, very little ethanol has formed"; site correction 4's replacement text.
- Problem: *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* ferments glucose to ethanol even with oxygen present when sugar is plentiful (the Crabtree effect), so "mostly respires aerobically" and "little ethanol" are false at the glucose concentrations of a school fermentation, and an open flask normally does produce ethanol. A question that asks students to explain a result that would not occur cannot go to recording. Oxygen does divert some sugar to complete respiration and to yeast growth, so "less ethanol" is true.
- Replacement, scene 8: "A bung and delivery tube keep air **out**, because with oxygen, yeast can respire aerobically, breaking some sugar down fully to carbon dioxide and water, so less ethanol forms." (+11 characters.)
- Replacement, scene 8 side chip: "with O₂: some sugar → CO₂ + H₂O, less ethanol".
- Replacement, key note 3: "air kept out (with oxygen, yeast respires some sugar aerobically to CO₂ and water, so less ethanol forms)".
- Replacement, A1 stem and feedback 1: as learning S2 (two flasks; air bubbled through flask B; "less ethanol than flask A").
- Replacement, site correction 4's "Correct:" text: "With oxygen, yeast can respire aerobically, breaking some sugar down fully to CO₂ and water, so less ethanol forms. Excluding air keeps the sugar going to ethanol."
- Author note g: replace "with plenty of oxygen, yeast mostly respires aerobically; brewer's yeast can still ferment at high sugar concentrations" with "brewer's yeast is Crabtree-positive: at high sugar concentrations it ferments even in air, but oxygen diverts some sugar to aerobic respiration and growth, so excluding air gives more ethanol". Update the Open decision on attempt 1 (the "open to the air" and "much less ethanol than expected" options are both withdrawn).
