# m6b20 learning review: why can social context limit long-term genetic change?

Reviewer: independent AI reviewer, 30 October 2026. Did not author the design.

Input: `../lessons/bio-m6-b20.md`, Revision 1, read in full; brief 115, brief 109, standing rules and `../../student-learning-review.md`. Compared the narration and boundaries of Biology M6 B08, B10, B11, B13, B18, B19 and G02. Inspected the actual reused rice and Bt cotton PNGs on black. Inspected the written shot plan, not an implemented video. No recording, playback, human listening or real student learning was checked.

Main question: why can a biotechnology that functions biologically leave little lasting genetic change? A student should connect a specific context to planting or release, then to the actual spread of genetic material, and make a reasoned judgement about reach.

## Findings

### 1. Blocker: the main population explanation contradicts B08, and its memory line removes pollen-mediated spread

Scenes 3 and 10, the field meter, key notes and the stated stop boundary.

Scene 3 says an allele's frequency is "the share of plants carrying them". B08 correctly taught share of gene copies. A carrier count cannot generally supply an allele frequency. For example, four diploid plants hold eight copies at a locus: two heterozygous carriers give a carrier proportion of 2/4 but an insertion-allele frequency of 2/8. Field area is another quantity again. The six-by-eight field grid also treats 48 fields as 48 gene copies. Its one golden field is 1/48 of equally sized fields, not a measured genetic frequency.

The student then memorises "A crop's genes spread only as far as it's planted", despite B13's wheat question allowing pollen movement to a cross-compatible grass, and this lesson's own decision rule naming pollen. The intended Golden Rice explanation is useful, but the universal statement teaches the wrong transfer rule.

Replace scene 3 narration with:

> Think of all the rice plants in a region as one population. Its gene pool holds all their alleles. For Golden Rice, start with uptake: how widely farmers plant it. Choosing this seed adds plants carrying the inserted genes. Rice mostly pollinates itself, so pollen rarely passes these genes to other plants, and beta-carotene gives no known advantage in the field. Here, seed choice does most of the spreading. More fields planted with Golden Rice means more of the crop carries its added genes.

This is 495 characters, 61 fewer than 556. Relabel the meter "share of planted area growing Golden Rice", and label the grid "illustration, not measured area". Use equally sized fields. Remove the numerical 0 to 1 allele-frequency presentation; the crop adoption comparison does not require an allele calculation. Relabel the gene-pool outline: "rice crop in the region"; a gene pool is its alleles, not the plants themselves. Keep the pollen animation and the current planting comparison.

Replace scene 10 with:

> Two lines to keep. Biologically possible, socially decided. For Golden Rice, seed choice controls most of the spread.

117 characters, eight fewer than 125. Use the same scoped wording in notes, the memory check and the stop boundary. Do not turn it back into a statement about every crop.

Source for the already-taught distinction: B08 scene 2; the earlier counterexample is B13 attempt 2. Coordinate the scientific wording with the independent science check.

### 2. Blocker: the central source-reading scene has no identifiable sources or evidence to interpret

Scene 8 and its claim to teach B6-IQ3-07.

"Developer" and "campaign group", with a "paraphrased positions" tag, let the student sort claims into topics but do not let them identify the documents, check dates or follow the evidence. Attempt 2's sources are explicitly constructed, so it cannot supply the missing real-source comparison. The learner hears "check the evidence" without seeing what evidence either real source actually uses.

Keep the two-card layout, but use attributable, dated extracts, with paraphrases labelled as paraphrases. Two available original position documents are:

- [PhilRice, Golden Rice: a safe and healthy food, 13 September 2021](https://www.philrice.gov.ph/golden-rice-a-safe-and-healthy-food/). Its food-safety claim points to regulator assessments. It also presents Golden Rice as complementary to other foods.
- [Greenpeace Southeast Asia, Golden Rice, 28 February 2013](https://www.greenpeace.org/southeastasia/publication/1073/golden-rice/). Its nutrition-policy argument favours existing interventions and dietary diversity.

These are historical claims, not a claim that a 2013 article states a group's present view. Show each date and document title in its source line. Card A: "Food-safety assessments support eating it." Card B: "Spend resources on established nutrition measures and dietary diversity." Underline the exact claim being compared, rather than describing the entire documents as if either had only one argument. Briefly expose their evidence types: "regulator safety assessments" against "evidence for other nutrition measures". Date, evidence and source line pulse in that order; only the current comparison is bright.

Replace scene 8 narration with:

> Compare two dated sources. PhilRice reports food safety assessments. Greenpeace argues for existing nutrition measures instead. Are they answering the same question? One addresses safety; the other, how to reduce vitamin A deficiency. Check their dates, the evidence behind each claim, and who produced it. A source's interest is a reason to check, not dismiss it. Safety alone cannot settle which approach reaches children best.

429 characters, 58 fewer than 487. Tie "they" to the two highlighted claims. Keep the useful correction that an interested source is not automatically wrong. Do not infer that food approval establishes a demonstrated reduction in population vitamin A deficiency, or that preference for other interventions refutes the measured beta-carotene pathway. The independent science check should confirm the final paraphrases and dated facts.

### 3. Should fix: attempt 2 asks students to interpret conclusions that are not on either card

Scenes 11c and 11d; the duplicated question and model answer.

A reports dry-year yield results, and B reports market access. Neither recommends growing or not growing the wheat. The demand says they "reach different conclusions about whether farmers should grow" it, making students invent recommendations. Their different criteria are a strong transfer task if the demand asks how to use both.

Replace the spoken sentence:

> Interpret how the two sources should be used together to decide whether farmers should grow it.

Both old and new sentences are 95 characters. Replace the card demand with: "Interpret how Sources A and B should be used together to decide whether farmers should grow this wheat. (4 marks)"

The written fourth mark calls for a combined decision, but the current spoken feedback stops before it. Replace 11d with:

> Source A addresses yield in drought; B addresses whether the harvest can be sold. Neither alone settles whether to grow it. Each has an interest, so check independent trials for A and buyers' actual policies for B. The decision needs both performance and market access. Saying the company must be lying is the classic mistake.

326 characters, 37 fewer than 363. About 27.5 seconds including two seconds of padding and one-second settle, compared with about 30.3 seconds now. Keep the crossed mistake, the constructed-source label and the full question through the countdown. The answer table's final row should read "decision needs yield and market access".

## Learner trace and overlap

1. **Entry:** B08 already explains allele copies, selection and gene flow. B10 introduced Golden Rice and the difference between a safety question and a value judgement. B11 taught gene drives. B13/G02 taught pest selection, refuges and conditional verdicts. B18 taught how recombinant DNA is made; B19's final question hands on through G02 to this lesson.
2. **Hook and capability:** the rice is recognisable and the new case gives a reason to attend. Naming the maize/bacterial contributions and conversion is a recall of purpose, not a repeat of B18's cut/paste/put-in/pick method. The science review must keep biological capability distinct from demonstrated community health benefit.
3. **Population link:** the field grid could make uptake visible. As written, it replaces the earlier copy-count definition with a wrong one. Finding 1 keeps the intended causal link without re-teaching a full population-genetics lesson.
4. **Context gates:** a rule that stops planting is a concrete cause; a licence and consumer choice can explain access and demand. Do not let unsupported funding or seed-price inferences carry a judgement. Those factual claims are with the science reviewer. The gates should affect the field grid at their spoken cues.
5. **Bt cotton:** the new purpose is the link from adoption to crop reach, not another full ethical evaluation. The single resistance/refuge reminder is acceptable recall. B10, B13, B19 and G02 have already earned that mechanism. Do not add another pesticide-benefit lesson. The proposed caterpillar-frequency rise needs the science review's monitoring check.
6. **Sources and verdict:** the topic distinction is useful, but it needs real attributable documents and visible evidence before the student can interpret it (finding 2). A court decision establishes a legal constraint, not a measured level of public trust; resolve this with the science findings.
7. **Practice:** attempt 1 makes a causal transfer to a new organism and regulatory contrast. Its givens support the planned approval-to-planting-to-crop link. Attempt 2 transfers the source checks once finding 3 is applied. Both cards remain visible through the 10-second countdown, with no answer-bearing label before it. One crossed mistake follows each.
8. **Memory and notes:** the first memory line is useful after the gate explanations. The second must remain a Golden Rice statement. The notes need finding 4.

The source describes no specific Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander community in learner copy. Its general food-choice treatment does not depict communities as irrational. The owner's cultural-review question remains an author note. Nothing in this learning review clears that separate review.
