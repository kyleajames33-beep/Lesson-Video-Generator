## Facts confirmed (no change)

- **Chickenpox reinfection (hook).** A second case of chickenpox is uncommon but occurs; "Most people ... don't catch it again" and "usually don't get sick" are correctly hedged. Shingles (reactivation of latent virus) is rightly an author note: omitting it does not teach a wrong idea about reinfection.
- **Primary response (scene 2).** Few naive cells; selection and expansion over days; IgM first, then IgG; peak at about two weeks; illness often occurs while it builds; innate defences hold the pathogen back. Correct, with finding 2.
- **What stays (scene 3).** Most plasma cells die within weeks, so antibody falls; some long-lived plasma cells keep a lower level; memory B and T cells persist for years to decades and outnumber the original naive precursors many times over. Correct, with finding 1.
- **Secondary response (scene 4).** Shorter lag (about 1 to 3 days), higher and longer peak, mainly higher-affinity IgG; memory B cells quickly become plasma cells. Correct. Learning review finding 3 adds memory T cells.
- **Different antigen (scene 5, attempt 1).** A new antigen given with a repeat of a familiar one gets a primary response. Correct. Attempt 1's values are internally consistent: anti-P peaks at about 20 (day 14) and about 800 (day 48), a ratio of about 40; anti-Q at about 20 (day 54) with a week-long lag.
- **Memory aids.** "Second exposure: faster, bigger, longer" is correct for the same antigen. "Active: you do the work. Passive: you're handed the antibodies" matches the four quadrants correctly.
- **Vaccine types (scene 7).** Live attenuated (varicella: Oka strain), inactivated, subunit or protein (including toxoids), mRNA ("instructions that let your cells make that protein"). Correct.
- **Vaccination and boosters (scenes 7, 8).** A vaccine gives a primary response and memory without the illness; a booster triggers a secondary response and adds memory; a vaccine does not cure an established illness. Correct, with findings 3 and 7. No Australian schedule claim is spoken; the chickenpox vaccine is named without a schedule.
- **Four kinds of immunity (scenes 9 to 12).** Natural active (infection), artificial active (vaccination), natural passive (maternal antibodies), artificial passive (antibody injections such as antivenom). Active is slow to build and leaves memory; passive is immediate, leaves no memory and fades. Correct.
- **Maternal antibodies (scene 10).** IgG is the only class that crosses the placenta in useful amounts, carried by FcRn, mostly in the third trimester; secretory IgA in breast milk protects the baby's gut; protection fades over the first months. "Across the placenta and in breast milk" is correct, and the visual direction (maternal blood into fetal capillaries) is right. (searched) maternal antibody transfer review https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7098569/ ; Frontiers in Immunology 2017 https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2017.01294/full
- **Antivenom (scene 11, attempt 2).** Australian brown snake antivenom is made from the plasma of horses given small, increasing doses of eastern brown snake (*Pseudonaja textilis*) venom. "Often a horse" is right (some antivenoms use sheep, as L14 says). It is artificial passive immunity; a later bite needs antivenom again. Correct, with finding 6.
- **Jenner and cowpox (scene 7 tag; site error 1).** Jenner used cowpox material (1796). The 20th century smallpox vaccine used vaccinia, an orthopoxvirus of uncertain origin; genomic work on 19th century vaccine stocks points to horsepox as a likely ancestor. The site's "cowpox (a mild disease caused by vaccinia virus)" is wrong. (searched) Schrick et al., *N Engl J Med* 377:1491 (2017) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28836863/ ; RKI repository record https://edoc.rki.de/handle/176904/2893
- **Absolute words.** "only a few B cells and T cells fit" (true), "this only happens for the same antigen" (true), "can't cause the disease" (finding 3), "A bite victim needs those antibodies now" (true), "protection for the future needs a vaccine" (finding 4), "boosts every antibody" (true as the crossed mistake). No others flagged.
