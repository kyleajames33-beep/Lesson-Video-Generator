## Student learning trace (entry knowledge: C1 to C7, K and the ICE table)

- Main question (hook): "Will the tube make more hydrogen iodide, or break some down?" The learner outcome: calculate Q from a mixture and compare it with K to state which way the mixture runs, and explain why in terms of rates.
- The step from K to Q is a single move (same fraction, different numbers) and is drawn as a movement of the tube's numbers into a new fraction. The tags "settled values only" and "values right now" stay on screen, so a student cannot take Q to be a second constant. The missing square on the first Q fraction (SHOULD FIX 1) is the one place this move is not clean.
- The causal explanation is the rate link: forward over reverse is K over Q in the lesson's model, so Q below K means the forward reaction is faster and Q rises toward K, which is why the mixture stops at Q = K. Its assumptions are said aloud and drawn ("made-up rate scale, in this model"). The animation of Q climbing 6.25 to 49 with the bars converging is the beat where video earns its place.
- Two predictions are asked of the student (mid-lesson and the final attempt); both hold with all conditions on screen, "Pause here to answer." and a 10 s countdown. The first asks only for a comparison of two visible numbers (Q = 256 against K = 49). The second asks for a calculation and a justification; feedback names two wrong numerical paths and plots each.
- Application: the disturbance scene turns the previous Le Chatelier videos into a Q calculation; the "K stays the same" scene separates a change of Q from a change of K and states the temperature condition. The method (expression, concentrations now, Q, compare, state the direction) is the same in every case, so the five-step decision rule works as a transferable method.
- Honest scope: "direction only, not how far" and "the harder cases come next" (ICE with K) are stated in speech and on screen.
- Memory aid "Q chases K" plus "Q too small, make more products. Q too big, make more reactants." is applied in four scenes before it is named, has its conditions on screen ("same temperature, same equation as written"), and works for a new case (the ammonia feedback).
