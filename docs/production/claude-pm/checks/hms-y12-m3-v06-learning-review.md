The hook (name a condition, answer three questions, notice the one you could not) is a real self-test, and the four tests answer it. `ConditionMap` gives "choose for contrast" a picture. The type 2 diabetes profile is the right worked case: the death record card ("main cause: heart disease; also: diabetes") shows why deaths understate it, and the falling incidence against rising prevalence, tied to the bathtub, is the clearest example in the module of two measures moving apart. It also teaches the corrected fact (site correction 26). ## Findings

1. **Learning blocker, 9b marks the v01 answer wrong for dementia.** v01 taught that dementia is now the leading cause of death in Australia (scene 3 and key note 2). 9b places dementia "low on the map", "mostly disability and care", and accepts only "towards morbidity". A student who applies v01 and places dementia towards "mostly kills" is told they are wrong, and the feedback itself says "deaths are rising". Dementia both kills (leading cause) and disables (years of care), and on the burden data its fatal share is large, so "mostly disables" is doubtful as well as contradictory. Fix the feedback: "Dementia sits high on both: it's now the leading cause of death, and it brings years of disability and care before that. And gains are small from either side, because there's no cure and prevention is still limited. Either height scores if you justify it." Token: slides to mid-height near the centre. Change the marking to accept a mid or upper position with a reason that uses deaths or disability. Have the science check confirm dementia's fatal and non-fatal burden split. Students should be rewarded for using what v01 taught.

2. **Should fix, scene 6 says diabetes is "appearing in younger adults" straight after scene 5 says new cases have nearly halved.** A student asks: "If new cases fell by half, how can more young adults be getting it?" Add one clause: "And even as the overall rate of new cases falls, it's appearing in younger adults." (about 2 s), and have the science check confirm the young-adult trend against the same AIHW report. Students should see that a falling average rate can hide a rising group, which is v01's rule.

3. **Note, scene 7 repeats v05's "heart disease is prevention, cancer is treatment".** If v05's finding 1 is fixed (deaths fell from both prevention and treatment), say "heart disease gained most from prevention" here to stay consistent.

4. **Note, the map's mental health token.** "Large burden, fewer deaths" is fair, and the design keeps suicide out of narration under its sensitive-content rule. Keep the token's vertical position clearly above the foot, so students do not read "mental health conditions never cause death".

5. **Note, hook with no thinking time.** "Then answer three questions without looking anything up" is followed straight by the questions and the reveal. Add a 3 s hold after "And what would reduce it?" with the three cards and empty boxes.
