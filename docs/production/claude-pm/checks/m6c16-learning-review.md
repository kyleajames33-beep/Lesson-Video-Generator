## Main question and the useful outcome

Main question (as stated): "A pH probe, a direct titration, a back titration and a conductivity titration all analyse acids and bases. For a bottle of vinegar, an antacid tablet, a glass of red wine and a factory's waste water, which one fits, and what does each measurement actually tell you?"

One main question: yes ("which method fits, and what does it establish?"). The four samples are answered in scenes 5 to 7. Useful outcome: a student asks "pH or amount?", then "what is the sample like?", chooses and justifies a method against an alternative, says what the result establishes, and explains what a pH probe measures and why it is calibrated with two buffers.

## Findings

### B1. Blocker: scene 8's pituri example (project manager decision)

- Exact text: "In parts of central Australia, Aboriginal people have long prepared **pituri**, the leaves of a native shrub, by mixing them with **alkaline ash** ... It takes protons from the nicotine in the leaves, turning it into its **neutral** form, which passes into the body more easily."
- Problem: the project manager has excluded pituri (a nicotine-containing plant ties Aboriginal knowledge to drug use and raises cultural protocol concerns). The dotpoint does require the Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander application (`m6c16-cultural-review.md`).
- Repair: follow the cultural review. No documented, suitable acid-base example was found, so scene 8 states the documented knowledge framing, recalls the cycad leaching from Module 5 as solubility, and states the acid-base principle without a specific practice. Remove key note 5, the pituri plate, the map, the vocabulary item, the pronunciation and the YouTube line. Keep scene 8 at a similar length so the narration stays above 5,500 characters.
- Understanding enabled: the student meets the dotpoint's framing and the principle, without a misleading or culturally unsafe example. The gap is recorded for the owner.

### S1. Should fix: scene 4's buffer advice contradicts the video's own vinegar reading

- Exact text: "Choose buffers on either side of your samples, and rinse the probe between them."
- Student question: "The vinegar read 2.40, but the buffers were 4 and 7. Is that reading wrong?"
- Replacement: "A pH seven buffer sets the zero point, the reading at pH seven. ... Choose buffers close to your samples, ideally on **either side**, and rinse the probe between them." Tag: "buffers close to your samples, ideally either side; rinse between". Record in an author note that a pH 1.68 or 2.00 buffer would bracket vinegar.
- Understanding enabled: a correct rule that the video's own example obeys. (This also applies M2's plain meaning of "zero point".)

### S2. Should fix: scene 7 implies the wine endpoint is found at the jump

- Exact text: "So titrate it while a **pH probe** follows the curve. The titre gives the total moles of acid that reacted with the base". Visual: "a small curve drawing to a ring at the jump".
- Student question: "Where's the jump for wine?" Or the student later writes "find the middle of the jump" for wine.
- Replacement: "So put a **pH probe** in the wine, and stop the titration at a set pH on the meter, not at a colour. The titre counts all the acid that reacted with the base". The curve stops where it meets a dashed, unlabelled "set endpoint pH" line (science S1).
- Understanding enabled: the student learns how wine is actually analysed (a fixed pH endpoint), and why the titre is total acid rather than a count of molecules of one acid.

### S3. Should fix: "For a strong acid the two agree" (scene 9)

- Student question: "A pH of 1 and 0.1 mol/L agree?"
- Replacement: "For a strong acid, the pH does give the concentration. For a weak acid, it doesn't."
- Understanding enabled: the aid's limit, stated as a testable claim.

### M1. Minor: scene 1 end state

- The heading writes first and dims to grey as the chips write. The chips lead with their icons and short labels. Bright at the end: the four chips over the four plates.

### M2. Minor: "zero point" needs a plain meaning in speech

- Folded into S1's replacement: "sets the zero point, the reading at pH seven".

### M3. Minor: feedback 1 drops the 1 : 2 ratio

- Replacement: "Use a **back titration**: add a measured excess of standard acid, let the solid react completely, then titrate the leftover. Half the acid used gives the moles of calcium carbonate." Tag: "acid used ÷ 2 → n(CaCO₃) → %". The shorter procedure clause keeps the feedback under 30 s.
