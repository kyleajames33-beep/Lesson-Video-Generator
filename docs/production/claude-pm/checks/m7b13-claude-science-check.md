## Facts confirmed (no change)

- **Yogyakarta trial (hook, scene 9, key note 5).** (opened, PMC8103655) Utarini et al., *NEJM* 2021;384:2177: a cluster-randomised, open-label, test-negative trial with 24 clusters (12 wMel, 12 untreated) and febrile participants aged 3 to 45 at 18 primary care clinics. Virologically confirmed dengue was found in 67 of 2,905 (2.3%) in treated clusters against 318 of 3,401 (9.4%) in untreated clusters. OR 0.23; protective efficacy 77.1% (95% CI 65.3 to 84.9). Hospitalisation for VCD: 13 of 2,905 against 102 of 3,401, efficacy 86.2% (66.2 to 94.3). The lower confidence bound was above zero for all four serotypes. "Randomly chose which of twenty-four areas", "about two in a hundred ... about nine in a hundred", "people who came to clinics with a fever" and "weather and serotypes affected both groups alike" (all clusters were in one city in the same years) are all correct. The 2021 publication date is correct (results were announced in 2020).
- **2024 record.** (searched) WHO *WER* 100(52): 14,434,584 reported cases (7,718,585 laboratory-confirmed), 52,738 severe, 11,201 deaths, the highest ever recorded; the Americas had over 90 per cent and Brazil over 10 million. I found no full-year 2025 global total, and partial 2025 data are far lower. Finding 7 dates the wording.
- **Indonesian serosurvey (scene 4, key note 2).** (searched) Prayitno et al., *PLoS NTD* 11(6):e0005621 (2017): 3,210 children aged 1 to 18 in 30 urban clusters, October to November 2014. Adjusted seroprevalence 69.4% (64.4 to 74.3); 33.8% at 1 to 4 years and 89.0% at 15 to 18 years. "About seven in ten children had dengue antibodies, a sign of past infection" is correct. IgG ELISA cross-reactivity with other flaviviruses belongs in author notes only, as the design says.
- **Illness, viraemia and extrinsic incubation.** Symptoms last 2 to 7 days and viraemia about 5 days around onset (WHO fact sheet), so "lasts about a week" is correct. Extrinsic incubation of about 8 to 12 days is correct ("over a week"). A seasonal peak that lags rainfall by 1 to 2 months is well documented in South East Asia. The graph is labelled teaching data.
- **Serotype immunity (scene 6, attempt 2).** WHO fact sheet: recovery is believed to give lifelong immunity to that serotype; cross-immunity to the others is partial and temporary; later infection with another serotype raises the risk of severe dengue. "Covers only the serotype that infected you", with the grey "brief cross-protection at first" tag, "more likely to be severe" and "has to protect against all four types at once" are all correct. Dengvaxia's excess severe dengue in seronegative recipients is the real-world case for the last point.
- **Rates.** 2,000 / 1,000,000 × 100,000 = 200; 300 / 50,000 × 100,000 = 600; 12a: 500 and 1,000. All correct.
- **North Queensland.** Dengue is not endemic in Queensland, and outbreaks begin when viraemic travellers are bitten by local *Ae. aegypti* (Queensland Health; CDI outbreak reports). "Usually begin, with a traveller bringing the virus in" is correct. Bali, and Indonesia generally, is the most common place where Australian travellers acquire dengue (Australian NNDSS travel data, for Codex to open), so the Bali to Cairns arc is acceptable as illustration (author doubt c).
- **Water storage, crowding and community support.** These are correct as stated (WHO 2009 guidelines).
- **Climate.** "May let Aedes spread to new places" is hedged correctly (IPCC AR6 WGII ch. 7).
- **Tank model (author doubt b).** It is correct for current-case prevalence. Prevalence rises with incidence and falls with recovery or death; at steady state, prevalence is roughly incidence times mean duration. The second tank without a drain, for past infection, is a good contrast once finding 1 renames it seroprevalence.
- **Attempt 1.** Correct arithmetic, model and crossed mistake.
- **Attempt 2.** The chain is correct. One point for the model: the 20 per cent with no antibodies were also susceptible. The model's "most of the population" already covers this.
- **Malaria (scene 2).** *Plasmodium* (a protozoan) spread by female *Anopheles* is correct.
- **Absolute words.** "only the serotype" is hedged by a tag. "has to protect against all four" is correct. "only where dengue is common" is correct for WHO. Findings 2 to 4 cover the rest.

## Exam style

Both items are HSC Section II style. 12a is "calculate ... identify ... justify" (3 marks) with a small invented table. 12c is "explain ... with reference to the data" (4 marks). Both are tagged "HSC-style question, written for HSCScience", and neither copies a NESA item I know of. The models reward units ("per 100,000"), comparing rates rather than counts, serotype-specific immunity, mobility plus a local vector, and severity from a second heterotypic infection. Both feedback scripts fit the brief-answer rule.
