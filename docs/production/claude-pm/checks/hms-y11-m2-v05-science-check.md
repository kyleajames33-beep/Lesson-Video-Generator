# hms-y11-m2-v05 science check (efficiency of the cardiovascular system)

Revision 2 design checked 24 November 2026 (source-only: script, on-screen text, practice, key notes, memory aids and facts section; no build or playback). Independent check; the design was not edited.

## Confirmed
- Arithmetic (2, 3): 70 × 70 mL = 4,900 mL; 42 × 120 = 5,040; 76 × 66 = 5,016; 150 g/L × 1.34 mL/g × 0.98 ≈ 197 mL/L, shown as 200; 5 L/min × 0.2 = 1 L O₂/min. Correct.
- Endurance training enlarges the left ventricular chamber (eccentric hypertrophy) and raises stroke volume; a low resting heart rate is a result of fitness (2, 8). Correct.
- Altitude (4): oxygen fraction about 21 per cent at all altitudes; standard atmosphere about 560 mmHg at 2,500 m, so ambient PO₂ about 118 against 160 mmHg; ventilation and heart rate rise; VO₂ max falls. Correct. Showing ambient rather than humidified inspired values with "about" is acceptable.
- Acclimatisation (5): kidney erythropoietin, more red cells over weeks, live high and train low. Correct (see note 4).
- Haemoglobin (6): about 98 per cent of oxygen on haemoglobin; carrying capacity near proportional; iron binds oxygen in haem; at-risk groups. Correct.
- Vascular disease (7): atherosclerosis narrows and stiffens arteries; angina, heart attack, peripheral artery disease with pain on walking, stroke. Correct.
- Further factors (9): carbon monoxide affinity about 200 to 250 times oxygen's; dehydration lowers plasma volume and stroke volume (drift); heat diverts blood to skin; nicotine constricts vessels. Correct.
- 12a/12b: correct and matches the site's five marks.
- **12c/12d (blood donor, flagged).** A standard donation is about 470 mL. Plasma volume is almost fully restored within 24 to 48 hours; haemoglobin concentration falls about 7 to 8 per cent and is still about 4 per cent low at 14 days; VO₂ peak falls about 6 to 7 per cent and recovers by about 14 days (Ziegler and others, *Transfusion* 2015, 55:898 to 905; a meta-analysis of before-and-after studies, *Sports Medicine Open* 2016, doi 10.1186/s40798-016-0067-7, reporting about 7 per cent lower haemoglobin and VO₂ max at 24 to 48 hours). "The plasma refills in days, but red cells take weeks" is correct. A haemoglobin drop of about 8 per cent at one week needs about 8 to 9 per cent more cardiac output, which at a running heart rate near 140 to 150 is about 11 to 13 beats; "about ten beats higher" is a fair illustrative rise. No study reporting submaximal heart rate at one week was retrieved, so the number stays illustrative (it is in the stem, not claimed as data). The facts note's "four to eight weeks" for haemoglobin and red cell mass is fair. Marked answer correct.
- Memory aids sound with their stated assumptions. No people (the donor card shows a blood bag only); plates named correctly.

## Findings
1. **Should fix (scene 6 narration and cost meter).** "So the heart moves more litres, and resting heart rate climbs from seventy to nearly ninety". The model holds delivery fixed at 1 L/min, but at rest the body needs about a quarter of that and compensates for a 20 per cent haemoglobin fall mainly by extracting more oxygen; resting heart rate rises little until anaemia is much more severe (Weiskopf and others, *JAMA* 1998, needed haemoglobin near 50 g/L for large rises). The rise shows mainly in exercise. Corrected: "So the heart has to move more litres, and heart rate is higher at every workload, most of all in exercise." Cost meter: rise from 70 under a ghost tick "at a given pace", tagged "model". Key note unaffected.
2. **Should fix (scene 8 narration).** "With high blood pressure, the wall grew, the chamber has less room to fill, and stroke volume falls." In hypertensive (concentric) hypertrophy resting stroke volume is usually preserved; filling is impaired, so stroke volume cannot rise as well in exercise. Corrected: "With high blood pressure, the wall grew, the chamber fills less easily, and stroke volume can't rise as it should." Readout: "stroke volume: can't rise".
3. **Note (scene 4).** "your heart rate rises at every pace" is correct for acute exposure.
4. **Note (scene 5).** Red cell mass rises measurably only after about three to four weeks above about 2,000 m; early rises in haemoglobin concentration are mostly plasma loss. "Over two to four weeks" is acceptable with the facts note; "muscles grow more capillaries" at altitude has mixed evidence and could become "muscles may grow more capillaries".
