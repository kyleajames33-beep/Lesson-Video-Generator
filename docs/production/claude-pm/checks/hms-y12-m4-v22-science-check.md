Independent check; the design was not edited. Line numbers refer to `lessons/hms-y12-m4-v22.md` as of this check.

## Confirmed
- **Prohibited List structure (5).** The WADA Prohibited List is an International Standard updated annually and in force from 1 January (2026 List in force now); substances prohibited at all times (S0 to S5: non-approved substances, anabolic agents, peptide hormones and growth factors, beta-2 agonists, hormone and metabolic modulators, diuretics and masking agents; M1 to M3: blood manipulation, chemical and physical manipulation, gene and cell doping), substances prohibited in competition only (S6 stimulants, S7 narcotics, S8 cannabinoids, S9 glucocorticoids), and substances prohibited in particular sports (P1 beta-blockers, including archery and shooting). "some substances banned at all times and others only in competition" is correct.
- Health implications (3): anabolic agents (blood pressure, lipids, liver, hormones); stimulants (heart rate, blood pressure, arrhythmia, impaired cooling, heat illness); blood doping and erythropoiesis-stimulating agents (viscosity, clots, stroke, worse when dehydrated); beta blockers (low heart rate and blood pressure); diuretics as a silent row (dehydration, sample dilution). Correct. Extra risk of hormonal agents for adolescents. Correct.
- Ethics (4) and the coercion argument: standard philosophy-of-sport arguments, fairly answered.
- Testing (5): no-notice out-of-competition testing; A and B samples; strict liability (World Anti-Doping Code 2021, art. 2.1); contamination no defence, though a contaminated product can reduce the sanction (art. 10.6.1.2); new substances and microdosing can evade tests; a negative test does not prove clean. Correct. 8c/8d: a therapeutic use exemption covers only the approved medicine. Correct.
- **Sport Integrity Australia (task flag).** The design names no agency, by decision. For the record: Sport Integrity Australia (from 1 July 2020, absorbing ASADA) is Australia's national anti-doping organisation under the *Sport Integrity Australia Act 2020*: it runs testing, education and result management under the Code, and its app gives substance checks (Global DRO). Nothing in the video contradicts this. See note 3.
- Local anaesthetic injections are not prohibited (8a: "The injection is legal" is correct).
- Memory aids sound. No people. Course close (9): no science claims.

## Findings
1. **Should fix (line 48, hook).** Current: "One takes a strong painkiller, so a sore ankle won't bother them for eighty minutes. ... Only the second would be punished if tested." Before a match is in competition, and many strong painkillers are prohibited in competition: narcotics such as morphine and oxycodone (S7), and tramadol since 1 January 2024 (WADA 2024 List). A student could conclude that any strong painkiller is safe to take before a match. Corrected: "One takes a permitted painkiller, so a sore ankle won't bother them for eighty minutes." Token label "painkiller (permitted)". The rest of the hook stands.
2. **Should fix (line 53, scene 2).** Current: "... local anaesthetic injections that numb an area, and cortisone injections. They're mostly legal, often prescribed ..." Since 1 January 2022, glucocorticoids (cortisone) by any injectable route are prohibited in competition unless covered by a therapeutic use exemption (WADA 2022 List, S9; washout periods apply). "mostly legal" survives, but leaving cortisone injections unqualified next to it misleads. Corrected: "... and cortisone injections, which close to competition need an exemption. They're mostly legal, often prescribed ..." On screen, the "corticosteroid injection" chip adds "(exemption in competition)".
3. **Note (line 53).** "The second is drugs to improve performance, which are banned": caffeine is a performance drug that is not banned (monitored only). "drugs taken to improve performance, many of them banned" would be exact; key note 1's "(prohibited)" likewise.
4. **Note (line 98, 8d; marking line 124).** "The B sample is tested to confirm or reject the A result": B-sample analysis is the athlete's right on request (it can be waived), not automatic. "She can have the B sample tested to confirm or reject the A result" would be exact.
5. **Note (line 73, scene 6).** "ask a doctor or a sports dietitian" is sound; adding "or check it with Sport Integrity Australia's app" would give school athletes the national tool without naming a product on screen. Optional.

## Verdict
Pass with fixes. 0 blockers, 2 should fix, 3 notes.
