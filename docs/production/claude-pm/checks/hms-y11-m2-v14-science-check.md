# hms-y11-m2-v14 science check (immediate responses to training)

Revision 2 design checked 24 November 2026 (source-only: script, on-screen text, practice, key notes, memory aids and facts section; no build or playback). Independent check; the design was not edited.

## Confirmed
- Heart rate (2): anticipatory rise from sympathetic activity and adrenaline; rapid rise as vagal (parasympathetic) tone is withdrawn; steady state; response against adaptation. Correct.
- Ventilation (3): ventilation = rate × tidal volume; rest about 15 × 0.5 L ≈ 7 L/min; above 100 L/min in hard exercise in adults; depth first, then rate; ruled breathing-drive sentence (treated as given); acidosis adds drive at high intensity; hyperventilation before underwater swimming lowers carbon dioxide without adding much oxygen, so blackout can come before the urge to breathe. Correct.
- Stroke volume (4): about 70 rising toward 110 mL; venous return from the muscle and respiratory pumps; Frank-Starling; plateau at about 40 to 60 per cent of VO₂ max in untrained people, attributed to shortened filling time. Correct as the textbook account; the elite exception is shown.
- Cardiac output arithmetic (5): 4.9, 14.3, 21.45 L/min; 21.45 ÷ 14.3 = 1.5. Correct; 21.5 L/min is a realistic untrained maximum.
- Lactate (6): continuous production and removal; rest about 1 to 2 mmol/L; lactate threshold about 50 to 60 per cent of VO₂ max untrained and about 70 to 80 per cent trained (root ruling 3). Correct.
- 9a/9b: 15.9 ÷ 0.118 = 134.7; 22.2 ÷ 0.117 = 189.7. Correct. 9c/9d: correct.
- Memory aids sound with stated assumptions. No people; plates named correctly.
