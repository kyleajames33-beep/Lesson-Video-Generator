## Facts confirmed (no change)

- **CRISPR-Cas9 mechanism.** A guide RNA of about 20 nucleotides pairs with a target DNA sequence next to a PAM (not mentioned); Cas9 makes a double-strand break; non-homologous end joining often leaves small insertions or deletions that can disrupt the sequence; off-target cleavage at partially complementary sites is documented (Jinek et al., Science 2012;337:816; Fu et al., Nat Biotechnol 2013;31:822). Scene 2 is correct at this level, and the project manager's decision to keep it is supported.
- **First CRISPR approval.** Casgevy (exagamglogene autotemcel): MHRA 16 November 2023 (sickle cell disease and beta thalassaemia, 12 and over), FDA 8 December 2023, EU conditional authorisation 9 February 2024 (EMA EPAR, searched). "In late 2023, a treatment made with the gene-editing tool CRISPR was approved for the first time, for sickle cell disease": correct.
- **Mechanism.** Autologous CD34+ stem and progenitor cells are edited ex vivo at the *BCL11A* erythroid enhancer, reducing BCL11A and raising foetal haemoglobin; the sickle allele is not corrected. "After birth, a protein switches the foetal gene off": BCL11A is the main repressor of foetal globin as it falls over the first months of life; correct at this level. "Foetal haemoglobin keeps most red cells from sickling": HbF inhibits HbS polymerisation; correct.
- **Trial figure.** Frangoul et al., NEJM 2024;390:1649 (searched, PMID 38661449): 44 patients dosed; of 30 with sufficient follow-up, 29 (96.7 per cent) were free of vaso-occlusive crises for at least 12 consecutive months, and all 30 were free of hospitalisation for them. "Twenty-nine of the thirty patients followed for long enough had no severe pain crises for at least a year": correct.
- **Conditioning, hospital time, price.** Myeloablative busulfan conditioning; weeks in hospital for engraftment (FDA label; Frangoul et al.); US list price US$2.2 million (Vertex, December 2023). "Over two million dollars": correct; recheck near recording.
- **Many genes.** Type 2 diabetes is polygenic with strong environmental factors (Revision 2 example). Correct.
- **Directions.** In-body editing in human trials (liver-targeted editing, for example PCSK9 and transthyretin amyloidosis); gene-edited crops in development and some approved overseas; expanded DNA screening. Correct as general directions.
- **Gene drive mechanism.** A homing drive encodes Cas9 and a guide RNA against the wild-type allele; germline cleavage and homology-directed repair copy the drive, so heterozygotes transmit it to well over half (often over 95 per cent) of offspring. "The editor cuts the matching spot on the other chromosome, and the cell repairs the cut by copying the drive across. So nearly all the offspring inherit it": correct. "Cheats that rule": informal, not misleading (the project manager's decision stands).
- **Suppression target.** The Kyrou drive disrupts the female-specific exon of *doublesex*; heterozygous females are fertile, homozygous females are intersex and sterile. "A gene that female mosquitoes need to lay eggs ... Females with one copy can still breed ... two copies can't": correct at this level.
- **Ecology.** *Anopheles gambiae* is one of several hundred *Anopheles* species; effects of suppressing one are expected to be small but uncertain. "Might affect animals that eat them, though other mosquitoes would remain": correct as hedged.
- **"Hard to call back."** Reversal drives are proposed but unproven in the field. "May be very hard to call back": correct as hedged.
- **PCSK9 (11a, 11b).** PCSK9 promotes degradation of liver LDL receptors; loss of PCSK9 raises receptor numbers and lowers blood LDL. The card's plain wording ("reduces the liver's ability to remove LDL") is correct; "liver cells are body cells", so the edit is not inherited: correct.
- **11d.** "Edited cells already treat sickle cell, a single-gene disease": correct ("treat", not "cure", fits the retained allele). "Most tissues are still hard to reach": correct.
- **Absolute words checked.** "spread to every mosquito" (correct for the cages; Revision 2 adds "small"); "no gene drive has been released" (correct, date-sensitive); "it can't be undone" (11b; the base edit is permanent in those cells, correct); "the sickle allele is still there" (correct); "Advanced isn't the same as proven" (a reasoning rule with its limit stated). No "always", "completely" or discontinued-product claims.
- **Memory aids.** "Advanced isn't the same as proven" (site L10 key point) and "need, feasibility, fairness" (site card 4): both correct as reasoning frames, used after the explanation.
- **Em dashes:** none in the design.
