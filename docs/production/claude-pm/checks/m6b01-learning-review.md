Main question traced: *How does one changed base in one organism end up as a new allele in a whole population's gene pool, and did the antibiotic cause it?* Useful outcome: the student can trace a new allele from one base change in one cell to the gene pool, explain why the antibiotic selects rather than creates the allele (with replica-plating evidence), and separate making alleles from reshuffling them.

## Strengths to keep

- **The case puts the cause before the name.** Scene 6 says a bacterium "can't sense penicillin and make the exact mutation it needs" before it names "random with respect to need". Scene 8 shows the cells that die and the cells that divide before it names natural selection.
- **Scene 7 (replica plating) is the best beat in the video and should stay.** The hook asks "made by penicillin, or already there?", and the dashed lines that run back to dish 1's unexposed spots answer it with evidence. Video does this better than text: the velvet carries the pattern across and the matched positions light up. This is an addition to the site, and it earns its time.
- **Visual referents are concrete.** One amber notched ring always marks the new allele. Its copying down the `Lineage` tree in scene 5 shows the cause of inheritance in bacteria without extra words.
- **Recall stays recall.** Scenes 3 and 5 use one clause each for B7 and B8, so they will not feel like a repeat of B8.
- **The memory line comes after its four processes are explained** (scenes 3 to 10), and it fixes the bank's "only source" overstatement.
- **People.** The design has no people or human body figures. Animals appear only as B8's fly plates. The scene 5 inset is a coded fly outline with two cell icons, which is acceptable; keep it a fly and not a generic body.
