## Findings

### S1. Should fix: the hazard tag sits by the 1.0 mol/L acetic acid label (scene 8)

- Exact text (visual): "On "safe" a small grey hazard tag "weak ≠ safe" pins by the acetic acid label." The label is "acetic acid, 1.0 mol/L"; the narration says "concentrated acetic acid still burns skin".
- Problem: 1.0 mol/L acetic acid is about 6% by mass. Under the EU CLP harmonised classification (Annex VI, acetic acid) it is corrosive to skin at 25% or more (Category 1A at 90% or more) and an irritant from 10%; household vinegar is about 0.8 mol/L. Pinning the tag by the 1.0 mol/L label implies that solution burns skin.
- Replacement: keep the narration; the tag reads "weak ≠ safe: concentrated acetic acid is corrosive" and sits apart from the 1.0 mol/L chip. Add an author note with the classification limits.

### M1. Minor (author note only): ion pairing in saturated Ca(OH)₂

- "every bit that does dissolve splits completely into ions" is the HSC convention that makes Ca(OH)₂ a strong base. In a saturated solution a measurable share is present as CaOH⁺. Keep the learner wording; record the point in author note (f) so a later reviewer does not treat it as an oversight. Do not extend this to Mg(OH)₂ (decision 6).

### M2. Minor (author note only): HNO₂ Ka varies between sources

- Author note (h) uses Ka 4.5 × 10⁻⁴ (pH 2.19). The IUPAC-sourced pKa 3.15 (Ka 7.1 × 10⁻⁴) gives pH 2.09. A1 supplies pH 2.2 as data, so the question is valid; record the range.

### M3. Minor: mnemonic wording (learning review S3)

- Revision 1 says "Each word starts with the key letters". Check any rewrite: the words do not start like the formulas (every formula starts with H), so say "each word gives the key letters of one acid", not "starts like the formula". S, I, Br, N, Cl and Cl match H₂SO₄, HI, HBr, HNO₃, HCl and HClO₄: correct.
