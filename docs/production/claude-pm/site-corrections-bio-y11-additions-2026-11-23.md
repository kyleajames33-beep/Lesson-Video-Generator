### FA2 L07: Xylem & Phloem

- [L] L07 "Why ring-barking kills a tree" and short answer 3 model: "phloem sits just inside the bark, while xylem is deeper in the wood". In a tree trunk the phloem is the inner layer of the bark itself, which is why removing the bark removes it. Proposed: "phloem forms the inner layer of the bark, while xylem is deeper, in the wood". Minor. (Source: bio-y11-fa2-v10 item 1.)
- [L] L07 card 2 figure text: "Phloem sieve tube (living): ... sap moves in either direction." Read alone, it can suggest that one sieve tube carries sap both ways at once. Proposed: "sap can move up or down the plant; in any one sieve tube it flows one way at a time". Minor (route boundary). (Source: bio-y11-fa2-v10 item 2.)
- [L] L07 vocabulary: "Phloem: Vascular tissue made of living sieve tubes that transport sugars (sap) in any direction". Sap is the solution, not the sugars, and phloem tissue also contains companion cells, fibres and parenchyma. Proposed: "Vascular tissue whose living sieve tubes, supported by companion cells, carry sap (a solution of sugars, mainly sucrose) up or down the plant". Minor. (Source: bio-y11-fa2-v10 item 3.)

### FA2 L08: Translocation in Phloem

- [**H**] L08 card 1: "translocation is described as moving sugar from 'high concentration source to low concentration sink'". The phrase invites the diffusion idea that the lesson's own mechanism replaces: the sap moves by bulk flow down a pressure gradient, and sugar in a sink is often stored as starch, not at low concentration everywhere. Proposed: "translocation is described as moving sap by bulk flow from a high-pressure source to a low-pressure sink". **Matters** for the route's boundary (cells do not move sap by diffusion or pumping along the tube). (Source: bio-y11-fa2-v11 item 1. Live on main today.)
- [L] L08 memory hook (l08): "Load sugar, water follows, pressure rises, sap squirts to the sink." "Squirts" suggests a jet; phloem sap flows steadily (about a metre an hour or less). Proposed: "... sap flows to the sink" (this video's aid). Minor. (Source: bio-y11-fa2-v11 item 2.)
- [logged once elsewhere] L08 Explain walk-through step 1 and the vocabulary "Active loading": "This is active transport against a concentration gradient". True for most crop plants (apoplastic loaders); some trees load passively. Not an error at Year 11; author note only, no correction proposed. (Source: bio-y11-fa2-v11 item 3. already logged in substance (FA2 L07 phloem loading, 3 Nov); no further action)

- [L] L09 hook: "a mountain ash lifts it more than ten times higher". (Source: bio-y11-fa2-v12 item 1.)

- [logged once elsewhere] L09 card 3: "This is why a plant that cannot transpire (e.g. blocked stomata) also struggles to take up minerals". ### FA2 L10: Factors Affecting Transpiration

The gradient steepens because the leaf's internal air spaces, saturated at the leaf's temperature, hold more vapour when the leaf is warmer, while the outside air's vapour content is unchanged. Proposed: "a warmer leaf's air spaces hold more water vapour, while the outside air holds the same amount, so the gradient out of the leaf steepens". Minor (the design uses the proposed wording). (Source: bio-y11-fa2-v13 item 1. Live on main today.)
- [L] L10 card 2 summary and the hooks file: "Light, temperature and wind each increase the rate of transpiration; higher humidity is the one factor that decreases it." Its only stated exception is extreme wind; very high temperature with water shortage also closes stomata and lowers the rate. Proposed: add "(under water stress, very high temperatures or strong wind can close the stomata and lower the rate)". Minor; the route stop asks for stress closure. (Source: bio-y11-fa2-v13 item 2.)

### FA2 L14: Human Digestion

- [**H**] L14 card 1, vocabulary and walk-through step 3: "Mechanical (physical) digestion is the physical breakdown of food". The page treats mechanical and physical digestion as one process, but the 2025 content point names "mechanical, physical and chemical digestion" separately, and the page never describes emulsification of fats by bile as physical digestion (bile is only "added"). Proposed: keep "Mechanical digestion: chewing by the teeth and churning by the stomach"; add "Physical digestion: emulsification, in which bile salts break large fat droplets into many tiny droplets without breaking any bonds, giving lipase a much larger surface to act on." The page's own vocabulary ("into the blood or lymph") is correct. Proposed: "small molecules, which can cross the gut wall; glucose and amino acids enter the blood, while most fat enters the lymph through the lacteals". (Source: bio-y11-fa2-v18 item 1. Live on main today.)
- [L] L14 card 1: "protease splits protein into amino acids" (in the mouth and stomach context of the card). The card does not say where, so this is minor; the "Where digestion happens" panel is correct. Proposed: "proteases split protein into shorter chains and then amino acids". (Source: bio-y11-fa2-v17 item 2.)
- [L] L14 explain walk-through step 6 and its full answer: "elimination removes material that never entered the body's cells". ### FA2 L20: Renal Dialysis

- [L] L20 card 1: "so only the wastes leave". Small useful solutes absent from the dialysate are also lost (for example amino acids and water-soluble vitamins).
