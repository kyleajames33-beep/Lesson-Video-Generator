# Draft for owner review: chem-m5-g03b

## 1. Explain (3 marks)

**Question:** Explain how to decide whether an equilibrium question needs direct substitution, an ICE table or a Q calculation.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for settled concentrations directly giving K; 1 for starting and final data needing an ICE table; 1 for current mixture and known K needing Q comparison.

**Sample answer:** Use settled concentrations directly to find K. Use an ICE table when a start and a change or an unknown final composition are given. Use Q when a current mixture must be compared with a known K to predict direction.

## 2. Apply (4 marks)

**Question:** For A(g) ⇌ B(g), Kc = 2.0. A vessel has [A] = 0.30 and [B] = 0.90 mol L⁻¹ at one instant. Identify the question type, calculate the required ratio and predict direction.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for Q type; 1 for Q = [B]/[A]; 1 for Q = 3.0; 1 for net reverse direction.

**Sample answer:** This is a Q versus K question. Q = 0.90/0.30 = 3.0. Since Q > 2.0, net reverse reaction forms A.

## 3. Analyse (5 marks)

**Question:** A vessel at equilibrium for A(g) ⇌ B(g) has [A] = 0.40 and [B] = 0.80 mol L⁻¹. At the same temperature, extra B is added so [B] is 1.20 mol L⁻¹ immediately after addition. Calculate K and Q after addition, predict direction and explain why K is unchanged.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for K = 2.0; 1 for Q = 3.0; 1 for Q > K; 1 for reverse shift; 1 for constant temperature keeping K fixed.

**Sample answer:** Before addition, K = 0.80/0.40 = 2.0. Immediately after addition, Q = 1.20/0.40 = 3.0. The mixture shifts in reverse, consuming B. K stays at 2.0 because the temperature did not change.
