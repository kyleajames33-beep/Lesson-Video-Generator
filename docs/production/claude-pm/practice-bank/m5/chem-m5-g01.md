# Draft for owner review: chem-m5-g01

## 1. Explain (3 marks)

**Question:** Explain what a negative Gibbs free energy change indicates for a reaction under stated conditions.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for ΔG < 0; 1 for thermodynamic favourability in the stated direction; 1 for distinguishing favourability from reaction speed.

**Sample answer:** A negative ΔG means the reaction is thermodynamically favoured in the forward direction under those conditions. It does not show how quickly the reaction occurs.

## 2. Apply (4 marks)

**Question:** A reaction has ΔH = −40 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔS = −0.10 kJ K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. Calculate ΔG at 300 K and predict whether the forward reaction is favoured at that temperature.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for ΔG = ΔH − TΔS; 1 for consistent units; 1 for −10 kJ mol⁻¹; 1 for forward reaction favoured.

**Sample answer:** ΔG = −40 − 300(−0.10) = −10 kJ mol⁻¹. Since ΔG is negative, the forward reaction is favoured at 300 K.

## 3. Analyse (5 marks)

**Question:** A reaction has ΔH = +60 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔS = +0.20 kJ K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. Calculate ΔG at 250 K and 400 K. Analyse how temperature changes favourability and explain why a favourable reaction need not produce a fast observable change.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark each for ΔG values of +10 and −20 kJ mol⁻¹; 1 for temperature making TΔS larger; 1 for forward favourability at 400 K but not 250 K; 1 for activation energy or kinetic barrier.

**Sample answer:** At 250 K, ΔG = 60 − 250(0.20) = +10 kJ mol⁻¹. At 400 K, ΔG = 60 − 400(0.20) = −20 kJ mol⁻¹. The positive entropy term favours the reaction more at higher temperature. Even at 400 K, a large activation energy could make the reaction slow.
