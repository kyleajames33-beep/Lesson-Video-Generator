# Draft for owner review: chem-m5-c09

## 1. Explain (3 marks)

**Question:** Explain why changing temperature can change K whereas adding a catalyst cannot.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for temperature changing the relative favourability of directions; 1 for changed equilibrium composition and K; 1 for catalyst speeding both directions without changing final ratio.

**Sample answer:** Temperature changes the relative rates and the final equilibrium ratio, so K changes. A catalyst speeds both directions but leaves the final ratio, and hence K, unchanged at that temperature.

## 2. Apply (4 marks)

**Question:** For R(g) + heat ⇌ S(g), a vessel is heated from 300 K to 330 K. Predict how the settled amount of S and K change. Explain your predictions.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for endothermic forward reaction; 1 for more S; 1 for larger K; 1 for temperature favouring heat-absorbing direction.

**Sample answer:** The forward reaction absorbs heat, so heating favours S. The new equilibrium contains more S, and the product-to-reactant ratio K is larger.

## 3. Analyse (5 marks)

**Question:** For 2T(g) ⇌ U(g) + heat, Kc is 12 at 300 K and 4 at 350 K. Analyse whether these values are consistent with the reaction equation. Predict the effect of cooling on the settled amount of U and explain why the original K of 12 cannot be used at 350 K.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for exothermic forward direction; 1 for K falling on heating; 1 for data consistency; 1 for cooling favouring U; 1 for K tied to temperature.

**Sample answer:** The forward reaction releases heat. Heating favours the reverse direction, so the lower K at 350 K is consistent. Cooling favours formation of U. K = 12 describes only the 300 K equilibrium; at 350 K the equilibrium ratio is 4.
