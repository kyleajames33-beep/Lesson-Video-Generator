# Draft for owner review: bio-m5-mould

## 1. Explain (3 marks)

**Question:** Explain how bread mould produces a new colony on a separate food source.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for spores made and released; 1 for dispersal through air; 1 for germination and thread growth on suitable food.

**Sample answer:** Mould releases spores from spore cases. Air carries a spore to the new food. If conditions suit growth, it germinates and forms fungal threads.

## 2. Apply (4 marks)

**Question:** Two open dishes of cooked rice are placed on opposite sides of a room. After several days, fungal growth appears in both dishes. Explain why the rice did not need to touch and identify two conditions that would affect whether a landed spore grows.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for airborne spore dispersal; 1 for landing in the second dish; 1 each for two relevant conditions such as moisture, nutrients or suitable temperature.

**Sample answer:** Airborne spores can land in both dishes without contact between the rice samples. A spore needs moisture and nutrients to germinate; temperature also affects growth.

## 3. Analyse (5 marks)

**Question:** Identical bread samples are kept in sealed transparent bags. Bag A contains dry bread; bag B contains moist bread. Both have visible spores on the surface at day 0. By day 4, only B has extensive fungal threads and spore cases. Analyse the observations and explain why visible spores at day 0 did not prove that new mould was growing.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for spores present on both; 1 for moisture enabling germination in B; 1 for thread growth; 1 for spore cases as new reproductive structures; 1 for dormant deposited spores versus active colony growth.

**Sample answer:** Spores were already present in both bags, but moisture in B enabled them to germinate. Fungal threads then spread across the bread and produced new spore cases. Spores seen on day 0 could simply have landed there and remained dormant, so they did not establish active growth.
