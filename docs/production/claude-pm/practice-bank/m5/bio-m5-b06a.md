# Draft for owner review: bio-m5-b06a

## 1. Compare (3 marks)

**Question:** Compare the location and form of the main chromosome in a typical bacterium and a human cell.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for bacterial nucleoid; 1 for circular bacterial chromosome; 1 for linear human chromosomes in a nucleus.

**Sample answer:** A typical bacterium has a circular chromosome in its nucleoid, without a membrane-bound nucleus. Human cells have linear chromosomes inside a nucleus.

## 2. Apply (4 marks)

**Question:** A microscopic cell contains a circular main chromosome in a nucleoid and three small circular DNA molecules. Identify the cell type and explain the likely identity of the smaller molecules and one way they differ from the main chromosome.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for prokaryotic cell; 1 for plasmids; 1 for smaller separate DNA circles; 1 for transfer or accessory genes.

**Sample answer:** It is a prokaryotic cell, such as a bacterium. The small DNA circles are plasmids, separate from the main chromosome. They can carry accessory genes and may transfer between cells.

## 3. Analyse (5 marks)

**Question:** A student states that bacterial DNA is loose because bacteria lack a nucleus, while eukaryotic DNA is compact only when a cell divides. Analyse both statements using nucleoid organisation, proteins and chromosomes.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for bacterial DNA coiled and organised; 1 for nucleoid; 1 for proteins helping pack bacterial DNA; 1 for eukaryotic DNA associated with histones; 1 for chromosomes remaining organised outside division, though less condensed.

**Sample answer:** Bacterial DNA is organised in the nucleoid, coiled and associated with proteins despite the absence of a nucleus. Eukaryotic DNA wraps around histones and is packaged as chromosomes. It becomes more condensed for division, but it is still organised between divisions.
