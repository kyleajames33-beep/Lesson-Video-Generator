# Draft for owner review: bio-m5-g01

## 1. Explain (3 marks)

**Question:** Explain why sister chromatids can have different alleles after meiosis I, even though they began as copies of one chromosome.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for homologous chromosomes pairing; 1 for crossing over between non-sister chromatids; 1 for exchanged segments changing allele combinations.

**Sample answer:** Homologous chromosomes pair during meiosis I. Non-sister chromatids can exchange matching segments in crossing over. After the exchange, sister chromatids may carry different allele combinations.

## 2. Apply (4 marks)

**Question:** One DNA strand reads 5′-GATCCA-3′. Write the complementary strand in the opposite direction. Identify the number of A–T pairs and G–C pairs in this six-base-pair section.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for 3′-CTAGGT-5′; 1 for antiparallel direction; 1 for three A–T pairs; 1 for three G–C pairs.

**Sample answer:** The complement is 3′-CTAGGT-5′. There are three A–T pairs and three G–C pairs.

## 3. Analyse (5 marks)

**Question:** A diploid cell has 2n = 6. It has completed DNA replication but not meiosis I. State the chromosome and chromatid counts now, after meiosis I in each daughter cell, and after meiosis II in each gamete. Explain the two separations.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for 6 chromosomes and 12 chromatids before meiosis I; 1 for 3 chromosomes and 6 chromatids after meiosis I; 1 for 3 chromosomes and 3 chromatids after meiosis II; 1 for homologues separating in I; 1 for sister chromatids separating in II.

**Sample answer:** The replicated diploid cell has 6 chromosomes with 12 chromatids. Each cell after meiosis I has 3 chromosomes with 6 chromatids because homologues separate. Each gamete after meiosis II has 3 chromosomes with 3 chromatids because sister chromatids separate.
