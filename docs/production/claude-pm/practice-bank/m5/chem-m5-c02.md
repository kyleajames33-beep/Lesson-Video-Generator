# Draft for owner review: chem-m5-c02

## 1. Explain (3 marks)

**Question:** Explain why the rate of the reverse reaction can rise after a reversible reaction begins with reactants only.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for products forming; 1 for increasing product concentration or collision frequency; 1 for faster reverse reaction.

**Sample answer:** The forward reaction makes products. As their concentration rises, product particles collide more often and the reverse reaction speeds up.

## 2. Apply (4 marks)

**Question:** In a sealed vessel, A(g) reversibly forms B(g). At 10 minutes the forward rate is 8 mmol min⁻¹ and the reverse rate is 5 mmol min⁻¹. Predict the immediate direction of net change in the amount of B and describe what happens to the two rates as the system approaches equilibrium.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for net B formation; 1 for 3 mmol min⁻¹ net rate; 1 for forward rate tending to decrease; 1 for reverse rate tending to increase until equal.

**Sample answer:** B increases at a net rate of 3 mmol min⁻¹ at that instant. As A is used the forward rate tends to fall; as B builds up the reverse rate tends to rise. At equilibrium the rates are equal.

## 3. Analyse (5 marks)

**Question:** A graph for X(g) ⇌ Y(g) shows [X] falling from 0.80 to 0.45 mol L⁻¹ and [Y] rising from 0.10 to 0.45 mol L⁻¹ over 6 minutes. Both curves then become horizontal. Analyse what the graph shows and what it cannot, by itself, show about molecular events after 6 minutes.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for X being consumed; 1 for Y forming; 1 for constant concentrations after 6 minutes; 1 for equal rates if dynamic equilibrium is established; 1 for graph alone not showing stopped versus continuing molecular reactions.

**Sample answer:** X decreases while Y increases, then both concentrations stay constant. In a reversible closed system, this is consistent with dynamic equilibrium, where forward and reverse rates are equal and nonzero. A concentration graph alone does not directly show individual reactions continuing; rate or tracer evidence would establish that.
