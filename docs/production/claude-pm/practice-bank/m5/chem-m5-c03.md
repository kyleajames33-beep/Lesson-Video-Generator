# Draft for owner review: chem-m5-c03

## 1. Explain (3 marks)

**Question:** Explain why adding a reactant to a mixture at equilibrium can increase the amount of product after the mixture settles again.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for the immediate rise in reactant concentration; 1 for the forward reaction becoming faster than the reverse; 1 for net product formation until rates become equal.

**Sample answer:** Adding reactant immediately raises its concentration. The forward reaction speeds up relative to the reverse reaction, so more product forms until the two rates become equal again.

## 2. Apply (4 marks)

**Question:** For A(aq) + B(aq) ⇌ C(aq), the mixture is at equilibrium. Some C is removed without changing temperature. Predict the immediate concentration change and the subsequent shift. Explain the shift using reaction rates.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for immediate decrease in [C]; 1 for shift right; 1 for initially slower reverse reaction; 1 for net C formation until equal rates return.

**Sample answer:** Removing C immediately lowers [C]. The reverse reaction slows because fewer C particles are available, while the forward reaction initially continues at its previous rate. Net forward reaction makes more C until the rates balance again.

## 3. Analyse (5 marks)

**Question:** For P(aq) + heat ⇌ Q(aq), a vessel at equilibrium is heated. A graph shows both reaction rates jumping, with the forward rate jumping more, then converging. Analyse the graph and predict the change in the settled amount of Q.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for heat as a reactant; 1 for both rates increasing immediately; 1 for forward rate increasing more; 1 for net forward reaction; 1 for higher settled Q amount.

**Sample answer:** The forward reaction takes in heat. Heating speeds both reactions, but the forward rate increases more, making Q faster than it is consumed. The mixture shifts right until the rates meet again, leaving more Q at the new equilibrium.
