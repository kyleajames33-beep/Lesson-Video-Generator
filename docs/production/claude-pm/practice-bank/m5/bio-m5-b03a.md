# Draft for owner review: bio-m5-b03a

## 1. Explain (3 marks)

**Question:** Explain how hCG helps maintain the uterine lining soon after implantation.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for embryo producing hCG; 1 for hCG maintaining corpus luteum; 1 for continued progesterone maintaining lining.

**Sample answer:** After implantation, the embryo produces hCG. This keeps the corpus luteum active, so it continues to secrete progesterone. Progesterone maintains the uterine lining.

## 2. Apply (4 marks)

**Question:** An embryo reaches the uterus but does not implant. Explain why an established pregnancy is unlikely, using the roles of implantation, hCG, the corpus luteum and progesterone.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for implantation failure; 1 for inadequate embryonic hCG signal; 1 for corpus luteum not maintained; 1 for progesterone falling and lining not maintained.

**Sample answer:** Without implantation, the embryo does not establish itself in the lining. Its hCG signal does not maintain the corpus luteum. Progesterone then falls and the uterine lining is not maintained to support the pregnancy.

## 3. Sequence and explain (5 marks)

**Question:** In an early pregnancy, progesterone remains high after the time when the corpus luteum would normally stop functioning. A blood test detects rising hCG. Analyse how fertilisation, blastocyst implantation and this hormone result are causally connected.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for fertilisation in oviduct; 1 for cleavage and blastocyst formation; 1 for implantation in uterine lining; 1 for hCG maintaining corpus luteum; 1 for continued progesterone maintaining lining.

**Sample answer:** Fertilisation occurs in the oviduct. The embryo undergoes cleavage and forms a blastocyst as it moves to the uterus, where it implants. The implanted embryo produces hCG, which keeps the corpus luteum active. The corpus luteum continues making progesterone, maintaining the uterine lining.
