## 1. Explain (3 marks)

**Question:** Explain why the molar solubility of a salt cannot always be read directly from its Ksp value.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for Ksp as product of ion concentrations; 1 for stoichiometric ion ratios; 1 for powers in expression affecting solubility.

**Sample answer:** Ksp is the product of equilibrium ion concentrations, each raised to its coefficient. One formula unit may release different numbers of each ion, so their concentrations are multiples of molar solubility rather than always equal to it.

## 2. Calculate (4 marks)

**Question:** The NESA data sheet gives Ksp(AgCl) = 1.77 × 10⁻¹⁰ at 25 °C. Calculate the molar solubility of AgCl in pure water.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for AgCl(s) ⇌ Ag⁺ + Cl⁻; 1 for Ksp = s²; 1 for square root; 1 for s = 1.33 × 10⁻⁵ mol L⁻¹.

**Sample answer:** Dissolving s mol L⁻¹ AgCl makes s of each ion. Ksp = [Ag⁺][Cl⁻] = s², so s = √(1.77 × 10⁻¹⁰) = 1.33 × 10⁻⁵ mol L⁻¹.

## 3. Analyse (5 marks)

**Question:** The NESA data sheet gives Ksp(PbI₂) = 9.8 × 10⁻⁹ at 25 °C. Derive its Ksp expression, calculate its molar solubility in pure water, and explain the factor of 2 in the calculation.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for PbI₂(s) ⇌ Pb²⁺ + 2I⁻; 1 for Ksp = [Pb²⁺][I⁻]²; 1 for s(2s)² = 4s³; 1 for s = 1.35 × 10⁻³ mol L⁻¹; 1 for two iodide ions per formula unit.

**Sample answer:** PbI₂(s) ⇌ Pb²⁺(aq) + 2I⁻(aq), so Ksp = [Pb²⁺][I⁻]² = s(2s)² = 4s³. Thus s = (9.8 × 10⁻⁹/4)^(1/3) = 1.35 × 10⁻³ mol L⁻¹. Each dissolved unit releases two iodide ions, so [I⁻] = 2s.
