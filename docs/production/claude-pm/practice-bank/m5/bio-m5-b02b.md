# Draft for owner review: bio-m5-b02b

## 1. Explain (3 marks)

**Question:** Distinguish pollination from fertilisation in a flowering plant.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for pollen transfer to stigma; 1 for sperm reaching ovule via pollen tube; 1 for sperm and egg fusion in fertilisation.

**Sample answer:** Pollination transfers pollen to a stigma. A pollen tube then grows towards an ovule and carries sperm. Fertilisation occurs when a sperm fuses with the egg.

## 2. Apply (4 marks)

**Question:** A flower receives compatible pollen, but its pollen tubes fail to grow. Predict whether seeds can form and explain the sequence of events that is interrupted.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for pollination occurring; 1 for failed sperm delivery; 1 for no fertilisation; 1 for no seed from that ovule.

**Sample answer:** Pollination has occurred, but sperm cannot travel to the ovule without a pollen tube. The egg is not fertilised, so that ovule cannot develop into a seed.

## 3. Analyse (5 marks)

**Question:** A grower plants ten genetically identical strawberry runners and ten seedlings produced from seeds. A new fungal disease kills nine runners but three seedlings survive. Analyse how the two propagation methods could explain the result. State one limit of what this observation proves.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for runners as asexual propagation; 1 for similar genotype among runners; 1 for seed formation through sexual reproduction; 1 for variation among seedlings; 1 for a limit such as small sample or environmental differences.

**Sample answer:** Runners are asexual copies, so they may share susceptibility to the fungus. Seedlings arise through sexual reproduction and vary genetically, so some may carry resistance. The result is consistent with this explanation, but ten plants per group and uncontrolled growing conditions cannot establish genotype as the only cause.
