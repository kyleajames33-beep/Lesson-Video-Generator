# Draft for owner review: chem-m5-c11

## 1. Explain (3 marks)

**Question:** Explain how water molecules separate ions from the surface of an ionic crystal.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for water polarity; 1 for oxygen end attracting cations and hydrogen end attracting anions; 1 for hydration stabilising separated ions.

**Sample answer:** Polar water molecules attract surface ions. Their oxygen ends face cations and hydrogen ends face anions. Water surrounds the separated ions, helping keep them dispersed in solution.

## 2. Apply (4 marks)

**Question:** A crystal of potassium bromide dissolves in water. Write its dissolution equation with state symbols and describe the orientation of water around each ion.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for KBr(s) → K⁺(aq) + Br⁻(aq); 1 for one of each ion; 1 for oxygen towards K⁺; 1 for hydrogen towards Br⁻.

**Sample answer:** KBr(s) → K⁺(aq) + Br⁻(aq). Water molecules turn their partially negative oxygen ends towards K⁺ and their partially positive hydrogen ends towards Br⁻.

## 3. Analyse (5 marks)

**Question:** A student draws dissolved calcium chloride as separate CaCl₂ molecules floating among water molecules. Analyse the diagram, state the particles actually present, and explain how water keeps them dispersed.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for rejecting intact dissolved units; 1 for Ca²⁺; 1 for two Cl⁻ per formula unit; 1 for correct water orientations; 1 for ion hydration.

**Sample answer:** Dissolved CaCl₂ separates into Ca²⁺(aq) and 2Cl⁻(aq), not intact CaCl₂ molecules. Water surrounds each ion. Its oxygen ends face Ca²⁺ and its hydrogen ends face Cl⁻, stabilising the dispersed ions.
