# Draft for owner review: bio-m5-b02c

## 1. Explain (3 marks)

**Question:** Explain why DNA must be copied before a single-celled organism completes binary fission or budding.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for DNA carrying inherited information; 1 for copying before separation; 1 for each offspring receiving genetic information.

**Sample answer:** DNA carries the cell's inherited information. Copying it before separation allows each new cell, whether formed by fission or budding, to receive the information needed to function and reproduce.

## 2. Apply (4 marks)

**Question:** A yeast cell makes a small daughter cell attached to its surface. A bacterium lengthens and separates into two similar-sized cells. Identify each reproductive method and explain one structural observation supporting each identification.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for yeast budding; 1 for smaller daughter outgrowth; 1 for bacterial binary fission; 1 for division into similar-sized cells.

**Sample answer:** The yeast reproduces by budding because a smaller daughter grows from the parent. The bacterium reproduces by binary fission because the parent divides into two cells of similar size.

## 3. Calculate and explain (5 marks)

**Question:** A culture starts with 250 bacterial cells. Every cell divides by binary fission once every 30 minutes, and none dies. Calculate the cell number after 2 hours. Explain why the answer is not 250 plus four cells.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for four division rounds; 1 for doubling in each round; 1 for 250 × 2⁴; 1 for 4000 cells; 1 for explaining that every descendant also divides.

**Sample answer:** Two hours contains four 30-minute rounds. The population is 250 × 2⁴ = 4000 cells. Every cell present at each round divides, including daughters from earlier rounds, so growth is repeated doubling rather than adding one cell per round.
