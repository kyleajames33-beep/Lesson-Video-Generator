# Draft for owner review: bio-m5-b03b

## 1. Explain (3 marks)

**Question:** Explain how artificial pollination allows a grower to choose both parent plants.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for choosing pollen donor; 1 for choosing recipient flower; 1 for controlled pollen transfer leading to selected cross.

**Sample answer:** The grower chooses a pollen donor and a flower on the recipient plant, then transfers that pollen to its stigma. The resulting seeds have the chosen parents if unwanted pollen is excluded.

## 2. Apply (4 marks)

**Question:** A dairy farmer wants offspring from a bull on a distant farm without transporting the bull. Explain how artificial insemination can achieve this and why the calf is not a clone of the bull.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for collected sperm; 1 for insemination of selected cow; 1 for sperm and egg fusion; 1 for genetic contribution from both parents.

**Sample answer:** Sperm from the chosen bull is collected and placed in the selected cow's reproductive tract. A sperm fertilises an egg. The calf inherits genetic information from both the bull and cow, so it is not a clone.

## 3. Evaluate (5 marks)

**Question:** A grower can propagate a high-yield crop by cuttings or controlled crosses between several high-yield parents. Evaluate the methods for rapid establishment and resilience to a new pathogen.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for quick uniform cuttings; 1 for shared genotype and vulnerability; 1 for crosses producing varied offspring; 1 for variable yield among seedlings; 1 for justified choice or combined strategy.

**Sample answer:** Cuttings rapidly reproduce the known high-yield genotype and make a uniform crop, but many plants may share susceptibility to a new pathogen. Controlled crosses take longer and offspring may vary in yield, but genetic variation may include resistance. I would use crosses to build longer-term resilience while retaining some cuttings for immediate production.
