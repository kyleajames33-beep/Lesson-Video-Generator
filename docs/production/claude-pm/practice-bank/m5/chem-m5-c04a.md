# Draft for owner review: chem-m5-c04a

## 1. Explain (3 marks)

**Question:** Explain why compressing an equilibrium mixture can favour the side with fewer gas particles.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for increased gas concentrations or pressure; 1 for different gas stoichiometric totals; 1 for the shift reducing the total gas particle count.

**Sample answer:** Compression crowds all gas particles. If one side of the equation has fewer gas particles, forming that side reduces the pressure increase, so the mixture shifts towards it.

## 2. Apply (4 marks)

**Question:** A sealed vessel contains 2A(g) ⇌ B(g) + C(g) at equilibrium. Its volume is halved at constant temperature. Predict whether the composition shifts and justify your answer using gas particle counts.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for two gas particles on the left; 1 for two on the right; 1 for no composition shift; 1 for explaining that neither direction reduces gas particle count.

**Sample answer:** Both sides contain two gas particles per reaction event. Compression initially raises every gas concentration, but neither direction reduces the total gas particle count, so there is no equilibrium composition shift.

## 3. Analyse (5 marks)

**Question:** For 2D(g) + E(g) ⇌ F(g) + G(s), a piston compresses the mixture at constant temperature. Predict the direction of the shift, explain why G is excluded from the gas count, and state what happens to F during adjustment.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for three gaseous particles on the left; 1 for one on the right; 1 for shift right; 1 for solid G excluded from pressure count; 1 for net F formation.

**Sample answer:** The left side has three gas particles and the right has one. G is a solid, so its particles do not contribute to gas pressure. Compression favours the right, producing more F until the gas reaction rates balance at the new composition.
