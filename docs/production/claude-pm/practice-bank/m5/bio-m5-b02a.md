# Draft for owner review: bio-m5-b02a

## 1. Explain (3 marks)

**Question:** Explain why fertilisation success may be low when external fertilisation occurs in moving water.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for gametes released outside bodies; 1 for water dispersing or diluting gametes; 1 for fewer sperm and eggs meeting.

**Sample answer:** Sperm and eggs are released outside the parents' bodies. Moving water can disperse and dilute them, so fewer sperm encounter eggs and fertilisation success may be low.

## 2. Apply (4 marks)

**Question:** A reef animal releases sperm and eggs into seawater on only one night each year. Explain two ways this timing and the water affect its reproductive success.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for simultaneous release; 1 for increased gamete encounter; 1 for water carrying sperm; 1 for risk of dilution or loss.

**Sample answer:** Releasing gametes at the same time increases the chance that sperm and eggs meet. Water allows sperm to move or be carried between eggs, but it can also disperse and dilute gametes, so many may never meet.

## 3. Evaluate (5 marks)

**Question:** Species A deposits 12 protected eggs in a nest and guards the young. Species B releases 20,000 eggs into a river and provides no care. Evaluate the claim that B must produce more surviving adults because it produces more eggs.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for rejecting egg count alone; 1 for external gamete or egg loss in B; 1 for protection in A; 1 for parental care in A; 1 for survival to reproduction as the relevant outcome.

**Sample answer:** More eggs do not guarantee more surviving adults. Many of B's eggs may fail to be fertilised or may be eaten. A protects fewer eggs and guards the young, which can increase survival per egg. The number that survive and later reproduce determines continuity, so adult output cannot be inferred from egg number alone.
