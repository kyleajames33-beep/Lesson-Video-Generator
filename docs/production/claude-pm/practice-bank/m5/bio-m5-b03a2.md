# Draft for owner review: bio-m5-b03a2

## 1. Explain (3 marks)

**Question:** Explain how high progesterone levels during pregnancy reduce the chance of another ovulation.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for progesterone negative feedback; 1 for reduced pituitary FSH and LH; 1 for inhibited follicle development or ovulation.

**Sample answer:** Progesterone feeds back to the brain and pituitary, reducing FSH and LH secretion. Without the usual hormone rise, another follicle does not mature and ovulate.

## 2. Apply (4 marks)

**Question:** During labour, stretching of the cervix stimulates oxytocin release. Oxytocin strengthens uterine contractions, which increase cervical stretch. Identify the feedback type and explain each link in the loop.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for positive feedback; 1 for stretch stimulating oxytocin; 1 for oxytocin strengthening contractions; 1 for stronger contractions causing more stretch.

**Sample answer:** This is positive feedback. Cervical stretch triggers oxytocin release. Oxytocin increases uterine contraction strength, which presses the baby against the cervix and increases stretch, reinforcing the signal.

## 3. Analyse (5 marks)

**Question:** A student's diagram shows progesterone increasing FSH and LH secretion during pregnancy, followed by monthly ovulation. Analyse the two errors and redraw the causal chain in words. Then contrast this feedback with the labour loop.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for progesterone inhibiting FSH; 1 for inhibiting LH; 1 for no monthly ovulation; 1 for negative feedback during pregnancy; 1 for positive oxytocin loop in labour.

**Sample answer:** Progesterone reduces FSH and LH, so monthly follicle maturation and ovulation are suppressed. This is negative feedback that stabilises pregnancy. During labour, cervical stretch triggers oxytocin, stronger contractions cause more stretch, and the positive feedback amplifies labour until birth.
