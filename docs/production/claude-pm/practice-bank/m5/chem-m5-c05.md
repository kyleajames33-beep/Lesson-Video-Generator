# Draft for owner review: chem-m5-c05

## 1. Explain (3 marks)

**Question:** Explain why coefficients become powers in an equilibrium constant expression, and state how a pure solid is treated.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for each aqueous or gas concentration raised to its coefficient; 1 for products over reactants; 1 for pure solids omitted.

**Sample answer:** The balanced equation sets each concentration's power. Product terms are divided by reactant terms. A pure solid is left out because its activity is constant.

## 2. Apply (4 marks)

**Question:** Write Kc for 2NO₂(g) ⇌ N₂O₄(g). Explain how the expression changes if the equation is reversed.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for [N₂O₄]/[NO₂]²; 1 for coefficient 2 as power; 1 for reciprocal on reversing; 1 for reversed expression [NO₂]²/[N₂O₄].

**Sample answer:** Kc = [N₂O₄]/[NO₂]². The coefficient 2 becomes the power on [NO₂]. Reversing the equation swaps numerator and denominator, giving [NO₂]²/[N₂O₄].

## 3. Analyse (5 marks)

**Question:** For CaCO₃(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO₂(g), a student writes Kc = [CaO][CO₂]/[CaCO₃]. Correct the expression and explain why adding more CaCO₃(s) at fixed temperature does not change Kc while both solids remain present.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for Kc = [CO₂]; 1 each for omitting CaCO₃ and CaO; 1 for pure solid activity being constant; 1 for Kc depending on temperature, not solid amount.

**Sample answer:** Kc = [CO₂]. Both CaCO₃ and CaO are pure solids, so their activities are constant and excluded. Adding CaCO₃ does not change the equilibrium CO₂ concentration or Kc at the same temperature, provided both solid phases remain present.
