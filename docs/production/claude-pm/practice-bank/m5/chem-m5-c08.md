# Draft for owner review: chem-m5-c08

## 1. Explain (3 marks)

**Question:** Explain how comparing Q with K predicts the direction of net reaction.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for Q using current concentrations; 1 for Q < K favouring products; 1 for Q > K favouring reactants.

**Sample answer:** Q is calculated from the mixture's present concentrations. If Q is below K, net forward reaction raises Q. If Q is above K, net reverse reaction lowers Q until Q equals K.

## 2. Apply (4 marks)

**Question:** For A(aq) ⇌ B(aq), Kc = 4.0 at a stated temperature. A mixture has [A] = 0.20 mol L⁻¹ and [B] = 0.40 mol L⁻¹. Calculate Q and predict the net direction.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for Q = [B]/[A]; 1 for Q = 2.0; 1 for Q < K; 1 for net forward reaction.

**Sample answer:** Q = 0.40/0.20 = 2.0. Since 2.0 < 4.0, the mixture forms more B as it approaches equilibrium.

## 3. Analyse (5 marks)

**Question:** For 2C(g) ⇌ D(g), Kc = 5.0 at 300 K. A gas sample has [C] = 0.40 mol L⁻¹ and [D] = 1.60 mol L⁻¹. Calculate Q and explain the direction of change in both concentrations.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for Q = [D]/[C]²; 1 for Q = 10; 1 for Q > K; 1 for net reverse direction; 1 for [D] falling and [C] rising.

**Sample answer:** Q = 1.60/(0.40)² = 10. Because Q exceeds K, net reverse reaction occurs. D is consumed and C is formed until Q falls to 5.0.
