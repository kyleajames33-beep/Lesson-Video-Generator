# Draft for owner review: bio-m5-b09

## 1. Explain (3 marks)

**Question:** Explain how two parents with a dominant phenotype can have an offspring with a recessive phenotype.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for both parents heterozygous; 1 for each passing the recessive allele; 1 for homozygous recessive offspring.

**Sample answer:** Each parent may carry one dominant and one recessive allele. If both pass the recessive allele, the offspring is homozygous recessive and shows the recessive phenotype.

## 2. Apply (4 marks)

**Question:** In a plant, purple flowers (P) are dominant to white flowers (p). Cross a heterozygous purple plant with a white plant. Give the possible offspring genotypes and expected phenotype proportions.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for Pp × pp; 1 for gametes P,p and p; 1 for Pp and pp offspring; 1 for 1:1 purple:white.

**Sample answer:** Pp × pp gives gametes P and p from the first parent and p from the second. Half the offspring are Pp (purple) and half are pp (white), giving a 1:1 phenotype ratio.

## 3. Analyse (5 marks)

**Question:** In a pedigree, two unaffected parents have an affected daughter. Assume one autosomal gene with complete dominance. Infer the likely inheritance pattern and parental genotypes. Calculate the chance their next child is affected, then explain whether the sex of the affected daughter changes that probability.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for recessive affected trait; 1 for both parents carriers Aa; 1 for aa affected child; 1 for 1/4 probability; 1 for sex not changing autosomal probability.

**Sample answer:** The trait is likely autosomal recessive because unaffected carrier parents can have an affected child. Both parents are Aa, and the daughter is aa. An Aa × Aa cross gives aa in 1 of 4 outcomes. Because the assumed gene is autosomal, the daughter's sex does not alter the 1/4 risk for the next child.
