# Draft for owner review: chem-m5-p03

## 1. Explain (3 marks)

**Question:** Explain why a well-plate investigation of solubility rules can use nitrate salts and sodium salts as sources of the ions being tested.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for nitrate salts generally soluble; 1 for sodium salts generally soluble; 1 for any precipitate attributable to the tested ion pair.

**Sample answer:** Nitrate and sodium salts usually dissolve, providing separate ions in solution. If mixing two wells forms a solid, the tested cation and anion are the likely precipitating pair.

## 2. Apply (4 marks)

**Question:** A well-plate row tests Mg²⁺ with Cl⁻, CO₃²⁻ and NO₃⁻. Only the carbonate well forms a cloudy solid. State the rule supported by this row and one control that would improve the validity of the comparison.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for MgCO₃ poorly soluble; 1 for MgCl₂ soluble in test; 1 for Mg(NO₃)₂ soluble in test; 1 for equal drop volumes or concentrations as control.

**Sample answer:** The observations support magnesium carbonate being poorly soluble under the test conditions, while magnesium chloride and nitrate remain dissolved. Keeping solution concentrations and drop volumes equal makes the wells more comparable.

## 3. Evaluate (5 marks)

**Question:** A student reports an unexpected cloudy well. The pipette had previously been used for a different ion solution and was not rinsed. Evaluate the effect on validity and design a repeat that distinguishes contamination from a real precipitation result.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for possible cross-contamination; 1 for unclear ion identity; 1 for reduced validity; 1 for clean separate pipettes or rinsing; 1 for repeat and comparison with a blank or fresh solutions.

**Sample answer:** Residual ions from the earlier solution could produce the cloudiness, so the well no longer tests only its intended pair and the result is not valid evidence for a rule. Repeat with fresh solutions and clean dedicated pipettes, include a blank, and compare repeated wells for consistent precipitation.
