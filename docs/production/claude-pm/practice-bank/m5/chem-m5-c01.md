# Draft for owner review: chem-m5-c01

## 1. Explain (3 marks)

**Question:** Explain how a sealed vessel can contain an equilibrium mixture while molecules continue to react.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for forward and reverse reactions continuing; 1 for equal rates; 1 for constant macroscopic concentrations.

**Sample answer:** Both reactions continue, but their rates are equal. Each substance is formed as quickly as it is used, so its concentration stays constant.

## 2. Apply (4 marks)

**Question:** A sealed transparent flask contains a liquid and its vapour at constant temperature. Liquid molecules enter the gas at the same rate that gas molecules return to the liquid. Analyse whether this is static or dynamic equilibrium.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for dynamic; 1 for continuing evaporation; 1 for continuing condensation; 1 for equal rates and constant amounts.

**Sample answer:** This is dynamic equilibrium. Evaporation and condensation both continue. Their rates are equal, so the amounts of liquid and vapour remain constant.

## 3. Analyse (5 marks)

**Question:** Two sealed chambers exchange gas through a narrow tube. At noon, 24 molecules per second move from A to B and 24 move from B to A. Chamber A contains 600 gas molecules and B contains 400. A student claims that equilibrium is impossible because the chambers contain different amounts. Analyse the claim and state what extra condition is needed before concluding that the system is at dynamic equilibrium.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for rejecting equal amounts as a requirement; 1 for identifying equal opposing transfer rates; 1 for recognising transfer continues; 1 for constant chamber amounts if rates remain equal; 1 for requiring sustained equal rates under unchanged conditions.

**Sample answer:** Equal amounts are unnecessary. The opposing transfers are both 24 molecules per second, so there is no net change while both transfers continue. If the rates remain equal under unchanged conditions and the chamber amounts remain constant, the system is at dynamic equilibrium. A single instant of equal rates does not establish that it has settled.
