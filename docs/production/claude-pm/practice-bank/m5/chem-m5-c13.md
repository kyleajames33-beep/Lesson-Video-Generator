# Draft for owner review: chem-m5-c13

## 1. Explain (3 marks)

**Question:** Explain the difference between a molecular equation and a net ionic equation for a precipitation reaction.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for molecular equation showing whole formulas; 1 for soluble ionic substances dissociating in the ionic equation; 1 for spectator ions removed in net ionic equation.

**Sample answer:** A molecular equation writes complete compound formulas. An ionic equation separates soluble substances into ions. The net ionic equation removes unchanged spectator ions and shows only particles that form the precipitate.

## 2. Apply (4 marks)

**Question:** Aqueous calcium nitrate and sodium carbonate are mixed. Predict the precipitate and write a balanced net ionic equation. Identify one spectator ion.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for CaCO₃(s); 1 for Ca²⁺(aq) + CO₃²⁻(aq) → CaCO₃(s); 1 for charge and atom balance; 1 for Na⁺ or NO₃⁻ spectator.

**Sample answer:** Calcium carbonate precipitates. Ca²⁺(aq) + CO₃²⁻(aq) → CaCO₃(s). Na⁺ and NO₃⁻ stay dissolved and are spectator ions.

## 3. Analyse (5 marks)

**Question:** Equal volumes of 0.20 mol L⁻¹ Ca(NO₃)₂(aq) and 0.10 mol L⁻¹ Na₂CO₃(aq) are mixed. Identify the precipitate, write its net ionic equation, and determine which reacting ion remains in excess after complete precipitation.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for CaCO₃; 1 for balanced net ionic equation; 1 for equal-volume mole ratio 2:1 Ca²⁺:CO₃²⁻; 1 for CO₃²⁻ limiting; 1 for excess Ca²⁺.

**Sample answer:** CaCO₃(s) forms by Ca²⁺ + CO₃²⁻ → CaCO₃(s). In equal volumes, the calcium concentration is twice the carbonate concentration, so there are twice as many calcium ions. Carbonate is used up first and calcium ions remain in solution.
