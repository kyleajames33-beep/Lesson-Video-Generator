# Draft for owner review: bio-m5-b01

## 1. Explain (3 marks)

**Question:** Explain how sexual reproduction can help a population continue when its environment changes.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for offspring inheriting DNA from two parents; 1 for variation; 1 for some variants surviving and reproducing under changed conditions.

**Sample answer:** Sexual reproduction combines genetic information from two parents, producing varied offspring. If conditions change, some offspring may have traits that help them survive and reproduce.

## 2. Apply (4 marks)

**Question:** A freshwater plant spreads rapidly by producing new plants from stem fragments. A newly introduced insect eats most plants, but a few survive. Compare the likely benefits of vegetative reproduction and sexual reproduction for this population.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for rapid propagation without a partner; 1 for similar offspring from fragments; 1 for variation from sexual reproduction; 1 for explaining why variation matters under insect pressure.

**Sample answer:** Stem fragments can quickly produce many plants without a partner, but the new plants are genetically similar and may share susceptibility. Sexual reproduction is slower but produces varied offspring. Some may resist the insect and reproduce.

## 3. Evaluate (5 marks)

**Question:** A conservation program can produce either 100 clones of one disease-resistant shrub or 40 seedlings from crosses among several resistant shrubs. Evaluate the two choices for restoring a population that faces an unknown future disease.

**Marking criteria:** 1 mark for clone speed or numbers; 1 for shared clone genotype; 1 for genetic variation in seedlings; 1 for linking variation to uncertain disease resistance; 1 for a justified judgement.

**Sample answer:** Cloning provides more shrubs quickly and keeps the known resistant genotype. However, all clones share much of their genetic vulnerability. Crosses among several parents create variation, so some seedlings may resist a future disease that defeats the original genotype. I would favour the seedlings for long-term survival, while recognising their smaller initial number.
