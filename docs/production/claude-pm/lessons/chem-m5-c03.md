- **Cobalt system choice (accepted by Claude):** the site writes CoCl2·6H2O(s) ⇌ CoCl2(s) + 6H2O(g). That is a solid and gas system whose colour change is mostly about water vapour escaping, which needs an open-system argument that fights C1. The syllabus example is "heating cobalt(II) chloride hydrate"; the standard class demonstration is the solution form [Co(H2O)6]2+(aq) (pink) + 4Cl-(aq) ⇌ [CoCl4]2-(aq) (blue) + 6H2O(l), forward endothermic, in a closed tube of concentrated chloride. It is in the memory-aid bank ("Cobalt: hot is blue"). The video uses the solution form because the site's hydrate equation depends on water vapour escaping, which would need an open-system argument that contradicts C1's closed system, and because the route keeps the oven/water-removal hydrate example for a separate practical home. The departure from the site equation is recorded here only.
- **Cause chain taught, in order:** (1) an equilibrium sits at equal rates; (2) a concentration change alters **one** rate at once: the reaction that needs the added substance speeds up (it meets partners more often), the opposite reaction is untouched at that instant (adding Fe3+ raises the joining rate, not the splitting rate), so nothing has reacted yet but the rates no longer match; (3) the faster reaction wins, concentrations move, the faster rate falls and the slower rate rises (the C2 aid reused), and the rates meet again at a new level, so **the net reaction runs in one direction until the rates match** (that is a "shift"); (4) it only partly cancels the change (Fe3+ ends at 15, not back at 10, with 2 of the 7 added units used); (5) removing a substance slows only the reaction that uses it, so the other reaction is faster and the shift goes toward what was removed; (6) a temperature change does not touch any concentration at that instant, but it changes **both** rates: only collisions with enough energy cause a reaction, heating makes those high-energy collisions much more common, and the reaction that takes in heat needs more energy from each collision than the one that gives it out, so it gains most; it wins until the rates match again, and cooling is the mirror image (it removes more of the hard collisions that reaction relies on); (7) "write heat into the equation" turns that into a direction rule, with the placement taught: heat on the left if the forward reaction takes in heat, on the right if it gives out heat (then heating shifts left). This is the collision-theory explanation the syllabus asks for (p1071), in particle-meeting and plain high-energy-collision terms only; activation energy is not named.
- **Recalls are named, not numbered:** C3 follows G01 in the playlist, so the spoken recalls point at the sealed P C L five flask, not at "last time". A 2 second C2 flask thumbnail with the recalled rate bars appears at the first mention (scene 1).
- **Immediate versus response (route stop rule):** every disturbance is shown in two beats on the same board: "the moment of the change" (one rate jumps, nothing has reacted, the colour has not moved yet) and "the response" (net reaction, rates close in, colour changes). The graph scene names the two jobs aloud.

Main question: *A mixture sitting at equilibrium looks settled. If we add more of one substance, or warm it, which way does it go, and why?*
Entry knowledge (C1, C2, G01 and Year 11): dynamic equilibrium means equal non-zero rates in a closed system; rate means how much changes per second; more particles meeting means more events per second; the concentration of each substance is the net result of the two rates; the faster rate falls and the slower rate rises until they meet (C2); a reaction that takes in heat is endothermic and the reverse gives out the same amount (Year 11, recalled in scene 7, and heat in or out used in G01).
Stops after: predicting the direction of the shift for adding or removing a substance and for heating or cooling, explaining each as which rate changes first and which becomes faster, and telling the immediate change from the reaction's response. No K, no pressure or volume, no catalysts.
Handoff: C4, squeezing a gas mixture (pressure and volume) and adding a catalyst.

### 7b. warming it
> Now we warm it in a hot water bath. That adds no ions and removes none, so no concentration changes at that moment. Instead, heating changes the rates. ### 13. key notes (quiet)
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

- Le Chatelier's principle: when an equilibrium is disturbed, the mixture shifts in the direction that partly cancels the disturbance.
- Adding a dissolved substance, or a gas in a fixed-size container, that is in the equation speeds up the reaction that uses it, so the shift goes away from it. Removing it slows that reaction, so the shift goes toward it.
- Heating speeds up both reactions but helps the one that takes in heat (endothermic) more. Cooling slows both but slows the endothermic one more. If the forward reaction gives out heat, heating favours the reverse reaction.
- A change shows up in two steps: first the rates change (one rate for a concentration change, both rates for a temperature change), then the net reaction runs until the rates match. The disturbance is only partly cancelled.

Memory aid (large, on the notes screen): *Treat heat like a chemical. Add it and the shift goes away from it. Take it away and the shift goes toward it. It only partly cancels the change.* Condition tag: "for a dissolved substance, a gas in a fixed-size container, or heat".

## Memory aid check

"Treat heat like a chemical. Add it and the shift goes away from it. Take it away and the shift goes toward it. It only partly cancels the change." Assumptions: a closed system; the substance is dissolved, or a gas in a fixed-size container, and is in the equation; heat is written on the correct side (taught in scene 9: left if the forward reaction takes in heat, right if it gives out heat); the aid predicts direction only and never size. Works for every case in the lesson: add Fe3+ (right), remove SCN- (left), heat cobalt (heat is a reactant, right, blue), cool N2O4 (heat is a reactant, left, much paler), remove NO2 (right). It also works for an exothermic reaction once heat is written on the right: heating adds to the right and the shift goes left. The step "heat on the correct side" is now spoken and drawn. Do not offer the aid for pure solids, a catalyst, an inert gas, a volume or pressure change (C4), or water added to a solution (water is a participant in the cobalt equation, but adding it also dilutes every concentration, so the aid is not offered for it). Heat is not a concentration and never goes into a calculation or an equilibrium expression. The C2 aid "The faster rate falls, the slower rate rises, they meet and stay level" is reused only as a callback and holds after every disturbance here, temperature included (the new equilibrium is at a new steady temperature). From the bank, "Change T, change K. Change anything else, K stays." is not used (K waits for C5 and C9). "Squeeze it and it makes fewer gas moles" and the inert gas aid are C4.
