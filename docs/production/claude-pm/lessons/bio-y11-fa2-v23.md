### 5. size, not usefulness
> Because it's small. The filter sorts by size, not by usefulness. It can't tell glucose, which your body needs, from urea, which it doesn't. Both are small, so both go through, along with salts, amino acids and lots of water. The fluid caught in the cup is called filtrate. It's basically plasma with the big proteins left behind. So it's wrong to say the kidney filters out only the waste. At this stage, it pushes out almost everything small.

### 6. pressure, not diffusion: bulk flow
> Look at the glucose again: it's at the same concentration in the filtrate as in the blood. That tells you this isn't diffusion, which only moves particles down a difference in concentration. Here, pressure pushes the water out, and everything small dissolved in it is carried along. So the filtrate leaves with the same mix of small particles as the plasma. Moving fluid along with everything in it like this is called bulk flow.

> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

- Nephron: the kidney's filtering unit (about a million per kidney). It starts with the glomerulus (a knot of capillaries) inside Bowman's capsule (a cup), which leads into the tubule.
- Pressure in the glomerulus is high because blood enters through a wide vessel and leaves through a narrower one (wide in, narrow out).

- **Nephron count (scene 2).** About 1 million nephrons per human kidney (range roughly 0.2 to 2 million). "about a million": correct (matches the site).
