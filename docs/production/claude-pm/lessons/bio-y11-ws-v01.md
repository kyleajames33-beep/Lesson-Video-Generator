## Decisions

- **Main learner question.** *How does an observation become a hypothesis an experiment can test, and why should new evidence change it?* Heading on screen: "How does a question become a hypothesis you can test?" (the text fa1-v20's Next chip must carry; change C5 in the status file). One causal chain: an observation raises a question (1, 2); a question is scientific when data can answer it (2); a hypothesis predicts an answer and gives the biology reason (3); primary or secondary data test it (4); questions and hypotheses are judged against four checks (5, 6); new evidence shows what the first hypothesis missed, so the question and hypothesis change (7 to 9); the modified question needs a new plan (10, 12).
- **Placement (approved by root, 23 Nov 2026).** Between `bio-y11-fa1-v20` (enzyme graphs) and `bio-y11-fa1-v21` (the enzyme fair test). Reasons: fa1-v20 ends on the chip "how do we know the factor caused it?", which is this video's starting point; fa1-v21 plans the test, which is where this video stops; the biology borrowed (collisions, active site shape, optimum) is all taught in fa1-v18 to v20.
- **Restore-and-pass joins.** Opens on fa1-v20's final frame. Ends on the same frame, item for item as `bio-y11-fa1-v21.md` "Opening frame" lists it (three graph thumbnails, two step chips, `y11-catalase-apparatus-v1.png`, the "?" chip, the chip "Next: How do we fairly test enzyme activity?"), so fa1-v21 needs no change. The only neighbour edit is fa1-v20's Next chip and spoken Next line (change C5; listed, not made, because placement is root's call).
- **Main example (catalase).** Potato tissue foaming in hydrogen peroxide is the apparatus already on screen, students have just learned its curve, and a hypothesis built only from "warmer is faster" is genuinely contradicted by data above the optimum. That makes the modification honest: the evidence, not the narrator, changes the hypothesis.
- **Hypothesis form.** "If [the change], then [the predicted result], because [the biology reason]", on screen as three coloured slots (change slot navy, prediction slot dark ink, reason slot amber). Plain meaning: "a hypothesis: a testable prediction with a reason". The because-clause is what separates it from a guess.
- **Hypothesis 1 (scene 3).** "If the temperature of the potato and peroxide increases, then more oxygen is released in 60 s, because enzyme and substrate particles move faster and collide more often." Deliberately incomplete over a wide range (it ignores the optimum) so the evidence in scene 7 can contradict it.
- **Primary and secondary data (scene 4).** Primary: "measured by you" (the class's own syringe readings). Secondary: "collected by someone else" (a published table of catalase activity, or a long-term flowering record). One question only secondary data can answer: "Have the flowering times of a eucalypt changed over the last 50 years?" (records collected by others; no one student can wait 50 years). Reliability of secondary sources is ws-v02's.
- **Evaluating (scenes 5, 6).** Questions first: narrow enough to test with data we can get ("What affects enzymes?" too broad). Then four checks for hypotheses: testable (something to change, something to measure)? specific (which variable, which direction)? reasoned (the because matches known biology)? could it be shown wrong (some result would contradict it)? Candidates: "Enzymes like warmth" (vague, no measurement, no reason); "Catalase is an enzyme" (true but predicts nothing, so no result can contradict it); hypothesis 1 (passes all four over the range it names). "Falsifiable" is not used as a learner word.
- **New evidence and modification (scenes 7 to 9).** Illustrative class data, oxygen in 60 s: 10 °C 8 mL, 20 °C 15, 30 °C 24, 40 °C 27, 50 °C 12, 60 °C 3. Rises to 40 °C support hypothesis 1; the falls at 50 and 60 contradict it. Modified hypothesis: "If temperature increases, then the oxygen released in 60 s rises to a maximum at the optimum and then falls, because faster collisions speed the reaction until heat changes the active site's shape." Modified question: "What is the optimum temperature for potato catalase?", which needs more readings between 30 and 50 °C. Scene 9 names what changed and why it is good science, not failure.
- **Predictions (3 s each).** Scene 5: "Which is the best hypothesis to test?" (the three candidates), answered in 6. Scene 7: "What should happen at 50 and 60 degrees, if hypothesis 1 is right?" answered at once by the data.
- **No risk scene.** No investigation is run; risk belongs to fa1-v21 (which follows) and ws-v02. Scene 7's slate footer reads "illustrative data; the next video plans the real test" (no video codes on screen).

## Practice question and feedback

1. (11a, propose, 3 marks; original.) Pondweed by a sunny window gives off more bubbles than in a dim corner; photosynthesis releases oxygen and needs light.
   Model: (a) "Does light intensity affect the rate of photosynthesis in pondweed?" or "How does light intensity affect the number of oxygen bubbles released per minute?" (1 mark: names an independent and a dependent variable). (b) "If light intensity increases, then the number of oxygen bubbles released per minute increases, because light provides the energy for photosynthesis, which releases oxygen" (1 mark for if and then with both variables, 1 mark for a biological reason). Classic mistake crossed: "the pondweed will bubble more". Other weak answers: a question about the window rather than light; "because plants like light".
2. (11c, modify, 3 marks; original.) The window tank is 26 °C and the corner tank 18 °C; enzymes work faster when warmer, up to an optimum.
   Model: (a) Light and temperature both differ between the tanks, so the first observation cannot show which caused the extra bubbles (1 mark); temperature alone could raise the rate (1 mark). (b) "At a fixed temperature, does light intensity affect the rate of photosynthesis in pondweed?" (1 mark). Classic mistake crossed: "the window proves light is the cause".
