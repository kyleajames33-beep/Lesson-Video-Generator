### 2. sketch it, with north lines
> Always start with a sketch. Draw a north line at the start, and another at every turning point, where the journey changes direction. ### 5. worked example: two legs at right angles
> Here's a worked example. A yacht sails twenty kilometres from port P on a bearing of zero four zero degrees true, to a buoy B. Then it sails fifteen kilometres on a bearing of one three zero degrees true, to point C. Find the distance P C, and the bearing of C from P. At the buoy, draw a north line. The way back to P is zero four zero plus one eight zero, two two zero degrees. The way on to C is one three zero degrees. They're ninety degrees apart, so there's a right angle at **B**. Always check that before using Pythagoras. P C squared is twenty squared plus fifteen squared, six twenty-five, so P C is twenty-five kilometres. C is further round clockwise from P than the buoy is, so add that to forty degrees. That's zero seven seven degrees true, to the nearest **degree**. Giving thirty-seven degrees as the bearing is the slip. It's measured from the first leg, not from **north**.

### 6. one leg, split into south and east
> Sometimes you need how far south and how far east one leg takes you. A ship sails eighty-five kilometres on a bearing of one three zero degrees true.
