### 2. what pH means

### 19. key notes (quiet, hold about 60 seconds)
Heading on screen: "Key notes: what to write in your book".
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

1. pH = −log₁₀[H₃O⁺] and [H₃O⁺] = 10⁻ᵖᴴ. One pH unit is a factor of 10. Decimal places in pH = significant figures in [H₃O⁺].

## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why a strong acid's [H₃O⁺] equals its concentration: every molecule gives its proton to water (2). Why pH uses a log and a minus sign: concentrations span powers of ten, and the minus keeps values positive (2). Why decimal places carry the precision: the whole-number part only encodes the power of ten (3). Why a base has a pH: water's self-ionisation always leaves some hydronium, fixed by Kw (5, 7). Why added hydroxide lowers hydronium: the equilibrium shifts back (5). Why neutral pH changes with temperature: the self-ionisation absorbs heat, so warming shifts it right and raises Kw, while the two ions stay equal (6). Why pH + pOH = 14: the negative log of Kw (7). Why calcium hydroxide doubles [OH⁻]: two hydroxide ions per formula unit (8). Why dilution changes pH: same moles in more volume (9). Why it never passes 7: water's own hydronium (9). Why mixing needs subtraction and the total volume: H⁺ and OH⁻ react first, and the leftover spreads through all the liquid (10 to 12).
