### 2. same angle, same shape
> Here's the key idea. Copy the roof's triangle, keep the thirty-five degree angle, and make it smaller, or bigger. The sides change, but the shape doesn't. So if you divide the height by the sloping side, you always get the same number, about zero point five seven. A slope of two metres rises about one point one five metres, and a slope of ten metres rises about five point seven four. Both divide out to the same **ratio**. A ratio here just means one side divided by another. So once you know the angle, the ratios are fixed, whatever the size.

### 3. naming the sides
> Before we use those ratios, we name the sides. The longest side, across from the right angle, is the hypotenuse. You met it with Pythagoras. The other two names depend on which angle we're using. The side across from our angle is the opposite side. The side that touches our angle, and isn't the hypotenuse, is the adjacent side. Adjacent just means next to. From the thirty-five degree angle at the gutter, the height is opposite, and the flat run underneath is **adjacent**. Now stand at the top corner instead. The hypotenuse stays put, but opposite and adjacent swap **over**. So always name the sides from the angle you're using.

### 4. sine, cosine and tangent
> Each pair of sides gives a ratio with its own name. The sine of an angle is the opposite over the hypotenuse. The cosine is the adjacent over the hypotenuse. And the tangent is the opposite over the adjacent. On screen they're shortened to sin, cos and tan, and the angle is often called theta, a Greek letter. To remember them, there's SOH CAH TOA. S O H, sine is opposite over hypotenuse. C A H, cosine is adjacent over hypotenuse. T O A, tangent is opposite over **adjacent**. On a calculator, sine thirty-five degrees is zero point five seven three six, the number our copies kept giving. Your calculator must be in degree mode, so check it once. Sine ninety should give exactly one. In the wrong mode, sine thirty-five gives a negative number, and a ratio of two lengths can't be **negative**.

### 5. the roof's height and run
> Now the roof. We know the angle, thirty-five degrees, and the hypotenuse, five point five metres. We want the height, which is opposite. Opposite and hypotenuse, that's S O H, so sine. Sine thirty-five degrees equals the height over five point five. The unknown is on top, so multiply both sides by five point five. The height is five point five times sine thirty-five degrees, which is three point one five metres, to two decimal **places**. Keep the calculator's full value until the end, because rounding sine to zero point five seven first gives three point one four, which is a little off. The flat run is adjacent, so that's cosine. Five point five times cosine thirty-five degrees is four point five one **metres**.

### 6. worked example: the unknown underneath
> Here's a worked example where the unknown is underneath. In a right-angled triangle, one angle is forty-eight degrees, the side adjacent to it is nine metres, and the hypotenuse is unknown. Adjacent and hypotenuse, so cosine. Cosine forty-eight degrees equals nine over H. This time the unknown is the bottom of the fraction. So multiply both sides by H, then divide both sides by cosine forty-eight degrees. H is nine divided by cosine forty-eight degrees, which is thirteen point four five **metres**. Multiplying, nine times cosine forty-eight degrees, is the slip. That gives six point zero two metres, shorter than the nine metre side, but the hypotenuse is always the longest **side**.
