  1. **L17 Compare model:** "none creates selection for surviving a chemical, so none produces insecticide resistance". True for insecticides, but the passage then treats gene drives as free of resistance problems. Proposed: add "Gene drives can select for resistance to the drive itself, so designs target genes where such variants are rare or harmful."
  2. **L21 hook and COVID-19 card:** "Sweden ... schools open, restaurants open" (also logged in g01 as error 6; log once). Sweden kept schools for younger students open but moved upper secondary schools and universities to distance teaching from March to June 2020. Proposed: "schools for younger students open".

Correct in substance, but the same page elsewhere says New Zealand had "a swift 7-week lockdown at first case" (already logged as an error); the Evaluate model and the hook should share one corrected timeline. ### 2b. q1-link-proof (which link, what proof)
> Evaluate means judge against criteria. Start with the **link**. Fewer mosquitoes means fewer bites that carry the parasite. And because nearly all offspring inherit the gene, it **spreads by itself**, so a small release could reach the whole species. Now the **proof**. It's strong for cages: every population died out. But a cage isn't an island. Wild mosquitoes meet weather, predators and mates the cage didn't have, so the **wild result** is still unknown.

### 2c. q1-cost-verdict (who pays, then the verdict and its condition)

It's justified to a moderate extent as a **staged trial**, **provided** both islands' communities agree first, and the spread is monitored as it goes.

### 2d. q1-mistake (the classic mistake)
> The classic mistake is writing that it worked in cages, so it will wipe out malaria. A cage shows the gene can spread. It doesn't show what happens in the wild, or whether people agree. Always ask where the proof was tested.

## Practice questions and model answers

1. (Q1, 6 marks.) Island M gene drive (card text in scene 2a). Evaluate the proposal as a strategy to control malaria on Island M.
   Model: Link: the drive reduces the one species that spreads almost all malaria on the island, so fewer infective bites and fewer new cases (1 mark). Because nearly all offspring inherit the gene, a small release can spread through the whole species without repeated releases (1 mark). Proof: in large cages, every population died out within about a year (1 mark), but it has not been tested in the wild, where weather, predators and mating differ (1 mark). Who pays: the drive is hard to reverse and could spread to Island N, whose people have not been consulted (1 mark). Judgement: promising, but justified only to a moderate extent as a staged trial, provided both islands' communities consent first and the spread and its effects are monitored (1 mark). Credit, but do not require: other mosquito species could take over a small share of transmission; removing one species could affect animals that feed on it (research so far suggests few, if any, depend on it alone); resistance to the drive could evolve. Classic mistake crossed: "it worked in cages, so it will wipe out malaria".
2. (Q2, 8 marks.) Island Nation Q (case in scene 3a). Evaluate Island Nation Q's strategy, using biological and social evidence.
   Model: Link: border closure and 14-day hotel quarantine (longer than the usual incubation period, so most infected arrivals were detected before release) blocked imported cases, and isolation, tracing and quarantine of contacts broke local chains of transmission (2 marks). Proof: local spread stopped for most of 2020, and deaths were 5 per million against 900 in a neighbouring country (1 mark), but a comparison with another country cannot isolate the strategy's effect, because island geography, population and reporting differ (1 mark). Who pays: tourism workers lost income and citizens overseas waited months to return, so costs fell unevenly (1 mark). Condition: quarantine depends on finding cases before they spread; the 2021 variant spread before symptoms and outbreaks began from quarantine hotels, so elimination became harder to sustain (1 mark). Value: the strategy bought time until vaccines were widely available and about 90% of adults were vaccinated (1 mark). Judgement: highly effective in 2020 as a temporary strategy until vaccination, provided its costs were supported fairly, and much harder to sustain once a more contagious variant spread before symptoms (1 mark). Classic mistake crossed: "elimination worked, so Q should have kept it forever".
3. (Q3, 5 marks.) Plant W partnership (card text in scene 4a). Evaluate the partnership as a strategy to develop a new treatment for skin infections.
   Model: Protocols: consent was given before research began, and the community controls which knowledge is shared and how samples are used (1 mark); knowledge holders are credited as co-authors and benefits were agreed in advance, so Indigenous cultural and intellectual property is recognised and protected (1 mark). Evidence: the extract stops *S. aureus* growing, which is promising (1 mark), but it kills human skin cells at the same concentration, so it is not yet a safe treatment; an active compound would need to be identified and tested for a safe, effective dose (1 mark). Judgement: a strong partnership that has not yet produced a treatment; worth continuing provided any new use returns to the community for consent and a compound proves safe and effective (1 mark). Classic mistake crossed: "the extract killed bacteria, so it's ready to treat skin sores".
