### 2. one formula, three forms
> Speed tells you how far you go in each hour, or each second. Speed equals distance divided by time. Rearrange it, the way we did two videos ago, and you get two more forms. Distance equals speed times time, and time equals distance divided by speed. On a real trip the speed goes up and down, so this gives the average speed, the steady speed that would cover the same distance in the same time. And the units come from the formula. Kilometres divided by hours gives kilometres per **hour**.

### 3. minutes into hours
> So, three hours and ten minutes. Ten minutes is ten sixtieths of an hour, a sixth, about zero point one six seven. On the calculator, type three plus ten divided by sixty. With three point one zero, you'd get about ninety-one point nine, and that's wrong, because point one zero of an hour is only six minutes. In the same way, fifteen minutes is a quarter of an hour, so it's zero point two five, not zero point one **five**.

### 4. how far? distance = speed × time

Twenty-four times forty-five would say more than a thousand kilometres, in under an hour, so a quick size check catches **it**.

### 6. kilometres per hour into metres per second
> Road signs use kilometres per hour, but over a few seconds, metres per second is more useful. One kilometre per hour means a thousand metres in an hour, and an hour is three thousand six hundred seconds. So each second, you go a thousand over three thousand six hundred metres, which is one over three point six. That's why, to change kilometres per hour into metres per second, you divide by three point **six**. Here's a check. A kayak paddling at three point six kilometres an hour moves exactly one metre every **second**. A car at sixty kilometres an hour covers sixty over three point six, almost seventeen metres every second. To go back, multiply by three point **six**.
