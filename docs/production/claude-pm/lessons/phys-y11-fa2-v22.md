### 5. total internal reflection
> Go past it, to fifty degrees, and no light escapes at all. It all reflects back inside, fifty degrees in and fifty degrees out, by the law of reflection. That's total internal reflection. And no angle has a sine bigger than **one**.

### 6. finding the critical angle
> So we can find the critical angle from the law of refraction. Start in the slower medium: n one sin theta one equals n two sin theta two. At the critical angle, theta one is theta c, and theta two is ninety degrees. Sin ninety is one, so n one sin theta c equals n two. Divide both sides by n one: sin theta c equals n two over n **one**. Here, n one is the medium the light is in, and n two is the one it's trying to enter.

### 7. worked example: glass into air

### 9. only from slow to fast
> No. Going into glass, light slows down, so it bends towards the normal. The ray inside is always closer to the normal than the ray outside, so it can never reach ninety degrees. The light must be heading into a medium with a lower refractive index, where it speeds up. And its angle of incidence must be bigger than the critical **angle**.

### 18. key notes (quiet, hold about 60 seconds)

> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

1. Light leaving a slow medium (higher n) for a faster one bends away from the normal; the outside angle grows faster than the inside one, and more light reflects as it does.
2. Critical angle θc: the angle of incidence at which the refracted ray travels along the boundary (θ₂ = 90°). From n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂: sin θc = n₂/n₁.
