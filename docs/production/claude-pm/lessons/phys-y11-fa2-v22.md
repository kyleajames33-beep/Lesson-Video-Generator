### 6. finding the critical angle

## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why the light bends away leaving glass: it speeds up (1, v19). Why the half-disc: the light enters along a radius, a normal, so it doesn't bend (2). Why there is a critical angle: the outside angle grows faster and reaches 90° first (3, 4). Why nothing escapes past it: escaping would need a sine above 1 (5). Why the formula has 90°: the critical angle is defined by the outside ray lying along the surface (6). Why air into glass is never trapped: the ray bends towards the normal, so the inside angle never reaches 90° (9). Why a fibre's large critical angle still traps light: rays along it meet the wall at grazing angles (10). Why diamonds sparkle: a small critical angle traps most rays inside (11).
- **Misconceptions addressed:** TIR at any steep boundary (8, 9, the checklist; site misconception 1); at θc all the light already reflects (4; site misconception 2); skimming carries on past θc (hook chip crossed, 5); the indices either way up (6, 12, feedback 1 crossed); the critical angle belongs to the block alone (feedback 2 crossed); a large critical angle makes TIR easy (10; site correction 1).

## Practice questions and model answers

**Attempt 1 (scene 13; original wording, HSC style, 3 marks suggested, not an official marking scheme).** "A small lamp sits on the bottom of a swimming pool. The refractive index of water is 1.33 and of air is 1.00. (a) Calculate the critical angle for light travelling from the water to the air. (b) A ray from the lamp strikes the water surface at an angle of incidence of 55°. State what happens to this ray, and explain why."
Model answer: (a) sin θc = n₂/n₁ = 1.00 ÷ 1.33 = 0.752, so θc = 48.8°. (b) The ray is totally internally reflected back into the water: the light is travelling into a medium of lower refractive index (water to air), and its angle of incidence (55°) is greater than the critical angle (48.8°), so no refracted ray is possible. Classic mistake (crossed): "sin θc = 1.33 ÷ 1.00 = 1.33" (upside down; a sine cannot exceed 1).

**Attempt 2 (scene 15; original wording, HSC style, 4 marks suggested).** "A ray of light inside a clear plastic block just skims along the plastic-air surface when it strikes that surface at an angle of incidence of 42.0°. (a) Calculate the refractive index of the plastic. (b) The block is placed in water (n = 1.33). Determine whether a ray striking the surface from inside the block at 50° is now totally internally reflected."
Model answer: (a) The refracted ray skims the surface, so 42.0° is the critical angle. With air outside, sin θc = 1.00 ÷ n, so n = 1 ÷ sin 42.0° = 1.49. (b) In water, sin θc = 1.33 ÷ 1.49 = 0.893, so θc ≈ 63°. The ray's angle of incidence (50°) is less than the new critical angle, so it is not totally internally reflected: it refracts out into the water (bending away from the normal). Classic mistake (crossed): "the critical angle is still 42°, so the 50° ray is trapped".
