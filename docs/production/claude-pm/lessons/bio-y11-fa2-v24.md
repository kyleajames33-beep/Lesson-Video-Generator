### 2. the tubule and its capillaries: two directions
> Remember the blood that left the glomerulus? It flows into a net of capillaries wrapped around the tubule. Moving useful substances from the filtrate back into the blood is called reabsorption. Moving extra substances from the blood into the filtrate is called secretion.

### 8. why filter, then reclaim
> So why throw almost everything out, then take most of it back? Because a filter that sorts by size catches every small waste, even a new drug the body has never met, without a special carrier for each one. The body only needs carriers for the things it wants to keep. And because it chooses how much to take back, it can adjust the water and salt in your blood. Filter everything small, take back what you want, add what you don't.

### 14. key notes (quiet, about 60 seconds)

> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

- Along the tubule, filtrate and blood (in a capillary net round the tubule) are separated by one layer of cells. Selective reabsorption: filtrate → blood (useful substances). Secretion: blood → filtrate (extra wastes and ions).
- Glucose and amino acids: all reabsorbed by carrier proteins, by active transport (ATP). Carriers have a limit, so very high blood glucose (uncontrolled diabetes) puts glucose in urine.

## Numbers or facts to verify

Self-check (2 Nov). The Codex science check must confirm each before voicing.
- **99 per cent (scene 1).** 1.5 L ÷ 180 L is about 0.83 per cent; over 99 per cent of the filtrate volume is reabsorbed. Correct.
- **One layer of cells (scene 2).** The tubule wall is a single layer of epithelial cells; peritubular capillaries lie alongside. Correct.
- **Glucose reabsorption (scenes 3, 4, 12b).** In the proximal tubule, SGLT2 and SGLT1 cotransport glucose with sodium into the cells (secondary active transport driven by the sodium-potassium pump's ATP use); glucose leaves the cells into the blood by facilitated diffusion (GLUT2). Learner copy says "carrier proteins ... use energy from ATP, so this is active transport" (FA1 root decision 14; secondary detail in this author note). All filtered glucose is normally reabsorbed; the transport maximum is exceeded at blood glucose of about 10 to 11 mmol/L (renal threshold), causing glycosuria. Correct. "move it towards the blood" is used because carriers move glucose into the cells first.
- **Amino acids (scene 3).** Reabsorbed by sodium-coupled carriers in the proximal tubule. Correct.
- **"diabetes mellitus" (scene 4).** "Diabetes" from Greek for siphon (passing through); "mellitus" from Latin for honeyed, sweet. "sweet siphon" (the site's gloss): acceptable.
- **Sodium and water (scene 5).** Sodium is actively transported out of the tubule (basolateral Na⁺/K⁺ pumps); water follows by osmosis (about two thirds in the proximal tubule). Correct. On a hot day with sweating, ADH raises water reabsorption (ADH not named, as the route requires); urine darker and smaller. Correct.
- **Urea (scene 6, 12a).** About 40 to 50 per cent of filtered urea is passively reabsorbed; urine urea commonly about 10 to 35 g/L (varies with diet and hydration) against plasma about 0.15 to 0.4 g/L; "about sixty times" and the table's 20 against 0.3 (about 67 ×) are plausible. Revision 2 learner copy: "Some urea is reabsorbed, but a much bigger share of the water goes back" (urea is substantially reabsorbed and recycled, including by transporters in the collecting duct; author note; Open decision 3 resolved).
- **Secretion (scene 7).** H⁺ secretion (acid-base balance), K⁺ secretion (distal tubule and collecting duct), organic anion transporters secrete penicillin and many drugs (penicillin's rapid renal secretion is the classic example). Correct.
- **Protein-bound drugs (12c).** Protein-bound drug fractions are poorly filtered; active secretion by proximal tubule transporters removes both unbound drug and, by shifting the binding equilibrium, bound drug. Correct at this level.
- **Collecting duct, ureter, bladder (scene 11).** Correct. Several nephrons drain into each collecting duct (not stated).

## Memory aid check
