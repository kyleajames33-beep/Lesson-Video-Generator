### 2. why: a weak acid stays mostly whole
> Propanoic acid is **weak**. At any moment only a small fraction of its molecules have given a proton to water, and the rest stay whole, so the hydronium is far less than the acid's concentration. Molecules and ions sit in **equilibrium**, and you've already written its constant, **K a**: hydronium times propanoate, over the whole acid, with water left out. So the real question is how much of the acid ionises. Call that amount **x**.

Visual: the board clears. `m6-beaker-v1.png` small at the left with the same label; a large particle lens opens from it: about 100 amber-outlined propanoic acid molecules among pale blue water. On "weak" one molecule hands a teal token to a water molecule (C01 hop) while, elsewhere, a propanoate takes one back from a hydronium (dashed reverse hop, C06); the swap keeps going, so about one ion pair is present at any moment; a small counter under the lens reads "ionised: about 1 in 100". ### 3. worked example 1: the task and the ICE table
> Here's the full method, with K a for propanoic acid one point three times ten to the minus five. An **ICE table** tracks the concentrations: initial, change, equilibrium. At the start there's zero point one five zero of the acid and, ignoring water's own tiny share, **no ions**. Each molecule that ionises makes **one hydronium and one propanoate**, so the acid goes down by x and each ion goes up by x. At equilibrium, that's zero point one five zero minus x, then x, and x. So K a equals **x squared over zero point one five zero minus x**.

Visual: the board clears to the three zones. Task line: "Find the pH". Equation strip: "CH₃CH₂COOH(aq) + H₂O(l) ⇌ H₃O⁺(aq) + CH₃CH₂COO⁻(aq)". Givens card: "propanoic acid: 0.150 mol/L" | "Ka = 1.3 × 10⁻⁵ (25 °C)". On "no ions" the I row fills "0.150", "0", "0", with a small grey tag on the zeros "ignore water's 10⁻⁷" that dims. On "one hydronium and one propanoate" the C01 hop plays once on the strip (one teal token from the acid to water) and the C row fills "−x", "+x", "+x", each cell lighting as the 1 : 1 : 1 coefficients on the strip pulse.
