## Decisions

- **Learner question kept (route).** "How does a family of primitives become one curve?" The answer, built in order: a gradient rule such as f′(x) = 2x is satisfied by many curves (hook); each is called a primitive, and finding one runs differentiation backwards (2); adding a constant slides a curve without tilting it, because the derivative of a constant is zero, so F(x) + C is a family of primitives (3); and every primitive is in that family, because the gap between two primitives has zero gradient everywhere and so is constant (4); primitives are found by reversing the power rule, sums and multiples (5, 6), written ∫f(x) dx = F(x) + C (6); one known point fixes C, which picks one curve (8).
- **One question, many short points (route root decision).** The eleven content points are not listed. Each enters as the next link in the chain above: "primitive" names what the hook found (2); "+ C" and "differ by a constant" explain the family (3, 4); the power, sum and multiple rules are the tool for finding one member (5, 6); the notation is the way the family is written (6); the initial condition is what chooses the curve (8). Key notes are four lines, one per link, not eleven.

Scene 5 builds it: differentiation multiplies by the power and lowers it by one, so the reverse raises the power and divides. ### 2. what a primitive is
> Here's the name for what we're finding. If a function, capital F, has derivative f, then capital F is called a **primitive** of f. So x squared is a primitive of two x. ### 4. why nothing else fits
> But could a curve that isn't a slid copy also have gradient two x? Take any two primitives of the same function, and look at the gap between them. ### 5. reversing the power rule
> So how do we find one primitive? Reverse the power rule. Differentiating multiplies by the power, then lowers it by one. To undo it, raise the power by one, then divide by the new power. Try x squared. Raising the power gives x cubed. But x cubed differentiates to three x squared, three times too **big**. So divide by three. x cubed over three differentiates to x **squared**. Watch its gradient. At every x, the gradient of x cubed over three equals the height of x **squared**. In general, x to the n plus one, over n plus one, differentiates to x to the n. The n plus one cancels, as long as it isn't **zero**. So for x **above** zero, it works for every real n except minus **one**.

### 6. sums, multiples and the integral sign
> Two more facts handle whole polynomials. The derivative of a sum is the sum of the derivatives. So if F is a primitive of f, and G is a primitive of g, then F plus G is a primitive of f plus **g**. And k times F is a primitive of k times **f**, for any constant k. Now the notation for the whole family. The integral of f of x, d x, equals F of x, plus **C**. The function inside is called the **integrand**, and d x says that x is the **variable**. This is called the indefinite integral, and finding it is called **integration**. So the integral of x to the n is x to the n plus one, over n plus one, plus C.

## Teaching checks

## Memory aid check

- **"The gradient rule fixes the shape. One point fixes the height."** Written for this lesson. Meaning, as said in scene 9: integrating gives the family F(x) + C, whose members all have the same shape (the same gradient at every x); one known point fixes C, which is the curve's height. Assumptions checked: "shape" means the gradient at every x, which is identical for all members of F(x) + C (author note a); "one point fixes the height" holds when the family is a single F(x) + C, which is true on an interval (author note b; all functions in this video are polynomials on all real x); the point must be on the required curve (it is a given).
