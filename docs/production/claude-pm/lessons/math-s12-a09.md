### 1. hook: falls, turns, rises

### 2. the middle line: vertex and axis
> Start with the table. The middle hanger is number two, with a height of four. Look either side of it. One step left is five, and one step right is five. Two steps left is eight, and two steps right is **eight**. The heights match in pairs around x equals two. The point two, four, where the cable turns, is called the vertex. Here it's a minimum, the lowest value. The vertical line through the vertex, x equals two, is the axis of **symmetry**. Fold the graph along that line, and the two halves land exactly on each **other**. Notice the axis is a line, x equals two, but the vertex is a point, two, four. If you want a second check, the gaps between heights are minus three, minus one, one and three, and the gaps between those are always two. Constant second differences confirm a **quadratic**.

### 3. four ways to show one quadratic
> The cable's heights come from an equation, y equals x squared, minus four x, plus eight. The highest power of x is x squared, and that's what makes it a quadratic. So one relationship can be shown four ways. An equation, a table of values, a set of ordered pairs, and a **graph**. You can move between them. Take hanger three, and use the equation. Three squared is nine, minus twelve, plus eight, which is **five**. That's the table's five, the ordered pair three, five, and a point on the graph. When you draw the graph, join the points with a smooth curve, not straight lines. A parabola has no sharp **corners**.

### 4. worked example: equation to table
> Here's one to work through. For y equals x squared, minus two x, minus three, complete a table for x from minus one to three. Then find the vertex and the axis of symmetry. Start with x equals minus one. Minus one, squared, is plus one. Then minus two times minus one is plus two. So it's one, plus two, minus three, which is **zero**. Carry on along the row, and the values are zero, minus three, minus four, minus three, and **zero**. They pair up around x equals one, so the axis of symmetry is x equals one, and the vertex is one, minus **four**, a minimum. Squaring minus one to get minus one is the classic slip. It gives minus two, and the symmetry **breaks**. A broken pattern is your warning to check.

### 5. the sign of a: valley or hill
> Why does that one open upwards? Look at the number in front of x squared, called a. Here's y equals a x squared, plus one. When a is positive, the parabola opens upwards, like a valley, and the vertex is a minimum. Slide a down towards zero, and the curve gets wider and **flatter**. At zero, there's no x squared at all, just a straight line, so it isn't a quadratic. Keep going, so a is negative, and the parabola opens **downwards**, like a hill. Now the vertex is a maximum, the highest value. Here's why. Far from the middle, x squared gets huge, so a times x squared takes over. If a is positive, both ends shoot up. If a is negative, both ends head down. Only the sign of a decides which way it opens. A big number on the end, like the plus eight on the cable, moves the curve up, but it doesn't flip **it**.

### 9. feedback 1 (brief)
