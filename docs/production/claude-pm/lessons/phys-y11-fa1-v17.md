### 3. the third law
> This is Newton's third law. If object A exerts a force on object B, then B exerts a force on A that's equal in size and opposite in direction. These two forces are called an interaction pair, or an action and reaction. Three things are always true about a pair. The forces are the same type, here both contact pushes. They're equal in size and opposite in direction. And they act on two different **objects**, one on A, and one on **B**.

### 4. why the pair does not cancel on either object
> Back to our puzzle. A force can only cancel another in an object's net force if both act on that object. So draw a boundary around just one object, the way you do for a free-body diagram. Each trolley's weight and the track's upward push balance, so look along the track. Inside the boundary around B, there's only one force of the pair, A's push to the **right**. Nothing balances it, so B accelerates to the right. Inside the boundary around A, there's only B's push, to the **left**, so A accelerates to the left. A pair's two forces never appear on the same free-body diagram, so neither cancels the other on either **object**. That's why things move.

### 5. balanced is not a pair
> Here's a common trap. A block rests on a bench. Gravity pulls it down with its **weight**. The bench pushes it up with a normal force, the same size. They balance because the block isn't accelerating. The partner of the weight is the block pulling the Earth **up**. The partner of the normal force is the block pushing down on the **bench**.

### 9. propulsion
> So how does anything get itself moving? It pushes something else backwards, and the partner force pushes it forwards. A rocket in deep space has nothing to push against. Its engine pushes exhaust gas backwards out of the nozzle, and the gas pushes the rocket **forwards**. That forward push is called thrust, and it doesn't need any air. A car does the same thing through its tyres. The driven tyres push backwards on the road, and the road pushes forwards on the **tyres**. That forward push from the road is a friction force, and it's what accelerates the car.

## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why there must be a partner force: trolley A moves backwards and B supplies the only unbalanced horizontal contact force on it (2). Why pairs do not cancel on either object: a free-body diagram holds only the forces on one object, and the pair's forces are on two objects (4); as one system the pair is internal (screen note).
