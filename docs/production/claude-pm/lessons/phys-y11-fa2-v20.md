- **Prediction 2 (3 s, scene 10).** "In shallow water, the waves slow down. What happens to their frequency?" Chips "it drops?", "it stays the same?". Answered in 11.
- **Why the frequency stays (the cause; `cibc9c30ac` applied).** "The bar makes ten waves every second. Each crest that reaches the boundary sends one across. Crests can't pile up at the boundary or vanish, so ten cross every second, on both sides." (Revision 2 wording; a little energy reflects, as scene 6 shows.) Then v = fλ (v05): same f, lower v, so a shorter λ, by the same factor. Illustrative tank values: f = 10 Hz; deep λ 3.0 cm, v 0.30 m s⁻¹; shallow λ 2.0 cm, v 0.20 m s⁻¹ (λ measured across five wavelengths, six crests, with a ruler in the tank, author note f). At an angle the crests turn towards the normal (v19's pivot, seen in water).
- **The conclusion (`ci8856aeb0` applied).** "When a wave crosses into a new medium, its speed changes, so its wavelength changes by the same factor, and unless it arrives along the normal, its direction changes. Its frequency doesn't, because the source sets it." For light: one line, "Light behaves the same way: its colour, set by its frequency, stays the same in glass, while its wavelength shortens", with a grey tag "too small to see here".

### 1. hook (test the prediction)
> Last time, we predicted that light slows down in glass and bends towards the normal. Today we test it in the lab, tracing a beam through a glass block, then watching water waves cross into shallow water. We'll find out what changes as a wave crosses a boundary, and what **doesn't**.

Visual: opens on v19's last frame (the beam through the block, the traced outline, the four dots, the ripple tank inset, the chips). The "Next" chip becomes the heading: "The refraction investigation"; the memory line fades. On "predicted" a grey tag "prediction (v19)" by the bent ray. On "tracing a beam" the block's outline and the dots pulse; on "water waves" the ripple tank inset grows slightly; on "doesn't" a four-row checklist at the right: "speed?", "direction?", "wavelength?", "frequency?" (all grey).

### 2. aim, media and variables
> Our aim is to demonstrate refraction between two media. For light, that's air and glass. For water waves, it's deep and shallow water, because a change of depth changes their speed, so it acts like a new medium. With the light, we change the angle of incidence and measure the angle of refraction in the glass. The same block and face keep the glass the same, the same ray box and slit keep the beam the same, and a darkened room with white paper makes the beam easy to **see**.

Visual: board change: the paper and block top view (left), the ripple tank plate small (right) with a coded pale rectangle in the tray (tag "clear plastic sheet: shallow water"). Gloss "demonstrate: show that it happens, and how". On "air and glass" the paper scene highlights; on "deep and shallow" the tank highlights. A variables panel builds at the right (v17 style): "change: angle of incidence", "measure: angle of refraction", then each control with its reason as spoken: "same block, same face → same glass", "same ray box and slit → same beam", "dark room, white paper → beam visible".

### 3. the method
> Trace round the block, and draw the normal where the beam will enter. Turn the ray box so the beam hits that point at the angle you want. Put two dots on the beam coming out, far apart. Lift the block off, join the dots back to where the beam left the block, and join that point to where it went in. That's the path inside the glass. Measure the angle of refraction from the normal. A narrow beam and far-apart dots give a sharp **line**.

Visual: camera continue on the paper (trace sequence). On "Trace round" a dashed outline draws round the block plate; on "draw the normal" the protractor plate (`y11p-protractor-v1.png`, read as `phys-y11-protractor-v1.png`) sets a dashed normal at the entry point. On "Turn the ray box" the ray box plate turns by code until the arc reads "40°". On "two dots" two pencil dots place themselves on the emerging beam, far apart. ## Practice questions and model answers

**Attempt 1 (scene 15; original wording, HSC style, 4 marks suggested, not an official marking scheme).** "In an investigation, a ray of light passes from air into a perspex block. (c) θr < θi, so the ray bends towards the normal on entering the perspex: light travels more slowly in perspex than in air. Classic mistake (crossed): "the angle of refraction is proportional to the angle of incidence".

**Attempt 2 (scene 17; original wording, HSC style, 4 marks suggested).** "In a ripple tank, a straight bar vibrating at 12 Hz produces waves that travel at 0.24 m s⁻¹ in deep water. In the shallow region, the wavelength is 1.5 cm. (a) Calculate the wavelength in the deep water. (b) Calculate the speed of the waves in the shallow water. (c) Explain why the frequency of the waves is the same in both regions."
Model answer: (a) λ = v/f = 0.24 ÷ 12 = 0.020 m = 2.0 cm. (b) v = fλ = 12 × 0.015 = 0.18 m s⁻¹. (c) The frequency is set by the source (the bar, 12 Hz).
