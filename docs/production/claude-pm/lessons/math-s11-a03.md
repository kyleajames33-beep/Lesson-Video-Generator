### 1. hook: how much does a little cube hold?
> Water comes in two kinds of unit. A box's volume is in cubic centimetres or cubic metres. But the water you drink, or pay for, is in millilitres, litres and kilolitres. Volume is how much space something takes up. Capacity is how much a container holds. They measure the same space, in different units. You pay for water by the kilolitre, but a pool builder measures the pool in metres, so you need a bridge between the two. So how many little one centimetre cubes of water would fill this **jug**? And how do cubic centimetres and cubic metres become millilitres, litres and **kilolitres**?

### 2. a cube of a litre
> Here's the key fact. A millilitre is defined as the space inside a one centimetre cube. So one cubic centimetre is exactly one **millilitre**. Now build a bigger cube, ten centimetres on each side. A row along the bottom holds ten of the little cubes, a layer holds a hundred, and ten layers hold a **thousand**. That's a thousand cubic centimetres, which is a thousand millilitres, and that's one **litre**. So a box ten centimetres on each side holds exactly a litre of water, about what's in a carton of **milk**. You've seen this bridge before, maybe without noticing. A scooter can have a hundred and twenty-five cc engine. The cc means cubic centimetres, so that's a hundred and twenty-five **millilitres**.

### 3. the fish tank

If the height had been given as nought point four metres, you'd change it to forty centimetres first. Its volume is sixty times thirty times forty, seventy-two thousand cubic **centimetres**. Every cubic centimetre is a millilitre, so it holds seventy-two thousand millilitres. A litre is a thousand millilitres, so divide by a thousand. The tank holds seventy-two **litres**. Dividing by a hundred instead, and saying seven hundred and twenty litres, is the **slip**. That would need ten tanks' worth of **water**.

### 4. a cubic metre is a kilolitre
> Now go up one size. A cube one metre on each side. Each edge is ten of our litre cubes long. So it's ten by ten by ten litre cubes, a thousand **litres**. One cubic metre holds a thousand litres, and a thousand litres is called a **kilolitre**. That's a box you could sit in, and it holds as much as a thousand cartons of milk. So every cubic metre is a **kilolitre**. That makes big containers easy. A cubic metre count is a kilolitre count, with the same **number**. Remember the five kilolitre rainwater tank? It holds five cubic metres, the same as a box two and a half metres long, two metres wide and one metre **high**.

### 6. megalitres
