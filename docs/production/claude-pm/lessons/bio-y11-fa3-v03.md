- **Site errors found (this read).** Root to add to `../site-corrections-bio-y11.md`.
  1. L03 hook: "Fishers who only keep big fish should be left with a sea full of small ones, and that is exactly what has happened to several cod populations." Overstated and mixes two effects: fishing removes large fish directly (fewer big fish, an immediate effect), while the evolutionary evidence is a shift toward maturing at younger ages and smaller sizes (Olsen and colleagues 2004, northern cod). ## Numbers or facts to verify

Self-check (3 Nov). The Codex science check must confirm each before voicing.
- **Peppered moth (scenes 3 to 6).** Biston betularia rests by day on trunks and, more often, on the undersides of branches (Majerus; not spoken: "rests on tree bark" is the standard simplification and is kept). Birds are visual predators (Majerus's 2001 to 2007 Cambridge study, published by Cook and colleagues 2012). The dark form carbonaria was first recorded near Manchester in 1848; by 1895 it was about 98 per cent of the Manchester population (standard textbook figure, Kettlewell). The mutation is a transposable-element insertion in the cortex gene, dated to about 1819 (van't Hof and colleagues 2016; author note only). Lichens died from sulfur dioxide pollution and soot darkened bark. The UK Clean Air Act was passed in 1956; carbonaria frequency then fell across Britain to low levels (for example below 10 per cent near Liverpool by the late 1990s and near zero in many sites since; Cook 2003, Cook and colleagues 2012). Spoken: "Today, in most of Britain, it's uncommon"; the grey tag "below 1 in 10 in most places surveyed" is removed if the check cannot confirm it.
