  1. L03 hook: "Fishers who only keep big fish should be left with a sea full of small ones, and that is exactly what has happened to several cod populations." ## Numbers or facts to verify

Self-check (3 Nov). The Codex science check must confirm each before voicing.
- **Peppered moth (scenes 3 to 6).** Biston betularia rests by day on trunks and, more often, on the undersides of branches (Majerus; not spoken: "rests on tree bark" is the standard simplification and is kept). Birds are visual predators (Majerus's 2001 to 2007 Cambridge study, published by Cook and colleagues 2012). The dark form carbonaria was first recorded near Manchester in 1848; by 1895 it was about 98 per cent of the Manchester population (standard textbook figure, Kettlewell). The mutation is a transposable-element insertion in the cortex gene, dated to about 1819 (van't Hof and colleagues 2016; author note only). Lichens died from sulfur dioxide pollution and soot darkened bark. The UK Clean Air Act was passed in 1956; carbonaria frequency then fell across Britain to low levels (for example below 10 per cent near Liverpool by the late 1990s and near zero in many sites since; Cook 2003, Cook and colleagues 2012). Spoken: "Today, in most of Britain, it's uncommon"; the grey tag "below 1 in 10 in most places surveyed" is removed if the check cannot confirm it. "Coal smoke killed the lichen" is a simplification of sulfur dioxide from coal burning; accepted for Year 11, science check to judge.
- **Cod (scenes 7 to 9).** Atlantic cod (Gadus morhua) vary in age and size at maturation; female fecundity rises steeply with body size (standard fisheries biology). The northern cod off Newfoundland and Labrador collapsed and a moratorium began in 1992. Olsen and colleagues (2004, Nature 428: 932 to 935) found probabilistic maturation reaction norms shifting toward earlier ages and smaller sizes in the cohorts before the collapse, which allows for changes in growth; interpretation as partly genetic is widely accepted but debated (author note: the narration says "part of this change is inherited", not all, after naming the growth effect). Ages on screen ("about age 4", "about age 7") are labelled illustrative; northern cod historically matured at about 6 to 7 years, falling to about 5. Science check to confirm or replace the illustrative ages. Supporting experiment (not spoken): Conover and Munch 2002 (Science 297: 94 to 96), silversides under size-selective harvest for four generations evolved smaller size.
- **Artificial selection (scene 10).** Kale (selected for leaves), broccoli and cauliflower (selected for enlarged flower clusters, inflorescences) are cultivars of Brassica oleracea derived from wild cabbage. "Flower buds" is the plain wording for the inflorescence. Correct.
- **Fast change (scene 11).** Human pressures often strong, consistent and geographically widespread: site's point ("can be extremely strong, the population can shift in just a few generations"). Correct as a general statement ("can" and "often" in narration).
- **Chemicals (scene 11).** Herbicides and antibiotics kill susceptible individuals; resistant variants already present survive and reproduce. Correct; site's card. ("It kills the others": within a sprayed or treated population; not every susceptible individual is reached. Accepted simplification.)
- **Cane toads (12a).** Released in Queensland in 1935 (site; correct). Phillips and Shine 2004 (PNAS 101: 17150 to 17155): in toad-exposed populations, two snake species (red-bellied black snake and green tree snake) show reduced relative head size (gape) and increased body size; large-gaped snakes can ingest toads large enough to deliver a lethal dose.
