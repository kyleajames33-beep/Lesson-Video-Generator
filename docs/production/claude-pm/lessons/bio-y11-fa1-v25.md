### 15. writing the comparison
> To compare, take each feature and mention both cells in one sentence. A typical bacterium has one circular chromosome, while a eukaryote has several linear ones. ## Numbers or facts to verify

Self-check (2 Nov). The Codex science check must confirm each before voicing.
- **About 2 m of DNA per human body cell (1, 12, notes).** A diploid human cell has about 6.2 billion base pairs; at about 0.34 nm per base pair that is about 2.1 m. Correct ("about two metres"; site's figure).
- **Nucleus about 6 µm across (1, notes).** Typical mammalian nuclei are about 5 to 10 µm. The site uses six; "about". Correct.
- **Tennis ball (2, 3).** A tennis ball is about 6.7 cm across; 6.7 cm ÷ 6 µm ≈ 11,000; 2 m × 11,000 ≈ 22 km. "More than ten kilometres" holds for any nucleus up to about 13 µm. DNA width 2 nm × 11,000 ≈ 22 µm, "about a fiftieth of a millimetre" (screen tag). Correct.
- **Bacterial loop about 1.5 mm, several hundred times the cell (6, 12).** *E. coli* has about 4.6 million base pairs × 0.34 nm ≈ 1.6 mm; cell length about 2 µm, so about 800 times. "Several hundred" is safe. Correct.
- **"About a thousand times more DNA" (12, notes).** 6.2 billion ÷ 4.6 million ≈ 1,350. "About a thousand" is safe. Correct.
- **Twisting and proteins (6).** Bacterial chromosomes are negatively supercoiled by DNA gyrase (an enzyme) and organised by nucleoid-associated proteins (such as HU and H-NS). Bacteria generally lack histones (archaea have histone-like proteins; not mentioned). Correct.
- **Plasmids (7).** Small circular DNA, replicated independently, often carry resistance genes, transferred between bacteria (conjugation). "Small" relative to the main chromosome is true of most plasmids. Correct.
- **Charges (10).** The phosphate backbone of DNA is negatively charged; histones are rich in the positively charged amino acids lysine and arginine. Correct.
- **Compaction (11).** Nucleosomes compact DNA about sevenfold; further coiling and looping give interphase compaction of about a thousandfold and mitotic chromosomes about ten thousandfold. "Thousands of times shorter" is safe. Condensed chromosomes are visible by light microscopy in mitosis. Correct.
- **Organelle DNA (13).** Mitochondrial DNA is a small circular molecule in animals (plant mitochondrial genomes are larger and more complex but are usually drawn as circles at this level); chloroplast DNA is usually described as circular. Correct at Year 11; the science check judges "much like a bacterium's".
- **Attempt 2.** A nucleus with chromosomes wound on histone cores identifies a eukaryote (some bacteria have histone proteins and some prokaryotes have linear chromosomes, so the nucleus carries the weight); small circular DNA is consistent with mitochondrial or chloroplast DNA (yeast plasmids are a rare alternative, which the model's "most likely" allows). Correct.
