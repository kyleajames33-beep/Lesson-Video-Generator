### 2. resilience is a process
> **Resilience** is the process of adapting well in the face of adversity, and recovering afterwards. Three words in that definition do the work. Process, not trait: it happens over time, and it can be helped or blocked, so saying someone is resilient describes an outcome and explains nothing. Adversity: resilience only means something against a real threat. And adapting well isn't the same as being unaffected. Someone who's visibly struggling and still functioning is showing resilience. Someone who shows nothing might be coping, or hiding it.

### 3. what produces it, and why the advice fails
> So what produces it? The most common factor in young people who do well is at least one stable, supportive relationship, the same trusted adult from earlier. Mark who supplies each, and most come from other people. That's why two students can move towns in the same year, and one is doing well six months later while the other isn't. So be more resilient is worse than useless. It turns hardship into personal failure, and it lets everyone off doing anything about the hardship itself.

### 4. coping, by timescale
> Next, **coping strategies**, what you do to manage a stressor or the feelings it causes. They split into adaptive and maladaptive, and the split is about time, not morality. Take a big assignment due. Putting it off and doing anything else gives real relief, fast, but the deadline keeps growing. Breaking it into steps feels harder now, and shrinks the problem. So maladaptive strategies persist because they work, at least today. Telling someone their coping is unhealthy changes nothing. It has to be replaced by something that also brings relief, or the stressor has to shrink. And context matters. Avoiding a threat you truly can't change is sensible, and exercise is adaptive until it becomes compulsive.

### 5. a sense of purpose
> A **sense of purpose** means having goals and commitments that make the future worth investing in. It sounds soft, but its mechanism is direct. Sleep, training, study and saving all cost now and pay later, so they only make sense if later matters. Purpose also brings commitments, a team, a job, a role at home, and commitments bring routine. And it gives you a reason to recover. Purpose doesn't have to be a career. It can be a person, a craft or a community. But if it's too narrow, an identity built on one thing can collapse when that thing is lost, like an athlete who's injured or dropped.

### 6. ethical behaviour
> **Ethical behaviour** means acting with honesty, fairness and respect for others. It sounds like a lecture, but it works through health mechanisms. It protects other people directly, because consent and respect in relationships are health matters. It decides whether help is there later, because trusted relationships only form around people who are trustworthy. It protects you, since acting against your own values, and hiding it, is a lasting stress. And it shapes a group's norms. When a group chat starts sharing a photo of a classmate, one person saying that's not okay, and leaving, changes what's acceptable. That's health promotion at the scale a sixteen year old actually controls.

### 7. connectedness
> Last, **connectedness**: feeling accepted, valued and that you belong, in a family, a team, a school or a community. Of all nine skills, it has one of the strongest links with young people's health, especially mental health. It buffers stress, so the same trouble does less damage when you're not alone. It puts someone close enough to notice a change. It gives the reason to seek help and keep going. And it spreads a group's habits, good or bad. But it's the least like a skill, because nobody can do it alone. It's produced by conditions: affordable local sport, schools where difference isn't punished, and transport to where people are. For Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander young people, connection to community, culture and Country protects wellbeing, and communities sustain it.

### 9d. feedback, item two
