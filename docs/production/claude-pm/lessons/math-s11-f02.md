### 1. hook: the $41 bill
> The bill says forty-one dollars. How far did the parcel go? Last time, we knew the distance and found the cost. Now the cost is known, and the distance is missing. So put forty-one in for C. Eight plus one point five zero k equals forty-one. That's an equation, a statement that two amounts are equal, with one unknown to find. So how do we turn a word problem into an equation, solve it, and check the answer makes **sense**?

### 2. solving on a balance
> Solving means finding the value of the unknown that makes the equation true. Think of the equation as a balance, with the same value on both sides. Whatever you do to one side, do to the other, and it stays level. Now ask what's been done to k. It was multiplied by one fifty, then eight was added. The adding was done last, so undo it first. Take eight from both sides, and one point five zero k equals **thirty-three**. Then undo the multiplying. Divide both sides by one point five, and k equals twenty-**two**. Check by substituting. Eight plus one fifty times twenty-two is eight plus thirty-three, forty-one. So the parcel went twenty-two **kilometres**.

### 3. worked example: four steps
> Most problems come as words, so here are four steps. Adult tickets cost eighteen dollars each, and a booking fee of six dollars is added. The total is seventy-eight dollars. How many tickets? Step one, name the unknown, with units. Let t be the number of tickets. Step two, write the equation from the words. The fixed fee, plus the price times the number, equals the total, so six plus eighteen t equals seventy-**eight**. Step three, solve. Take six from both sides, eighteen t equals seventy-two, then divide by eighteen, and t equals **four**. Step four, check it in the words, and answer in a sentence. Four tickets at eighteen dollars is seventy-two, plus six is seventy-eight, so four tickets were **bought**. Swapping the fee and the price is the slip. Six t plus eighteen gives ten tickets, but ten tickets would cost a hundred and eighty-six **dollars**.

### 4. a falling balance
> Some amounts go down. You have a hundred and twenty-five dollars, and spend nine dollars each week. How many weeks until fifty-three dollars is left? Let w be the number of weeks. What's left is the start, minus nine for each week, so a hundred and twenty-five minus nine w equals fifty-**three**. The money spent is the gap between what you started with and what's left. So nine w equals a hundred and twenty-five minus fifty-three, which is seventy-two, and w equals **eight**. After eight weeks, fifty-three dollars is left.

## Teaching checks
