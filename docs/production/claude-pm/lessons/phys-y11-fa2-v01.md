### 3. why the energy moves along

### 5. transverse: oscillating across the energy's path
> Now a rope, fixed to the same driver, turned to move up and down. The energy travels along the rope, to the right. But a ribbon tied to the rope moves straight up and down, across that **direction**. When particles oscillate perpendicular, at right angles, to the energy's direction, it's a transverse wave. Why does the shape travel? The rope is stretched, so it's under tension, a pull along its length. When one piece of rope lifts, the tension drags the next piece up after it, and then pulls the first piece back **down**. Once again, each piece passes its motion on and stays near home.

### 8. classifying a wave step by step

### 9. why sound in air is longitudinal
> Why does it matter? To carry a longitudinal wave, the particles only have to push back when they're **squeezed**. Solids, liquids and gases all do that. A transverse wave needs more. When one layer slides sideways, it has to drag its neighbours back, like the rope does through its tension. Solids can do that, but layers of air or water just slide past each other **freely**. So sound in air is longitudinal. Earthquakes send both kinds of wave through the Earth, but the transverse ones can't get through its liquid outer **core**.
