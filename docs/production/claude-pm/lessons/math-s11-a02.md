### 1. hook: is a square metre a hundred square centimetres?
> Last time ended with a puzzle. A metre is a hundred centimetres, so is a square metre a hundred square centimetres? Most people say yes. It seems to follow. But look at this floor tile. It's thirty centimetres on each side, and it covers less than a tenth of the square metre. If a square metre were only a hundred square centimetres, this tile would be nine times too **big**. Something doesn't add up. So why do area and volume conversions work differently from **length**?

### 2. square units: the factor twice
> Start small. A square centimetre is the area of a square one centimetre on each side. Now measure that square in millimetres. Each side is ten millimetres, so the square holds ten rows of ten little squares. That's a hundred square **millimetres**. The length factor, ten, gets used twice, once for each side. Ten times ten is a **hundred**. Area is length times length, so when you change the unit, both lengths change. That's why area factors are squared. Going back up, from square millimetres to square centimetres, you divide by a **hundred**.

Visual: stage change (simulation 1): a teal square (700 px) labelled "1 cm" on two sides (blue); grey chip "square centimetre: a square 1 cm each side". On "in millimetres" each side gains ten millimetre ticks and the labels re-read "10 mm"; on "ten rows of ten" grid lines draw across, a counter "0 → 100" fills cell by cell along a row, then the row copies down; hand tag "10 rows of 10". On "millimetres" row "1 cm² = 100 mm²" (52 px, framed green). On "used twice" an amber arc on each side "× 10" and "× 10"; on "hundred" row "10 × 10 = 100" (amber), hand tag "factor twice". On "hundred" (last) a small ladder at the right, bigger unit at the top as in a01: "cm²" above "mm²", "× 100" pointing down and "÷ 100" pointing up.

### 3. the square metre: a hundred times a hundred
> Back to the square metre. Each side is a hundred centimetres, so a row along the bottom holds a hundred centimetre squares, and there are a hundred rows. A hundred times a hundred is ten thousand. So one square metre is ten thousand square **centimetres**, not a hundred. Now say a floor is five square metres. In square centimetres that's five times ten thousand, fifty thousand square **centimetres**. Multiplying by a hundred gives five hundred, and that's the classic **slip**. It only converts one side of the square. Going back up is a divide. A small tile of four hundred square centimetres is four hundred divided by ten thousand, nought point nought four of a square **metre**.

### 4. hectares and square kilometres
> Land is measured in bigger squares. A hectare is the area of a square a hundred metres on each side. A hundred times a hundred, so one hectare is ten thousand square **metres**. How big is that? A netball court is about four hundred and sixty-five square metres, so a hectare has the area of about twenty-one and a half **courts**. A square kilometre is a square a thousand metres on each side. A thousand times a thousand is a million square metres, which is a hundred **hectares**. So a paddock of three point six hectares is three point six times ten thousand, thirty-six thousand square **metres**, or nought point nought three six of a square kilometre.

### 5. how many tiles?
> Here's where this matters. A bathroom floor is two point four metres by one point eight metres, and the tiles are thirty centimetres square. How many tiles, ignoring the gaps? The floor's area is two point four times one point eight, four point three two square metres. In square centimetres, times ten thousand, that's forty-three thousand two hundred. Each tile is thirty times thirty, nine hundred square centimetres. Forty-three thousand two hundred divided by nine hundred is forty-eight **tiles**. Here's a check that skips the area units completely. Convert the lengths first. Two hundred and forty centimetres holds eight tiles across, a hundred and eighty holds six down, and eight times six is forty-eight **again**. Writing four point three two square metres as four hundred and thirty-two square centimetres gives under half a tile, which can't be **right**.

### 6. cubic units: the factor three times
> Volume works the same way, but a cube has three directions. A cubic centimetre is the space inside a cube one centimetre on each side. In millimetres, it's ten by ten by ten, a thousand cubic **millimetres**. This toy block shows the idea. It's three small cubes along each edge, so its face has nine squares, but the whole block has twenty-seven **cubes**. Watch the slider. Double the length, and the area is four times bigger, but the volume is eight times **bigger**. So one cubic metre is a hundred times a hundred times a hundred, a million cubic **centimetres**. And a cubic kilometre is a thousand cubed, a billion cubic **metres**. To go up, divide. A cereal box of forty-eight hundred cubic centimetres is forty-eight hundred divided by a million, nought point nought nought four eight of a cubic **metre**.
