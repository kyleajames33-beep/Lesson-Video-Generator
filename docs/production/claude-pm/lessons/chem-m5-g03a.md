## Decisions

- **One main learner question:** *a calculation gives you some concentrations or amounts. How do you know whether to find K, build an ICE table, or work out Q?* The decision rule is "what you are given and what you are asked decide the method" (scenes 1, 7).
- **Types in this video (numbered as in C6a, C6b and the five-card board):** (1) settled amounts, find K; (2) a start plus one settled value, ICE table, find K; (4) a mixture and a K, asked for a direction, Q against K. Types 3 and 5 are part 2 (G03b). The board shows all five cards with cards 1, 2 and 4 lit and cards 3 and 5 dimmed with the tag "part two".
- **Why this attempt (new):** the scene 2 concentrations (0.150, 0.0750, 0.300 mol/L) are put at the scene 3 temperature (K = 229). The same working now gives Q = 53.3, not K, because nothing says the mixture has settled; Q is below K, so it runs forward. It reuses numbers already in the video, needs no new arithmetic, and tests the one decision this video is about: K comes only from an equilibrium mixture; any other mixture gives Q. The carbon Q question (4a) stays as the worked pause-and-answer for type 4.
- **Cause chain taught, in order:** (1) the givens and the demand decide the method (1); (2) K is a ratio of concentrations, so amounts in a vessel are divided by its volume first, and coefficients are powers (2); (3) the measured value goes in the Equilibrium row, its change is equilibrium minus initial, and the coefficients set the other changes; only equilibrium values go into K (3); (4) Q has the form of K with the current concentrations; solids stay out; Q below K means too little product, so the forward reaction wins (4a, 4b); (5) the same numbers give K only when the mixture is at equilibrium; otherwise they give Q, which is compared with K (5, 6).
- **Entry knowledge:** C1 to C8 (including C6a and C6b) and Year 11 (mole ratios, c = n / V, powers). The learner knows K expressions with powers and without solids and water (C5); the three calculation types and ICE tables with the measured value in the Equilibrium row first (C6a); Q and the Q against K rule, "Q chases K" (C8).
- **Start and stop:** starts at "what is given and what is asked" for a calculation question; stops after the learner can find K from settled amounts, find K from a start plus one settled value with an ICE table, decide a direction from Q against K (with a solid present), and say why the same numbers are K only at equilibrium. No x, no shortcuts, no amount added (G03b).
- **Handoff:** chem-m5-g03b (part 2: find concentrations and the amount added), said in scene 7. G03b hands off to chem-m5-g03c (practice set), then C9.
- **Units policy:** K written without a unit (C5 and C6 policy, tag "K: no unit written" at each substitution). Every concentration carries mol/L, amounts carry mol.
- **K values for repeated equations:** 2SO2 + O2 ⇌ 2SO3 has K = 53.3 (scene 2) and 229 (scenes 3, 5, 6), each with the tag "a different temperature, so a different K"; C + CO2 ⇌ 2CO has K = 1.90 (4a). The attempt says "the same temperature as the ICE table question", so K = 229 applies.

### 2. direct-k (type 1: settled amounts, find K)
> Type one: settled amounts. A **two litre** vessel holds 0.300 moles of sulfur dioxide, 0.150 of oxygen and 0.600 of sulfur trioxide. K uses concentrations, so **divide by two first**: 0.150, 0.0750 and 0.300 moles per litre. Write the expression with the coefficients as **powers**, not multipliers, and K is 0.300 squared, over the product of 0.150 squared and 0.0750, which is **53.3**. Use moles and you get 26.7, which is wrong.

Visual: as G03 scene 2 (unchanged): task "Find K", equation strip 2SO2(g) + O2(g) ⇌ 2SO3(g), givens card "vessel: 2.00 L", "SO2: 0.300 mol", "O2: 0.150 mol", "SO3: 0.600 mol", all at equilibrium. On "divide by two first" each amount divides into a "mol/L" column: 0.150, 0.0750, 0.300. On "powers" the C5 builder writes K = [SO3]^2 / ([SO2]^2 [O2]) with a crossed ghost "2 x [SO2]" captioned "power, not multiplier". The working writes in beats: "0.300^2 = 0.0900", "0.150^2 = 0.0225, 0.0225 x 0.0750 = 0.0016875", "K = 0.0900 / 0.0016875 = 53.3", tag "K: no unit written". On "26.7" a crossed side chip "moles used: 0.600^2 / (0.300^2 x 0.150) = 26.7", caption "K needs mol/L". Silent card: CaCO3(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO2(g), "K = [CO2]", "solids and pure liquids stay out of K". Memory aid "Concentrations, not moles." The mol/L column (0.150, 0.0750, 0.300) is kept as a small thumbnail labelled "vessel 1" for scene 6. Hold about 2 seconds.

### 3. ice-one-value (type 2: a start plus one settled value)
> Type two: a start and **one** settled value. Put 0.400 moles of sulfur dioxide and 0.300 of oxygen in **another** two litre vessel, at a **different temperature**, and at equilibrium sulfur trioxide is **0.160 moles per litre**. The start is 0.200 and 0.150 moles per litre. The measured 0.160 goes in the **equilibrium row** first, so sulfur trioxide's change is **plus 0.160**. Then the coefficients: sulfur dioxide **falls** 0.160 and oxygen falls **half** of that. The equilibrium row is 0.0400 and 0.0700, and **only** that row goes into K, giving **229**.

Visual: as G03 scene 3 (unchanged): the C6 ICE table with SO2, O2, SO3 columns (mol/L); the tag "a different temperature from the last question, so a different K"; Initial 0.200, 0.150, 0; the C6a pen arrow puts 0.160 into the SO3 Equilibrium cell and the "E - I" bracket gives "+0.160"; SO2 "-0.160" (chip "2 : 2"), O2 "-0.0800" (chip "1 : 2", pen "÷ 2"); Equilibrium 0.0400, 0.0700; "0.160^2 = 0.0256", "0.0400^2 x 0.0700 = 0.000112", "K = 0.0256 / 0.000112 = 229" with "equilibrium values only". Three unspoken crossed chips: "0.160 used as the start of SO3", "initial values in K", "O2: -0.160 (not halved)". Aid chip "Measured value: Equilibrium row first". The result "K = 229 (this temperature)" lifts into a small teal card labelled "ICE table question" that stays in a corner for scene 5. Hold about 2 seconds.

### 4b. q-vs-k-answer
> **Leave the solid out.** Q is 0.800 squared over 0.500, which is **1.28**, below K. That is too little product, so the **forward** reaction wins and carbon monoxide builds up. Q chases K: below it, Q climbs. If carbon monoxide were **1.50 moles per litre**, Q would be 4.50, above K, and it would run **back**.

Visual: as G03 scene 6b (unchanged): on "Leave the solid out" the carbon greys; Q = [CO]^2 / [CO2], "0.800^2 = 0.640", "0.640 / 0.500 = 1.28"; the Q marker lands left of K, "<" writes, the forward arrow lights blue and CO dots rise. "Q chases K" writes with a pen arrow from Q to K. On "1.50" a second marker lands at 4.50 right of K, the sign turns to ">", the reverse arrow lights amber, then fades. Unspoken crossed chips "left: rule flipped" and "carbon in Q". Hold about 2 seconds.

### 6. feedback (same numbers: K only at equilibrium)

You only calculate K from a mixture **at equilibrium**.

### 8. key-notes (reuse take: key-notes; quiet, hold about 45 seconds)
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

- What you are given and what you are asked decide the method. Settled amounts: find K. A start plus one settled value: ICE table, then K. A mixture and a K, asked which way: work out Q and compare.
- K and Q use concentrations, never moles: divide each amount by the vessel volume. Coefficients are powers, not multipliers. Pure solids, pure liquids and water as the solvent stay out of K and Q; dissolved species stay in.
- ICE table: the measured value goes in the Equilibrium row first; its change is equilibrium minus initial; only the change row follows the coefficients. Put only equilibrium values into K.
- Q has the same form as K, using the concentrations right now. Q below K: too little product, so the forward reaction wins. Q above K: the reverse reaction wins. Only a mixture at equilibrium gives K.

Memory aids (large, on the notes screen): *What you are given and what you are asked decide the method.* Second line: *Concentrations, not moles. Q chases K.* Condition tag: "same temperature, equation as written".

## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why divide by the volume (2); why the measured value goes in the Equilibrium row and why one known change fixes the others (3); why the solid leaves Q and why Q below K runs forward (4b); why the same ratio is K in one vessel and Q in another: only an equilibrium mixture gives K (6).
- **Misconceptions and slips addressed:** moles in K (2); coefficient as multiplier (2); measured value used as the change, initial values in K, whole change on a 1 : 2 species (3 chips); solid in Q, inequality flipped (4b); any computed ratio called K (6).
- **Response opportunities:** two, each 10 s with the countdown and nothing answer-bearing: 4a (direction with a solid) and 5 (K or Q, then direction). The attempt is new: it uses numbers the learner has seen, so it tests the decision, not the arithmetic, and its answer (forward) differs from a given value.
- **Reading load:** one stage per scene; the givens card holds every value of the current question; earlier boards clear, except the small "vessel 1" thumbnail and the teal K card, which the attempt needs.
- **Avoided double teaching:** ICE tables and Q are C6a and C8 callbacks; G03a applies them to new cases and names the slips.
- **Out of scope by design:** x, shortcuts and the amount added (G03b), marks allocation, the quadratic formula, temperature and K (C9).
