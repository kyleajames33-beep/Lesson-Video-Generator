### 2. the time ladder: steps of sixty
> Start with a ladder, just like the metric ones. Hours at the top, then minutes, then seconds. Each step is sixty. Going down to a smaller unit, you need more of them, so you multiply by sixty. Going up to a bigger unit, you need fewer, so you divide by sixty. The rule is the same as before. Only the number has changed. So one hour is sixty minutes, and each of those minutes is sixty seconds. One hour is sixty times sixty seconds, which is three thousand six hundred **seconds**. That's worth knowing by heart. A two hour movie is two times three thousand six hundred, seven thousand two hundred seconds. A seven minute ad break is seven times sixty, four hundred and twenty **seconds**. The slip is the metric habit. Times a hundred would make an hour a hundred **minutes**.

### 3. going up: leftovers are the next unit down
> Going up the ladder is where time gets tricky, because the division often doesn't come out even. Back to the song. Sixty goes into two hundred three times. That's a hundred and eighty seconds, with twenty seconds left over. The leftover didn't make a full minute, so it stays as seconds. The song is three minutes and twenty **seconds**. Same idea one rung higher. A playlist runs for a hundred and forty-five minutes. Sixty goes in twice, that's a hundred and twenty minutes, with twenty-five left. So it's two hours and twenty-five **minutes**. On a calculator, do it in two steps. Two hundred divided by sixty is three point three three. Keep the three whole minutes. Then multiply the decimal part, nought point three three recurring, by sixty, and the twenty seconds come **back**. Leftovers are the next unit down, never the digits after the point.

### 4. decimal hours: when pay needs one number

Going back, two point four hours is two hours and nought point four of an hour. Nought point four times sixty is twenty-four minutes, so it's two hours twenty-four **minutes**. Here's why it matters. ### 5. the 24-hour clock
> Now the other half of time, the clock itself. A twelve-hour clock goes round twice a day, so nine fifteen could be morning or night, and we add am or pm, before noon or after noon, to tell them apart. A twenty-four-hour clock counts the hours straight through the day, from midnight, nought nought nought nought, to one minute before the next midnight, twenty-three fifty-nine. The midnight that ends a day is sometimes written twenty-four hundred. Train timetables, flight boards and many phones use it, because there's no am or pm to get wrong. Morning times keep their digits, written with four digits, so nine fifteen am is nought nine fifteen. Afternoon and evening times add twelve to the hour, because twelve hours have already passed since midnight. Seven forty-five pm is nineteen forty-**five**. To go back, take twelve off any hour from thirteen up, and write pm. Twenty-two fifty-eight is ten fifty-eight **pm**.
