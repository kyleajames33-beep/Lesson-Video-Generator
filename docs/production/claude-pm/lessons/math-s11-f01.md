### 2. one rule, many cases
> Look at what we did. For any trip, the cost is eight, plus one fifty times the number of kilometres. For five kilometres, that's eight plus seven fifty, fifteen dollars fifty. For thirty kilometres, eight plus forty-five, fifty-three **dollars**. Same steps every time, only the distance changes. So write the rule once, with letters. Let C be the cost in dollars, and k the number of kilometres. Then C equals eight plus one point five zero k. That's a formula, a rule written with letters that works for every **case**. One point five zero k means one fifty times k. A number and a letter side by side are multiplied, so three x means three times x, not three plus **x**.

### 3. worked example: substitute, then work in order
> Here's the method, with the twelve kilometre trip. The letter on its own, C here, is called the subject. It's the quantity the formula finds. Step one, write the formula, and say what each letter means, with its units. Step two, substitute. That means swap each letter for its value, and put the value in brackets. So C equals eight plus one point five zero, times bracket twelve. Step three, work it out in the right order. Multiply before you add, so one point five zero times twelve is eighteen first, then eight plus eighteen is **twenty-six**. Step four, answer in a sentence, with units. The delivery costs twenty-six **dollars**. Working left to right is the slip. Eight plus one fifty is nine fifty, and times twelve gives a hundred and fourteen dollars. That's far too much for twelve kilometres, so a quick size check catches **it**.

### 4. two letters, and checking a claim

### 5. a negative value goes in brackets
> Now a trap. Evaluate x squared plus three x, when x is minus four. Put minus four in brackets, everywhere x appears. Bracket minus four, squared, means minus four times minus four, which is sixteen, because a negative times a negative is **positive**. Three times bracket minus four is minus twelve. So sixteen, minus twelve, is **four**. Leave the brackets off, and minus four squared means minus, four squared, which is minus sixteen. The square only grabs the four, and your calculator reads it that way too. That's why every value goes in **brackets**.

### 6. a power on the calculator
> Some formulas have powers, like the area of a circle, A equals pi r squared. A family pizza is thirty centimetres across, so its radius, half the width, is fifteen centimetres. On the calculator, press pi, times fifteen, then the x squared key. That's about seven hundred and seven square **centimetres**. Here's the surprise. A twenty centimetre pizza, radius ten, has only about three hundred and fourteen. So the big pizza is only half as wide again, but it has more than twice the **pizza**. For other powers, use the power key, the one with a little hat, or x to the y. One point zero five cubed means three lots of one point zero five, multiplied **together**.
