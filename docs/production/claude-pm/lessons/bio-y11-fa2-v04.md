### 12. key notes (quiet, about 60 seconds)
Heading on screen: "Key notes: what to write in your book".
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

- Levels of organisation, smallest to largest: organelle → cell → tissue → organ → system → organism. Each level is built from the level below it.
- Organelle: a specialised structure inside a cell with its own job. Tissue: cells (usually similar) and the material around them, working together on one shared job. Organ: several different tissue types working together. System: several organs working together on one broad life process.
- Example: mitochondrion → heart muscle cell → cardiac muscle tissue → heart (cardiac muscle, nervous, connective and epithelial tissue) → circulatory system (heart, blood vessels, blood) → organism.
- Each level can do something the level below cannot (cell contracts; tissue contracts as one; heart pushes blood one way; system reaches every tissue): division of labour, so the parts depend on each other.
- Plants: chloroplast → palisade cell → palisade tissue → leaf → shoot system → whole plant.

Memory: count the tissue types, not the cells. A tissue is a team of cells; an organ is a team of tissues.

## Numbers or facts to verify

Self-check (2 Nov). The Codex science check must confirm each before voicing.
- **Skin is the largest organ (scene 2).** Standard: the skin is the largest organ by surface area (about 1.5 to 2 m² in adults) and mass (several kilograms). Some recent sources argue for the interstitium or count the skeleton as a system; "your largest organ" is the textbook statement. Correct at this level.
- **Skin tissues (scene 3).** Epidermis: stratified squamous (keratinised) epithelium. Dermis: dense irregular connective tissue (collagen and elastic fibres); hypodermis: adipose (a connective tissue) and loose connective tissue. Arrector pili: smooth muscle attached to each hair follicle, which raises the hair. Nervous tissue: free nerve endings and sensory receptors for touch, temperature and pain. Blood vessels also present. "Four different tissue types": epithelial, connective, muscle, nervous. Correct. Note: the plate is a standard textbook block; the arrector pili is visible beside the large hair, the touch receptor is the yellow layered oval at the left.
- **Waterproof barrier (scene 2).** The keratinised epidermis with its lipid layer limits water loss and entry. "Waterproof" is the usual simplification (water-resistant). Acceptable; flag for the science check.
- **Gut epithelium "several metres" (scene 4).** The small intestine of a living adult is about 3 to 5 m long (about 6 to 7 m at autopsy), lined throughout by a simple columnar epithelium (with absorptive and goblet cells; one tissue). Correct.
- **Eyelid skin "less than a millimetre" (scene 4).** Eyelid skin is the thinnest in the body, about 0.5 mm including the dermis (epidermis plus dermis), and still contains epidermis, dermis, small muscle fibres, nerves and vessels. Correct.
- **Cardiac muscle and mitochondria (scene 5).** Cardiac muscle cells are branched, striated and joined end to end at intercalated discs, forming a branching network; mitochondria make up about 25 to 35 per cent of their volume ("packed with them"; no number spoken). Mitochondria make most of a cell's ATP (v16: glucose is first broken down in the cytoplasm, and the mitochondria use the smaller pieces); "it makes most of the cell's ATP" avoids saying glucose enters. Correct.
- **Heart tissues (scenes 5, 7).** Cardiac muscle (myocardium); epithelial tissue (endothelium lining the chambers and covering the valves; epicardium's mesothelium); connective tissue (the fibrous skeleton, valve cusps, pericardium); nervous tissue (autonomic nerve fibres that adjust the rate). Heart valves are mainly dense connective tissue covered by endothelium. The beat starts in the sinoatrial node (modified cardiac muscle); nerves speed up (sympathetic) or slow down (vagus) the rate. Correct. Learner copy avoids "nerves set the rhythm" (site error 2).
- **Circulatory system (scene 6).** Heart, blood vessels and blood. Correct (the site's grouping; blood is a tissue within the system, author note).
- **Tissues and organelles (scenes 2, 4, 11; Revision 2).** Tissues can contain distinct cell types and extracellular material (science check item 4), so the definition is "cells, usually similar, plus the material around them". Many structures and molecules inside cells are not organelles (item 1), so the placing rule asks for "a specialised structure inside a cell".
- **Emergent abilities (scene 7).** A single isolated cardiac muscle cell contracts; coupled cells contract in a coordinated wave; chambers and valves give one-way flow; vessels distribute blood to tissues; blood carries oxygen and nutrients to tissues (Revision 2: not "every cell needs oxygen and glucose", since mature red cells have no mitochondria; science check item 2). "one strong squeeze" is a simplification of coordinated contraction. Correct.
- **Leaf (scene 9).** Chloroplasts capture light energy; palisade mesophyll cells are column-shaped and hold most of the leaf's chloroplasts; the palisade layer is the main site of photosynthesis; the leaf has upper and lower epidermis, palisade and spongy mesophyll and vascular bundles (xylem and phloem). Shoot system: stems, leaves, flowers (and fruits); root system: roots. "Palisade" is from the French and Latin for a fence of stakes (pale, palus). Correct.

Digestive system includes stomach, small intestine, liver. Correct.
