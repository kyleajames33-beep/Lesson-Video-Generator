### 2. one method for every problem
> Every force problem yields to the same four steps. One, draw a free-body diagram of one object, showing every force on it. Two, choose a positive direction. Three, add up the forces along each direction. Four, ask the key question: is the object accelerating? If it isn't, it's at rest or moving at a constant velocity, and the net force is **zero**. That's called equilibrium. If it is accelerating, the net force equals m **a**. So the deciding question isn't "is it moving?" It's "is it accelerating?"

### 3. at rest and at constant velocity
> Back to the crane. When the load hangs still, it isn't accelerating, so the forces balance. Take up as positive. The tension up minus the weight down is zero, so the tension equals the weight, m g. That's two hundred times nine point eight, nineteen hundred and sixty **newtons**. And if the crane lifted the load at a steady speed, the answer would be exactly the same. Constant velocity is still zero acceleration, so nothing extra is needed to keep it **going**.

### 4. speeding up
> Now the crane speeds the load up, at zero point five oh metres per second squared, upwards. Now there must be a net force upwards, so the tension has to beat the weight. Tension minus weight equals m a. So the tension is the weight plus m a, nineteen sixty plus two hundred times zero point five oh, which is two thousand and sixty **newtons**. The cable pulls harder than the weight only while the load accelerates **upward**.

### 5. two directions, one crate
> Here's a problem with both cases at once. A twenty-five kilogram crate is pulled across level ground by a horizontal rope, with a tension of ninety newtons. The coefficient of kinetic friction is zero point two oh. Up and down, the crate isn't accelerating, so F N balances the weight. Twenty-five times nine point eight, two hundred and forty-five **newtons**. Friction is zero point two oh times that, forty-nine newtons, **backwards**. Along the ground, the crate is accelerating. The net force is ninety minus forty-nine, forty-one newtons, so a is forty-one over twenty-five, one point six metres per second **squared**. Equilibrium in one direction, F equals m a in the other.
