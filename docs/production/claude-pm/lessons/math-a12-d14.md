### 2. two ingredients
> A line needs two things: one point it passes through, and its gradient. You met the formula in Year 11. y minus y one equals m, times x minus x one. ### 4. why the normal's gradient flips
> The normal goes through the same point, at right angles to the tangent. You know its gradient from Year 11: perpendicular gradients multiply to minus one. Here's why, in one picture. And that needs m to be non-zero, because one over zero doesn't **exist**.

## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why a gradient is not enough: many parallel lines share it (1). Why the point comes from f: the point is on the curve, and f gives its height; f′ gives a gradient, not a height (2, 10). Why a tangent can meet the curve again: it is defined by the point and the gradient there, not by touching once (2). Why the normal's gradient is −1/m: a quarter turn swaps the run and rise and reverses one of them (4). Why m ≠ 0 is needed: a flat triangle turns into an upright segment, and an upright line has no gradient (4, 6). ## Practice questions and model answers

**Attempt 1 (scene 9; original wording, HSC style, 3 marks suggested, not an official marking scheme).** "Find the equations of the tangent and the normal to the curve y = e^(2x) at the point where x = ½. Give your answers in exact form."
Model answer: y = e^(2 × ½) = e, so the point is (½, e). dy/dx = 2e^(2x); at x = ½ the gradient is 2e. Tangent: y − e = 2e(x − ½), that is y = 2ex. Normal gradient −1/(2e); normal: y − e = −(1/(2e))(x − ½). Classic mistake (crossed): "point (½, 2e)" (the gradient used as the height).

**Attempt 2 (scene 11; original wording, HSC style, 3 marks suggested).** "Find the equations of the tangent and the normal to the curve y = sin x + cos x at the point where x = π/4. Give your answers in exact form."
Model answer: y = sin(π/4) + cos(π/4) = √2/2 + √2/2 = √2, so the point is (π/4, √2). dy/dx = cos x − sin x; at π/4 it is 0. The tangent is horizontal: y = √2. The normal is vertical: x = π/4. Classic mistake (crossed): "normal x = √2".

## Memory aid check
