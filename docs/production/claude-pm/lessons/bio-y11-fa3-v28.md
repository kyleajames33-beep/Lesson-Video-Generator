### 2. the idea, and a prediction
> Ecologists use a clever trick. Catch some animals, give each a harmless mark, and let them go. Wait for them to mix back in, then catch a second batch and count how many carry a mark. Say you marked 40 beetles, and a week later you catch 50, and 10 of them are marked. Prediction: what fraction of all the beetles carries a mark?

### 3. from proportion to formula
> About one fifth, because 10 out of 50 is one fifth. If the marked beetles mixed evenly, your second catch is a fair sample, so the 40 you marked are about a fifth of the whole population. That makes about 200 beetles. As a formula, the population, N, equals M times C, divided by R. M is the number marked first, C is the whole second catch, and R is the marked ones in it.

### 9. lost marks push N up
> Too high. A beetle that loses its mark gets counted as unmarked, so R falls. R is on the bottom of the formula, so a smaller R makes N bigger. You can test this in the model: swap a few red beads for white ones before the second scoop. R drops from 12 to 10, and the estimate jumps from 250 to 300. Births, deaths and movement also bias it, high or low, depending on who joins or leaves.

### 10. fair catching, and timing
> Catching has to be fair too. If marked beetles learn that a trap holds food and come back, R rises and the estimate comes out too low. If the first catch scares them off traps, it's too high. And timing matters. Too soon, and the marked beetles are still bunched near where you let them go. Too late, and births, deaths and movement change the population. Wait long enough to mix, but not so long that the population changes.

### 15. key notes (quiet, about 60 seconds)
Heading on screen: "Key notes: what to write in your book".
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

- Mark-release-recapture (for mobile animals): catch and mark M; release; allow time to mix; catch C; count marked R.
- Logic: R ÷ C = M ÷ N, so N = M × C ÷ R (Lincoln index). C is the whole second catch. Check N ≥ M and N ≥ C. Never add the catches.
- Bead model: scoop M, swap for red, return, shake, scoop C, count R. 40 × 50 ÷ 8 = about 250. Repeat and average (250, 288, 223: mean about 254); the spread shows chance.
- Assumptions: marks stay on and are harmless; marked animals mix in; nothing joins or leaves (closed); every animal is equally easy to catch. R is on the bottom, so losing marks inflates the answer. Lost marks or trap-shy animals → too high; trap-happy → too low; births, deaths, movement → biased, either way. Interval: long enough to mix, short enough to stay closed.
- Repeats fix chance; only a better method fixes bias.
- Risks (bead model): nothing near the mouth; work over a tray, sweep spills; check legume allergies, use plastic beads.
