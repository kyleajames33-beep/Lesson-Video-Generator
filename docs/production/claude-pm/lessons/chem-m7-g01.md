### 2b. q1-names (which bonds: the main chain and its numbering)

Its longest chain has five carbons, with a methyl branch. The double bond wins the numbering, so count from the **right-hand** end. That puts the double bond at carbon two, and the methyl on carbon four: **four-methylpent-two-ene**.

### 2c. q1-isomers (atoms check, then what changed)
> Now count the atoms. Each is C six H **twelve**: six carbons, and one double bond, so two fewer hydrogens than the alkane. Same formula, different structures, so they're structural isomers. Both are **alkenes**, so it's not functional group isomerism. What changed is the carbon **skeleton**: A is a straight chain, and B is branched. So they're **chain isomers**.

### 2d. q1-mistake (the classic mistake)
> The classic mistake is numbering B from the left, the way you read it. That gives two-methylpent-three-ene. The branch gets the low number, but the double bond is named in the ending, so it gets first claim on the low numbers.

### 7. key-notes (reuse take: key-notes; quiet, about 60 seconds)
Heading on screen: "Key notes: what to write in your book".
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

1. Naming: trace the longest chain containing the multiple bond; number from the end nearer the multiple bond (it beats branches). Each carbon in a C=C has one fewer H.
2. Isomers (same molecular formula): skeleton changed → chain; same skeleton, group moved → position; different family → functional group.
3. Alkane + X₂ (UV light) → haloalkane + HX: substitution, two products. Alkene or alkyne + H₂, X₂ or HX: addition, one product, one piece on each carbon; an alkyne adds twice with excess reagent.
4. Working back: count the added atoms (2X₂ means two extra bonds); if all of them sit on the same two carbons, it was a triple bond there. Bromine water shows unsaturation; for an open-chain hydrocarbon with one multiple bond, the formula decides: alkene CnH2n, alkyne CnH2n−2.

1. (Q1.) The skeletal formulas of two hydrocarbons, A (hex-2-ene, drawn) and B (4-methylpent-2-ene, drawn with its branch on the left), are shown. (a) Give the IUPAC name of each compound. (b) Identify the type of structural isomerism shown by A and B. Justify your answer.
   Model: (a) A: longest chain 6 C with one C=C from C2, so hex-2-ene. B: longest chain 5 C containing the C=C; numbered from the end nearer the C=C (the right), the double bond is at C2 and the methyl at C4, so 4-methylpent-2-ene. (b) Both are C₆H₁₂ and both are alkenes, so they are structural isomers of the same family; the carbon skeleton differs (straight six-carbon chain against a branched five-carbon chain), so they are chain isomers. Crossed mistake: "B: 2-methylpent-3-ene".
2. (Q2.) (a) Pent-2-ene reacts with hydrogen chloride at room temperature. (b) Pentane reacts with chlorine gas in the presence of UV light. For each, identify the type of reaction and write a balanced equation, using structural formulae, for the formation of one organic product.
   Model: (a) Addition: CH₃CH=CHCH₂CH₃ + HCl → CH₃CHClCH₂CH₂CH₃ (2-chloropentane; 3-chloropentane, CH₃CH₂CHClCH₂CH₃, also accepted). (b) Substitution: CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ + Cl₂ → CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₂Cl + HCl, UV light (1-chloropentane; 2- or 3-chloropentane with HCl also accepted). Crossed mistake: "CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ + Cl₂ → CH₃CHClCHClCH₂CH₃".
3. (Q3.) Hydrocarbon X reacts with excess chlorine at room temperature in the dark. The only organic product is 1,1,2,2-tetrachlorobutane, CHCl₂CCl₂CH₂CH₃. Identify X. Explain your reasoning, including the type of reaction involved.
   Model: No UV light, so the reaction is addition, not substitution. Four Cl atoms means two Cl₂ molecules added, so X has two extra bonds. All four Cl are on C1 and C2, so both additions were across the same two carbons: a triple bond between C1 and C2. X is but-1-yne, HC≡CCH₂CH₃ (C₄H₆). HC≡CCH₂CH₃ + 2Cl₂ → CHCl₂CCl₂CH₂CH₃. Crossed mistake: "X = but-1-ene" (would give 1,2-dichlorobutane).
4. (Q4.) X is an open-chain hydrocarbon with one multiple bond. Its molecular formula is C₅H₈. X decolourises bromine water at room temperature, without UV light. A student concludes: "X is an alkene, because it decolourises bromine water." Evaluate the student's conclusion.
   Model: The conclusion is incorrect. - **Originality.** All four items were written for this video. They are standard HSC types (name and classify; predict products; work backwards; evaluate a test), so the reviewer and Codex should check that no recent NESA paper uses the same compounds and wording.
- **Pronunciation.** "hex-two-ene", "four-methylpent-two-ene", "pent-two-ene", "two-chloropentane", "one, one, two, two-tetrachlorobutane" (commas give short pauses), "but-one-yne" ("bute-one-ine"), "pent-one-yne", "C n H two n minus two" as letters and numbers, "H Cl" and "Cl two" as letters, "UV" as letters, "IUPAC" as "eye-you-pac".
- **Drawn geometry (science M4).** Hex-2-ene and 4-methylpent-2-ene each have E and Z forms; a zigzag skeletal drawing shows one of them. Draw both as the E (trans) zigzag. HSC names do not require E/Z; the answer box names without a stereodescriptor.
- **Doubts.** (a) Q3's "only organic product" excludes mixtures; chlorine addition to alkynes can give some side products in practice, so the stem states it as a given. Chlorine and alkynes are normally reacted in a solvent or with an iron(III) chloride catalyst; gas-phase mixtures can react violently in light. The stem states the result as a given ("reacts with excess chlorine ... in the dark"), which is acceptable for an HSC-style item; do not show apparatus (science M2). Q3's proof is position, not count: buta-1,3-diene (C₄H₆) also adds two Cl₂, but gives 1,2,3,4-tetrachlorobutane, so only a C≡C between C1 and C2 puts all four Cl on C1 and C2 (Revision 2). (b) Q4's stem gives "open-chain" and "one multiple bond" so that the general formula is decisive; without them C₅H₈ could be a diene or a cycloalkene (c03 author note f, c04b correction 3). (c) Q2(b)'s "one organic product" follows c04b's teaching that substitution gives a mixture; a student who lists several products with HCl each is also right.
