### 3. set: three kinds of goal
> Set comes first, and the kind of goal matters more than its size. An **outcome goal** is a result, like winning the district final. It depends on opponents and luck, so you can do everything right and still miss it. A **performance goal** is a personal standard, like five kilometres in under twenty-five minutes. A **process goal** is an action you fully control, like three runs this week. That's the one that changes behaviour. Only you can fail it, and it gives you a win every week, which feeds competence. Make it specific, measurable, reachable but challenging, and time-bound.

### 5. monitor, then evaluate: the same week, two futures
> Phase two is **self-monitoring**: recording what you actually did. Memory flatters us. People remember more sessions than their records show. And just recording a behaviour tends to change it, because a gap you can see is hard to ignore. Phase three is **self-evaluation**: comparing what you did with the plan, and deciding what it means. This is where most attempts die. Take one missed week. One person says, I missed three sessions because I've got no discipline. That **attribution**, the reason you give yourself, is personal and permanent, so next week looks hopeless, and she stops. Another says, I missed them because it was exam week. That's specific and temporary, so she starts again. Same week, opposite futures. And if a cause really is permanent, change the plan, not the person.

### 7. self-regulation inside performance
> Self-regulation isn't only about turning up. It's a performance skill too. A self-regulating athlete sets her own goal for each session, watches her own execution, judges it honestly, and adjusts.
