### 2. running the ratio backwards
> Think of sine as a machine. Put in an angle, thirty-five degrees, and out comes a ratio, zero point five seven three six. To find an angle, we run the machine backwards. Put in the ratio, and out comes the angle. The backwards machine is called inverse sine. Inverse means it undoes the sine. It's written sin with a small minus one, but that's just a label, it doesn't mean one over sine. It means the angle whose sine is this number. Cosine and tangent have inverses too, and inside a right-angled triangle, each ratio gives just one angle. On most calculators, press shift, then the sine, cosine or tangent **key**.

### 4. worked example: an angle from two sides
> Here's a worked example. A right-angled triangle has an opposite side of seven centimetres, and a hypotenuse of eleven centimetres. Find the angle, to one decimal place. Opposite and hypotenuse, so sine. Sine of the angle is seven over eleven. Keep that as a division, and don't round it. The angle is inverse sine of seven over eleven, which is thirty-nine point five **degrees**. Writing the angle as zero point six four is the slip. That's the ratio, not the **angle**. And if you round the ratio to zero point six four first, inverse sine gives thirty-nine point eight, which is slightly off.
