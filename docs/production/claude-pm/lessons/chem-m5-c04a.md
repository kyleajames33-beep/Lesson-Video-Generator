## Decisions

- **Site alignment:** site lesson 06 (`site-extracts/chem-m5-l06.md`, "Le Chatelier's Principle, Pressure, Volume & Catalysts") supplies ideas 1 to 4: compression favours fewer gas moles, expansion more, equal totals give no shift (idea 1); compression raises every gas concentration, giving unequal changes in the opposing rates (idea 2); an inert gas at constant volume leaves the reacting concentrations unchanged (idea 3); the NO2 and N2O4 syringe darkens at once and then fades to a shade paler than the immediate colour (idea 4). The site examples reused: N2O4/NO2 syringe (hook and worked example), N2 + 3H2 ⇌ 2NH3 (4 to 2), H2 + Cl2 ⇌ 2HCl style equal case (taught as H2 + I2 ⇌ 2HI so the colour is visible), and a solid case (the site's Fe3O4/Fe/H2/H2O case is replaced by C(s) + CO2(g) ⇌ 2CO(g), which has a gas count of 1 and 2 so that "count only gases" changes the answer instead of giving no shift). The site's constant-pressure inert gas is not taught (route: extension). Not used: Keq statements ("Keq unchanged"; K waits for c05 and c09), the combined 550 C and 300 atm Haber question (needs K and temperature trade-offs; C7), the catalyst ideas 6 and 7 (C4B), the simulator numbers.
- **Syringe system and direction of the equation:** the site writes 2NO2(g) ⇌ N2O4(g), delta H = -57 kJ per mole. C3's attempt wrote the same system the other way round (N2O4 ⇌ 2NO2, delta H = +57). This lesson writes it the site's way, so forward = joining of two NO2 and reverse = splitting of N2O4. The board shows the equation with the forward arrow blue and the reverse arrow amber, as in C2 and C3. Nothing spoken points back at C3 for this; the iron tube is the only named callback.
- **Entry knowledge:** C1, C2 and C3. A student who has watched C3 knows two-beat disturbances ("the moment of the change", "the response"), that the faster rate falls and the slower rate rises, that a disturbance is only partly cancelled, and that a brown NO2 tube changes colour at once when NO2 is the species touched. Gas pressure as particles hitting the walls is Year 11.
- **Cause chain taught, in order:** (1) a gas fills its container, so squeezing the same particles into half the space makes every gas twice as crowded (every gas concentration doubles); (2) that is the moment of the change: nothing has reacted yet, but the brown NO2 is more crowded, so the colour is already darker; (3) each reaction's speed depends on how often its reacting particles meet; a reaction that needs two particles to meet gains twice over when both are twice as crowded (x2 x2 = x4), a reaction that needs one particle only doubles (x2); (4) so the reaction that uses more gas particles gains more, the rates no longer match, and the faster reaction wins: the net reaction runs toward the side with fewer gas particles, using up some of the extra crowding; (5) as the faster rate falls and the slower rate rises, the rates meet again at a new equilibrium; the colour ends between the original and the instant-after-squeeze shade (partial cancellation); (6) the same reasoning gives the counting rule for any gas equation (count only gases; more on the reactant side means the forward reaction gains more); (7) equal gas counts mean both rates gain the same factor, so nothing shifts, though everything is more crowded; (8) an inert gas added in a fixed volume leaves the reacting gases exactly as crowded as before, so it favours neither direction and nothing shifts (no claim is made that the rates are unchanged: real N2O4 steps depend on added gas, so only the concentrations and the no-shift conclusion are stated; Claude science check U1), and total pressure by itself is not the cause.
- **Rate model (teaching model, same status as C3's iron model):** forward = 0.5 x [NO2]^2 and reverse = 8 x [N2O4] on the made-up scale. This is the correct rate law for the elementary step 2NO2 ⇌ N2O4, but in learner copy it is "joining needs two N O two to meet, splitting needs one N two O four". For other equations, learner copy says only that the reaction using more gas particles gains more; no rate exponents or overall rate law are claimed (route: "without inventing an overall rate law"). The direction conclusion is mechanism independent. See author notes.
- **Numbers:** concentrations in "units" (same convention as C3, labelled "units"; made-up). Equilibrium: NO2 8, N2O4 4, forward 0.5 x 8^2 = 32, reverse 8 x 4 = 32, gap 0. Halve the volume: both double, NO2 16, N2O4 8. Forward 32 x 2 x 2 = 128, reverse 32 x 2 = 64, gap 64. One reaction step (each joining event removes 2 NO2 and makes 1 N2O4, here one unit of change): NO2 14, N2O4 9, forward 98 (0.5 x 196), reverse 72 (8 x 9), gap 26. New equilibrium: nitrogen atoms are conserved (NO2 + 2 x N2O4 = 32) and reverse = forward gives N2O4 = NO2^2 / 16, so n^2 + 8n - 256 = 0, n = 12.49, N2O4 = 9.75, both rates 78.0. Shown only as "about 12.5", "about 9.8" and "about 78" on labelled tags on the graph, never as dots. The colour claim follows from the algebra for every system with K > 0: new NO2 is greater than the original (n + 2Kn^2 = 2x + 4Kx^2 exceeds x + 2Kx^2 at n = x) and less than double (at n = 2x the left side is larger), so it is always between the original and the immediate shade. A chip says "made-up numbers to show the pattern" in scene 2a. The syringe is "squeezed slowly and left to return to room temperature", said once, because real compression warms the gas and the forward reaction is exothermic. Codex should confirm the arithmetic, the n and N2O4 values, and the claim "ends between the original and immediate shade".
- **Equal case (H2 + I2 ⇌ 2HI):** forward = k[H2][I2] and reverse = k'[HI]^2 are both second order in concentration, so each rate rises by the same factor of 4 when every concentration doubles; no new rates are computed on screen, only the factor four on each bar. Spoken as "both reactions need two particles to meet". Codex also to confirm the school-level second-order claim is acceptable. Colour: iodine vapour is purple and hydrogen iodide is colourless, at a hot temperature so everything is a gas (said once as "hot gases"). The colour darkens at once and then stays, because nothing shifts.
- **Solid case:** C(s) + CO2(g) ⇌ 2CO(g), gas count 1 left, 2 right. Squeezing shifts left. Pure solids and pure liquids do not count because squeezing does not change how crowded a pure solid or liquid is (its "concentration" is its density, which does not change). Learner copy says "pure" and gives only that plain reason (no activity, no K), so dissolved gases and aqueous ions are not ruled out. The carbon case uses the scene 5 counting rule only, with no rate factors, because carbon gasification does not follow simple first and second order kinetics (Claude science check, finding 1); the direction (left) is mechanism independent. No bars, no "joining" or "splitting" words.
- **Inert gas scene condition:** always "fixed volume" and "the plunger locked". Constant pressure inert gas is not taught (the volume grows, a concentration drop, and a different disturbance). The aid tag carries "Squeeze: volume changes. Argon: volume fixed." The pressure gauge is introduced in scene 7 only (after "A squeeze raises the total pressure"), where both disturbances raise the total pressure by the same factor (x2) so that equal pressure and different crowding is visible; it is not shown in the worked example. Argon is real and inert in the model; the school model has no argon-reactant interaction, and the lesson does not say argon particles never collide with reacting ones (draft preparation warned against this).
- **Attempt system (review blocker, resolved):** 2NO(g) + O2(g) ⇌ 2NO2(g) (unseen; gas count 3 to 2; NO and O2 colourless, NO2 brown). The shift goes right, so the brown does NOT fade after its immediate darkening, which contrasts with the worked syringe on purpose: colour follows which side the shift favours. The hook shows NO2 in equilibrium with N2O4 at room temperature, so the attempt now states "The cylinder is hot, so the N O two stays as single molecules and does not join to make N two O four" (spoken, as the tag "hot cylinder: no N2O4", and on the feedback board), and feedback says the brown keeps darkening "because more N O two forms each second than is used up, until the rates match again (13 Oct, voiced review finding 2; the old "nothing here removes" wording was wrong)". No temperature value is shown. **Flag for Codex:** confirm a temperature at which N2O4 is negligible and 2NO + O2 ⇌ 2NO2 still has all three gases present. If that cannot be stated plainly, swap to a system whose coloured gas has no second equilibrium. No delta H is needed because the temperature is steady and the question does not ask a temperature effect. The feedback is built from the same counting and crowding steps as the lesson.
- **Mistake scene:** none separate. The common wrong instincts ("higher pressure always shifts right", "pressure from argon shifts it", "the colour always fades back") are corrected in scenes 6a, 7 and 10 and named in the feedback.
- **Reuse:** C2 and C3 rate bars (made-up units), stage table with gap column, net arrow, concentration and rate graphs with snapshot dots and the two brackets, countdown, key notes board, colour tokens (forward blue, reverse amber, equal teal). New in C4a: a coded syringe or piston with a draggable plunger, a crowding dial for the reacting gases, a total-pressure gauge (scene 7 onward), and an argon particle set. The existing `PressureDiagram.tsx` piston artwork can be reused if its labels are removed; no other prototype components are used.
- **Author notes (not learner copy):** (a) At constant temperature the quantitative statement is that Q changes by a factor of 2^(delta n of gas) on halving the volume, which is the K-based proof (c05); the rate argument above is a teaching model and gives the same direction for every equation. For multi-step mechanisms (Haber), the rate laws are not the coefficients, so only "the reaction that uses more gas particles gains more" is stated, never a factor. (b) Real compression warms the gas; the forward reaction here is exothermic so heating pushes the mixture back toward NO2, and the temperature condition is stated for that reason. (c) A real syringe also changes optical path length with plunger position only if viewed down the barrel; the view is side on. (d) Non-ideal behaviour at high pressure and condensation are out of scope. (e) Pronunciation: "N O two", "N two O four", "N O" for nitric oxide (check it is not heard as "no"), "H I" for hydrogen iodide, "C O two", "C O". (f) The route stop rule asks to "distinguish inert gas at fixed volume from compression" and "justify gaseous-coefficient reasoning"; both are here. The remaining stop-rule item, catalyst scope, is C4B.

Main question: *Squeeze a gas mixture that is sitting at equilibrium. Which way does it go, and does every rise in pressure do the same thing?*
Entry knowledge (C1, C2, C3 and Year 11): dynamic equilibrium means equal non-zero rates in a closed system; rate means how much changes per second; more particles meeting means more events per second; the faster rate falls and the slower rate rises until they meet; a change shows up as "the moment of the change" then "the response", and it is only partly cancelled; gas pressure is particles hitting the walls; the numbers in front of species in a balanced equation count particles.
Stops after: predicting the shift on compression or expansion by counting only gas particles, explaining it as which reaction gains more when every gas is more crowded, handling an equal count and a solid, and telling a squeeze from adding an inert gas at fixed volume. No K or Q, no constant-pressure argon, no temperature or catalyst in the prediction.
Handoff: C4B, adding a catalyst.

### 5. why the squeeze favours fewer gas particles
> So why did joining gain more? Because it depends on more gas particles. That gives a rule. A squeeze speeds up most the reaction that depends on more gas particles, so the shift goes toward the side with fewer gas particles. Count only the gases. Chemists say moles of gas. It is the same count: the numbers in front of the gases in the balanced equation. In nitrogen plus three hydrogen making two ammonia, there are four gas particles on the left and two on the right, so the forward reaction depends on more gas particles and gains more. Squeezing shifts it right. Expanding does the opposite, so the shift goes toward the side with more gas particles.

### 7. squeeze or argon (the contrast)
> A squeeze raises the total pressure, because the crowded particles hit the walls more often. But pressure can also rise without a squeeze. Take our N O two syringe, lock the plunger so the volume cannot change, and pump in argon. Argon is an inert gas, which means it takes no part in the reaction. But the N O two and the N two O four have the same room as before, so each is exactly as crowded as before. Argon does not crowd N O two any closer to N two O four, so it does not favour either direction, and there is no shift and no colour change. A squeeze and an argon top-up can raise the total pressure equally, but only the squeeze crowds the reacting gases. So total pressure by itself is not the cause. Crowding of the reacting gases is.

### 11. decision rule and handoff

### 12. key notes (quiet, hold about 60 seconds)
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

- Squeezing a gas mixture at a steady temperature makes every gas more crowded at once. The reaction that depends on more gas particles gains more, so the shift goes toward the side with fewer gas particles (fewer moles of gas). Expanding does the opposite.
- Count only gases, using the numbers in the balanced equation. If both sides have the same number of gas particles, changing the volume causes no shift. Pure solids and pure liquids do not count.
- A squeeze shows up in two steps: every concentration changes at once, then the net reaction runs until the rates match. It only partly cancels the change.
- The coloured gas darkens at once when squeezed. After that it fades part of the way back if the shift uses it up, darkens further if the shift makes it, and stays the same if nothing shifts.
- Adding an inert gas in a fixed volume raises the total pressure but changes no reacting concentration, so it favours neither direction and nothing shifts.

Memory aid (large, on the notes screen): *Squeeze it and the shift goes to fewer gas particles. Argon in a fixed box does not shift it.* Condition tag: "gases only, temperature steady. Squeeze: volume changes. Argon: volume fixed."

## Memory aid check

"Squeeze it and the shift goes to fewer gas particles. Argon in a fixed box does not shift it." Assumptions: temperature steady; volume changed by compression or expansion of a sealed gas mixture at equilibrium; count only gases using the balanced coefficients (pure solids and pure liquids do not count); if the totals match, no shift; direction only, never size; school ideal gas model. The second sentence holds only for a fixed volume, a steady temperature, an ideal mixture and no reacting gas added or removed. In the bank, "does nothing" means "does nothing to the equilibrium"; the total pressure does rise (scene 7 says so), which is why the aid now says "does not shift it" and the tag gives each half its own condition ("Squeeze: volume changes. Argon: volume fixed."). It works for every case in the lesson: N2O4 syringe (2 to 1, right), ammonia (4 to 2, right), expansion of the syringe (toward more particles, left), H2 + I2 (2 to 2, none), C(s) + CO2 (1 to 2, squeeze left), 2NO + O2 in a hot cylinder (3 to 2, right), argon in a fixed box (none). Do not offer the aid for: adding a reacting gas in a fixed box (a concentration disturbance), constant-pressure argon (volume grows), a mixture with no gas (pure solids and pure liquids only), temperature changes, catalysts, or when the question asks for the size of the shift. The aid does not predict colour at an instant (colour is the concentration of the coloured species), which is why the colour note is separate. "Squeeze it and it makes fewer gas moles" is reworded to "the shift goes to fewer gas particles" because "makes" suggests the squeeze itself reacts the gas; the shift does, and "moles of gas" is spoken once in scene 5 and written in note 1. The bank's "Change T, change K" is not used (K waits for C5).
