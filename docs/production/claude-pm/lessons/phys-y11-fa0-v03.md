## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why group B scattered: its drops weren't done the same way (2). Why weigh every cone: mass is controlled, and a cone can differ without looking different (3). Why square on and the scale just behind the drop: so the scale and the cone line up, not seen at an angle (4). Why record source details as you go: so they can be credited later (5). Why check the frame rate: a specification is a claim (5). Why the control run first: if the method failed, every cone reading would be wasted (6). Why rounds: a change during the session spreads over every cone (7). Why raw readings: every later step can be checked (8). Why note and repeat: deleting hides, keeping as normal spoils (10). Why stop for the lead: it's a new hazard the plan didn't have (11).
- **Misconceptions addressed:** any way of following the plan is fine (1, 2); a cone that looks right is right (3); the camera's position doesn't matter (4); a specification is always right (5); check the method afterwards (6); do one cone at a time (7); write down worked-out times (8; A1 crossed); delete odd drops (9, 10; A1 crossed); safety ends with the written assessment (11; A2 crossed).
- **One idea at a time:** why conducting matters (1, 2), building (3), technology (4), sources (5), control run (6), routine (7), recording (8), the odd drop (9, 10), safety in action (11).
- **Video makes it easier:** two results strips make "same plan, different spread" visible (1); each group-B fault pulses its own dot (2); the balance flags the odd cone (3); the camera screen shows the skewed scale straightening (4); the timer film counts 240 frame intervals (5); the frame counter freezes on release and landing (6, 8); the draught lands on one cone's block or spreads over all (7); the record sheet fills cell by cell with a note that never disappears (8 to 10); the lead visibly crosses, then clears, the walkway (11).
