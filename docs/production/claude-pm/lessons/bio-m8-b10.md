> You've met two of these with dengue. **Incidence** counts new cases in a set time, and **prevalence** counts everyone who has the disease at one moment. ### 7. why the three numbers differ
> Side by side: two hundred new cases, five thousand five hundred living with it, and twenty deaths, all per hundred thousand. ### 12. key notes (quiet, about 60 seconds)
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

- Incidence: new cases in a set time (new cases ÷ population × 100,000, per year). Prevalence: everyone living with the disease at one time (existing cases ÷ population, as a % or per 100,000). Mortality: deaths from the disease in a set time (deaths ÷ whole population × 100,000, per year).
- Every rate defines a case, the population at risk and the time window. Incidence is the tap, prevalence is the tank.
- A long-lasting disease builds high prevalence; better treatment can raise prevalence while incidence stays steady.
- Compare rates, not counts. When age structures differ, compare age-standardised rates, not crude rates.
- Put values on one denominator before comparing. Line graph for change over time, bar graph for groups, units on the axis.
