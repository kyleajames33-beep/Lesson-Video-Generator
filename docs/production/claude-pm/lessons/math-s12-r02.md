### 3. three steps
> Here's the method, in three steps. Step one, count the parts. Five plus three is eight parts **altogether**. Step two, find one part. The whole four hundred and eighty dollars is all eight parts, so one part is four hundred and eighty divided by eight, sixty **dollars**. Step three, multiply out. The first cousin gets five parts, five times sixty, three hundred dollars. The second gets three parts, three times sixty, a hundred and eighty **dollars**. Now check. Three hundred plus a hundred and eighty is four hundred and eighty, the whole **takings**. A common slip is to divide by five, the first term. But the total is eight parts, not **five**.

### 5. total or part?
> Sometimes the question gives you one share, not the total. A mortar mix for laying path pavers is cement to sand, one to three. There's already twelve kilograms of sand in the barrow. How much cement is needed? Twelve kilograms isn't the total. It's the sand's share, which is three **parts**. So one part is twelve divided by three, four kilograms, and cement is one part, four **kilograms**. Dividing twelve by four, as if it were the total, is the **slip**. So always ask yourself, have I been given the total, or a part?

### 6. worked example: a bake sale batch

The order of the ratio is the order the ingredients are **named**.

## Teaching checks
