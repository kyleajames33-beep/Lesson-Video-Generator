## Decisions

- **Learner question kept (route).** "How do we differentiate a quotient and keep its domain?" The answer, built in order: when x changes, the top's change pushes the fraction one way and the bottom's change pulls it the other way, so (f/g)′ has a minus sign: (f′g − fg′)/g². The rule comes from d07's product rule, because f/g = f × (1/g). The domain comes from the original function (the bottom is never zero; ln needs a positive input), is written before differentiating, and stays with the answer, including after a factor cancels.
- **Recall, not re-teach (route start and root decision).** The quotient rule is named as the Year 11 rule (scene 3) and rebuilt in four rows from d07's product rule and the Year 11 chain rule. This explains the shape (the order, the minus, the square) in about 35 s; it is not presented as new.
- **The hook surprise (graph driven; d07's handoff frame).** y = eˣ/(x + 1) (site section 06 example 2). The tempting guess "divide the derivatives" gives eˣ/1 = eˣ, which is 1 at x = 0. The graph's tangent at (0, 1) is flat. The guess line (slope 1) and the true tangent are drawn through the same point. The gap at x = −1 is shown first, so the domain is part of the question from the start.
- **Push and pull, measured (scene 2).** Two readout rows on the graph, computed from the same x: "top's turn: f′/g" and "bottom's turn: −fg′/g²". At x = 0: 1.000 and −1.000, gradient 0. At x = 1: e/2 ≈ 1.359 and −e/4 ≈ −0.680, gradient e/4 ≈ 0.680. This is d07's "two turns" with the bottom's turn negative (author note a). It makes the minus sign a cause, not a rule to memorise.
- **Rebuilding the rule (scene 3).** Rows: f/g = f × (1/g); (1/g)′ = −g′/g² (Year 11 chain rule on g⁻¹, g ≠ 0); (f/g)′ = f′ × (1/g) + f × (−g′/g²) (d07's two turns, teal and blue); = (f′g − fg′)/g² (common denominator g², boxed with "g ≠ 0"). The site's "f/g = f · g⁻¹, so both rules work" is the same idea.
- **Domain first (route stop; pack section 4).** Every worked example and attempt writes the domain strip before any derivative: x ≠ −1 (scene 4); x > 0 and cos x ≠ 0 (scene 5); x ≠ 0 (scene 7); x ≠ −2 (A1); x > 0 (A2). `DomainExclusions` carries the strip down to the boxed answer.
- **Worked example 1 (site section 06 example 2; the hook function).** y = eˣ/(x + 1), x ≠ −1: f = eˣ, f′ = eˣ, g = x + 1, g′ = 1. Numerator eˣ(x + 1) − eˣ · 1 = eˣ(x + 1 − 1) = xeˣ. dy/dx = xeˣ/(x + 1)², x ≠ −1. At x = 0: 0, the flat tangent. Site numbers kept.
- **Worked example 2 (route primary `we-2`, derivative and gradient only).** y = ln x/cos x, domain x > 0 and cos x ≠ 0 (x ≠ π/2, 3π/2, ...). f = ln x, f′ = 1/x; g = cos x, g′ = −sin x. dy/dx = ((1/x)cos x + ln x sin x)/cos² x (the two minus signs make a plus, the site's note). At x = 1: ln 1 = 0, so the second term on top vanishes, and dy/dx = cos 1/cos² 1 = 1/cos 1 ≈ 1.851 (1 radian). The bottom's turn is zero there because the top is zero, which echoes d07's sin x turn at π/2. The site's tangent equation is not written (route recommendation); the site's "sec 1" is written 1/cos 1 (sec is a later home).

### 1. hook (divide the derivatives?)
> Last video ended on this curve: e to the x, over x plus one. At x equals minus one, the bottom is zero, and you can't divide by zero, so there's no curve **there**. Now the slope at x equals zero. A quick guess: divide the derivatives. The top's derivative is e to the x, and the bottom's is one, so the guess gives a slope of one at x equals zero. But the tangent there is **flat**. ### 2. the top pushes, the bottom pulls
> Think about what each part does. For this positive fraction, top growth pushes up and bottom growth pulls **down**. At x equals zero, the top, e to the x, is growing at a rate of one, and that pushes y up at a rate of one. The bottom, x plus one, is also growing at a rate of one, and that pulls y down at a rate of **one**. The push and the pull cancel, so the slope is **zero**. At x equals one, the push is about one point three six, and the pull is about point six eight, so the slope is about point six **eight**. That pull is the minus sign in the quotient rule.

### 3. where the rule comes from
> You met the quotient rule in Year 11. Here's why it has that shape. Dividing by g is the same as multiplying by one over g, so use last video's product rule. The top's turn is f prime, times one over **g**. For the bottom's turn, the chain rule says one over g has derivative minus g prime, over g **squared**. So that turn is minus f g prime, over g squared. Put both turns over g squared, and you get f prime g, minus f g prime, all over g **squared**. The top's derivative comes first, the minus is the bottom's pull, and the whole bottom is squared. Check our curve at zero: f prime g is one, f g prime is one, so the top is one minus one, which is **zero**.

### 4. domain first, then the rule

### 5. worked example: the natural log of x, over cos x

### 6. the gradient at x equals one

### 7. cancelling keeps the domain
> One more habit, for simplifying. Differentiate y equals sine x, over x **squared**. The bottom can't be zero, so x can't be zero. That's allowed only because x isn't zero. So the answer is x cos x, minus two sine x, over x cubed, and it still comes with x not equal to **zero**. Cancelling a factor never gives back a point the original function didn't have.

## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why dividing the derivatives fails: the slope of a fraction depends on both parts' heights as well as their slopes (1, 2). Why the minus sign: for this positive fraction, the bottom's growth makes it smaller (2), and algebraically the derivative of 1/g is −g′/g² (3). Why the bottom is squared: the chain rule on g⁻¹ gives g⁻², and the common denominator is g² (3). Why the domain comes first: the derivative only exists where the function does (1, 4, 5). Why the gradient of ln x/cos x at 1 is 1/cos 1: the top is zero there, so the bottom's turn vanishes (6). Why cancelling x is allowed: x ≠ 0 on the domain (7). Why order matters for a quotient but not a product: subtraction is not symmetric, addition is (8).
- **Misconceptions addressed:** "divide the derivatives" (1, refuted by the graph; 12, crossed); "order doesn't matter" (8, said positively; 10, crossed); "square only part of the bottom" (3, the whole g² bracketed; key note 1); "cancelling removes the restriction" (7, said positively; key note 3); "cos 1 in degrees" (6, said and crossed; author note b); "order reversed" also checked on the graph (6: a falling dashed tangent against a rising curve).
