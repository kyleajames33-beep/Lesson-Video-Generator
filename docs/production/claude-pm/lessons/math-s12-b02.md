### 2. a scatter plot, with a scale that fits
> A graph like this, with one dot for each pair, is called a scatter plot. Each dot is one day. We don't join the dots, because the days aren't a sequence, they're separate cases. Each axis needs a label, with its units, and a scale that fits the data. If both axes start at zero, the points bunch up in one part of the graph, and the rest is empty space. So start each scale just below the smallest value, and draw a small zigzag, a **break**, to show the axis doesn't start at zero. Now the points spread across the graph, so any pattern is easier to **see**.

Visual: camera continue. Hand tags "one dot per day", then "don't join the dots" as a grey dotted join-the-dots line draws and is crossed. ### 5. strength: how close to a straight line?
> Last, strength. Strength is how closely the points hug a straight line. Picture a straight line through the middle of the points, and a band around it that holds the points. A thin band means the points sit close to the line, and that's strong. A wider band is moderate. A very wide band, with points spread all over, is **weak**. The kiosk's points sit in a thin band, so it's **strong**.

Visual: stage change: simulation 2 on the kiosk plot at full size (about 1,150 × 720 px). ### 6. steep isn't strong
> Here's the classic trap. Strength isn't how steep the line is. A steep line only means y changes quickly as x changes. Look at these two plots. The left one is steep, but its points are spread a long way from the line, so it's weak. The right one is gentle, but its points hug the line tightly, so it's strong. Look at the band, not the **angle**.
