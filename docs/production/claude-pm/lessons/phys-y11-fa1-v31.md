## Decisions

- **Learner question kept (route; it is already causal and specific).** "Does the measured total momentum survive a collision?" The answer, built in order: real tracks add small external forces, so the measured total after might differ (1); the apparatus is chosen to make the system nearly isolated, and each choice has a reason (2); the plan changes the momentum before and measures the momentum after (3); one run gives a 1.5 per cent shortfall and an inelastic energy loss (4); repeats expose an outlier with a cause (5); the graph of after against before has gradient 1.00 and a small negative intercept (6); the gradient shows no tilt, so the data show no proportional loss; the constant gap suggests an outside effect (modelled here as drag, with a timing offset or calibration error as other possibilities), but the fitted line alone cannot identify the cause or rule out a collision contribution, so the claim stays conditional on the illustrative model and independent checks (7, 8; Revision 3, error check finding 1); the data are precise, slightly below the model prediction by more than their scatter and the stated limits, and valid because the system is nearly isolated (9); improvements and a further investigation (10); risks (11). So, in this illustration: the totals agree to about 1.5 per cent, a small steady shortfall remains, and a real practical must supply real evidence.
- **The hook (owner: a useful question; route stop "do not report predictions as observations").** "Will the measured total after come out equal, a little less, or a little more?" 3 s prediction. The answer comes from the data (scenes 6 to 8): a little less, by a constant amount, from outside the collision.
- **Method (site activity 1 and Q3, made specific; owner: apparatus as painted plates).** Linear air track with a blower, levelled by the stay-still test (v13's balance: a glider at rest with no net force stays at rest). Glider A (0.200 kg, with a 10.0 cm flag and a hook-and-loop pad) fired by v23's spring launcher at five settings, three runs each; glider B (0.300 kg: glider plus glider mass, a pad facing A) at rest between the gates. Gate 1 times A's flag before the collision; the pads stick; gate 2 times the same flag on the stuck pair. Masses on v15's electronic balance (±0.001 kg). Velocity = flag length ÷ blocking time; Σp before = m_A u; Σp after = (m_A + m_B) v. The site's motion sensors and ticker tape are alternatives; Revision 3's one-line key notes no longer name them (author record only).
- **Choosing the apparatus (`ci1ba1d4e1`, partial).** Each choice tied to the law's condition or to reliable data: air track (almost no friction, so almost no external force); level track checked by the stay-still test (weight and normal force balance; it shows the tilt is too small to notice, within the glider's friction and the eye's resolution, not that the tilt is zero); spring launcher (the same launch each time, no push); light gates (a tenth of a millisecond against a stopwatch's reaction time of about 0.2 s); electronic balance (masses to a gram). The site's "level, low-friction track; known trolley masses" become these reasons.
- **Variables (`ci9ce164ec`, applied, not claimed: v15 covered it).** Independent: Σp before (changed by the launcher setting, measured at gate 1). Dependent: Σp after. Controlled: both masses, the level track, the flag, the gate positions, sticking pads. Three runs per setting.
- **Illustrative data (generated from a stated model; labelled).** Model for the data: Σp after = Σp before − 0.0012 kg m s⁻¹ (a constant external impulse from air drag over the fixed stretch between the gates, author note c) plus random scatter of up to ±0.0004 kg m s⁻¹; blocking times shown to 0.1 ms. Said aloud in scene 4: "These numbers are illustrative, made up to show the method." On screen every table, graph and readout carries the grey tag "illustrative data". One run at setting 4 is an outlier (Σp after 0.1045, before 0.0995: ratio 1.05), caused by glider B drifting forward before the collision on a slightly tilted track; it is shown, explained, and replaced by a repeat after re-levelling (`ci8856aeb0`, `ci7afa7b23`).
- **One run in full (setting 3, run 1).** Gate 1 250.0 ms: u = 0.100 ÷ 0.2500 = 0.400 m s⁻¹, Σp before = 0.200 × 0.400 = 0.0800 kg m s⁻¹. Gate 2 634.5 ms: v = 0.100 ÷ 0.6345 = 0.158 m s⁻¹ (0.1576), Σp after = 0.500 × 0.1576 = 0.0788 kg m s⁻¹: 1.5 per cent less. KE: 0.0160 J before, 0.00621 J after: 39 per cent kept, so inelastic (v30's classification, now measured; the sticking model predicts 40 per cent for these masses, author note e; not said).
- **Representations (`ci0fca96de`, applied).** A table of setting means with units in the headings and the repeat-scatter (half-range) column; a graph of Σp after (vertical) against Σp before (horizontal) with scatter-only bars, the dashed model line "Σp after = Σp before (model prediction)" through the origin with gradient 1, and the best-fit line. v24's memory aid "Plot it straight, then read the gradient" recalled in one line and extended: "and the intercept".
- **Reading the graph (the key analysis; owner quality bar: the cause made visible).** Gradient 1.00 (best fit 1.0005): the momentum after matches the momentum before in proportion, so the data show no proportional loss. Intercept −0.0012 kg m s⁻¹: every point sits below the model line by about the same amount, more than its bar. Revision 3 (error check finding 1): the reading is conditional. A constant gap suggests an effect outside the collision; the line alone cannot prove it, and each possible cause needs an independent check. Contrast shown with a ghost line (tag "if the collision lost a fixed share"): a loss that is a fraction of the momentum tilts the line (gradient below 1); a constant loss shifts it down. Pattern (`ci7afa7b23`): the percentage shortfall shrinks from 3.0 per cent at setting 1 to 1.0 per cent at setting 5, as a constant loss must.
- **Precision, accuracy and validity (`ci15366d75`, partial: precision named for the first time in FA1; v10 named accuracy and reliability, v15 validity).** Precise: the repeats agree closely (half-ranges within about 2 per cent). Slightly below the model prediction: every total after is below what conservation predicts, by more than the repeat scatter and the stated measurement limits (about 0.6 per cent of each total, author note l), which may be a systematic error (v10's term); if it is, more repeats would not remove it (Revision 3, error check finding 2: stated as a possibility, since the data have not established the cause). fa0-v05 now sits earlier in the playlist and defines precise, accurate, reliable and valid (FA0 root decisions 3 and 6), so scene 9 applies those meanings with a small slate tag "fa0-v05" and does not re-teach them. The prediction is the model value, not a known true measurement. Valid: the test checks the collision only because the air track makes the system nearly isolated; on a rough track it would test friction.
- **Prediction 2 (3 s, scene 7).** "Lost in the collision, or somewhere else?" Answered by the gradient (8).
- **Limitations, improvements, further investigation (`ci914a5e7e`, `ci7e38e278` applied).** (1) Drag between the gates (the modelled cause): put the gates as close to the collision as the gliders allow, with the flag clearing the sticking pads outside the collision before it breaks each beam. (2) A tilt gives B a drift (the outlier): re-level and do the stay-still test before each setting. (3) Further investigation: spring bumpers in place of the pads, to test whether kinetic energy also survives (elastic). The flag length cancels in the ratio of the two totals because the same flag passes both gates (author note d; not said).

**Attempt 1 (scene 12; original wording, HSC style, 4 marks suggested, not an official marking scheme).** "On a level air track, glider P (0.250 kg) moves to the right at 0.480 m s⁻¹ and glider Q (0.150 kg) moves to the left at 0.300 m s⁻¹. (b) Assess whether the results support the conservation of momentum."
Model answer: (a) Taking right as positive: Σp before = 0.250 × 0.480 + 0.150 × (−0.300) = 0.120 − 0.0450 = 0.0750 kg m s⁻¹; Σp after = (0.250 + 0.150) × 0.185 = 0.0740 kg m s⁻¹. (b) Percentage difference = (0.0750 − 0.0740) ÷ 0.0750 × 100 ≈ 1.3%, which is within the ±2% uncertainty, so the results support conservation of momentum (the small shortfall is within the uncertainty; drag between the measurements is one possible contributor). Classic mistake (crossed): "Σp before = 0.120 + 0.0450 = 0.165 kg m s⁻¹, so most of the momentum was lost".

**Attempt 2 (scene 14; original wording, HSC style, 3 marks suggested).** "Two groups test the conservation of momentum using gliders that stick together. Group Y: a straight line through the origin with gradient 0.90. Which group's line shows a constant offset, and which shows a proportional discrepancy? Suggest one possible cause for each."
Model answer: Group X shows the constant offset: its gradient of 1.00 means the totals keep pace, and its intercept of −0.003 kg m s⁻¹ is the same shift at every momentum, so it points to something outside the collision, for example drag, friction or a timing offset. ## Safety facts check (self-check, 2 Nov; Codex to confirm)

- **Air blower on mains power.** Air-track blowers are mains-powered (240 V in NSW). Hazards: a damaged or wet lead can cause electric shock; a lead across the floor or bench edge is a trip hazard and can drag the blower off the bench. Controls: inspect the lead and plug, check the electrical test tag is current, keep liquids away, tape the lead flat along the bench. Learner copy as scene 11.
- **Gliders leaving the track.** A glider launched at a high setting can leave the end of the track, fall and strike feet or be damaged. Controls: end buffers fitted at both ends, low launcher settings, clear floor below the ends. Learner copy as scene 11.
- **Spring launcher.** A compressed spring releases suddenly and can pinch fingers between the plunger and the housing or the glider. Controls: fingers clear of the plunger and the glider, release with the trigger. Learner copy as scene 11.
- **Noise (teacher note).** Blowers are loud; run only while measuring. Not in learner copy.
- **Track surface (teacher note).** Separate stuck gliders and move gliders only with the blower on, to avoid scratching the track (equipment care, not a learner risk row).

Description: Full lesson, notes and practice questions: https://hscscience.com.au/subjects/physics/year11/fa1-fundamentals-of-mechanics/lesson28.sa.html

On paper, the total momentum before a collision equals the total after. Does it survive a real measurement? See how to test conservation of momentum with gliders on an air track: choose the apparatus and justify each choice, measure velocities with light gates, repeat each setting and deal properly with an outlier, then plot momentum after against momentum before. Learn what the gradient and the intercept tell you, why the data can be precise but sit slightly below the model prediction, how to improve the method, and the risk assessment. The data in this video are illustrative. Two exam-style questions at the end.
