## Decisions

- **Learner question kept (route; it is already causal and specific).** "Does the measured total momentum survive a collision?" The answer, built in order: real tracks add small external forces, so the measured total after might differ (1); the apparatus is chosen to make the system nearly isolated, and each choice has a reason (2); the plan changes the momentum before and measures the momentum after (3); one run gives a 1.5 per cent shortfall and an inelastic energy loss (4); repeats expose an outlier with a cause (5); the graph of after against before has gradient 1.00 and a small negative intercept (6); the constant gap is not a collision loss: the gradient shows no tilt, and an outside effect (modelled here as drag, with a timing offset or calibration error as other possibilities) is the illustration's assumed cause (7, 8); the data are precise, slightly below the model prediction by more than their scatter and the stated limits, and valid because the system is nearly isolated (9); improvements and a further investigation (10); risks (11). So, in this illustration: the totals agree to about 1.5 per cent, a small steady shortfall remains, and a real practical must supply real evidence.
- **The hook (owner: a useful question; route stop "do not report predictions as observations").** "Will the measured total after come out equal, a little less, or a little more?" 3 s prediction. The answer comes from the data (scenes 6 to 8): a little less, by a constant amount, from outside the collision.
- **Method (site activity 1 and Q3, made specific; owner: apparatus as painted plates).** Linear air track with a blower, levelled by the stay-still test (v13's balance: a glider at rest with no net force stays at rest). Glider A (0.200 kg, with a 10.0 cm flag and a hook-and-loop pad) fired by v23's spring launcher at five settings, three runs each; glider B (0.300 kg: glider plus glider mass, a pad facing A) at rest between the gates. Gate 1 times A's flag before the collision; the pads stick; gate 2 times the same flag on the stuck pair. Masses on v15's electronic balance (±0.001 kg). Velocity = flag length ÷ blocking time; Σp before = m_A u; Σp after = (m_A + m_B) v. The site's motion sensors and ticker tape are named as alternatives in the key notes only.

### 1. hook (equal, less or more?)
> In the momentum video, theory said the total momentum before a collision equals the total after. But real gliders feel a little air drag, and no track is perfectly level. So when we measure a real collision, will the total momentum after come out equal, a little less, or a little **more**?

Model answer: (a) Taking right as positive: Σp before = 0.250 × 0.480 + 0.150 × (−0.300) = 0.120 − 0.0450 = 0.0750 kg m s⁻¹; Σp after = (0.250 + 0.150) × 0.185 = 0.0740 kg m s⁻¹. Classic mistake (crossed): "Σp before = 0.120 + 0.0450 = 0.165 kg m s⁻¹, so most of the momentum was lost".

## Safety facts check (self-check, 2 Nov; Codex to confirm)

- **Air blower on mains power.** Air-track blowers are mains-powered (240 V in NSW). Hazards: a damaged or wet lead can cause electric shock; a lead across the floor or bench edge is a trip hazard and can drag the blower off the bench. Controls: inspect the lead and plug, check the electrical test tag is current, keep liquids away, tape the lead flat along the bench. Learner copy as scene 11.
- **Gliders leaving the track.** A glider launched at a high setting can leave the end of the track, fall and strike feet or be damaged. Controls: end buffers fitted at both ends, low launcher settings, clear floor below the ends. Learner copy as scene 11.
