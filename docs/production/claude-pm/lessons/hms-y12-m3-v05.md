### 2. cardiovascular disease: deaths down, living with it up
> **Cardiovascular disease** means conditions of the heart and blood vessels, like coronary heart disease and stroke. Most of it starts with plaque narrowing the arteries. Its four measures don't move together. Mortality has fallen hugely over decades, though the fall has slowed. Incidence is falling too, mostly from less smoking and better blood pressure control. But prevalence stays high, because people now survive heart attacks and strokes that once killed them. And morbidity has shifted to years of heart failure, or disability after a stroke. Think of a bathtub: new cases pour in, the water is everyone living with it, and deaths drain out. Treatment narrows the drain, so the water stays high.

### 3. cancer: more diagnoses, read carefully
> Cancer reads differently. **Cancer** is many diseases, in which abnormal cells divide without control and can invade other tissue. Mortality is falling, but more slowly. Incidence is rising as a count, mainly because the population is bigger and older, and partly because screening finds cancers once missed. Prevalence is rising strongly, because more are diagnosed and more survive. So more people diagnosed could mean more disease, the same disease found earlier, or more people living long enough to get it. You need a second measure to tell. And check for counts against an **age-standardised** rate, which adjusts for an older population. Counts can rise while that rate falls.

### 4. one pathway, four measures
> One measure is never enough. Picture one disease pathway: exposure to risk, onset, diagnosis, then the outcome. Prevention acts at exposure and onset. Screening acts at diagnosis. Treatment acts at the outcome. Incidence reads onset, so it falls only if prevention works. Mortality reads the outcome, and it falls if prevention or treatment improves, so on its own, it can't tell you which one worked.

### 5. risk factors: sort, then overlap

### 6. protective factors at three levels

The strongest in our history have been structural. Tobacco control saved more lives than any one decision to quit.
