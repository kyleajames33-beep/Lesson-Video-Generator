### 2. get the right DNA first
> First, you need the right piece of DNA. Remember that most human genes contain **introns**, stretches our cells cut out of the mRNA before it's read. Bacteria can't do that cutting, so a gene taken straight from a human chromosome would give them a scrambled message. Instead, scientists use a version with no introns, copied from the cell's finished mRNA, or built from scratch. For the first bacterial insulin, in the late nineteen-seventies, scientists built DNA for insulin's two chains from scratch.

### 12. key notes (quiet, about 60 seconds)
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

- Recombinant DNA is DNA joined from two sources. For bacteria, use an intron-free copy of a human gene (made from mRNA or built from scratch), because bacteria cannot remove introns.
