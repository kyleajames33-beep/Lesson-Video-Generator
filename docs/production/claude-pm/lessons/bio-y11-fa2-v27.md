### 3. why it's called negative
> The body would overshoot and get too hot. Because the response opposes the change, it switches itself down as it succeeds. This is called negative feedback: the response acts in the opposite direction to the change. Negative doesn't mean bad, and it doesn't mean something is decreasing. A rise gets pushed down, and a fall gets pushed up. That's how the body maintains homeostasis, keeping its internal conditions within a stable **range**.

### 5. constructing the loop diagram
> So here's how to construct a loop diagram for an exam. Put the variable in the middle, with its normal range. Draw one loop for a rise, and one for a fall. Without that last arrow, it's a chain, not a **loop**.

### 5b. reading a loop you haven't seen
> You'll also be asked to read loops you've never seen. Try carbon dioxide, from the last video's question. During exercise, blood carbon dioxide rises. Chemoreceptors detect it, the breathing centre in the brainstem signals the breathing muscles, and breathing gets faster and deeper.
