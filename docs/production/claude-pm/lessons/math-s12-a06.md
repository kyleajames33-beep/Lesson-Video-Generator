### 2. k: where the curve starts
> Start with y equals two to the x. It passes through zero, one. Now multiply every value by three. The table goes from one, two, four, to three, six, **twelve**. The whole curve stretches upwards, and now it crosses the y-axis at three, not one. That's the form y equals k times a to the x. Some books write A b to the x, but it's the same idea. Put x equals zero, and a to the zero is one, so y equals k. So k is the starting value. It's the vertical intercept, where the graph crosses the vertical **axis**. And a is still the multiplier for each step. Change k, and the curve starts somewhere else. Change a, and it grows at a different **rate**.

### 3. a: from a percentage to a multiplier
> For the rent, k is six hundred and fifty dollars. What's a? Each year, the new rent is the old rent, plus four per cent of it. That's a hundred per cent, plus four per cent, which is a hundred and four per cent. Per cent means out of a hundred, so that's one point zero **four**. So the model is R equals six hundred and fifty times one point zero four to the t, where t is the number of years. Watch the ladder. Six hundred and fifty, times one point zero four, is six hundred and seventy-six. Times one point zero four again is seven hundred and three dollars and four cents, and so **on**. After five years, it's about seven hundred and ninety-one dollars a week. Using one point four for four per cent is the slip. One point four would mean a forty per cent **rise**.

### 5. decay: keep what's left
> Values can shrink, too. A car bought for twenty-four thousand dollars loses eighteen per cent of its value each year. The key is what's kept, not what's lost. A hundred per cent, minus eighteen per cent, leaves eighty-two per cent, so the multiplier is zero point eight **two**. V equals twenty-four thousand times zero point eight two to the t. After five years, that's about eight thousand eight hundred and ninety-eight **dollars**. Round only at the end. Using zero point one eight is the slip. That says the car keeps only eighteen per cent each year, and after five years it would be worth about four dollars and fifty **cents**. Later, in the money videos, this kind of falling value gets its own name, **depreciation**.

### 6. decay written another way
> Exam questions sometimes write decay as k times a to the minus x. Here's why that's the same idea. A negative power means divide, so one point two five to the minus t means dividing by one point two five, t times. And dividing by one point two five is the same as multiplying by zero point **eight**, because one point two five times zero point eight is exactly one. So a value that keeps eighty per cent a year, seven hundred and fifty times zero point eight to the t, is exactly seven hundred and fifty times one point two five to the minus t. Watch them land on top of each **other**. So don't be fooled by a number bigger than one. With minus t in the power, it's still **decay**.
