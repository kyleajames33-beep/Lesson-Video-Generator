### 3. the gradient: rise over run
> Now, how steep is the line? Pick two clear points on it, like zero, twenty and twelve, eighty. Going across, the run is the change in minutes, twelve. Going up, the rise is the change in litres, sixty. The gradient is the rise divided by the run, so sixty divided by twelve, which is **five**. And because the rise is in litres and the run is in minutes, the gradient is five litres per minute. That's how fast the tank fills. Slide a small triangle anywhere along the line, and it's always up five for every one across. That's what makes it a straight line, the same rate the whole **way**. One warning. On this graph, one square across is one minute, but one square up is ten litres. Counting squares would give six over twelve, a half, and that's wrong. Read the numbers on the **axes**.

### 4. up, down or flat
> Gradients have a sign too. A tank draining at four litres a minute has a line going down, so its gradient is minus four litres per **minute**. A fridge holding steady at three degrees has a flat line, so its gradient is **zero**, zero degrees per hour. And savings growing by twenty-five dollars a week climb, so the gradient is plus twenty-five dollars per **week**. Up is positive, down is negative, and flat is zero. A negative gradient isn't a mistake, it just means the amount is falling. And the steeper the line, the bigger the rate, whichever way it **goes**.

### 5. worked example: points that don't start at zero
> Here's the method on its own, with points that don't start at zero. A car has travelled forty kilometres after half an hour, and a hundred and sixty kilometres after two hours, at a steady speed. Find the gradient, and say what it means. The gradient is the change in output, divided by the change in input. Distance is the output, so the rise is a hundred and sixty minus forty, which is a hundred and **twenty** kilometres. Time is the input, so the run is two minus a half, one and a half **hours**. A hundred and twenty divided by one and a half is **eighty**. So the gradient is eighty kilometres per hour, the car's speed. The slip is dividing the other way, one and a half divided by a hundred and twenty. It's always output change over input **change**.

### 6. reading m and c from an equation
> Often you get the equation instead of the graph. A straight line can be written y equals m x plus c. The number multiplying x is m, the gradient, and the number on its own is c, the **y-intercept**. Take C equals twelve plus five h, the cost of a hire for h hours. The five is with the h, so the gradient is five dollars an hour. The twelve is on its own, so the y-intercept is twelve dollars. The twelve isn't the gradient just because it comes **first**. Now try y equals ten minus two x. The number with x is minus two, so the gradient is minus two, and the line falls. The minus sign belongs to the two, because it's attached to the x. The y-intercept is **ten**. And the aquarium? L equals five t plus twenty. The five is the gradient we measured, and the twenty is where the line started. The graph and the equation tell the same **story**.
