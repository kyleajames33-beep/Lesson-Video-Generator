### 13. key notes (quiet, about 60 seconds)
Heading on screen: "Key notes: what to write in your book".
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

1. Food molecules (starch, proteins, fats) are too large or insoluble to cross the gut lining and cell membranes, so they are broken into small, soluble units: glucose, amino acids, fatty acids and glycerol.
2. The gut is one tube open at both ends: mouth → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine → large intestine → anus. Liver and pancreas add juices. Food in the lumen is outside the body until absorbed.
3. Mechanical digestion: teeth, tongue and stomach churning make smaller pieces (same molecules), increasing the surface area for enzymes. Physical digestion: bile (made in the liver, stored in the gall bladder) emulsifies fat into tiny droplets, breaking no bonds.
4. Chemical digestion: enzymes break bonds. Mouth: amylase (starch → smaller sugars). Stomach: pepsin (protein → shorter chains); HCl gives low pH, kills microbes, digests nothing. Small intestine: pancreatic amylase, proteases and lipase, and enzymes of the lining, finish the job.

## Numbers or facts to verify

Self-check (2 Nov). The Codex science check must confirm each before voicing.
- **Amino acids (1, 2).** Proteins in all organisms are built from the same 20 standard amino acids (selenocysteine and pyrrolysine are rare additions; author note). A typical protein has a few hundred amino acids. Chicken muscle protein must be broken down to amino acids, which the body reassembles into its own proteins on its ribosomes (FA1). Correct.
- **Starch and fat (2).** Amylose has hundreds to thousands of glucose units; amylopectin many thousands. "thousands of glucose units" is correct. A fat (triglyceride) is glycerol joined to three fatty acids; it is insoluble in water and forms droplets. Lipase products are mainly fatty acids and monoglycerides, with some glycerol; "fatty acids and glycerol" is the standard Year 11 simplification (author note). Correct at this level.
- **"Molecules like these can't cross the lining of your gut" (2).** Starch and proteins are too large; whole triglycerides are not absorbed as such (they are hydrolysed first). Correct.
- **Tube and lumen (3).** The gut is a continuous tube from mouth to anus; its lumen is topologically outside the body. The liver and pancreas are accessory organs whose ducts join the duodenum; food does not pass through them. Correct.
- **Cube (4).** A 1 cm cube has surface area 6 cm². Cut into eight 0.5 cm cubes: each 6 × 0.25 = 1.5 cm², total 12 cm² (double). Calculated by script (2 Nov).
- **Mouth (4, 5).** Teeth cut and grind; the tongue forms a bolus for swallowing; salivary amylase begins starch digestion to maltose and short chains. Correct.
- **Stomach (4 to 6).** Muscular wall churns food into chyme. Pepsin (secreted as pepsinogen, activated by acid; author note) works best at about pH 1.5 to 2 and breaks proteins into polypeptides. HCl gives a pH of about 1.5 to 3.5, activates pepsinogen, and kills many swallowed microorganisms. Salivary amylase has an optimum near pH 6.7 to 7 and is inactivated by the acid (some activity continues inside the bolus until acid penetrates; author note). The acid denatures proteins (unfolding them) but does not hydrolyse starch or fat; "doesn't turn bread into glucose" is correct. Flag for the science check: acid unfolding food proteins helps pepsin, which is still "setting conditions", not digesting.
- **Bile (7).** Made by liver cells, stored and concentrated in the gall bladder, released into the duodenum via the bile duct after a fatty meal. Bile salts emulsify fat into small droplets, greatly increasing surface area for lipase; bile contains no digestive enzymes. Emulsification breaks no covalent bonds. Correct. Bile also helps neutralise acid (author note).
- **Pancreas and lining (8).** Pancreatic juice contains hydrogencarbonate (neutralises acid; small intestine pH about 7 to 8), pancreatic amylase, proteases (trypsin, chymotrypsin, carboxypeptidase) and lipase. The intestinal lining (brush border) carries maltase, sucrase, lactase and peptidases that complete digestion. End products: monosaccharides (glucose), amino acids (and some di- and tripeptides), fatty acids and monoglycerides (with glycerol). Correct at this level.
- **Transit times (9).** Gastric emptying of a mixed meal takes about 2 to 4 hours (up to about 5); small-intestine transit about 3 to 5 hours (range about 2 to 6); colonic transit often 1 to 2 days (normal range roughly 10 to 59 hours). "a few hours ... a few more ... often a day or more" and the bar's ranges are correct; the grey tag notes the variation. Flag for the science check (ranges vary by source).
- **Fat digestion site (9).** Fat digestion occurs mainly in the small intestine (lingual and gastric lipases contribute a small fraction; author note). Correct.
- **Fibre and large intestine (10).** Humans lack enzymes to digest cellulose and most dietary fibre (gut bacteria ferment some; author note). The large intestine absorbs much of the remaining water (about 1 to 1.5 L a day) and salts; the small intestine absorbs most water overall (site error 1 in v16). "takes back much of the remaining water" is correct. Faeces leave via the rectum and anus. Correct.
- **Attempt 1.** Egg white (albumen) suspended or cubed in pepsin at 37 °C and acid pH is digested and clears; smaller pieces digest faster. Standard school demonstration. Correct.
- **Attempt 2.** A gallstone in the common bile duct (or cystic duct) prevents bile reaching the duodenum; fat emulsification fails, fat digestion and absorption fall, and fatty, pale faeces result (steatorrhoea). Correct.
- **Time-sensitive facts spoken.** None.
