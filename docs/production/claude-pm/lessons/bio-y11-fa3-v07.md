   Model (any two, 2 marks each: 1 for the problem, 1 for the improvement linked to it): The hunting time was not controlled, so differences in how long each hunt lasted could change how many dots of each colour were removed; hunt for a fixed time, such as exactly 15 s, every round. The sample was small and the model was run once, so the result could be due to chance; use about 100 dots and repeat the whole model at least three times, calculating a mean. The student knew the aim, so she may have picked red dots on purpose; use a predator who does not know the aim, who looks away while the dots are scattered and takes the first dot seen. There was no control, so a preference for red could not be ruled out; run the same dots on a plain white background where both colours are equally visible. Classic mistake crossed out: "run more generations" (does not fix uncontrolled hunting time, bias or chance). Other weak answers: "be more careful" (no specific change); "use more colours" (changes the investigation).
