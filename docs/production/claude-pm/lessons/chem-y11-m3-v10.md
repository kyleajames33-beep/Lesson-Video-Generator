- **Cause chain taught, in order:** (1) a half-equation shows one side of an electron transfer, so its electrons sit where OIL RIG puts them (2); (2) atoms cannot be made or lost, and neither can charge, so both must balance, and electrons carry the missing charge (3 to 5); (3) every electron lost must be gained, so the two halves must be scaled to the same number before adding (6, 7); (4) equal electrons on both sides cancel, leaving a balanced ionic equation (7, 8); (5) a balanced-looking equation with unequal charge has lost an electron, so it is wrong (11); (6) spectators did not change, so adding them to both sides keeps the balance (12).

### 1. hook: three and two
> Here's where we stopped, and here's the **real reaction**. Aluminium foil sits in copper sulfate solution, and brown copper grows on it. **Zoom in** to the metal's surface. Each aluminium atom loses **three electrons**, but each copper ion takes only **two**. Electrons can't be **left over**, and they can't go missing, so how do these two halves fit together? Let's write each half, then **combine** them.

### 2. what a half-equation is
> A half-equation shows just one half of a redox reaction, the oxidation or the reduction, with the electrons written in. In an oxidation, electrons are lost, so they appear on the right, as **products**. In a reduction, electrons are gained, so they appear on the left, as **reactants**. **Oil rig** tells you which side.

Visual (stage A continued, the lens splits; about 23 s): the plate shrinks to a thumbnail at the far left; the lens moves left of centre and a dashed line splits it: left half framed teal ("oxidation"), right half framed violet ("reduction"). Gloss chip at the top, 34 px: "half-equation: one half of a redox reaction, with the electrons written in". On "products" the left half replays the Al atom leaving and its three orange dots fly out of the lens into the right-hand end of a line that writes at 64 px to the right: "Al(s) → Al³⁺(aq) + 3e⁻" (teal, "3e⁻" orange); teal hand tag "e⁻ lost: right side". On "reactants" two dots fly from the left-hand side of "Cu²⁺(aq) + 2e⁻ → Cu(s)" (violet, 64 px, under the first) into the right half's Cu²⁺, which turns brown; violet hand tag "e⁻ gained: left side". On "Oil rig" the OIL RIG card pulses and parks at the top right as the header of the two-checks panel.

### 3. two checks: zinc
> Every half-equation needs two checks, the **atoms** and the **charge**, and electrons fix the charge. Take **zinc**. A zinc atom leaves the metal as zinc two plus, and its two electrons stay **behind**. One zinc on each side, so the **atoms are fine**. The charge is zero on the left and plus two on the right, so **add two electrons** to the right. Plus two, minus two, gives **zero**.

### 4. reductions: copper and chlorine
> **Copper ions** go the other way. A copper two plus ion **picks up two electrons** and becomes a copper atom, and both sides add to zero. Now **chlorine gas**. Balance the atoms first. A chlorine molecule has two atoms, so it makes **two chloride ions**. That's minus two on the right, so **add two electrons to the left**. Writing chlorine plus one electron gives one chloride is a common slip, because one chlorine atom goes **missing**.

### 5. ion to ion
> Some half-equations have **no metal** at all. This **pale green** solution contains iron two plus ions. An iron two plus ion can **lose one electron** to become iron three plus. So iron two plus gives iron three plus, plus one electron. Plus two on the left, and plus three minus one on the right, which is also plus two. **Balanced**.
