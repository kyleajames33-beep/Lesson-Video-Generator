## Practice question and feedback

1. (11a, justify and describe, 3 marks; original.) Beetroot cylinders in sugar solutions; temperature and soaking time kept the same; three per concentration.
   Model: (a) A longer soaking time lets more water move by osmosis, so a difference in time would change the mass as well as the sugar concentration; keeping it the same means any difference is due to the sugar (1 mark for the reason, 1 for the link to validity). (b) Cylinders in pure water (0 M sugar) show the change with no sugar, so the effect of sugar can be separated from other changes (1 mark). Classic mistake crossed: "the control is keeping the temperature the same". Other weak answers: "to make it fair" with no reason.
2. (11c, identify and propose, 4 marks; original.) Sports drink, classmates' heart rates, results posted with names.
   Model: Consent: participants must give free, informed consent and be able to withdraw; exclude anyone with a heart condition or on medication, with teacher and parent approval (1 + 1). Privacy: results must be anonymous or coded, not posted with names (1 + 1). Also accept: possible harm from caffeine or sugar (use a caffeine-free drink, screen participants). Classic mistake crossed: "ethics is only about animals".

Feedback lengths about 24 s (11b) and 22 s (11d) by script.

## Numbers or facts to verify

- **Osmosis (2 to 6).** Higher temperature increases the rate of osmosis (faster particle movement); larger cylinders have more surface and mass, so absolute mass change differs; surface water adds false mass. Potato tissue in pure water gains mass; in concentrated salt it loses mass. Changes of about 0.1 to 0.3 g for 2 to 3 g cylinders in 30 minutes are typical (illustrative, tagged). Correct.
- **Risk (8).** Cork borers and scalpels are the main hazard; dilute sodium chloride solutions are low hazard (eye irritation possible). Correct; matches the FA1 risk tables.
- **Ethics (9).** The 3Rs (replacement, reduction, refinement) are the basis of the Australian code for the care and use of animals for scientific purposes; human research requires voluntary, informed consent and privacy (National Statement on Ethical Conduct in Human Research). Neither document is named on screen. Correct.
- **No em dashes** in this file (searched by script).

## Memory aid check

- **"Who, how, when, why, agree?"** Assumption: a secondary source's reliability can be judged by its author's expertise, its method, its date, its purpose and its agreement with other sources. Limit: agreement can mislead if sources copy each other; "how" (method shown) is the strongest check. Correct for Year 11.
