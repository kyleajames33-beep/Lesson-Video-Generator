- **Entry knowledge (do not re-teach; recall in one clause each).** From C08: a weak acid ionises partly; the ICE table (c, 0, 0; −x, +x, +x; c − x, x, x); Ka = x²/(c − x); drop x when Ka is tiny next to c, so x = √(Ka × c); check x ÷ c under 5%, a rule of thumb; if the check fails, solve the full equation; x has the significant figures of Ka, so pH has that many decimal places. From C06: amphiprotic species give or take a proton depending on the partner; the proton chains H₂CO₃, HCO₃⁻, CO₃²⁻ and H₃PO₄, H₂PO₄⁻, HPO₄²⁻, PO₄³⁻; NaHCO₃ and KH₂PO₄ dissolve to give the amphiprotic anion with a spectator cation. ### 2b. q1-estimate (Ka and c given: the shortcut)
> K a and a concentration, so set up the ICE table: the acid goes down by x, and each ion goes up by x. K a is **tiny** next to zero point zero five zero zero, so drop the x from the bottom. Then x squared is K a times c, **one point five times ten to the minus nine**, and x is **three point nine times ten to the minus five** moles per litre.

Visual: the card shrinks to a strip. Type badge "Ka given: shortcut". "Ka(HOCl) = 3.0 × 10⁻⁸" and "0.0500 mol/L" underline amber; the check strip's "what data?" row lights. ### 3b. q2-check-fails (try the shortcut, and check it)

### 7. key-notes (reuse take: key-notes; quiet, about 60 seconds)
Heading on screen: "Key notes: what to write in your book".
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

1. Choose the method from the data: Ka and c → ICE table forwards; pH and c → ICE table backwards; an amphiprotic ion → compare Ka and Kb.
2. Forwards: x ≈ √(Ka × c), then check x ÷ c × 100% < 5%. If the check fails, keep the x: x² + Ka·x − Ka·c = 0, and take the positive root.
3. Backwards: [H₃O⁺] = 10⁻ᵖᴴ = [A⁻]; [HA] = c − [H₃O⁺]; Ka = [H₃O⁺]² ÷ [HA]; pKa = −log Ka. [H₃O⁺] is measured, so no 5% check is needed. The pKa is not the pH.
4. Amphiprotic ion: compare Ka (donating) with Kb = Kw ÷ Ka of the acid one proton up (accepting); the larger constant wins. HCO₃⁻ mainly accepts (NaHCO₃ basic); H₂PO₄⁻ mainly donates (KH₂PO₄ acidic).
5. Precision: forwards, x has the significant figures of Ka, and pH has that many decimal places; backwards, Ka has as many significant figures as the pH has decimal places.

Memory aid (large): *For an ion's Kb, use the Ka one proton up.* Second line: *Weak acid: x ≈ √(Ka × c), then check x < 5% of c.* Condition tag: "25 °C; Ka × Kb = Kw for a conjugate pair only".

- **"For an ion's Kb, use the Ka one proton up."** (new, not in the bank; flagged). Science: when an ion accepts a proton it becomes the species with one more proton, and that species and the ion are a conjugate pair, so Kb(ion) = Kw ÷ Ka(the species one proton up). Assumptions: 25 °C (Kw = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴); "up" means one more H⁺ in the proton chain as C06 drew it (H₂CO₃ above HCO₃⁻, H₃PO₄ above H₂PO₄⁻); the ion's own Ka describes its other reaction (donating). It holds for every amphiprotic ion in the course (HCO₃⁻, H₂PO₄⁻, HPO₄²⁻). Science check (21 Oct): confirmed; kept, with the conjugate-pair reason now spoken in 5b and 5d. Science check to confirm; if the reviewer prefers bank wording only, the fallback is the bank line "Ka × Kb = Kw for a conjugate pair" with the tag "accepting: pair with the acid it becomes".
