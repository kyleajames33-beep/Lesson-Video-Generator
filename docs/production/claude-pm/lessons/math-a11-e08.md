### 3. features of the log graph
> Now read off the features of y equals log base a of x, for a base bigger than one. It crosses the x-axis at one, zero, because the log of one is **zero**. It passes through a, one, because log base a of a is one, and through one over a, minus one. For base two, that's two, one and a half, minus one. There's a vertical asymptote, x equals zero, and the curve never reaches it. The domain is x greater than zero. That's the exponential's range, swapped over, and it's why there's no log of zero or a negative **number**. The range is all real y, which was the exponential's domain. As x heads down to zero, y heads to minus infinity. And as x grows, y grows too, but slowly. Saying the range is y greater than zero is the **slip**.

### 4. e to the x, and ln x
> The most important pair is e to the x, and the natural log of x. The natural log is log base e, so its graph is the reflection of e to the x in y equals **x**. Reflecting in that line swaps each point's x and y. The point zero, one on e to the x lands on one, zero. The point one, e lands on e, one. That's about two point seven one eight across, and one **up**. And the point on e to the x at height two, where x is the natural log of two, about zero point six nine, lands on the point two, natural log of **two**. So the graph of the natural log of x crosses the x-axis at one, has the y-axis as its asymptote, and passes through e, one. Its domain is x greater than zero, and it rises slowly for ever. Just how slowly? To reach a height of five, x has to get all the way to e to the five, which is about a hundred and forty-**eight**.

### 5. a base between zero and one

A half to the x falls, so its reflection falls as well. This log graph is **decreasing**. It still passes through one, zero, it still has the wall x equals zero, and its domain is still x greater than zero. When is the plain card worth fifty dollars? In a graphing app, slide the base through any value above one, or between zero and one, skipping one. The exponential and the log are always mirror images in y equals **x**. Saying every log graph rises is the **slip**.
