## Decisions

- **Scope and split (route entry bio-m5-b03a, stop rule "Analyse the required features and hormonal control using a scoped mammalian model").** The site lesson (`site-extracts/bio-m5-l04.md`) holds eight to ten ideas: gamete features, fertilisation, cleavage and the blastocyst, implantation, the placenta, embryo and fetus, five hormones (oestrogen, progesterone, hCG, oxytocin, prolactin), negative feedback during pregnancy, positive feedback at birth and lactation. Designed as one video it would need roughly 2,000 words or more, well over 10 minutes at the real rate with crowded boards, and it would repeat the B2b mistake of stuffing a lesson. **Proposed split:**
  - **Part 1 (this file): how a pregnancy is established and kept.** Fertilisation, the gametes' features, cleavage, the blastocyst, implantation, progesterone and the corpus luteum, hCG, the placenta taking over, placental exchange, embryo versus fetus. Ends with the placenta in charge.
  - **Part 2 (not designed here): how birth starts, and why labour speeds up.** Progesterone's negative feedback on FSH and LH during pregnancy (why ovulation stops), the oestrogen and oxytocin story at the end of pregnancy, the oxytocin and cervix positive feedback loop, why positive feedback is rare, prolactin and oxytocin in lactation, the site's 5-mark "negative and positive feedback" Explain model. Part 2 entry knowledge is this video. It needs the loop diagram and a second comparison; there is no room for it in part 1.
  - Both parts are the one route entry. The coverage ledger row for p1031 should read "part 1 designed" and not "covered" until both parts exist. (I have not edited the ledger or the route; Claude decides.)
- **One main question:** *A sperm has just joined an egg. What has to happen next before there is a pregnancy?* The tail is *and why would a tiny embryo need to send a message?* (the hormone a pregnancy test detects). The heading stays on the first question. The tail pays off in scene 6.
- **Hook choice.** The site opens on IVF success rates (HFEA 2021 figures, 23 percent and 6 percent). Those numbers are not verified here and the IVF statistics add nothing to the cause chain, so the hook uses the pregnancy test instead: a student has seen one, and "the embryo makes a chemical that is sent out" is a real surprise that the lesson explains. The site's IVF framing survives in attempt item one (a clinic case) and the site's "fertilisation is not the bottleneck" idea is carried by the main question.
- **Site alignment:** mirrors `site-extracts/bio-m5-l04.md` key ideas 1 to 3 and the first half of idea 4, and the Analyse model's timing spine ("each stage prepares a narrow window for the next"): sperm flagellum, mitochondria and acrosome; egg cytoplasm supporting early development; oviduct fertilisation; cleavage to blastocyst; implantation in the receptive endometrium; progesterone from the corpus luteum; hCG sustaining the corpus luteum so menstruation does not occur; the placenta taking over progesterone; placental exchange without direct blood mixing; embryo and fetus. I read "birth control" as "control of birth" and have not added contraception. If Claude wants it, it is a separate short video after part 2, not a part of this one. See doubts.
- **Scoped mammalian model: a human, with the numbers given for humans.** The video says "in a human" at the start and keeps every timing human. Other placental mammals follow the same pattern with different timings, and marsupials and monotremes differ (a short-lived placenta in marsupials, eggs in monotremes). None of that is in the learner copy; it is in the author notes. The Year 12 course and the site both use the human case.
- **Numbers for the Codex fact check (all human; all dates counted from fertilisation, not from the last period).** (1) The zygote reaches the blastocyst stage after about five days. (2) Implantation happens about a week after fertilisation (reported as roughly 6 to 10 days; "about a week" is spoken, no range). (3) The endometrium is receptive for only a few days. (4) With no pregnancy, the corpus luteum breaks down about two weeks after ovulation. (5) The placenta takes over most progesterone production over the first two to three months after fertilisation (the literature dates the corpus luteum's essential period to about weeks 7 to 9 from the last period, which is about 5 to 7 weeks after fertilisation). (6) Attempt item two: losing the corpus luteum three weeks after fertilisation likely ends the pregnancy, and losing it five months after fertilisation does not. (8) Before implantation the embryo lives on the egg's food store (the learner copy says only that; the embryo also takes in oviduct and uterine fluid). (9) Sperm structure (tail, mitochondria in the midpiece, acrosome enzymes) and the egg's food store. (10) A home pregnancy test detects hCG.

### 11. key notes (quiet, about 60 seconds)
- Fertilisation (a sperm joins an egg to make a zygote) usually happens in the oviduct. The zygote divides by cleavage as it moves toward the uterus and becomes a blastocyst, which implants in the uterine lining about a week after fertilisation. Fertilisation and implantation are different events, and a pregnancy begins at implantation.
- After the egg is released, the corpus luteum makes progesterone, which keeps the uterine lining thick and ready. With no pregnancy the corpus luteum breaks down about two weeks after ovulation and the lining is shed.
- After implantation the embryo's outer cells make hCG. hCG acts on the corpus luteum, so it keeps making progesterone. Progesterone acts on the lining.
- Over the first two to three months after fertilisation, the placenta takes over making progesterone. The placenta passes oxygen and nutrients from the mother's blood to the baby and carbon dioxide and wastes back, without the bloods mixing.
- A sperm has a tail to swim, mitochondria for energy and an acrosome with enzymes to get into the egg. The egg is large because it carries a food store for the early embryo.
- Memory aid (large): Progesterone is pro-gestation. hCG is the embryo's message to the corpus luteum. Rail: meet, divide, attach, signal, hand over.
