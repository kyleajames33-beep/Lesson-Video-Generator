### 3. convergent evolution
> Because they faced the same problem: moving between trees without climbing down, which costs energy and risks predators. In each lineage, some individuals had a little more loose skin between their legs, so they glided a bit further, survived more and bred more. Over many generations, selection built a flap in both. That's convergent evolution: distantly related lineages evolving similar traits because they face similar selective pressures.

> Features like the glider's flap and the thylacine's snout are called analogous structures. They do the same job and can look alike, but they evolved separately, so they don't share an origin. ### 10. how to tell which
> So when two species look alike, ask two questions. How closely are they related? And is the likeness only in the job, or in the underlying plan too? Distant relatives, same job, different plans: convergent. Close relatives, one shared plan, different jobs: divergent. CON-vergent: they converge from far apart. DI-vergent: they divide from one point.

### 14. key notes (quiet, about 60 seconds)
Heading on screen: "Key notes: what to write in your book".
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

- Convergent evolution: distantly related lineages evolve similar traits because they face similar selective pressures. Examples: sugar glider (marsupial) and flying squirrel (placental), gliding membranes; thylacine and grey wolf, skulls.
- Analogous structures: same job, separate origins (evidence of convergence). Looking alike does not show close relatedness.
- Divergent evolution: related lineages become different because they adapt to different environments. Example: Darwin's finches, different beaks from one ancestor.
- Homologous structures: same inherited plan, different jobs (evidence of divergence). Example: bat wing, whale flipper, cat foreleg: one upper bone, two lower bones, wrist bones, five digits.
- Bat and bird wings: homologous as forelimbs, analogous as wings. Plant: cactus spine and oak leaf are homologous (both leaves).
