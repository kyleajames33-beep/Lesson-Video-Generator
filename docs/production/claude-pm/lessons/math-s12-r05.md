### 2. what a scale drawing is
> A scale drawing shrinks every length by the same number, so the shape stays exactly the same, just smaller. That number is the scale. From map scales, you already know how to go from a real length to the drawing. Convert the real length to centimetres, then divide by the scale number. The hard part is choosing the scale, so that the drawing fits the **space**.

### 3. choosing a scale
> First, change the room's lengths to centimetres. Three point six metres is three hundred and sixty centimetres, and three metres is three **hundred**. Now ask, how many times must each length shrink to fit? The long side has ten centimetres, so three hundred and sixty divided by ten is thirty-six. The short side has eight, so three hundred divided by eight is thirty-seven point **five**. Both sides must fit, so the scale number must be at least the bigger one, thirty-seven point five. Use thirty-six, the smaller one, and the short side comes out eight point three centimetres, which spills off the **page**. So round up to a friendly scale, one that's easy to divide by, like one to fifty. One to forty would just fit too, but one to fifty is easier to divide by, and leaves a margin for **labels**. Rounding up shrinks the drawing a little more, so it still **fits**.

### 8. worked example: a bigger room
> Here's an exam-style one. A room is six metres by four metres. It must be drawn inside a box fifteen centimetres by ten centimetres. Choose a suitable scale, and give the room's dimensions on the plan. In centimetres, the room is six hundred by four **hundred**. Six hundred divided by fifteen is forty, and four hundred divided by ten is forty **too**. So one to forty would fill the box exactly, edge to edge. That leaves no room for labels, so go up to the next friendly scale, one to **fifty**. Six hundred divided by fifty is twelve, and four hundred divided by fifty is eight. So the room is twelve centimetres by eight on the **plan**, with a margin. Rounding down to one to thirty is the slip. That makes the room twenty centimetres long, too big for the **box**.

## Practice questions and model answers
