> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

## Numbers or facts to verify

Self-check (1 Nov) against standard references. The Codex science check must confirm each before voicing.
- **Division time (scene 1).** *E. coli* in rich medium at 37 °C divides about every 20 minutes; many bacteria are far slower. Spoken: "in ideal conditions some bacteria divide about every twenty minutes". Correct.
- **Size (scene 1 tag).** A typical rod-shaped bacterium is about 1 to 5 micrometres long. Grey tag: "a few micrometres long". Correct.
- **Capsule (scene 3).** Usually polysaccharide (some are polypeptide, for example *Bacillus anthracis*); functions: resistance to drying, adherence (including dental plaque biofilms), evasion of phagocytosis. Spoken: "mostly made of sugars"; "the film that builds up on teeth". Correct at this level.
- **Cell wall (scene 4).** Peptidoglycan: glycan chains cross-linked by short peptides; maintains shape and resists osmotic lysis; a damaged wall leads to lysis in a dilute surrounding. Spoken: "sugar chains joined by short chains of amino acids". Correct. "when water moves in" is stated without naming osmosis (v09 to v11).
- **Cell membrane (scene 5).** Phospholipid bilayer, partially permeable; in aerobic bacteria the electron transport chain enzymes are in the cell membrane (glycolysis and the Krebs cycle enzymes are in the cytoplasm). Spoken: "many of the enzymes that release energy in respiration sit right in the membrane". Correct (hedged "many").
- **Cytoplasm and size (scene 6).** Mostly water with dissolved substances; most metabolic reactions occur there; small size means short diffusion distances. Correct.
- **Nucleoid (scene 7).** Usually a single circular chromosome, not membrane-bound, carrying essential genes (site error 1 notes exceptions). The tag "unfolded, it is about a thousand times longer than the cell": the *E. coli* chromosome is about 1.5 mm against a cell about 2 micrometres long (roughly 750 times). Codex to confirm or change the tag to "hundreds of times longer". Spoken: "one long, circular chromosome". Correct at this level.
- **Plasmids (scene 8).** Small, usually circular, replicate independently, often carry resistance genes, usually non-essential, transferable between cells. Spoken: "can usually live without its plasmids". Correct.
- **Ribosomes (scene 9).** 70S in prokaryotes, 80S in the eukaryotic cytoplasm; no ribosome is membrane-bound. "thousands": an *E. coli* cell has thousands to tens of thousands. Correct.
- **Flagella (scene 10).** A helical filament rotated by a motor in the cell envelope (anchored through membrane and wall); propels the cell; chemotaxis towards nutrients. Number varies; some have none. Correct.
- **Pili (scene 11).** Attachment pili (fimbriae) grip surfaces and cells; sex pili join cells for conjugation, transferring a copy of a plasmid while the donor keeps its own. Spoken: "Some pili do something more surprising." Correct.
- **Attempt 2 biology.** Conjugative resistance plasmids transfer between cells of the same species in mixed culture over hours; recipients become resistant. Tetracycline resistance genes on plasmids are common. Correct.
