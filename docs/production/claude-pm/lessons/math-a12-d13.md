- **Anchor: a drone's camera tilting forward (painted plates, live).** A drone hovers 1 unit above flat ground, and its camera tilts forward from pointing straight down at a steady rate. With the tilt θ radians from straight down, the spot it films is tan θ ahead along the ground. The spot's speed is a composite rate with two factors from two places:
  - **the outside's slope at the inside (teal)**: how far the spot moves for each radian of tilt, sec²θ, read at the current tilt;
  - **the inside's slope (blue)**: how fast the tilt itself grows, in radians per second;
  - **spot speed (violet)** = sec²θ × the tilt rate. Tilt twice as fast and the spot moves twice as fast at the same tilt; at a steeper tilt each radian moves the spot further;
  - **the inside decides the domain**: once the tilt reaches π/2 the camera looks at the horizon and the spot is gone; a faster tilt reaches that wall sooner.
  The anchor appears in scene 1 (narrated) as the picture of "two factors from two places", returns as the tan row's meaning in scene 3 (narrated), sits as a thumbnail beside scene 2's two factors and scene 5's walls (tags), and is the badge on the memory card.
- **Why this anchor.** The lesson's message is that every composite keeps two separate factors (the outside's slope at the inside and the inside's slope) and that the inside decides the domain. A tilting camera makes both physical, with a tan outside that is in this lesson's slot table. No pool context gives 3^(2x) itself a natural everyday meaning (compound growth with a doubled clock would repeat d02's savings or d03's app users), so the hook's measurement stays on the graph and the drone carries the two-factor idea beside it. Everyday, not science; differs from d01 to d12 (d11's lighthouse beam is a single angle with no rate of turning; here the inside's rate is the point) and from d14 (headlights).

### 3. the three outsides
