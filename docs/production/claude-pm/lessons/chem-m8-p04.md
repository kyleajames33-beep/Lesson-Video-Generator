## Decisions

- **Main question:** *Your eye can rank two blue solutions, but it can't tell you how much copper is in either. How do you make a set of copper(II) standards, turn their absorbances into a calibration line you can trust, and use it to find the copper in a sample you don't know?* Heading on screen: "Calibration practical: from standards to an unknown copper(II) concentration".
- **Why copper(II), and why a spectrophotometer:** the route review names Cu²⁺; copper(II) sulfate is blue, cheap, stable in solution, and its colour comes from the hydrated copper(II) ion itself, so no colour reagent is needed (g02 Q2 then handles a colour reagent and its blank). A UV-Vis spectrophotometer lets the video show a wavelength scan and choose the peak, which a fixed-filter colourimeter cannot; the narration and a tag say a school colourimeter can't reach 800 nm, so it uses its red filter (for example 635 nm, Vernier COL-BTA manual) with its own calibration, never this slope (author note b; Revision 2). The wavelength choice is new here: chem-m5-p02 chose blue light for a red ion; p04 shows *why the peak*: the biggest change in absorbance for each change in concentration.
- **Why a sixth standard that bends:** the route stop says "evaluate an unknown result", and the site's range and dilution fixture says "never extrapolate". A standard above the linear range that falls below the line gives the linear range as *evidence* from the student's own data, rather than a rule to memorise, and it explains why the strong unknown is diluted. The undiluted unknown's reading (1.52) would, by extending the line, give 0.128 mol/L, lower than the diluted result (0.142 mol/L): the same direction as the bending standard (author note c). The narration does not compute the extended value; the screen shows it once, crossed, in scene 7.
- **Why a check standard:** accuracy needs a true value. The teacher makes a 0.0500 mol/L check standard from a separate weighing of copper(II) sulfate pentahydrate (not from the same stock), so it tests the whole method, including the stock. It links back to p02's "test the method on a sample you already know" in one line.

### 2. the aim and the variables
> The aim is to find the concentration of copper ions in that rinse water. Each solution whose concentration you know is called a standard. ### 4. which light, and the blank

### 5. measure carefully

### 18. key notes (quiet, hold about 60 seconds)
Heading on screen: "Key notes: what to write in your book".
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

1. Standards by dilution: c(standard) = c(stock) × V(stock) ÷ V(total). Example: 3.00 mL of 0.200 mol/L stock + 7.00 mL water → 0.0600 mol/L. Rinse each burette with the solution it will hold.
2. Set the wavelength where the solution absorbs most (copper(II): about 800 nm, near infrared, on a spectrophotometer; a school colourimeter's red filter, for example 635 nm, with its own calibration). Zero with a blank of the same distilled water and plot it as a point at zero.

5. Reliable: repeat readings agree. Accurate: a check standard reads close to its known value. Valid: only copper absorbs at that wavelength (filter a cloudy sample); same wavelength, cuvette and blank. Copper waste goes into heavy-metal waste, never the sink.
