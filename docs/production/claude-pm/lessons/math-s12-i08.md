### 2. each one in plain words
> First, what each one is. A savings account lets you take money out any time, but its rate is variable, which means the bank can change it, and bonus rates often come with **conditions**. ### 3. four measures
> To compare them, use four measures. Return, what it's likely to earn. Risk, how likely you are to lose some of the money you put in. Access, how quickly it turns back into cash. And cost to enter, what you pay just to **start**. Savings accounts have a lower return, no risk to your money, instant access, and no cost to enter. Term deposits pay a bit more and are safe, but you can't get at the money. Shares can earn more, with real risk. Property can earn rent and growth, but it's slow to sell and costs a lot to enter. Notice that no column decides on its **own**.

### 4. a calculation: savings account or term deposit?
> Here's where the maths helps. Fifty thousand dollars isn't needed for five years. A savings account pays five point two per cent a year, and a five-year term deposit pays five point six, both compounded annually. These are example rates, and rates change every year. The savings account grows to fifty thousand times one point zero five two to the five, sixty-four thousand four hundred and twenty-four dollars and fifteen **cents**. The term deposit grows to sixty-five thousand six hundred and fifty-eight dollars and twenty-nine **cents**. That's one thousand two hundred and thirty-four dollars and fourteen cents more, because this term deposit's rate is higher and fixed. Term deposits often pay more for money that's locked away, but not always. Some savings accounts pay bonus rates that match them, so always compare the actual **rates**.

### 5. a cost to enter: transfer duty
> Property's cost to enter is easy to underestimate. In New South Wales, buying property means paying transfer duty, a government charge, sometimes called stamp duty. For a price between three hundred and eighty-seven thousand and one point two nine million, it's eleven thousand six hundred and two dollars, plus four dollars fifty for every hundred dollars over three hundred and eighty-seven thousand. Duty changes every year, and these are the 2026-27 rates. For a six hundred and fifty thousand dollar unit, the part over is two hundred and sixty-three thousand dollars. That's two thousand six hundred and thirty lots of a hundred, at four fifty, eleven thousand eight hundred and thirty-five. Add eleven thousand six hundred and two, and the duty is twenty-three thousand four hundred and thirty-seven **dollars**. Charging four and a half per cent on the whole price is the **slip**.
