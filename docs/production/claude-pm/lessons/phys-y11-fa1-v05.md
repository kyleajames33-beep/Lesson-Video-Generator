## Decisions

- **Learner question kept (route).** "How do instantaneous and average speed and velocity differ?" The answer, built in order: both averages divide by the same time, but speed puts the distance on top and velocity puts the displacement on top, so a trip that reverses or returns gives two different averages (hook, 2 to 4); an instantaneous value describes one moment, which is what a speedometer shows (5); it is measured over a time interval so short that the speed hardly changes in it (6, 7); instantaneous velocity is that speed plus the direction of motion at that moment, so it can change even when the speed doesn't (5, 8).
- **The hook surprise (site hook, kept with its numbers).** A car laps a 2.0 km track in 80 s and finishes where it started: average speed 25 m/s, average velocity zero. "Finishes back at the start line" replaces the site's "returns to the start" so scene 8 can use a flying lap at a steady 25 m/s (author note a).
- **The cause made visible (owner quality bar).** On the lap, an amber path trace grows with an odometer readout while a navy displacement arrow from the start line stretches and then shrinks to nothing at the finish; the clock runs under both. The student sees why one top line keeps growing and the other cancels. The straight-road example repeats it: the odometer keeps counting on the drive back west while the arrow shortens.
- **Worked example (site `we-1`, same numbers, object changed).** The site's cyclist becomes a car (owner rule: no people). 300 m east, then 100 m west, 40 s in total; east positive; distance 400 m, displacement +200 m; average speed 10 m/s, average velocity 5.0 m/s east. **Precision:** the givens are treated as two significant figures (stated on screen as a grey tag "data to 2 significant figures"), so both answers are given to two significant figures, matching the site's "10" and "5.0".

### 2. two averages, one time
> Both averages divide by the same time, eighty seconds. The difference is what goes on top. Average speed uses the distance, the whole length of road the car covers. So it's two thousand metres over eighty seconds, twenty-five metres per **second**. Average velocity uses the displacement, the straight arrow from start to finish. As the car comes back around, that arrow shrinks, and at the finish it's **gone**. That's why speed is a scalar, just a size, and velocity is a vector, a size with a **direction**. In symbols, v av equals delta s over delta t, where delta means "change in", so delta s is the change in **position**.

### 4. worked example: the calculation
> The distance is three hundred plus one hundred, four hundred **metres**. The displacement is plus three hundred plus minus one hundred, which is plus two hundred metres, so two hundred metres **east**. Both get divided by the same forty seconds. Average speed is four hundred over forty, ten metres per **second**. Average velocity is two hundred over forty, five point oh metres per second, **east**. It's smaller, because the drive back west undid part of the displacement. The distance kept counting up, but the arrow got **shorter**. They're the same size only for straight-line motion with no turning **back**.

### 5. instantaneous speed and velocity
> So what does a speedometer show? Not either average. It shows how fast the car is going right now, at one instant. That's called instantaneous **speed**. The average, ten metres per second, is one number for the whole trip. Instantaneous velocity is that speed, plus the direction the car is moving at that instant. Heading east at twelve point five, the velocity is plus twelve point five metres per second. Heading west at ten, the speedometer still reads ten, but the velocity is minus **ten**.
