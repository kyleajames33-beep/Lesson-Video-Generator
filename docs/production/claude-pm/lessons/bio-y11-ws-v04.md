### 2. select what the question needs
> First, select. A results page from a transpiration study might list the date, the temperature, the humidity, the wind setting, the bubble distances and a note about a leak. If your question is about wind, pick out the wind setting and the rates. Keep the note about the leak, because it affects which readings you trust.

On "date", "temperature", "humidity", "wind setting", "bubble distances" and "a note about a leak" each column head takes a dark-ink tick in turn. On "Keep the note about the leak" the notes line takes a violet ring and joins the clean table, violet hand tag "affects trust". The "select" chip lights. Hold one second.

### 3. a distance isn't a volume, and a prediction
> Now convert. The bubble moves along a narrow tube, so a distance isn't a volume. The volume is the tube's cross-section area times the distance. If the inside radius is half a millimetre, the area is pi times zero point five squared, about zero point seven nine square millimetres. Prediction: how many microlitres a minute is eight millimetres a minute?

Visual: board change (the tube, solid in one move): `m8-potometer-v1.png` small at the left; at the centre a coded enlarged length of capillary tube (horizontal) with a scale in mm and the bubble at 0; a coded end-on circle beside it. On "the bubble moves along a narrow tube" the bubble slides 8 mm along the scale, readout (52 px) "distance: 8 mm in 1 min". On "cross-section area times the distance" a teal line writes (38 px): "volume = area × distance". On "inside radius is half a millimetre" the end-on circle takes a radius line labelled "r = 0.5 mm"; on "about zero point seven nine square millimetres" the working writes (52 px): "A = π × 0.5² ≈ 0.79 mm²". On "Prediction" a "Predict" chip writes; **a 3 s countdown ring** runs.

### 4. the conversion
> Eight times zero point seven nine is about six point three cubic millimetres a minute. And one cubic millimetre is one microlitre, so that's about six point three microlitres a minute. For an hour, multiply by sixty: about three hundred and eighty microlitres, or zero point three eight millilitres. Distance isn't volume: times the area.

Visual: the tube stays. On "Eight times zero point seven nine" the working line writes (52 px): "8 mm/min × 0.79 mm² ≈ 6.3 mm³/min"; the tube between 0 and 8 mm fills with a teal slug, labelled "6.3 mm³". On "one cubic millimetre is one microlitre" a teal gloss (30 px): "1 mm³ = 1 µL"; the line continues "≈ 6.3 µL/min". ## Practice question and feedback

1. (11a, calculate, 3 marks; original.) 15 mm in 5 min; r = 0.5 mm; 1 mm³ = 1 µL.
   Model: rate = 15 ÷ 5 = 3 mm/min (1); area = π × 0.5² ≈ 0.785 mm² (1); volume rate = 3 × 0.785 ≈ 2.4 mm³/min = 2.4 µL/min (1). Classic mistake crossed: "3 µL/min".
2. (11c, select and justify, 3 marks; original.) Five species; four wind speeds.
   Model: species: bar chart, because species are discrete categories with no values between them (1.5); wind speed: line graph with wind speed on the horizontal axis, because wind speed is continuous and the line shows the trend between measured points (1.5). Classic mistake crossed: a line joining species.

Feedback lengths about 24 s (11b) and 19 s (11d) by script.
