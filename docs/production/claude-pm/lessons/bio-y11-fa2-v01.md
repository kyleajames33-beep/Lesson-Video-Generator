### 8. writing a compare answer
> When a question asks you to compare, use the same criteria for all three levels, not three separate stories. Start with what they share: all are made of cells, each with a membrane, cytoplasm and DNA. ### 11. key notes (quiet, about 60 seconds)
Heading on screen: "Key notes: what to write in your book".
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

- Unicellular: the whole organism is one generalist cell that does every job of life. Paramecium: cilia (move, feed), contractile vacuole (pumps out water). Amoeba: pseudopodia (move, engulf food).
- Colonial: similar cells living together; each is still a generalist and, in simple colonies such as Gonium, can survive alone and start a new colony. Volvox is a large colony near the line: a few cells specialise in reproduction.
- Multicellular: many specialised cells (built for one job), which are interdependent (rely on each other to survive). Example: the red blood cell has no nucleus and is full of haemoglobin, but cannot feed, pump out water or divide.
- The trade: each cell gives up independence for specialisation. The test is separating the cells, not counting them.

## Numbers or facts to verify

Self-check (2 Nov). The Codex science check must confirm each before voicing.
- **Paramecium (scenes 1, 2).** Paramecium caudatum is about 0.2 to 0.3 mm long; tag "about 0.2 mm long". It has two contractile vacuoles that expel water entering by osmosis in fresh water; cilia move it and sweep food into the oral groove. Correct.
- **Red blood cell in pond water (scenes 1, 3).** Pond water is strongly hypotonic to red cells; water enters by osmosis and the cell swells and lyses (FA1 v11 says "may burst"; here "swells and bursts" describes fresh water, which is dilute enough to lyse red cells within seconds to minutes). Mature human red cells have no nucleus, contain haemoglobin, are biconcave discs, cannot divide and have no contractile vacuole. "In your body it never needs them, because the blood plasma around it is kept at just the right concentration": plasma osmolarity is regulated (kidneys); correct at this level. Flag "bursts" for the science check against FA1 v11's "may burst" ruling: in fresh water, lysis is the expected result.
- **Amoeba (scene 2).** Amoeba proteus is up to about 0.5 to 0.7 mm; pseudopodia for movement and phagocytosis. Correct. The plate is the first frame of B2c's fission sequence; Codex confirms it reads as an ordinary amoeba (no dividing nucleus visible).
- **About 30 trillion cells (scene 4).** Sender, Fuchs and Milo (2016, PLOS Biology) estimate about 3.0 × 10¹³ human cells in a reference adult (red blood cells about 84 per cent by number). "Nearly all of them are specialists": true (most are differentiated; stem cells are a small fraction). Correct with "about".
- **Specialised cells (scene 4).** Nerve cells carry signals; smooth muscle cells contract; liver cells store glucose as glycogen. The liver cell's supply: oxygen carried by red blood cells, nutrients absorbed by the gut, and the kidneys regulating the water and salt content of blood. Correct.
- **Gonium (scene 5).** Gonium pectorale: a flat plate of 16 (also 8 or 4) cells in a jelly matrix; each cell is biflagellate with a cup-shaped chloroplast; during asexual reproduction every cell divides to form a daughter colony. Cells are not differentiated. About 0.05 to 0.1 mm across ("about 0.1 mm"). Whether an isolated Gonium cell survives and forms a colony: each cell is capable of forming a new colony; the science check to confirm the wording "Separate them, and each cell can divide and build a new colony".
- **Volvox (scenes 5, 6).** Colonies of about 500 to 50,000 cells ("hundreds to thousands"); somatic cells biflagellate with a chloroplast and eyespot, connected by cytoplasmic bridges in many species; flagella beat in a coordinated way and the colony rolls and swims towards light (phototaxis); a few large gonidia form daughter colonies inside. Somatic cells in Volvox carteri do not divide and die after the daughter colonies are released. Correct; site error 1.
- **Nerve cell up to a metre (scene 7).** Motor neurons from the lower spinal cord to the foot have axons about 1 m long in adults. "can stretch up to a metre". Correct.
- **Muscle fibres (scene 7).** Muscle cells contain protein filaments (actin and myosin) that slide to pull. "packed with protein fibres that pull" is a simplification at Year 11 level. Correct.
- **All cells share a membrane, cytoplasm and DNA (scene 8).** True of living cells; mature red cells have lost their nucleus and DNA (a known exception; the board says "all: cells with membrane, cytoplasm, DNA" about the three organism levels; the science check to judge whether the red cell exception needs a grey tag; Open decision 3).

- **Attempt 2 plausibility.** Mammalian cell culture: 37 °C, a medium with glucose, amino acids, salts at physiological osmolarity, and gas exchange (oxygen; usually 5 per cent carbon dioxide). "glucose, oxygen and salts at the same concentration as blood" is a simplification for the stem. Correct.
