## Decisions

- **Learner question (route placeholder rewritten; root to adopt).** "Why did Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples use inclined planes to move heavy objects?" It is the syllabus question in causal form ("Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander Peoples" is the syllabus's own wording; spoken and written in this video as "Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples", which the dot point allows). The answer, built in order: a straight lift needs the whole weight (2); on a smooth ramp the ramp holds the squash part and you supply only the slide part, mg sin θ (3); in numbers, about a quarter of the force at 15° (4); the weight is unchanged, the load is shared (5, 6); the price is distance: gentler means smaller force over a longer path, with force × distance the same (7); friction adds to the push (8); so a ramp turns an impossible lift into a sustainable push for a small group, with less strain and risk (9); this is knowledge built by observation and testing over many generations, which the equation later described (10).

### 2. lifting straight up
> To lift the stone at a steady speed, the forces on it must balance. So the upward pull has to match the whole weight, m g. For a fifty kilogram stone, that's fifty times nine point eight: four hundred and ninety **newtons**, all of it, the whole way **up**. That's a big force to supply at once, and to keep supplying.

### 3. on the ramp
> Now put the stone on a smooth ramp. Split its weight, as we did before. The squash part, m g cos theta, presses into the ramp, and the ramp pushes straight back, so you don't have to hold that part up. Only the slide part, m g sin theta, pulls the stone back down the slope. So to push it up at a steady speed, you need only m g sin **theta**.

### 9. why inclined planes were used
> This is why Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples used inclined planes to move heavy objects, such as large stones and timber. A ramp or sloping path turns a lift that's too heavy for anyone into a push that a small group can keep up. Less force at any moment means less strain and less risk of injury, and loads that couldn't be lifted at all could still be **moved**. And because a gentler slope needs less force, the slope could be chosen to suit the load and the people moving it.

## Practice questions and model answers

**Attempt 1 (scene 12; original wording, HSC style, 3 marks suggested, not an official marking scheme).** "A 120 kg stone block must be raised onto a bank 0.80 m high. Take g = 9.8 m s⁻². Model answer: (a) At steady speed the forces balance: F = mg = 120 × 9.8 = 1176 N = 1.18 × 10³ N. **Attempt 2 (scene 14; original wording, HSC style, 4 marks suggested).** "A 200 kg stone is dragged at a steady speed up a slope inclined at 10.0° to the horizontal, by a rope parallel to the slope. Model answer: (a) At steady speed the forces along the slope balance: the rope force equals the slide part plus friction: F = mg sin θ + f = 200 × 9.8 × sin 10.0° + 300 = 340 + 300 = 640 N.
