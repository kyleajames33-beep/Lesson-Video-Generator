### 5. the red cell's shape, and what it threw away
> Now the shape. A red cell is biconcave, dented in on both sides. That gives it a large surface for its volume, and no part of the inside is far from the surface, so oxygen gets in and out fast. Red cells threw out the nucleus to fit more haemoglobin in. And they're flexible. They bend and fold to squeeze through capillaries narrower than themselves.

### 8. platelets, the pieces that plug
> The rest of that pale layer is platelets, and they're not even cells. They're tiny fragments, pinched off a giant cell in the bone marrow, with no nucleus. Being so small, there are huge numbers of them, so they gather fast. When a vessel is torn, platelets stick to the damaged wall and to each other, and plug the gap. They also release chemicals that start a chain of reactions in the plasma. A dissolved plasma protein turns into long threads, called fibrin, that trap red cells and form a clot. ## Numbers or facts to verify

Self-check (2 Nov). The Codex science check must confirm each before voicing.
- **Centrifuge layers (2).** Plasma about 55 per cent of blood volume, red cells about 45 per cent (haematocrit roughly 40 to 50 per cent), a buffy coat of white cells and platelets under 1 per cent. Correct as "about".
- **Blood as a tissue (2).** Blood is classed as a (fluid) connective tissue. Correct.
- **Plasma composition (3).** About 90 to 92 per cent water; carries glucose, amino acids, ions, hormones, carbon dioxide (most of it as hydrogencarbonate ions), urea and plasma proteins (albumin, clotting proteins such as fibrinogen and some globulins, mostly made in the liver; antibodies are made by white cells, so "proteins made in the liver" is worded as some of the proteins, not all); distributes heat. Correct as worded ("along with proteins made in the liver").
- **Haemoglobin (4).** Each red cell holds about 270 million haemoglobin molecules (so "millions" is safe); haemoglobin is an iron-containing protein; it loads oxygen in the lungs and unloads it in respiring tissues. Correct. The red colour comes from the iron-containing haem group. Iron-deficient cells contain less haemoglobin, look paler (hypochromic) and carry less oxygen. Correct.
- **Biconcave shape (5).** The biconcave disc has a larger surface area than a sphere of the same volume and a short diffusion distance to the centre. Correct. Mature mammalian red cells lack a nucleus and mitochondria, and respire anaerobically (glycolysis), so they do not consume the oxygen they carry. Correct. They deform through capillaries narrower than their resting diameter (about 7.5 µm). Correct.
- **Lifespan and production (6).** About 120 days; the bone marrow produces about 2 million red cells per second in an adult (commonly cited 2 to 3 million). Confirm "about two million every second". Without a nucleus a red cell cannot synthesise new proteins (it cannot transcribe new mRNA), repair itself or divide. Correct.
- **White cells (7).** Nucleated; make enzymes (for example in neutrophil granules) and, in B lymphocytes and plasma cells, antibodies; leave capillaries by squeezing between endothelial cells (diapedesis); neutrophils and macrophages engulf microbes (phagocytosis). Ratio about 1 white cell to 500 to 1,000 red cells, so "hundreds of red cells for every white one" is safe. White cells are larger than red cells (most 10 to 15 µm). Correct. `m7-macrophage-engulf-digest-v1.png` shows a macrophage (a white cell that has entered tissue); labelled "a white cell engulfing a bacterium", acceptable.
- **Platelets (8).** Fragments of megakaryocytes in the bone marrow, about 2 to 3 µm, no nucleus, about 150 to 400 thousand per microlitre; at damage they adhere to the exposed wall and to each other (platelet plug) and release chemicals that, with plasma clotting factors, lead to thrombin converting fibrinogen into insoluble fibrin threads that trap cells. Correct as simplified ("start a chain of reactions in the plasma").
- **Low platelets (10c, 10d).** Thrombocytopenia causes prolonged bleeding and easy bruising (and small skin bleeds). Correct as a prediction; no medical advice.
