## Decisions

- **Main question:** *Fish in a river die at first light, though a reading at noon looked fine. How much oxygen is in the water, how do chemists measure it, and how fast will it be used up?* Heading on screen: "Oxygen now, oxygen later: dissolved oxygen and BOD".
- **Australian hook, cited and dated (author note a).** The March 2023 fish deaths in the Darling-Baaka River at Menindee: millions of fish (estimates vary, from several million to about 20 to 30 million in press reports); the NSW Chief Scientist and Engineer's independent review (September 2023) found hypoxia, low dissolved oxygen, the most likely immediate cause; the NSW DPI Fisheries submission to the review reported large daily swings in dissolved oxygen from heavy algal growth, with the largest die-offs at first light, and warm water in the days before. The narration says "millions", "most likely cause" and "around first light". This replaces the site's Hawkesbury-Nepean 2007 hook, whose figures have no source (site correction 1).
- **DO is a snapshot; BOD is a forecast (site aid, pack section 2).** The site's own framing ("Dissolved oxygen gives a snapshot. BOD gives a forecast") carries the video, made concrete by the c05 48-hour trace (noon high, dawn low) and the Menindee first-light deaths.
- **Two ways to measure DO.** A DO meter (a probe in the water, fast, can log all night; it must be calibrated; author note b) and the Winkler titration (slower, chemical, often used to check the meters). The site's "DO meter measures electrochemically" is not repeated (many field probes are optical; site correction 5).
- **Winkler in cause order, three steps, one per scene (pack question 7 answered: "Is Winkler the main calculation or an application after named analyses?").** Winkler is the main calculation, taught as a redox chain whose purpose is to turn invisible oxygen into something a burette can measure. Step 1, fix: manganese(II) sulfate and alkaline iodide are added; a pale manganese(II) hydroxide forms, and dissolved oxygen oxidises it to a brown solid, MnO(OH)₂, trapping the oxygen. Step 2, acidify: sulfuric acid dissolves the brown solid, and the manganese in it oxidises iodide to iodine (yellow-brown). Step 3, titrate: thiosulfate reduces the iodine back to iodide; starch near the end turns blue-black and the endpoint is the moment the blue disappears. The product is written MnO(OH)₂ throughout (site correction 2: the site writes MnO₂). Each equation sits on screen on its step; the narration names species in words.
- **The ratio chain, built from the equations.** 1 O₂ fixes 2 Mn (2Mn(OH)₂ + O₂ → 2MnO(OH)₂); each MnO(OH)₂ frees one I₂, so 2 I₂; each I₂ takes 2 S₂O₃²⁻, so 4 S₂O₃²⁻. The ratio is shown as a coded ladder (1 → 2 → 2 → 4) built one rung per clause, so students see where the 4 comes from rather than memorising it. The site's common error ("thiosulfate titrates oxygen directly") is said positively and its crossed chip is "1 : 1" (pack trap).
- **Sampling conditions, kept in learner copy.** No air bubbles and fix at once: air contains oxygen, and a bubble or a delay changes the oxygen being measured. This is a necessary condition (leaving it out would teach that any sample works), so it stays in the narration (scene 4).
- **The worked Winkler calculation: the site's own (L07 Worked Example 1).** 100.0 mL sample; 8.00 mL of 0.0100 mol/L sodium thiosulfate; n = 8.00 × 10⁻⁵ mol; ÷ 4 = 2.00 × 10⁻⁵ mol O₂; × 32.00 g/mol = 6.40 × 10⁻⁴ g = 0.640 mg in 100.0 mL; 6.40 mg/L. Staged as c01: task, equation strip (the ratio chain), grouped givens with units; stage 1 moles of thiosulfate; stage 2 ratio then mass; stage 3 per litre. Grams to milligrams and millilitres to litres are written as their own lines (pack trap: "mg/g and mL/L conversions lost"). Meaning: air-saturated water at 20 °C contains about 9.1 mg/L (c05 grey bar), so 6.40 mg/L is about 70% of air saturation, if the water was at 20 °C (screen only in scene 11 from Revision 2; water can be supersaturated by photosynthesis, so this is not a ceiling).
- **BOD₅ conditions, each with its reason (pack question 7: "Which BOD sample conditions will be explicit?").** Two bottles filled from the same sample; the DO of one is measured at once; the other is sealed and kept in the dark at 20 °C for five days. Sealed: no new oxygen gets in, so any drop is oxygen used. Dark: algae can't photosynthesise and add oxygen (pack trap: an illuminated bottle). 20 °C: every lab runs the test at the same temperature, so results compare. Five days: the standard time. Explicit on screen; all four reasons spoken in one scene. Dilution of strong samples and seeding are a grey tag only (author note e; pack: "simple BOD subtraction applies to undiluted, unseeded examples").
- **The worked BOD: the site's own (L07 Worked Example 2).** 8.4 − 4.9 = 3.5 mg/L. Interpretation in plain words: microbes in a litre of this water used 3.5 mg of oxygen in five days breaking down organic matter. The site's categories (below 2 clean, 2 to 8 moderate, above 8 heavy) are shown as a grey band labelled "a teaching guide, not a legal limit" (site L07's own caveat; pack). The narration says "moderate on this card's teaching guide" (Revision 2; the science check found no evidence that many courses use these exact thresholds). Zones read "under 2", "2 to under 8", "8 or more", so the boundaries are unambiguous.
- **Limits of BOD (route stop: "its monitoring limits").** BOD measures only oxygen used by microbes on biodegradable organic matter (site L07 "Critical limitation"); a low BOD doesn't prove water safe (BOD doesn't identify metals, pesticides or disease-causing microbes; pack trap "BOD assumed to detect every pollutant"); a toxic sample can poison the microbes and make BOD read falsely low (author note f); five days is slow, so it can't warn about tonight (the DO probe does that). Carried as three short lines on screen; two spoken.
- **Memory aid (bank, Mnemonic, checked).** The bank's "High BOD means bad water" is not used as worded, because a low BOD can also be bad water (the limit above). Used instead: "DO is oxygen now. BOD is oxygen used later." (the site's snapshot and forecast aid in the bank's plain style) and, for the calculation, "One oxygen, four thiosulfates." (author-written from the ratio chain; Memory aid check).

> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

1. Dissolved oxygen (DO) is the O₂ gas dissolved in water (mg/L). Warm water holds less. DO swings between day and night, so one reading is a snapshot. Measure with a DO meter (fast, can log) or a Winkler titration.
