### 1. hook: flip the ratios
> Here's tangent racing up near ninety degrees, because its bottom, the adjacent side, is shrinking. Every ratio we've used is one side over another. So let's flip them. Opposite over hypotenuse becomes hypotenuse over opposite. Adjacent over hypotenuse becomes hypotenuse over adjacent. And opposite over adjacent becomes adjacent over **opposite**. These flipped ratios turn up in real lengths. Here's a backyard flying fox. Its cable runs from a platform three metres up, down to the ground at an angle theta. The cable is the hypotenuse, and the platform's height is the opposite side, so the cable's length is three times hypotenuse over **opposite**, because multiplying the opposite side by hypotenuse over opposite gives the hypotenuse. So what are these flipped ratios, and what happens to them at zero and ninety degrees?

### 2. three names, three pairs
> Each flip has a name. Hypotenuse over opposite is the cosecant of theta, written cosec. Hypotenuse over adjacent is the secant, written sec. And adjacent over opposite is the cotangent, written **cot**. Each one is the reciprocal of an old ratio, which just means one over it. Sine is opposite over hypotenuse, so cosecant is one over sine. Cosine is adjacent over hypotenuse, so secant is one over cosine. And cotangent is one over **tangent**. To keep the pairs straight, notice that each pair has exactly one co. Sine pairs with cosecant, cosine with secant, and tangent with cotangent. Pairing secant with sine is the **slip**.

### 3. worked example: the flying fox

### 4. exact values at 30°, 45° and 60°
> Now all nine exact values, read straight from the two special triangles. In the half-square, the sides are one, one and root two. So at forty-five degrees, secant and cosecant are both root two over one, root **two**, and cotangent is one over one, which is one. In the half equilateral triangle, the sides are one, root three and two. At sixty degrees, the adjacent side is one, so secant sixty degrees is two over one, **two**. Cosecant sixty degrees is two over root three, and rationalised, that's two root three over **three**. And cotangent sixty degrees is one over root three, which is root three over three. At thirty degrees the sides swap roles, so secant thirty degrees is two root three over three, cosecant thirty degrees is two, and cotangent thirty degrees is root **three**. Each new row is an old row flipped, cell by cell. Writing cosecant thirty degrees as a half is the **slip**. That's sine thirty degrees.

### 5. sneaking up on zero degrees
> What about zero degrees? There's no triangle there, so let's sneak up on it. Fix the hypotenuse at one, and let the angle shrink. The triangle flattens. The adjacent side stretches towards one, and the opposite side shrinks towards **zero**. Secant is one over the adjacent side, so it settles at **one**. But cosecant and cotangent both have the opposite side on the bottom. At one degree they're already about fifty-seven. At a tenth of a degree, they're about five hundred and **seventy**. They grow without any limit, so at zero degrees they don't exist. On the flying fox, a nearly flat cable would have to be enormously long. So secant zero degrees is one, and cosecant and cotangent of zero degrees are **undefined**.

### 9. feedback 1 (brief)
> First the opposite side. By Pythagoras, it's the square root of six hundred and twenty-five minus forty-nine, which is twenty-**four**. ## Memory aid check

- **"Flip it: each pair has one 'co'. Sine and cosecant, cosine and secant, tangent and cotangent."** Original. Meaning, as said in scene 7: each reciprocal ratio is paired with the ratio it flips, and exactly one name in each pair begins with "co". Assumptions checked: the rule names the pairing only, not the sides (the definitions board gives those); it holds for all three pairs. It helps a new case: "sec" has no "co", so its partner must be "cos", and sec θ = 1/cos θ. Introduced after scenes 1 to 6.
- **"A side shrinks to 0: on top gives 0, on the bottom gives no value."** Original. Meaning: a ratio with the vanishing side as numerator tends to 0; with it as denominator, the ratio grows without limit and has no value at the endpoint. Assumptions checked: the other side stays non-zero (true in both limits: hypotenuse fixed at 1); at 0° and 90° "no value" means undefined, never "infinity". It helps a new case: cot 0° has the opposite side (→ 0) on the bottom, so it is undefined.
