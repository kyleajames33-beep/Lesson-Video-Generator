## Decisions

- **Route packUpdate applied in full.** "Replace imagined sources with named retrievable sources and an actual comparison/evidence table. Distinguish diffusion and fluid removal; no medical advice." (1) The site's imagined trio (a foundation webpage, a hospital leaflet, a journal article) is replaced by three named, retrievable sources, each checked by the designer on 2 Nov (see Source facts check): **S1** Kidney Health Australia, "Centre-based haemodialysis" (web page, kidney.org.au, "Last updated: October 2025"); **S2** National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases (NIDDK, part of the US National Institutes of Health), "Hemodialysis" (web page, niddk.nih.gov, "Last Reviewed January 2018"); **S3** Himmelfarb J, Ikizler TA. "Hemodialysis." *New England Journal of Medicine* 2010; 363(19): 1833-45 (peer-reviewed review article; PMID 21047227). (2) An actual **evidence table** builds through scenes 3 to 9 (criterion, kidney, dialysis, source) and is completed in scene 12. (3) Diffusion (wastes and salts) and pressure-driven fluid removal (water) are taught in separate scenes (3 to 4 and 5), with a closing two-line chip. (4) No treatment advice: the video describes what dialysis does in general; the one-row note in scene 11 says the sources describe treatment in general and decisions are made by a person's health team (screen only).
- **Why these three sources.** They differ in author, purpose, audience and date, so the reliability criteria give three different answers, which is the teaching point: S1 is current and Australian but written for patients (schedule, no mechanism); S2 is an expert government body with no product to sell but dated 2018 (currency check); S3 is peer-reviewed and synthesises many studies but is older and technical. S1 and S2 independently agree on the schedule (about 4 to 5 hours and about 4 hours, three times a week), which demonstrates agreement across independent sources. The site's commercial source type (a manufacturer leaflet) moves to attempt 2 as an exam-style stem with a generic, unnamed company (no real company named, no impersonation).
- **What each source is used for (claims only as verified).** S1: session length "usually last for four to five hours", usually three sessions a week; diet and fluid changes (verified on the page). S2: blood forced into "many, very thin, hollow fibers", dialysis solution flowing the other way, waste products moving into the solution, the solution made "to safely remove wastes, extra salt, and fluid"; about 4 hours, three times a week in a centre; dialysis "can replace part, but not all, of your kidney function" (verified on the page; quoted on screen in scene 12). S3: mechanism (diffusion of small solutes into dialysate, ultrafiltration by pressure for fluid removal, dialysate composition) and that haemodialysis does not replace the kidney's endocrine functions. **S3's text was not read in full by the designer (paywalled); Codex must confirm these claims against the article before voicing** (Open decision 1, with a fallback).
- **Main teaching move: outcome against mechanism.** The site's own line, "Dialysis copies the kidney's outcome (clean blood) but uses a simpler mechanism", becomes the comparison tool, set in scene 2 before any evidence and applied row by row. Glucose is the case that makes it vivid (scene 6): the kidney filters glucose out and takes it back using energy; the machine never lets it go, because there is no gradient. The site's Q2 model answer ("the machine achieves selectivity chemically, by choosing what the fluid contains, whereas the kidney achieves it biologically") is the source of the memory aid.
- **Hook (the site's own).** "How can a machine that only uses diffusion across a membrane 'know' to keep the glucose and lose the urea?" Spoken in scene 1 without "only uses diffusion" (packUpdate: fluid removal is pressure), answered in scene 4 ("The membrane doesn't know anything. The fluid on the other side decides what leaves.").
- **Fluid removal (scene 5).** Excess water is removed by a slightly higher pressure on the blood side than on the dialysate side (ultrafiltration, grey tag), linked to v23's pressure at the glomerulus. This corrects the site's "Excess water and salts also diffuse out" (site error 1).
- **Timing (scenes 7 and 8).** 3 sessions × 4 to 5 h = 12 to 15 h a week against 24 × 7 = 168 h. Spoken "about twelve to fifteen". The sawtooth (thumbnail, no values) shows build-up between sessions; the full sawtooth exercise stays in Year 12 (bio-m8-b17 territory is not used).

"those jobs need other treatments" replaces any drug names (no treatment advice).

- **Site errors found (this read, 2 Nov).** For `../site-corrections-bio-y11.md` (no FA2 L20 entries yet; root forwards after confirmation).
  1. L20 card 1 and the SA2 and Compare model answers: "Excess water and salts also diffuse out into the dialysate". Excess water is removed by ultrafiltration (a pressure difference across the membrane), not diffusion. Card 2's "dialysis ... uses diffusion only" and the comparison table's "dialysis only diffusion" repeat it. Proposed: "Extra salts diffuse out until they match the dialysate; extra water is pushed out by a pressure difference across the membrane (ultrafiltration)." (Same finding as B17's site error 2 for the Year 12 page.)
  2. L20 introduction: "without help they would die within days". Untreated kidney failure is usually fatal within weeks (dangerous potassium levels can arise within days, and some people on supportive care live longer). Proposed: "without treatment, kidney failure is usually fatal within weeks".
  3. L20 card 1: "so only the wastes leave". Small useful solutes absent from the dialysate are also lost (for example amino acids and water-soluble vitamins). Proposed: "so mainly wastes leave; small useful substances not added to the dialysate, such as amino acids, are partly lost". Minor; science check to confirm before logging.
  4. L20 vocabulary, Dialysate: "matched to healthy blood so only wastes diffuse out". Potassium is usually set below normal blood levels so that extra potassium is removed, and bicarbonate above. ### 15. key notes (quiet, about 60 seconds)

> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.
