### 2. pay as you go
> PAYG stands for pay as you go. Your employer takes some tax out of every pay, and sends it to the tax office, the ATO. That's called withholding. So by the end of the year, most of your tax is already **paid**. That way you never face one huge tax bill at the end of the year. It isn't an extra tax. It's the same income tax, paid in small pieces as you go. And the amounts allow for the Medicare levy too, which comes **later**.

### 4. the column: one threshold, one job
> So what's the tax-free threshold? It's the first eighteen thousand two hundred dollars of income each year, taxed at nothing. You claim it on a form for a new employer, the tax file number declaration, by ticking a box. But you get it once a year, not once per job. So for a second job, you don't tick the box, and that job uses the other column. Say this worker also has a weekend job paying five hundred and twenty a week. With no threshold, eighty-nine dollars comes out each week, instead of twenty-**four**. That's because none of the second job's income is tax-**free**. If both jobs claimed the threshold, too little would come out, and there'd be a tax bill at the end of the year.

### 6. close, but not exact
