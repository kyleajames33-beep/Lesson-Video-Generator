- **Answer frame for a temperature question (the structure q1 and the attempt drill):** (1) read the profile: which side is lower, so the sign of ΔH; (2) compare the barriers: the reaction that takes in heat climbs the taller barrier, taller by the size of ΔH; (3) both rates rise (or fall), the taller barrier changes more, and the rates started equal, so that rate is now bigger (or smaller); (4) net reaction and the named species; (5) the rates level out; K follows the new mixture (more product on top: K up). This is the C3 and C9 cause ("the reaction that takes in heat needs harder collisions, so it gains the most"), with the energy profile as the evidence.
- **Graph frame (q2):** axis label first (recall of g02), then the site lesson 8 diagnostic in three questions: did anything jump? how many lines jumped? which way did the lines curve afterwards? One jump: added or removed. Every line jumps: the volume. No jump, then curves: temperature, and the direction tells you heated or cooled. Flat after a jump of every line: equal gas counts.
- **Industrial frame (q3):** C7's "yield, rate, limit", one condition at a time. The pressure row is the contrast with Haber (C7: pressure helps, limited by cost at about 200 atm): here the yield is already high at about 1 atm, so extra pressure does not pay. The hook asks this question so the q3 pressure row pays it off.

Sealed: yes, Q reaches K, rates equal, some solid left. Open: no, the gas escapes, Q stays below K, the forward reaction runs on until the solid is gone. The crossed slip is "it shifts right because gas escapes" (restates the effect, gives no cause).

### 3d. q2-t3 (no jump, then curves: temperature)
> At t three, **no jump**, then the lines curve.
