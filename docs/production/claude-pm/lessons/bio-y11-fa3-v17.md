So scientists look for fine, well-dated layers with many specimens, and they measure features instead of judging by eye.

### 14. key notes (quiet, about 60 seconds)
Heading on screen: "Key notes: what to write in your book".
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

- Phylogenetic tree: a branching diagram of how species are related by descent; each branch point is a common ancestor. It shows rate only if time runs up and change in form runs across.
- Gradualism: small, steady change accumulating over long periods. Tree: branches lean steadily. Fossils: many intermediates.
- Punctuated equilibrium (Eldredge and Gould, 1972): long stasis (little or no change in form), short bursts of change mostly at speciation. Tree: vertical runs, sideways steps at splits. Fossils: few intermediates.
- Both work by natural selection and common descent; they differ only in tempo. A "burst" can still take thousands of generations.
- A cladogram without time or form axes shows branching order only: no rate. Fossils support both models in different lineages (horses: broadly gradual; trilobites, bryozoans: punctuated). A gap fits both.

Memory: gradualism is a ramp; punctuated equilibrium is a staircase.
