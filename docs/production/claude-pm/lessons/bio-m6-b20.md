### 1. hook (it works, so where is it?)
> In 2000, scientists reported a rice that makes beta-carotene in its grain, the orange pigment your body turns into vitamin A. ### 3. how a crop's gene pool changes (uptake)
> Think of all the rice plants in a region as one population with a gene pool. The added genes are new alleles, and their frequency is the share of plants carrying them. In the wild, natural selection decides which alleles reach the next generation. In a crop, farmers do, by choosing which seed to plant. And little else spreads these genes. Rice mostly pollinates itself, so little pollen reaches other fields, and beta-carotene gives the plant no known advantage in the field. So the frequency rises only as fast as uptake, how widely it's actually grown.

### 13. key notes (quiet, about 60 seconds)
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.
