### 2. temperature: the rise
> Start with temperature, one region at a time. On the vertical axis is the rate of reaction: how much product the enzyme makes each minute. That's why a chilled enzyme is slow, but not damaged. Warm it up, and it speeds up again.

### 4. denaturation
> Remember the weak bonds that hold an enzyme's shape? At high temperature, the enzyme vibrates so strongly that these bonds start to break. The chain loosens, and the active site loses its shape. Now the substrate doesn't fit, so it can't bind, and the enzyme stops working. That's denaturation: a change in shape that's usually permanent, so cooling it down doesn't bring the shape back. It's like cooking egg white. The protein changes shape, and it never goes clear again.
