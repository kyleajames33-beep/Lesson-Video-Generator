### 3. the optimum, and the fall

### 4. denaturation
> Remember the weak bonds that hold an enzyme's shape? At high temperature, the enzyme vibrates so strongly that these bonds break. The chain loosens, and the active site loses its shape. Now the substrate doesn't fit, so it can't bind, and the enzyme stops working. That's denaturation: a change in shape that's usually permanent, so cooling it down doesn't bring the shape back. It's like cooking egg white. The protein changes shape, and it never goes clear again.

### 7. pH explained
> Low, near pH two. Each enzyme's optimum suits the place it works. So why does the rate fall either side? An active site has charged parts, and these charges help hold its shape and attract the substrate. Changing the pH changes those charges, so the substrate binds less well, and the rate drops. A little way off the optimum, this can reverse if the pH comes back. Too far, and the enzyme denatures.

### 11b. feedback, item one
> From twenty to seventy-five, molecules move faster, so they collide more often and with more energy, and the rate rises. Seventy-five is the optimum, the fastest rate. > Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.
