### 2a. the string droops
> Look closely, though. The bung's weight makes the string droop a little below horizontal, just like the conical pendulum. So only the horizontal part of the tension pulls the bung inwards, and the circle's radius is a bit shorter than L. Both are shrunk by the same factor, the cosine of the droop angle. Now write the inward force as m v squared over r, which is m omega squared r. The same factor appears on both sides and cancels, leaving M g equals m omega squared **L**. That's the form we can test.

### 4. measuring the speed
> We can't read omega directly, so we measure a time and calculate it. Time twenty revolutions with a stopwatch, and divide by twenty to get the period. Timing twenty, not one, spreads your reaction time over many turns, so the period is much more **precise**. Repeat each run three times, and average. Then omega is one full turn, two pi radians, divided by the period, so omega equals two pi over **T**.
