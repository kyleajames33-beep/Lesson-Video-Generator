### 2. average speed: total distance over total time
> Average speed is the total distance divided by the total time, for the whole trip. It's the steady speed that would cover the same distance in the same time, as if the whole trip were spread out evenly. Here the total distance is eight hundred and eighty kilometres. The total time is ten hours and five minutes, and five minutes is five sixtieths of an hour, so on a calculator that's ten plus five divided by sixty, about ten point zero eight three **hours**. Eight hundred and eighty divided by that is about eighty-seven point three kilometres per **hour**. On the graph, it's the gradient of the dashed line from the start to the **finish**. It's lower than both driving speeds, because the stop added time, but no **distance**.

### 5. worked example: when and where?

To check, van A has driven two and a half hours at eighty, which is two hundred kilometres, and car B two hours at a hundred, also two **hundred**. Saying they have the same speed at eleven thirty is the slip. A crossing means same place, same **time**.
