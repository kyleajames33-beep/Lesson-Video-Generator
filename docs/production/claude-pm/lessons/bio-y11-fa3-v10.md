## Numbers or facts to verify

- **45 °C (1).** Inland arid Australia regularly exceeds 45 °C in summer; red kangaroos live there (site hook). Correct.
- **Forearm vessels and licking (3, 5, 6, 9, 12c).** Red kangaroos lick their forearms, where a superficial vascular network lies; evaporation of saliva cools the blood; licking increases at higher temperatures (Needham, Dawson and Hales 1974; Dawson 1995; site b1). Correct.
- **Resting and feeding times (5).** Red kangaroos rest in shade in the heat of the day and feed mainly from late afternoon through the night to early morning (site b1). Correct.
- **Kidneys and water from food (4).** Red kangaroos produce concentrated urine and obtain much water from forage, drinking little when green feed is available (site b1; Dawson 1995). Correct.
- **Koala liver enzymes (4).** Koalas have expanded cytochrome P450 gene families active in the liver, which metabolise eucalypt terpenes and phenolics (Johnson and colleagues 2018, Nature Genetics; site). "Large amounts of liver enzymes" is a plain-language version; the P450 name is not used. Correct.
- **Grey mangrove (3, 4, 8).** Avicennia marina has pneumatophores (aerial roots) that rise above waterlogged, low-oxygen mud and take in air through lenticels; it has salt glands on its leaves that excrete salt, often leaving visible crystals (site; standard). Correct.
- **Stomatal closing (7).** Guard cells lose water (turgor falls after ion movement) and the pore closes; closing in hot, dry conditions reduces water loss (site b2; FA2). Correct.
- **Heat acclimatisation (9).** After about 1 to 2 weeks of exercise in heat, sweating starts sooner and increases (FA2 v33's checked wording). Not inherited; the capacity to acclimatise has a genetic basis. Correct.
- **Spinifex hopping mouse (12a).** Notomys alexis: among the most concentrated urine of any mammal; can live without drinking free water; spends the day in deep burrows; has large ears (site). Correct.
