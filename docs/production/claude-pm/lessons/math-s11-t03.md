### 2. latitude: north or south of the equator
> Start with the equator, the imaginary line around the middle of the Earth, halfway between the North and South Poles. Latitude says how far north or south of the equator a place is. It's measured as an angle, in degrees, from the centre of the Earth. The equator is nought degrees. The North Pole is ninety degrees north, and the South Pole is ninety degrees south, so latitude never goes past ninety. Lines of equal latitude run around the globe, flat and parallel to the equator, like the rungs of a ladder. They're called parallels. Sydney is about thirty-four degrees south, a bit over a third of the way from the equator to the South **Pole**. Cape Town, in South Africa, is on almost the same parallel, so it's the same distance from the **equator**.

### 3. longitude: east or west of Greenwich
> Latitude alone isn't enough, because the thirty-four degrees south line goes all the way around the world. So we need a second number, for east and west. Longitude lines run from pole to pole, like the segments of an orange. They're called meridians. One of them was chosen as the start, nought degrees, the prime meridian, which runs through Greenwich in London. Longitude says how far east or west of the prime meridian a place is, again as an angle from the centre of the Earth. It goes up to a hundred and eighty degrees east, and a hundred and eighty degrees west, which meet on the far side of the world. Sydney is about a hundred and fifty-one degrees **east**. Where the thirty-four south parallel crosses the hundred and fifty-one east meridian, there's **Sydney**.

### 4. coordinates, and reading them off a map

Sydney is about thirty-three point nine degrees south, one hundred and fifty-one point two degrees **east**. Without a phone, you can read coordinates off a map of Australia with a grid of parallels and meridians, every ten degrees. Which city is at about thirty-two degrees south, one hundred and sixteen degrees east? Going the other way, Darwin sits between ten and twenty south, a little past ten, and between one hundred and thirty and one hundred and forty east, just past one thirty. So Darwin is at about twelve and a half degrees south, one hundred and thirty-one degrees **east**.

### 5. with digital tools: signs instead of letters
> Map apps write the same coordinates as decimals, and they use signs instead of letters. A minus latitude means south, and a minus longitude means west. So the pin's minus thirty-three point eight six is thirty-three point eight six degrees south, and one hundred and fifty-one point two one, with no minus, is east. That's the harbour in **Sydney**. It works the other way, too. Type coordinates into the search bar, and the app drops a pin on that exact place. The slip is dropping the minus sign. Thirty-three point eight six north is out in the Pacific Ocean, nowhere near **Sydney**. Atlases often split a degree into sixty minutes, just like an hour. So thirty-three degrees fifty-two minutes south is thirty-three and fifty-two sixtieths of a degree, about thirty-three point eight seven degrees **south**.

Brisbane, at about a hundred and fifty-three east, is a little further east than Sydney, so the Sun reaches its highest point there a little **earlier**. East and west turn out to matter for **time**.
