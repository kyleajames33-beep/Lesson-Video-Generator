### 2. four steps, and a picture is not a graph
> Here's the plan, in four steps. First, the axes. Time usually goes across, and the quantity that changes goes up, each with a label and units. Second, mark where the story starts. It doesn't have to be at zero. Third, take the story one part at a time. Does the quantity go up, go down, or stay the **same**? Staying the same makes a flat piece. Fourth, how does it change? Steady change, the same amount each day, makes a straight piece. Change that speeds up or slows down makes a curved **piece**. One more thing. A sketch is a graph of the quantity, not a picture of the place. A bike's speed slows going up a hill and picks up coming down, so its speed graph **dips**, even though the hill bumps up.

Visual: clear change to a four-step board at the left (44 px, numbered 1 to 4), each step lighting as it is said, with a small coded example beside it on empty axes at the right (900 × 560 px): step 1 the axes draw, "time" across (blue) and "quantity" up (teal), hand tag "time across"; step 2 an amber dot on the up axis above zero, hand tag "start here"; step 3, on "same", three short pieces draw side by side (a rise, a flat, a fall), labelled "up", "flat: no change", "down"; step 4, on "piece", a straight fall beside two curves (one bending steeper, one flattening), labelled "steady: straight" and "speeding up or slowing down: curved". ### 4. steeper means faster, and no gaps
> Look at the two sloping pieces over the same five days. The first drops five gigabytes. The last drops ten, so it's **steeper**. Same time, twice the drop. Steeper means faster. Using two a day is twice as fast as using one a day, so that piece falls twice as steeply. A flat piece means no change at all. And notice there are no gaps. Data can't jump from one amount to another, so each piece starts where the last one **ended**. Starting the graph at zero, zero is the classic **slip**. This story starts with forty gigabytes.

### 5. worked story: speeding up and slowing down
> Not every change is steady. Here's a mug of hot chocolate, made at about eighty degrees, then left on the bench. It cools quickly at first, then more and more slowly, as it gets close to room temperature, about twenty degrees. Time goes across, and temperature goes up, starting at eighty. The drop is fast at first, so the graph starts steep, then gets gentler and gentler, so the piece is a **curve** that flattens out towards twenty. After half an hour, the mug goes in the microwave for a minute, and heats up quickly and steadily. That's a short, steep, straight piece going **up**. A curve that gets flatter means the change is slowing down. A curve that gets steeper means it's speeding up. Drawing a curve for steady heating is the **slip**.
