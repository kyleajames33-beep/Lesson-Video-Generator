## Decisions

- **One main learner question:** given an equilibrium calculation you have never seen, how do you know which method to use, and which mistake catches people on that type? The decision rule is "what you are given and what you are asked decide the method" (scene 1 board, scene 10 board).
- **Five types, in the order the board shows them, numbered as in C6a and C6b:** (1) direct K from settled amounts (T10/T11); (2) ICE table from a start plus one measured equilibrium value (T12); (3) settled concentrations from K and a start (T14): no quadratic formula, look for a perfect square or a tiny K, practised on a tiny-K case with its test, then the huge-K mirror case; (4) Q against K for direction (T13); (5) the amount added to reach a new equilibrium (T15), as the full worked example, with a different case (a solid present) as the student attempt. Types 1 to 4 are short segments with one example each; type 5 has the longest share, as the research Video B plan sets out.
- **Types 3 and 4 differ by the demand, not the givens.** Both give K and a starting or current state. Settled concentrations ask "how much of each", which is x; a direction asks "which way", which is Q. The type cards, scene 1 and scene 10 say so.
- **Owner steers applied (binding):** recognise, method, working, classic mistakes and how to avoid them. Not a marks lesson. Hard ones emphasised: the tiny-K and huge-K shortcuts with the test (4a to 5c), the amount added (7a to 9). Every practice question ends "Pause here to answer." with a 10 s countdown (4a, 6a, 8). A worked example may use "Pause here and try it." with a 10 s hold at the end of its scene (7c). 15 Oct: the quadratic formula may be used, but HSC questions are written so you shouldn't need it (one sentence, 4a; softened wording, owner decision A); the measured or new value goes into the Equilibrium row first (3, 7b, 9).
- **Why these examples:** all are the authored examples in the research doc, so the numbers were already checked once and are recomputed below. B11.1 is used for scene 2 (the moles trap: 26.7 against 53.3) and B10.1 as a silent card. B12.2 (K = 229) has the 2:1 and 1:2 ratios and a square, so it tests the change row better than B12.1. No ICE table from scratch is re-taught: scene 3 recalls C6a in about 40 seconds and shows the working.
- **Cause chain taught, in order:** (1) the given quantities and the demand decide the method, so reading what is given and what is asked is the first step (1); (2) K is a ratio of concentrations, so amounts in a vessel are divided by its volume first (2, 3, 7a); (3) the measured value goes in the Equilibrium row, its change is equilibrium minus initial, and the coefficients set all the other changes (3, 7b, 9); (4) with K and a start, the change is x; HSC questions are written so you shouldn't need the quadratic formula: the expression is either a perfect square or has a tiny K; here the bottom is not a square but K is tiny, so x is small next to the start and can be dropped from "start minus x", which is then tested (4a to 4c); (5) with a huge K the reaction runs almost fully to products, so the small unknown is what is left of the reactants, including when you start from reactants in the equation's ratio (5a to 5c); (6) Q has the form of K with the current concentrations; Q below K means too little product, so the forward reaction wins (6a, 6b); (7) an addition changes Q and leaves K alone, so K from the old equilibrium is reused for the new one (7c); right after an addition, before anything reacts, the vessel is the old equilibrium plus the added substance, which is why y sits in the initial row, and the added substance is then partly used up, which is why the change row also has it (7b, 7d).

### 1. hook-board (what you are given and asked decides the method)
> Almost every equilibrium calculation is one of five types: the three from the ICE table videos, and two more. What you are **given**, and what you are **asked**, tells you which. Settled amounts, and you want K. A start plus **one** settled value. K and a **start**, asking for the settled concentrations. A **mixture** and a K, asking which way it goes. Or an old equilibrium, something **added**, and a new value. We take them in turn, and spend longest on the last one, the **hardest**.

Visual: heading "What you are given and asked decides the method" writes on over an empty board. The five type cards build as each is said, as a column on the left with a one-line "given" and "asked" on each, in the order of the video, each carrying the C6a mini ICE table picture where it applies: (1) "Settled amounts: find K"; (2) "Start + one settled value: ICE, find K"; (3) "Start + K: how much of each? ### 2. direct-k (type 1: settled amounts, find K)
> Type one: settled amounts. A **two litre** vessel holds 0.300 moles of sulfur dioxide, 0.150 of oxygen and 0.600 of sulfur trioxide. K uses concentrations, so **divide by two first**: 0.150, 0.0750 and 0.300 moles per litre. Write the expression with the coefficients as **powers**, not multipliers, and K is 0.300 squared, over the product of 0.150 squared and 0.0750, which is **53.3**. Use moles and you get 26.7, which is wrong.

Visual: task "Find K" top-left, equation strip 2SO2(g) + O2(g) ⇌ 2SO3(g) and the givens card: "vessel: 2.00 L", "SO2: 0.300 mol", "O2: 0.150 mol", "SO3: 0.600 mol", all at equilibrium, with the tag "HSC-style question, written for HSCScience". The working writes in beats under the cues: "0.300^2 = 0.0900", "0.150^2 = 0.0225, 0.0225 x 0.0750 = 0.0016875", "K = 0.0900 / 0.0016875 = 53.3". The tag "K: no unit written" stays on screen through the substitution. On "26.7" a side chip shows "moles used: 0.600^2 / (0.300^2 x 0.150) = 26.7" with a cross and the caption "K needs mol/L". ### 3. ice-one-value (type 2: a start plus one settled value)

Then the coefficients: sulfur dioxide **falls** 0.160 and oxygen falls **half** of that. The equilibrium row is 0.0400 and 0.0700, and **only** that row goes into K, giving **229**.

### 11. key-notes (quiet, hold about 45 seconds)
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

- What you are given and what you are asked decide the method. Settled amounts: find K. A start plus one settled value: ICE table. A start and a K, asked for concentrations: find x. A mixture and a K, asked which way: work out Q and compare. An old equilibrium, an addition and a new value: find the amount added.
- K and Q use concentrations, never moles: divide each amount by the vessel volume. Coefficients are powers, not multipliers. Pure solids, pure liquids and water as the solvent stay out of K and Q; dissolved species stay in.
- ICE table: the measured or new value goes in the Equilibrium row first; its change is equilibrium minus initial; only the change row follows the coefficients. Put only equilibrium values into K.
- K and a start: HSC questions are written so you shouldn't need the quadratic formula. Perfect square: take the square root. Tiny K: drop the x from "start minus x". Huge K: most of the start ends as products (reactants in the equation's ratio; an excess stays), so find the small amount left over. Test any shortcut: the amount taken off the start must be under about 5 per cent of the start (a school rule of thumb). No concentration can come out negative.
- Amount added: the added substance goes in the initial row as the old equilibrium plus y, and it is also used up in the change row. K is unchanged at the same temperature. Solve for y in mol/L, then multiply by the volume to give moles.

Memory aids (large, on the notes screen): *What you are given and what you are asked decide the method.* Second line: *Concentrations, not moles. Test it or don't use it.* Third line: *Adding changes Q, not K.* Condition tag: "same temperature, equation as written".

## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why divide by the volume: K is a concentration ratio (2, with the moles-only number shown wrong); why the measured value goes in the Equilibrium row and why one known change fixes the others (3, 7b, 9); why the square root does not apply to the N2O4 case (the bottom is not a square) and why the tiny K lets x be dropped, with the per cent test as the reason it is safe (4b, 4c); why a huge K means a small x, including from reactants in the equation's ratio (5a to 5c); why Q below K runs forward (6b); why the new table starts from the old equilibrium plus y, why K stays and Q changes in an addition problem (7b, 7c); why the added substance appears twice (7b, 7d).
- **Misconceptions and slips addressed:** moles used in K (2 with the number 26.7, 3, 7d); coefficient as multiplier (2); measured value used as the change (3 chip); initial values in K (3); whole change taken from a 1:2 species (3, 9); solid in Q or K (2, 6b, 9); inequality flipped (6b, "Q chases K"); a shortcut used without a test (4c, 5c); 2x not squared as 4x^2 (4b, 5b, unspoken chips); y in the change row, added substance not used, volume forgotten (7d, 9); thinking K changes when something is added (7c, 9 mini line); types 3 and 4 confused because they give the same things (1, 6a, 10).
- **Worked-example rule:** the amount-added worked example gives one stage per scene: setup and conversion (7a), the ICE table with the unknown in the initial row and why (7b), the K equation and a 10 s try-it in mol/L (7c), the solution, the volume conversion and the slips (7d). Types 1 to 4 are one or two scenes each, with the tiny-K answer split into choosing the shortcut (4b) and solving and testing (4c). - **Response opportunities:** four, each 10 s with the countdown ring and nothing answer-bearing until the hold ends: 4a (which shortcut, find NO2), 6a (direction from Q, a solid in the way), 7c (solve for y), 8 (a full amount-added problem with a solid, unseen, K given so it starts inside one pause). - **Reading load:** each scene shows one stage. The question cards in 4a, 6a and 8 hold every demand and condition while students work. The type-card board is a recurring reference, not new reading.
- **Avoided double teaching:** the ICE method, the perfect square, the tiny-K drop and its test are C6a and C6b callbacks; G03 applies them to unseen cases and names the slip. Only the huge-K shortcut and the addition problem are new methods here.
- **Out of scope by design:** how marks are allocated, command verbs and written-answer structure (video A), the quadratic formula, calibration graphs and colorimetry (p02), temperature and K (c09), Ksp and acid calculations (c10 to c15), reversing and scaling the equation (C6a), graphs and Q-time graphs (video A / g02).

## Memory aid check

- **"What you are given and what you are asked decide the method."** New, this lesson. Assumption: each question shows enough in its givens and its demand to place it in one of five types; types 3 and 4 share givens and differ by the demand (concentrations or direction), which is why "asked" is in the rule. Limit: real questions sometimes combine two types (for example an ICE table to find K, then Q to predict a direction, or a shortcut inside an addition problem). The board shows types as building blocks and the rule says "read what you are given and asked first", which still holds.
- **"Concentrations, not moles."** Bank trap. Assumption: a vessel of known volume. Holds in 2, 3, 7 and the 1.00 L attempt trivially. Limit: when the gas particles are equal on both sides (the CO + H2O example) the volume cancels in K, but not in the final amount added, which is why 7d multiplies by the volume.
- **"Q chases K."** Bank insight, recalled once (C8). Assumption: the same equation for Q and K, constant temperature, concentrations. Holds in 6b (both directions) and 9. Not re-explained.
- **"Test it or don't use it."** Assumption: the 5 per cent convention (a school rule of thumb, said so on the ruler and in the notes) applied to the amount taken off the start (x in 4c, 2x in 5c); a shortcut is used only when K is tiny compared with the start (or huge, from the product side) and the test shows the amount taken off is a small part of the start. Limit: for K given to two significant figures a 1 per cent error is within the uncertainty of K; the video does not claim the shortcut is exact (4c tag).
- **"Measured value: Equilibrium row first"** (C6a, card only). Holds in 3, 7b and 9, including the addition problems, where the new value is a non-zero equilibrium value and the subtraction matters (0.100 - 0.200 = -0.100; 0.500 - 0.400 = +0.100).
- **"Adding changes Q, not K" and "Initial row: plus y. K stays."** From the C8 idea. Assumption: constant volume and temperature, and the instant-after-adding picture. Holds in 7 and 9. Limit: dilution or compression at the same time as an addition is outside this video (C4a).
