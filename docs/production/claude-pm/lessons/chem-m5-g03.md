## Decisions

- **One main learner question:** given an equilibrium calculation you have never seen, how do you know which method to use, and which mistake catches people on that type? The decision rule is "what you are given and what you are asked decide the method" (scene 1 board, scene 12 board).
- **Five types, in the order the board shows them:** (1) direct K from settled amounts (T10/T11); (2) ICE table from a start plus one measured equilibrium value (T12); (3) Q against K for direction (T13); (4) settled concentrations from K and a start (T14), with the quadratic and root rejection as the full worked example and two approximations demonstrated with their checks; (5) the amount added to reach a new equilibrium (T15), as the second full worked example, with a different case (a solid present) as the student attempt. The hardest two types (4 and 5) have about 70 per cent of the narration; types 1 to 3 are quick segments with one short example each, as the research Video B plan sets out.
- **Types 3 and 4 differ by the demand, not the givens.** Both give K and a starting or current state. A direction asks "which way", which is Q; settled concentrations ask "how much of each", which is x. The type cards, scene 1 and scene 12 all say so.
- **Owner steers applied (binding):** recognise, method, working, classic mistakes and how to avoid them. Not a marks lesson. Hard ones emphasised: impossible quadratic root (scenes 5c to 6b), small-x and large-K approximations with the check (7a to 8c), amount added (9a to 9d, 10, 11). Every practice question ends "Pause here to answer." with a 10 s countdown (4a and 10). A worked example may use "Pause here and try it." with a 10 s hold at the end of its scene (6a, 9c).
- **Why these examples:** all are the authored examples in the research doc, so the numbers were already checked once and are recomputed again below. B11.1 is used for scene 2 (the moles trap: 26.7 against 53.3) and B10.1 as one spoken sentence. B12.2 (K = 229) has the 2:1 and 1:2 ratios and a square, so it tests the change row better than B12.1. B13.2 (heterogeneous Q) covers the solid-in-Q error and both directions of the rule. B14.1 is the quadratic. B14.3 is the huge-K case. The tiny-K case is an N2O4 version of B14.2: B14.2 itself is a perfect square (C6 would solve it exactly), so it cannot show why the shortcut exists; the N2O4 example (N2O4 ⇌ 2NO2, K = 4.0 x 10^-4, start 0.500 mol/L) needs the quadratic, so the shortcut is a real saving. B15.1 is the amount-added worked example, changed only in the vessel size (1.00 L in the research, 2.00 L here, same concentrations, so the answer is 0.525 mol/L or 1.05 mol) so that the moles-to-concentrations conversion, the second most common slip in the type, is on the board without new algebra. The attempt is B15.3 with the new equilibrium CO changed from 0.600 to 0.500 mol/L and K given, so that no answer equals a given value and the start fits one pause.
- **Leaves out, on purpose:** colorimetric K and calibration lines (planned p02); reversing or scaling the equation (C6); temperature and K (C9); Ksp and acids; the rate model for Q (C8); a worked example for a huge-K start from reactants (one spoken sentence only, scene 8c). No ICE table from scratch is re-taught: scene 3 recalls C6 in about 40 seconds and shows the working.
- **Cause chain taught, in order:** (1) the given quantities and the demand decide the method, so reading what is given and what is asked is the first step (1); (2) K is a ratio of concentrations, so amounts in a vessel are divided by its volume first (2, 3, 9a); (3) the change row follows the coefficients, with the one known change setting all the others (3, 5a, 9b); (4) when K is given and the start is not a perfect square, the equation is a quadratic and has two roots; only the root that keeps every concentration positive and not above what was started can be a real result (5c to 6b); (5) the shortcut "start minus x is about start" is used only after you state it, solve with it and test the amount taken off the start against the start (7a to 7c); (6) with a huge K the reaction runs almost fully to products, so the small unknown is what is left of the reactants, including when you start from reactants in the equation's ratio (8a to 8c); (7) an addition changes Q and leaves K alone, so K from the old equilibrium is reused for the new one (9c); right after an addition, before anything reacts, the vessel is the old equilibrium plus the added substance, which is why y sits in the initial row, and the added substance is then partly used up, which is why the change row also has it (9b, 9d).
- **Entry knowledge:** C1 to C8 and Year 11 (mole ratios, c = n / V, solving a simple equation, the quadratic formula from Year 11 maths; the video states the formula once in full, spoken and on screen, and offers the calculator alternative). The learner knows: K expressions with powers and without solids and water (C5); ICE tables, the change row following the coefficients, K from a start plus one value, a perfect-square find-x case with a positive root (C6); Q, the Q against K rule, "Q chases K" (C8); Le Chatelier's direction for an added reactant (C3). Nothing about the quadratic formula in chemistry, approximations, or addition problems is assumed.
- **Start and stop:** starts at "what is given and what is asked" for a calculation question; stops after the learner can pick the method, write the working for each of the five types, and name the classic slip on each. Handoff: chem-m5-c09 (temperature and K). If the playlist runs another video next, change the line in scene 12.
- **Learner-visible definitions:** quadratic (an equation with an x squared term, which has two answers); root (an answer to a quadratic, used in the on-screen working); "physical" is not used, the video says "a real concentration"; approximation (replacing 0.200 minus x by 0.200 because x is small), assumption check ("is the amount taken off the start under about 5 per cent of the start?"); "huge K" and "tiny K" are shown by their powers of ten, not defined further.
- **Units policy:** K written without a unit (C5 and C6 policy, tag "K: no unit written" at the first substitution of scene 2 and at each later substitution). Every concentration carries mol/L on the givens card and in the column headings, and amounts carry mol. y in the amount-added example is in mol/L until the last line of 9d. Strictly, K for several of these reactions has a unit (for example mol/L for the PCl5 splitting and the N2O4 splitting); this is the standing open decision from C5 and C6.
- **K values for repeated equations:** the illustrative K values are not temperature-matched (see open decisions), so each repeat carries a visible tag "a different temperature, so a different K": 2SO2 + O2 ⇌ 2SO3 has K = 53.3 (scene 2), 229 (scene 3) and 4.0 x 10^10 (scene 8a); C + CO2 ⇌ 2CO has K = 1.90 (4a) and 0.400 (attempt). Nothing contradicts the C5 rule that K is fixed at one temperature.
- **No separate mistake scene.** The wrong moves are named inside each type: moles for concentrations (2, 3, 9d), coefficient as a multiplier not a power (2), initial values in K (3), solid in Q or K (2, 4b), inequality flipped (4b), wrong root (6a, 6b), approximating without a check or when the amount taken off is not small (7a), forgetting the added species is also used up (9d, 11), forgetting the volume (9d). Per the AGENTS rule, a mistake scene is optional and is not used as a template.
- **Reuse (no prototype components):** the C6 ICE table look (three rows filling one at a time, columns per species with a mol/L header, coefficient chips flying into the change row, signs in the used-up and made colours) for scenes 3, 5, 7, 8 and 9; the C5 expression builder; the C8 log number line for scene 4 (K marker fixed, Q marker landing); the C6 algebra line (term slides, bracket expansion, root sign); a new quadratic board (the formula with a, b, c slots filling, two roots dropping out, one crossed); a new "check ruler" (a bar for the start with a bar for the amount taken off it, a percentage label and the 5 per cent line); the countdown ring; the key notes board. New in G03: the type-card board (scene 1 and 12), the quadratic board, the check ruler. Colour tokens as C2 to C8 (forward blue, reverse amber, equal teal, used-up and made colours from C6, pen highlight). One visual system throughout.
- **Memory aids** (bank: `docs/production/module5-memory-aids-2026-10-11.md`, read for "Work in concentrations, not moles", "Q chases K", and the ICE entry; the bank is a draft): (1) "What you are given and what you are asked decide the method" (new, this lesson's decision rule, not from the bank); (2) "Concentrations, not moles" (bank trap; scenes 2, 3, 9a); (3) "Q chases K" recalled once in 4b (C8); (4) "Test it or don't use it" for approximations (new, scene 7; assumptions: stated assumption, amount taken off the start under about 5 per cent of the start, same temperature); (5) "In an addition problem the added substance goes in the initial row, and K stays" (new, scene 9). The spoken ones are (1), (4) and (5), and (2) and (3) are on the notes screen. Each is checked in "Memory aid check".
- **Author notes (not learner copy):**
  (a) The quadratic is solved with the formula, shown once and spoken in full, with the b squared minus 4ac line printed in full (0.002025 + 0.036000 = 0.038025, whose square root is exactly 0.195).   (b) The 5 per cent test is the usual school convention, not a law: for K given to two significant figures a 1 per cent error in x is already inside the uncertainty of K. ### 1. hook-board (what you are given and asked decides the method)
> Almost every equilibrium calculation is one of five types, and what you are **given**, and what you are **asked**, tells you which. Settled amounts, and you want K. A start plus **one** settled value. A **mixture** and a K, asking which way it goes. K and a **start**, asking for the settled concentrations. Or an old equilibrium, something **added**, and a new value. We take them in turn, and spend longest on the last two, the hardest.

Visual: heading "What you are given and asked decides the method" writes on over an empty board. The five type cards build as each is said, as a column on the left with a one-line "given" and "asked" on each, in the order of the video: (1) "Settled amounts: find K"; (2) "Start + one settled value: ICE, find K"; (3) "Mixture + K: which way? ### 2. direct-k (type 1: settled amounts, find K)
> Type one: settled amounts. A **two litre** vessel holds 0.300 moles of sulfur dioxide, 0.150 of oxygen and 0.600 of sulfur trioxide. K uses concentrations, so **divide by two first**: 0.150, 0.0750 and 0.300 moles per litre. Write the expression with the coefficients as **powers**, not multipliers, and K is 0.300 squared, over the product of 0.150 squared and 0.0750, which is **53.3**. Use moles and you get 26.7, which is wrong.

Visual: task "Find K" top-left, equation strip 2SO2(g) + O2(g) ⇌ 2SO3(g) and the givens card: "vessel: 2.00 L", "SO2: 0.300 mol", "O2: 0.150 mol", "SO3: 0.600 mol", all at equilibrium, with the tag "HSC-style question, written for HSCScience". On "powers" the C5 builder writes K = [SO3]^2 / ([SO2]^2 [O2]) with the 2s flying from the equation strip to the exponents, and a crossed ghost "2 x [SO2]" with the caption "power, not multiplier". The working writes in beats under the cues: "0.300^2 = 0.0900", "0.150^2 = 0.0225, 0.0225 x 0.0750 = 0.0016875", "K = 0.0900 / 0.0016875 = 53.3". The tag "K: no unit written" stays on screen through the substitution. On "26.7" a side chip shows "moles used: 0.600^2 / (0.300^2 x 0.150) = 26.7" with a cross and the caption "K needs mol/L". Memory aid on the card: "Concentrations, not moles." Hold about 2 seconds.

### 3. ice-one-value (type 2: a start plus one settled value)

### 13. key-notes (quiet, hold about 45 seconds)
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

- What you are given and what you are asked decide the method. Settled amounts: find K. A start plus one settled value: ICE table. A mixture and a K, asked which way: work out Q and compare. A start and a K, asked for concentrations: find x. An old equilibrium, an addition and a new value: find the amount added.
- K and Q use concentrations, never moles: divide each amount by the vessel volume. Coefficients are powers, not multipliers. Pure solids, pure liquids and water as the solvent stay out of K and Q; dissolved species stay in.
- In an ICE table only the change row follows the coefficients. Equilibrium equals initial plus change. Put only equilibrium values into K.
- Quadratic: reject any root that makes a concentration negative, or that takes more off a starting species than there was.
- Shortcut such as "start minus x is about the start": state it, solve with it, test it. The amount taken off the start must be under about 5 per cent of the start (a school rule of thumb). If it fails, solve the full equation. Huge K: most of the start ends as products (reactants in the equation's ratio; an excess stays), so find the small amount left over and test it.
- Amount added: the added substance goes in the initial row as the old equilibrium plus y, and it is also used up in the change row. K is unchanged at the same temperature. Solve for y in mol/L, then multiply by the volume to give moles.

Memory aids (large, on the notes screen): *What you are given and what you are asked decide the method.* Second line: *Concentrations, not moles. Test it or don't use it. A root that makes any concentration negative is the wrong root.* Third line: *Adding changes Q, not K.* Condition tag: "same temperature, equation as written".

## Memory aid check

- **"What you are given and what you are asked decide the method."** New, this lesson. Assumption: each question shows enough in its givens and its demand to place it in one of five types; types 3 and 4 share givens and differ by the demand (direction or concentrations), which is why "asked" is in the rule. Limit: real questions sometimes combine two types (for example an ICE table to find K, then Q to predict a direction, or an approximation inside an addition problem). The board shows types as building blocks and the rule says "read what you are given and asked first", which still holds. It is not a bank item.
- **"Concentrations, not moles."** Bank trap. Assumption: a vessel of known volume. Holds in 2, 3, 9 and the 1.00 L attempt trivially (the worked example carries the volume test). Limit: when the gas particles are equal on both sides (the CO + H2O example) the volume cancels in K, but not in the final amount added, which is why 9d multiplies by the volume.
- **"Q chases K."** Bank insight, recalled once (C8). Assumption: the same equation for Q and K, constant temperature, concentrations, no overshoot. Holds in 4b (both directions) and 11. Not re-explained.
- **"Test it or don't use it."** New. Assumption: the 5 per cent convention (a school rule of thumb, said so on the rule card and in the notes) applied to the amount taken off the start (x in 7a and 7c, 2x in 8c); an approximation is only used when K is small compared with the start (or large, from the product side) and the test shows the amount taken off is a small part of the start. Limit: for K given to two significant figures a 1 per cent error is within the uncertainty of K; the video does not claim the shortcut is exact (7c tag).
- **"A root that makes any concentration negative is the wrong root."** Fully general for a positive starting concentration and a single physical process, including a root that makes a different species negative. Limit: a root can also be rejected because it is larger than the start (6b), which the video shows by name, and the key note says so; a positive root that takes more off a starting species than there was must also be rejected (the key note says it that way, not as "more of a reactant", because a root can legitimately raise a reactant when a product is present at the start and the reaction runs in reverse).
- **"Adding changes Q, not K" and "Initial row: plus y. K stays."** From the C8 idea. Assumption: constant volume and temperature, and the instant-after-adding picture (the initial row is the old equilibrium plus the addition). Holds in 9 and 11. Limit: dilution or compression at the same time as an addition is outside this video (C4a).
