Main question: *Why does a cell grow and copy its DNA before it divides, and what happens in each phase of the cell cycle?*
Entry knowledge: see above. Students have watched v01 to v26.
Stops after: a cell that divided straight away would give daughters too few chromosomes and too little cytoplasm; the cell cycle as the repeating series of stages from one division to the next; interphase (G1, S, G2, in order; most of the time) then mitosis (nucleus) then cytokinesis (cytoplasm); G1: growth, proteins, organelles, normal work, because division halves the cell; G1 checkpoint (size, nutrients, DNA damage; some cells stop here); S: DNA replicated, each chromosome becomes two sister chromatids joined at a centromere; same chromosome number, twice the DNA (count centromeres); G2: more growth, division proteins (spindle), DNA checked and repaired; G2 checkpoint: the cell checks that the DNA has been fully and correctly copied and delays division if it finds a problem, because an error that remains can pass to both daughters (checks lower the risk but cannot remove it); mitosis separates sister chromatids so each nucleus gets one copy of every chromosome (named phases next); cytokinesis (animal pinches; plant builds a new wall); two normally matching daughters, each (if it keeps dividing) back to G1; the order as a chain of dependencies.
Handoff: model the named phases of mitosis and cytokinesis (route entry bio-y11-fa1-v28).
