- **Site errors found (this read).** Root to add to `../site-corrections-bio-y11.md` after the science check confirms.
  1. L23 hook: "You make roughly two trillion new cells every day, each an exact copy of its parent, same DNA, same chromosome number." The best current estimate of human cell turnover is about 330 billion cells a day (Sender and Milo, 2021, *Nature Medicine*, "The distribution of cellular turnover in the human body"), about six times fewer, and most of them are red blood cells, which lose their nucleus and so do not keep the chromosome number. Proposed: "Your body replaces hundreds of billions of cells every day" and drop "each an exact copy ... same chromosome number", or say "most body cells made by mitosis are exact copies".
  2. L23 account card, G2: "it proofreads the newly replicated DNA, repairing errors". Proofreading is done by DNA polymerase during replication in S phase; in G2 the cell checks that replication is complete and repairs damage. Proposed: "it checks that the DNA has been completely copied and repairs any damage". Wording finding, minor.

### 2. why it can't divide straight away
> At best, two chromosomes each, so each new cell would be missing genes. And each would be half the size, with half the organelles. So before a cell divides, it has two jobs: grow, and copy every chromosome. That's why cells go through a cell cycle: the repeating series of stages from one division to the next.

Visual: on "two chromosomes each" the card's four chromosomes split two and two into the empty cards below (each "2: missing genes", in red); on "half the size" the cell outline halves along the dashed line into two small half-cells beside the cards (tag "half the cytoplasm and organelles"). On "cell cycle" the `CycleRing` draws round the cell as a plain loop with a travelling dot, and a gloss writes: "cell cycle: the repeating stages from one division to the next". At most three bright items. Hold one second.

### 3. the ring: interphase, then division
> Here it is as a ring. Most of it is interphase, when the cell grows and prepares. Interphase has three stages, always in the same order: gap one, synthesis and gap two. Then comes mitosis, which divides the nucleus, and finally cytokinesis, which divides the cytoplasm. In most dividing cells, interphase takes up most of the time.

Visual: the cell moves to the centre right; the `CycleRing` settles large at the left. On "interphase" a long bracket arcs round most of the ring, label "interphase: grow and prepare". On "gap one, synthesis and gap two" three arcs light in turn, labelled "G1", "S", "G2". ### 4. G1: grow
> Gap one, or G1. "Gap" is an old name for the time between dividing and copying, but the cell is far from idle. It grows, makes proteins and new organelles, and does its normal job. This matters because every division halves the cytoplasm and organelles. Without this growth, cells would get smaller every time they divided.

Visual: the G1 arc glows; the dot moves along it. On "grows" the cell plate scales up uniformly by about a fifth (tag "growing"); on "makes proteins and new organelles" small coded glints pop beside the painted mitochondria and ER. On "every division halves" a three-step strip draws at the bottom: a cell halving, its halves halving again with no growth between, each smaller (tag "no G1: smaller every division", in red), crossed through; beside it a second strip where each half grows back to full size before halving (tag "with G1: same size each time"). At most three bright items. Hold one second.

### 5. the G1 checkpoint
> Near the end of G1 there's a checkpoint: a stop point where the cell checks conditions before going on. Is it big enough? Are there enough nutrients? Is its DNA undamaged? If not, it pauses, or repairs the damage. Some cells, such as most nerve cells, stop here and don't divide again.

Visual: a gate mark draws across the ring at the end of G1 (label "checkpoint: check before going on"). On each question a small check chip writes beside the gate with a tick: "big enough?", "nutrients?", "DNA undamaged?". On "If not, it pauses" the dot stops at the closed gate and a small coded repair glint plays on the card's chromosomes; the gate then opens. On "most nerve cells" `y11-neuron-v1.png` appears small beside the ring, linked to a short side branch off the gate that ends at a stop mark (grey tag "G0: stopped dividing"). At most three bright items. Hold one second.

### 6. S: copy every chromosome, and a prediction

Each copy is called a sister chromatid, and they're held together at a narrow point called the centromere. Prediction: how many chromosomes does our cell have now?

### 9. the G2 checkpoint
> At the checkpoint at the end of G2, the cell only goes on if every chromosome has been fully and correctly copied. Why check here? Because after this point, a mistake becomes permanent. It would be copied into both daughter cells, and into every cell they produce.

### 10. mitosis: divide the nucleus
> Now the cell divides. First comes mitosis, the division of the nucleus. Its job is to separate the sister chromatids, so each new nucleus gets one copy of every chromosome. Mitosis has four named phases, and they're the next video. For now, treat it as one step: the chromatids are pulled to opposite ends of the cell.

### 12. two identical daughters, back to G1
> The result is two daughter cells, each with four chromosomes, identical to each other and to the parent cell. They're identical because the DNA was copied exactly in S phase, and shared exactly in mitosis. And each daughter starts its own cycle again, in G1.

### 13. why the order is fixed
> So why this order? Each stage depends on the one before. Grow first, so the cell is big and healthy enough to copy. Copy before dividing, or the daughters would be missing chromosomes. Check before dividing, so mistakes don't become permanent. And split the nucleus before the cytoplasm, or one cell could end up with no nucleus at all.

Visual: the ring at the centre, large; four order chips write round it, one per sentence, each with a short arrow to its arc: "grow (G1) → so it's ready to copy", "copy (S) → so each daughter gets every chromosome", "check (G2) → so errors aren't made permanent", "nucleus, then cytoplasm (M, cytokinesis) → so each cell gets a nucleus". On the last, a small what-if: a ghost cell splits through its middle while its X-shaped chromosomes are still together on one side (a coded outline cell with a card on one side only), one half labelled "no nucleus", crossed. At most three bright items. Hold one second.

### 14. one act, not the whole play
> Here's a way to hold the big picture. Mitosis is one act, not the whole play. Most of the cycle is interphase, getting ready. And mitosis followed by cytokinesis makes two cells, never four. Four cells with half the chromosomes is meiosis, a different process.

### 17. key notes (quiet, about 60 seconds)
Heading on screen: "Key notes: what to write in your book".
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

- Cell cycle: the repeating stages from one division to the next. Interphase (G1, S, G2) takes most of the time, then mitosis (nucleus divides), then cytokinesis (cytoplasm divides).
- G1: the cell grows and makes proteins and organelles (division halves the cell). Checkpoint: size, nutrients, DNA damage.
- S: DNA replicated; each chromosome becomes two identical sister chromatids joined at a centromere. Same number of chromosomes, twice the DNA (count the centromeres).
- G2: more growth, proteins for division (spindle); DNA checked. Checkpoint: only fully, correctly copied DNA goes on, or an error becomes permanent in both daughters.
- Mitosis separates sister chromatids so each nucleus gets one copy of every chromosome. Cytokinesis: animal cell pinches in; plant cell builds a new wall. Result: two identical daughter cells.

Memory: grow, copy, check, divide. Mitosis is one act, not the whole play: two cells, never four.

- **G1 checkpoint (5).** Checks size, nutrients and growth signals, and DNA damage; damage causes arrest and repair. Most mature neurons leave the cycle (G0). Correct.
- **After S: same chromosome number, twice the DNA (7, 15a).** A replicated chromosome (two sister chromatids at one centromere) counts as one chromosome; DNA content doubles from 2C to 4C. Correct.
- **G2 checkpoint (9).** Checks for complete replication and DNA damage before mitosis. An uncorrected mutation passes to both daughters and their descendants. Cancer link (screen tag). Correct.

## Memory aid check
