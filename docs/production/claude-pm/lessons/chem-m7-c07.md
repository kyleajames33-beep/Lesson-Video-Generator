**Boundary:** this video starts by writing alcohol combustion equations and stops at assessing energy, production and environmental trade-offs between ethanol and petrol. It teaches: complete combustion of an alcohol to carbon dioxide and water, balanced by carbon, then hydrogen, then oxygen, counting the oxygen the alcohol already carries (ethanol); the equation written per mole of fuel, with a fractional O₂ coefficient (methanol), and ΔH negative because heat is released (chem-m5-g01 recall); state symbols with liquid water for the data values; incomplete combustion when oxygen is limited, giving carbon monoxide (toxic) and soot (carbon), a yellow smoky flame and less energy released; soot as evidence about the oxygen supply; where the data values come from, through one compact recall of q = mcΔT with Codex's methanol hook dataset (0.74 g warms 100.0 g of water by 20.0 °C: 8.36 kJ, about 11 kJ per gram against about 22.7 kJ per gram in careful measurements, so the can caught about half; the reasons are chem-m7-p03); the molar enthalpies of combustion of methanol to butan-1-ol and the steady increase of about 650 kJ/mol per CH₂, explained by bonds broken and formed; energy per gram as the fuel comparison (ethanol 29.7, octane 47.9 kJ/g) and why alcohols give less (the oxygen adds mass and the carbon bonded to oxygen is already partly oxidised);
