### 3. stationary is not the same as turning
> Two words to keep apart. A stationary point is any point where the derivative is zero, so the tangent is horizontal. That's all it **says**. A turning point is where the curve changes direction. It's either a local maximum, higher than every nearby point on the curve, or a local minimum, lower than every nearby point. Local means compared only with points close by. So the point at three is stationary, and it's a turning point. The point at zero is stationary, but it's not a **turning point**.

### 4. the sign on each side decides
> So what decides it? The sign of the derivative on each side. If the curve rises into the point and falls after it, the sign goes from plus to minus. Nothing nearby is higher, so it's a local **maximum**. If it falls in and rises out, that's minus to plus, and nothing nearby is lower: a local **minimum**. And if the sign is the same on both sides, plus and plus, or minus and minus, the curve keeps going the same way. That's called a horizontal point of **inflection**. It's stationary, but it isn't a turning point.

## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why a flat tangent need not turn: the curve can level off and carry on (1, 3). ## YouTube metadata (draft)

Title: Stationary Points: When Does a Curve Turn? The Sign Test | Year 12 Maths Advanced

(Title length: 81 characters.)

Description: Full lesson, notes and practice questions: https://hscscience.com.au/subjects/maths-advanced/year12/applications-of-calculus/lesson06.sa.html

A flat tangent doesn't always mean a turn. The curve y = x⁴ − 4x³ has a horizontal tangent at x = 0 and at x = 3, but it only turns at 3. See why on the derivative's own graph: it crosses the axis at 3 and only touches it at 0. Learn the sign test: factorise f′(x), split the number line at its zeros and at any excluded values, test one easy value inside each interval, then classify each stationary point as a local maximum (+ to −), a local minimum (− to +) or a horizontal point of inflection (the same sign on both sides). Get the y-values from f, not f′. Two exam-style questions at the end.

Mathematics Advanced 11-12 (2024 syllabus), Year 12, applications of differentiation.
Syllabus content (NSW Mathematics Advanced 11-12, 2024):
"Analyse the stationary points of a function by testing values for f′(x), then classify the stationary points as local minima or local maxima where gradients change around the point, or horizontal points of inflection where gradients have the same sign on both sides of the point"
Official syllabus: https://curriculum.nsw.edu.au/learning-areas/mathematics/mathematics-advanced-11-12-2024/content/year-12/fa383c9104
