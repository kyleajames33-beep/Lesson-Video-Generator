## Numbers or facts to verify

Self-check (2 Nov). The Codex science check must confirm each before voicing.
- **Equation balance (scenes 5, 6, notes).** 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂. C: 6 = 6. H: 12 = 12. O: 12 + 6 = 18 = 6 + 12. Correct.
- **Van Helmont (scenes 2, 3, 9).** Reported in *Ortus Medicinae* (published 1648, experiment usually dated to the 1630s to 1640s): a willow of 5 pounds in 200 pounds of oven-dried soil, watered with rain water (or distilled water) for five years, grew to 169 pounds 3 ounces; the dried soil lost about 2 ounces. He concluded the mass came from water. Converted with modern pounds: 5 lb ≈ 2.3 kg ("about two kilograms"), 200 lb ≈ 91 kg ("ninety kilograms"), 169 lb ≈ 77 kg ("about seventy-seven"), gain ≈ 75 kg (screen), 2 oz ≈ 57 g ("less than sixty grams"). His pound differed slightly from the modern one; ratios hold. "About four hundred years ago": correct. The fallen leaves were not weighed (author note; does not change the conclusion). Flag for the science check.
- **"Seed lighter than a paperclip" (scene 1).** A paperclip is about 1 g; willow seeds weigh well under 1 mg; many tree seeds are under 1 g. Correct as stated for "a seed" (screen tag "under 1 gram").
- **"tonnes of wood" (scene 1).** Large mature trees can exceed several tonnes. Correct (site hook says tonnes).
- **Carbon dioxide in air (scene 3).** About 0.04 per cent (about 420 parts per million in 2025 to 2026). "less than a tenth of one percent": correct.
- **Most dry mass from carbon dioxide (scene 3).** Dry wood is about 50 per cent carbon, about 42 to 44 per cent oxygen and about 6 per cent hydrogen, with about 1 per cent minerals. Carbon all from CO₂; the oxygen in glucose comes from CO₂ (isotope tracing), and hydrogen from water. So most of the dry mass comes from carbon dioxide. Correct. ("About half the dry mass of wood is carbon", scene 9: correct.)
- **Chlorophyll (scenes 4, 7).** Absorbs mainly red and blue light and reflects (and transmits) green. Not consumed in the overall reaction. Correct.
- **Energy (scene 8).** Glucose stores far more chemical energy than CO₂ and water; light energy is converted into chemical energy. Correct.
- **Cellulose (scene 9).** Cellulose is a polymer of glucose and the main structural material of wood (with lignin and hemicellulose). "the main material of wood": correct.
- **Soil minerals (scene 9).** Van Helmont's lost mass was mostly mineral ions taken up by the roots (plus measuring error). "That's the missing sixty grams": a fair identification; flag "mostly" for the science check (length-neutral swap "That's most of the missing sixty grams" if required).
- **Net gas exchange (scene 12).** In bright light photosynthesis exceeds respiration (net O₂ release); in darkness only respiration (net O₂ uptake, CO₂ release). "In bright light" avoids the compensation point. Correct.
- **Sealed jar (13c).** A classic demonstration; survival depends on light and balance; the snail eats plant material. Correct.
- **Oxygen source (author note, not spoken).** The released O₂ comes from water (isotope studies, 1941). The summary equation's 6 H₂O hold only 6 oxygen atoms, while 6 O₂ hold 12; the full equation is 6CO₂ + 12H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ + 6H₂O. This video does not claim the source of oxygen, so the syllabus equation stays consistent.
- **Time-sensitive facts spoken.** Carbon dioxide "less than a tenth of one percent" (stable for the foreseeable future).
