The answer, built in order: a field is a region of push or pull without contact (2); in light, the electric and magnetic fields themselves oscillate, at right angles to the travel (3); each changing field makes the other, so they need no particles (4); so light crosses a vacuum, at c, whatever its colour (5, 6, 7); an accelerating charge starts the wave (8); the fields carry the energy, and the far charges are pushed and gain it (9).

### 2. what a field is
> To answer that, we need one new idea, a field. Bring a magnet near a compass, and the needle turns, without being touched. The region around the magnet where that happens is its magnetic field. Electric charges do the same thing. A charge pushes or pulls another charge from a distance. The region around it where that happens is its electric **field**. Now pump the air away. The needle stays turned, and the charge still pushes. A field doesn't need any **particles**.

Visual: board change. Left: the bar magnet plate (`phys-y11-bar-magnet-v1.png`) with three plotting compasses (`phys-y11-plotting-compass-v1.png`) around it; their coded needles swing to point along the field as the magnet slides in; faint ink field lines draw around the magnet. On "particles" both pulse; chip "fields: no medium needed".

### 3. what's waving: electric and magnetic fields
> Light is made of fields. Put a row of tiny test charges in a beam of light, and watch. Each charge is pushed up, then down, then up again, a moment after its neighbour. Nothing material hits them. What rises and falls is the electric field itself. So light is an electric field that **oscillates**, and with it, a magnetic field, at right angles. Both of them oscillate at right angles to the direction the wave travels. Remember transverse waves? That's what light is, a transverse wave of changing electric and magnetic fields. We call it an electromagnetic **wave**.

Visual: board change to the wave lane: a source glow at the left (coded), a row of nine small "+" beads on short vertical tracks across the middle (gloss "test charge: a small charge we use to feel the field"). The beads rise and fall in sequence, a travelling wave of motion (tag "slowed down"). On "electric field itself" the beads fade and short teal arrows remain at their positions, rising and falling: `EMWave3D` side view, label "E: electric field", amber outline "travel" pointing right. On "oscillates" the camera tilts to perspective: plum dashed arrows "B: magnetic field" grow in the horizontal plane, peaking where the teal ones peak; a right-angle mark between E and B and another between E and the travel. On "wave" a small v03 cord panel (transverse) flashes beside it with the tag "transverse, like v03's cord"; label "electromagnetic wave: changing electric and magnetic fields".

### 4. why it needs no medium
> But what keeps these fields going, with nothing there? Here's the key. A changing electric field makes a magnetic field. And a changing magnetic field makes an electric field. So as the wave moves, each changing field makes the next part of the other, and the pair keep each other going. That's why light doesn't need a medium. It isn't carried by particles at all. So it doesn't need an invisible material filling space. Light simply crosses a **vacuum**.

Visual: the regeneration loop on the lane: a teal E pulse grows; around it a plum B ring appears ("changing E makes B"); the B ring changes and a teal E pulse grows just ahead of it ("changing B makes E"); repeat three times along the lane, the chain moving right. The emptied bell jar's outline sits faintly behind (tag "no particles needed"). On "invisible material" the hook's chip "an invisible material filling space?" returns and takes a scribble cross; the other chip "nothing material at all" ticks. On "vacuum" camera back to the Sun-Earth gap: a teal-and-plum wave crosses it.

### 5. the speed of light, c
> In a vacuum, every electromagnetic wave travels at the same speed, the speed of light. We call it c. It's three point oh oh times ten to the eight metres per second. That's three hundred thousand kilometres every second, nearly a million times faster than sound in air. In materials like glass and water, light is slower, but that comes later. In a **vacuum**, it's always c.
