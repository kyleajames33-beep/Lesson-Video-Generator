### 3. gene flow holds a species together

So gene flow keeps one shared set of alleles, one gene pool, and that keeps one species.

### 9. from two species to many
> Now repeat it. Finches spread to more islands with different foods: small seeds, big seeds, insects, even cactus. Each split can follow the same four steps. When one ancestor spreads into many ways of living and splits into many species, that's adaptive radiation. Run that for millions of years, with extinctions pruning some branches, and one ancestor becomes a whole tree of species. ### 13. key notes (quiet, about 60 seconds)
Heading on screen: "Key notes: what to write in your book".
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

- Species (biological species concept): a group that can interbreed in nature and produce fertile offspring. Horse × donkey → mule, almost always sterile, so two species.
- Gene flow: alleles moving between populations when they interbreed. It keeps one gene pool, so one species.
- Speciation in four steps: (1) isolation stops gene flow; (2) different selective pressures (and chance) act on each part; (3) the gene pools diverge over many generations; (4) reproductive isolation: they can't interbreed and produce fertile offspring, even if they meet.
- Repeated speciation → diversity. Adaptive radiation: one ancestor spreads into many ways of living and splits into many species (Darwin's finches; eucalypts).
