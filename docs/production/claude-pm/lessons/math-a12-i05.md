- **Author notes (not learner copy):**
  (a) Flip: if g(x) = −f(x), then g′(x) = −f′(x): at the same x, every slope is reversed. For f = cos: g′ = sin. (The i04 mirror was left to right, at matching points (x, y) and (−x, y); this flip is upside down, at the same x. Both reverse slopes; the narration names each precisely.)
  (b) Squash: sin(5x) = S(5x) with S = sin; at matching points (u, sin u) and (u/5, sin u), d/dx sin 5x = 5 cos 5x. At x = 0: 1 → 5. For tan(2x − π/3): squash by 2, then slide by π/6 (2x − π/3 = 2(x − π/6)); the slide changes no slopes, the squash doubles them, and the 2 in front matches, so the factor in front of tan is 1. The narration says "the minus pi over three is a slide, and a slide changes no slopes" without stating the slide distance π/6, which would invite confusion with π/3.
  (c) cos(x²) has no elementary primitive (its antiderivative is a Fresnel integral); the narration says only "these rules can't integrate it", which is true and scoped to this video's rules. x cos(x²) would work: ½ sin(x²) + C (not shown).
  (d) Poles of tan x: x = π/2 + kπ. Poles of tan(2x − π/3): 2x − π/3 = π/2 + kπ, x = 5π/12 + kπ/2: −π/12 (k = −1), 5π/12 (k = 0), 11π/12 (k = 1). The general primitive of sec² x is tan x + C_k on each interval (−π/2 + kπ, π/2 + kπ), with independent C_k (as in i04 author note a). Scene 8: tan(sin x) is defined for every real x because −1 ≤ sin x ≤ 1 and the nearest poles of tan are ±π/2 ≈ ±1.5708; d/dx tan(sin x) = sec²(sin x) cos x. ∫ 3x² cos(x³) dx = sin(x³) + C has no restriction.

## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why cos x fails: its gradients are −sin x, opposite to the heights of sin x, shown at π/2 and 3π/2 (1, 2). Why −cos x works: turning a curve upside down reverses every slope at the same x, and the tracer lands on sin x (2). Why + C: sliding keeps every gradient (2). Why sin x for cos x: its gradients are cos x (3). Why radians: the derivative rules being reversed hold only in radians (3). Why tan x for sec² x, and why one piece: tan's gradients are sec² x ≥ 1, and the asymptotes split the curve (4). Why divide by the inner rate: the squash multiplies every slope by it (5). Why −π/3 adds no factor: a slide changes no slopes (6). Why the matched inside needs 2x out the front: the chain rule produces it, so reversing needs it (7, 8). Why tan(sin x) has no asymptotes: its inside never reaches ±π/2 (8).
- **Misconceptions addressed:** "∫ sin x dx = cos x + C" (1, 2; key note 2; memory aid; feedback 2 crossed by analogy); "multiply by the inner rate" (5; feedback 1, crossed); "the −π/3 changes the factor" (6); "tan's primitive is one curve" (4, 6); "every tan answer has asymptotes" (8); "any inside works" (7, contrast); "degrees work too" (3; key note 1).
- **One idea at a time:** one test point per sentence in 1 and 2; one trig function per scene in 2 to 4; one transformation per cue in 5 and 6; one example per scene from 5 on, except scene 8, which takes its two short examples in turn and clears the first before the second.
