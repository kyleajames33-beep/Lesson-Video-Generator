- **Learner question kept (route).** "How do proportional rates produce exponential growth and decay?" The answer, built in order: when the rate is always the same multiple of the amount, doubling the amount doubles the rate (hook); that rule is dQ/dt = kQ, and k's sign and units say what it means (2); Q = Ae^(kt) satisfies it for every A, and A is the starting value (3); the sign of k picks growth or decay, both curves approach the asymptote Q = 0, and a negative A reflects the curve but makes no sense for an amount (4); data fix k (5, 6, 7); and because the rule never changes, equal time steps always multiply by the same factor (8).

### 2. a rate proportional to the amount
> When a rate of change is always the same multiple of the amount, we say the rate is proportional to the amount. Watch what that does. A taller point gets a steeper tangent, so the curve bends upward as it **climbs**. For a quantity Q changing over time t, the rule is d Q d t equals k Q. The number k is the constant of proportionality. Here k is point zero six, and its unit is per year, because dollars per year divided by dollars leaves per **year**. If k is positive, a positive quantity grows, and faster as it gets bigger. If k is negative, a positive quantity shrinks, and more slowly as it gets **smaller**.

### 3. checking Q equals A e to the k t
> Here's a curve that fits. Q equals A, times e to the k t. Let's check it by substitution. From earlier, the derivative of e to the k t is k, times e to the k t. So d Q d t is A times k, e to the k t, which is k, times A e to the k t. And A e to the k t is Q itself, so d Q d t equals k **Q**. That works for any A. At t equals zero, e to the zero is one, so Q is A. A is the starting **value**. For the money, A is five thousand and k is point zero six, so B equals five thousand, e to the point zero six t. After eight years, that's five thousand e to the point four eight, about eight thousand and eighty dollars, thirty-seven **cents**.

### 4. growth, decay and the asymptote
> Now the shapes. Take A equals two, and change k. With k positive, the curve climbs ever more steeply. Turn k down through zero, and it becomes a flat line, then falls, flattening out as it **drops**. That decay curve gets closer and closer to the t axis, but never reaches it, because e to any power is positive. So Q equals zero is a horizontal **asymptote**. For growth, the same thing happens going back in time. Now make A negative. Every value changes sign, so the graph reflects in the t **axis**. It still obeys d Q d t equals k Q, with the same asymptote. But money or mass can't be negative, so models use a positive A, and t from zero **on**.

### 5. finding k from data
> Often k isn't given, so we find it from data. A town had ten thousand people in twenty ten, and twelve thousand in twenty fifteen. Assuming the growth is proportional, when will it reach twenty thousand? Let t be the years after twenty ten, so A is ten **thousand**. At t equals five, twelve thousand equals ten thousand e to the five k. Divide by ten thousand, so e to the five k is one point **two**. Take the natural log of both sides. Five k is the natural log of one point two, so k is the natural log of one point two, over five, about point zero three six five per **year**.

### 7. decay from a half-life
> Decay works the same way, with k negative. A substance has a half-life of twelve years. That means every twelve years, the amount halves. Start with two hundred grams. After twelve years there are a hundred grams, so a hundred equals two hundred e to the twelve **k**. That gives e to the twelve k equals a half, so twelve k is the natural log of a half, which is minus the natural log of two. So k is minus the natural log of two, over twelve, and that minus sign is the **decay**. After thirty years, Q is two hundred e to the thirty k, which is two hundred times two to the minus two and a half, about thirty-five point four **grams**.

### 14. key notes (quiet, hold about 60 seconds)
Heading on screen: "Key notes: what to write in your book".
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

1. If the rate of change of a positive quantity Q is proportional to Q, then dQ/dt = kQ. k > 0: growth. k < 0: decay. k has units "per unit of time".

## Memory aid check

- **"Equal times, equal factors."** Written for this lesson. Meaning, as said in scene 8 and on the notes screen: over any time gap h, the quantity multiplies by the same factor e^(kh), whatever its value at the start of the gap.
