- **Learner question kept (route).** "How do proportional rates produce exponential growth and decay?" The answer, built in order: when the rate is always the same multiple of the amount, doubling the amount doubles the rate (hook); that rule is dQ/dt = kQ, and k's sign and units say what it means (2); Q = Ae^(kt) satisfies it for every A, and A is the starting value (3); the sign of k picks growth or decay, both curves approach the asymptote Q = 0, and a negative A reflects the curve but makes no sense for an amount (4); data fix k (5, 6, 7); and because the rule never changes, equal time steps always multiply by the same factor (8).

## Practice questions and model answers

**Attempt 1 (scene 9; original wording, HSC style, 3 marks suggested, not an official marking scheme).** "A population P grows at a rate proportional to its size, so that dP/dt = kP. Model answer: (a) P = 800e^(kt). P(10) = 1600 gives e^(10k) = 2, so 10k = ln 2 and k = (ln 2)/10 (per year). (b) P(15) = 800e^(15k) = 800e^(1.5 ln 2) = 800 × 2^(3/2) = 1600√2 ≈ 2262.7, so about 2263. Classic mistake (crossed): "2400".

## Memory aid check

- **"Equal times, equal factors."** Written for this lesson. Meaning, as said in scene 8 and on the notes screen: over any time gap h, the quantity multiplies by the same factor e^(kh), whatever its value at the start of the gap. Assumptions checked: Q = Ae^(kt) with k constant (said: "this only works while k stays constant"); A ≠ 0 (always true for an amount); the factor depends only on the gap, because Q(t + h) / Q(t) = e^(kh). It is true for both growth (factor > 1) and decay (factor < 1); a half-life is the gap whose factor is ½. It helps a new case: a student can estimate or check any answer by stepping in equal gaps (the town passes 20,000 between 2025 and 2030; 30 years of a 12-year half-life lands between 50 g and 25 g; A1's 15 years is one and a half doublings, so between 1600 and 3200 and not on a straight line). It also prevents the linear-interpolation error crossed in feedback 1. Introduced after the cause (2, 3) and both data fits (5 to 7).
- **Considered, not used:** "the more you have, the faster it grows" (true only for k > 0 and Q > 0; it is the hook's idea, said once, not a rule to carry); the half-life formula as a mnemonic (author note c).

- **Revision 3, the car badge.** "× e^(−0.15) ≈ 0.861 every year" is the aid applied to the car (gap h = 1 year, factor e^(kh)); it holds because k is constant in the model. The car's value after 8 years, 30,000 × 0.861^8 ≈ 9036, agrees with scene 3.
