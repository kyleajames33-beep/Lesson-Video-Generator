### 2. one point, moment by moment
> Let's see how the graph is made. The marked point only moves up and down. At every instant, we plot its displacement, how far it is from its rest position, and which way. Above rest is positive. Below rest is negative. And along the bottom, the axis is **time**. So this graph is the story of one point over time. It isn't a photo of the cord. The cord's shape is spread out along distance. This graph is spread out along time, even though the two curves look **alike**.

### 3. reading the period and amplitude
> So the gap between two peaks is a time. It's the time for the point to go up, down and back up again, one full oscillation. That's the **period**. Here the peaks are at zero point one two five seconds and zero point six two five seconds, so the period is zero point five oh seconds. That matches the driver, at two point oh hertz. Any matching pair of points works, like two upward crossings of zero. But on a smooth, regular curve like this, a peak to the next trough is only half a period. The amplitude is the height of a peak above the zero line, three point oh **centimetres**.

### 4. worked example: reading a graph
> Let's read one. Task: a displacement-time graph shows a particle reaching peaks of plus four centimetres, with one peak at zero point one oh seconds and the next at zero point three oh seconds. Find the amplitude, the period and the frequency. The amplitude is the peak height, four centimetres, or zero point zero four **metres**. The period is the time between the peaks, zero point three oh minus zero point one oh, zero point two oh **seconds**. And f is one over T, one over zero point two oh, five point oh **hertz**.
