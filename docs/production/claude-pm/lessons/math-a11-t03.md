### 2. rays: one ray, many angles
> Put the spin on a grid, with the board's centre at the origin. Every angle starts on the positive x-axis and turns from there. Anticlockwise is positive, clockwise is negative. Where the turn stops, draw a ray, a line from the origin out in that direction. A hundred and twenty degrees lands on this **ray**. Go one more full lap, four hundred and eighty degrees, and you land on the same ray. Turn clockwise two hundred and forty degrees, to minus two hundred and forty degrees, and you're there too. Adding or taking away three hundred and sixty degrees never changes the ray. So one ray stands for infinitely many **angles**.

### 3. the definitions
> Now draw a circle of radius r, centred at the origin. The ray meets the circle at a point, x comma y. Call the angle theta, just a letter for the angle. Here's the definition. Cosine theta is x over r, sine theta is y over r, and tangent theta is y over **x**. Why is that fair? For an acute angle, drop a line from the point to the x-axis. That makes a right-angled triangle with adjacent side x, opposite side y, and hypotenuse r. So x over r is adjacent over hypotenuse, the cosine you already know. The new definition agrees with the old one, it just keeps working past ninety **degrees**. And the size of the circle doesn't matter. Double the radius, and x, y and r all double, so the ratios stay the **same**. On a circle of radius one, cosine is just x and sine is just y.

### 4. worked example: the point minus three, four
> Try a point. The point minus three, four is on a circle centred at the origin, and theta is the angle from the positive x-axis to that point. The radius is the distance from the origin, so by Pythagoras, it's the root of nine plus sixteen, which is **five**. Cosine theta is x over r, minus three over five. Sine theta is y over r, four over five. Tangent theta is y over x, four over minus three, so minus four **thirds**. The signs come straight from the coordinates. x is negative, so cosine is negative. Writing cosine theta as just minus three is the **slip**. Divide by r, unless the radius is one.

### 5. the angles on the axes
> Now the angles that land on the axes. At zero degrees, the point is r, zero. So sine zero degrees is zero over r, which is zero, cosine is one, and tangent is zero. At ninety degrees, the point is zero, r. Sine ninety degrees is one, and cosine is zero. Tangent is r over zero, and you can't divide by zero, so tangent ninety degrees is **undefined**. At a hundred and eighty degrees, the point is minus r, zero, so sine is zero and cosine is minus one. At two hundred and seventy degrees, the point is zero, minus r. Sine is minus one, cosine is zero, and tangent is undefined again. At three hundred and sixty degrees, you're back where you **started**. Calling tangent ninety degrees infinity is the **slip**. It isn't a number at all.
