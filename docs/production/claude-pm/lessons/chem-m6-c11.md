## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why the mixture holds both partners: the weak acid stays mostly whole, and the salt dissolves completely (2). Why added acid is taken up: ethanoate, a weak acid's conjugate base, pulls the proton back (3). Why added base is taken up: hydroxide takes a proton from ethanoic acid (4). Why the pH moves a little: the ratio of acid to base changes slightly, and [H₃O⁺] follows it (5, 6). Why moles work: the volume cancels (5). Why equal partners give pH = pKa: the ratio is one (5). Why HCl with NaCl fails: chloride does not take protons back (7). Why the buffer fails: its conjugate base runs out (8). Why capacity is not the pH: the pH depends on the ratio, capacity on the amounts (8). Why blood is buffered: proteins work best in a narrow pH range (9). Why blood copes with acid better: the 20 : 1 ratio, and the lungs and kidneys top it up (10).
- **Misconceptions addressed:** a buffer prevents all change (6, 11); a buffer must be neutral (7); pH = pKa for every buffer (7, the marker moves off pKa); a strong acid with its salt is a buffer (7, crossed); the weak acid takes up added acid (feedback 1, crossed); equal pH means equal protection (8, feedback 2, crossed); the buffer "partly" neutralises added acid (avoided: scene 3 says almost all of it reacts).
