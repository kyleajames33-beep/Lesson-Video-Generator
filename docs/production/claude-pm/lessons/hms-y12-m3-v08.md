### 2. six roles, and naming the role first
> A **healthcare system** is all the organisations, workers, funding and rules that deliver health services. It has six roles, and most people only picture the first. Treating illness and injury. Preventing illness and finding it early. Managing chronic conditions, now its biggest workload, and the one it was least built for. Rehabilitation, so treatment turns back into a life. Protecting people from financial hardship, because a system that cures you and bankrupts you has failed. And public health: tracking disease, controlling outbreaks, and regulating medicines and safety. So before you judge it, name the role. Judge it only on treatment, and it will always look good, because you've picked the test it passes.

### 3. effective at what? Row by row
> Now judge each role in turn, because the verdicts differ, and that's the finding. Treatment is strong: survival from major conditions compares well, and avoidable deaths are below the wealthy-country average. Universal coverage means cost rarely blocks a GP or a hospital bed, and medicines are subsidised. Public health is strong too, from immunisation to tobacco control. Dental care and much allied and psychological care sit largely outside universal coverage. And split federal and state responsibilities leave gaps people fall through. Say only the strengths and it's a brochure. Only the weaknesses, and it's a complaint. And effective isn't the same as efficient, which is outcome for the money.

### 4. equity of access: five gates in series
> Then the question of for whom. **Access** means whether you can actually get care when you need it, and **equity of access** means people with the same need can get the same care, wherever they live and whoever they are. Year 11's access gates regroup into five. Availability: does the service exist, with room to see you? Affordability: what's left to pay, including travel and time off work? Accessibility: can you get there, and get in? Acceptability: would you go, given language, culture and past experience? And appropriateness: does it meet the need you actually have? The gates are in series. Pass four and fail one, and you get no care. And you can see the failures in the data, because outcomes still follow postcode.
