### 2. one strip: the Year 11 rule
> Here's the Year 11 idea again. Draw a straight line to measure from, called a baseline. Then measure widths out to the curved edge, at right angles to it. These widths are called offsets. Now pretend the curve is a straight line between two offsets. The shape becomes a trapezium, and its area is its average width times its length. If the offsets are twelve and sixteen metres, the average width is twelve plus sixteen, divided by two, fourteen metres. The strip is eight metres long, so the area is fourteen times eight, a hundred and twelve square **metres**. Halving the sum and then multiplying by eight is the same as eight over two, times twelve plus **sixteen**. In symbols, A is about h over two, times d f plus d l. Here h is the strip width, d f the first offset, and d l the last **offset**.

### 3. more strips: ends once, middles twice
> With three offsets, there are two strips side by side. Say the offsets are ten, fourteen and twelve metres, six metres apart. The first strip uses ten and fourteen. The second uses fourteen and twelve. So the middle offset, fourteen, belongs to both strips, and it gets counted **twice**. That's the rule. The end offsets count once, and the middle ones count twice. Three times ten plus two lots of fourteen plus twelve is three times fifty, a hundred and fifty square **metres**.
