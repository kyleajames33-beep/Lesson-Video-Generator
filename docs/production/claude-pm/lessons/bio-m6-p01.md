A1 (27.0 %) follows the same rule. A2's corrected value (3.7 %) also uses one decimal place; 5.2 is the 52 ÷ 10 conversion, spoken as "about five point two" because the tenfold concentration is nominal. Science check to confirm this convention is acceptable under the owner's strict significant-figure rule. (f) Shape: survival falls by about 31 % in the first 15 s and the drop flattens towards zero; the video says "falls steeply at first, then levels off towards zero" and does not fit an exponential or claim a mechanism for the shape (shoulders and tails in survival curves have several causes). (g) Countable range: plates with roughly 30 to 300 colonies are commonly treated as countable for bacteria (some yeast protocols use about 30 to 300 or 25 to 250); the 60 s and 120 s plates fall below 30, so their percentages carry more chance variation. Scene 12's accuracy card says so on screen; A2's student had a real problem (low counts) but used an unfair fix; the fair fix is to plate a known larger volume or a known concentration and correct for it, applied to every time or recorded explicitly (feedback 2's "or correct for it" on screen). (h) Dose: UV dose (fluence) is irradiance (W/m²) × time (s) = J/m². A lamp's output changes as it warms up and ages, so "time is not dose" unless irradiance at the plate is measured (UV radiometer). (i) UVC hazard: UVC (100 to 280 nm) causes photokeratitis and erythema; germicidal low-pressure mercury lamps emit mainly at 254 nm; ARPANSA's occupational UV standard (RPS 12) sets exposure limits, and at 254 nm the effective limit can be reached in seconds to minutes close to an unshielded lamp, depending on its output. Some lamps also make ozone (185 nm line); cabinet lamps are commonly ozone-free. A broken lamp releases a small amount of mercury (teacher clean-up procedure). Narration says "can burn skin and injure eyes after a short exposure". ### 3. four fates under UV
> Now add UV, which joins neighbouring thymines, as you saw. Each cell then has one of four fates. No damage: it grows a colony. Damaged, but **repaired**: a colony. So damaged it can't copy its DNA: it stops dividing, and there's **no colony**. Or a changed base gets copied, the cell lives, and it grows a colony that looks just like the others. So the count measures **survival**, and any mutants are counted as survivors.

## Safety facts check (self-check, 21 Oct; science check to confirm)
