Mutation assays need a haploid strain (author note b). The decimal place is a presentation choice, not a precision claim: with a mean of 5 over three plates, the standard error is about 1.3 colonies, so 3.5 % carries about ±0.9 % of chance variation (science check), and A2's 3.7 % is consistent with it. The science check accepted this convention (one decimal place down a table column, the usual biology convention). (g) Countable range: plates with roughly 30 to 300 colonies are commonly treated as countable for bacteria (some yeast protocols use about 30 to 300 or 25 to 250); the 60 s and 120 s plates fall below 30, so their percentages carry more chance variation. Scene 12's accuracy card says so on screen; A2's student had a real problem (low counts) but used an unfair fix; the fair fix is to plate a known larger volume or a known concentration and correct for it, applied to every time or recorded explicitly (feedback 2's "or correct for it" on screen). (h) Dose: UV dose (fluence) is irradiance (W/m²) × time (s) = J/m². A lamp's output changes as it warms up and ages, so time alone is not dose unless irradiance at the plate is measured (UV radiometer). Within one run with a warmed-up, steady lamp, dose is proportional to time, so exposure time is still a valid independent variable (science check M3). (i) UVC hazard: UVC (100 to 280 nm) causes photokeratitis and erythema; germicidal low-pressure mercury lamps emit mainly at 254 nm; ARPANSA's occupational UV standard (RPS 12) sets exposure limits, and at 254 nm the effective limit can be reached in seconds to minutes close to an unshielded lamp, depending on its output (RPS 12 Schedule 1, ICNIRP basis: 30 J/m² effective in 8 h; relative effectiveness about 0.5 at 254 nm, so about 60 J/m² actual; at 1 mW/cm² that is reached in about 6 s; science check M6). Some lamps also make ozone (185 nm line); cabinet lamps are commonly ozone-free. A broken lamp releases a small amount of mercury (teacher clean-up procedure). Narration says "can burn skin and injure eyes after a short exposure". ### 3. four fates under UV
> Now add UV, which joins neighbouring thymines, as you saw. Each cell then has one of four fates. No damage: it grows a colony. Damaged, but **repaired**: a colony. Or a changed base gets copied, the cell lives, and it grows a colony that looks just like the others. So the count measures **survival**, and any mutants are counted as survivors.

### 12. validity, reliability and accuracy

And time alone isn't dose: to know the dose, measure the lamp's **output** at the plate.

## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why a colony marks a survivor: it grows from one cell that kept dividing (2). ## Safety facts check (self-check, 21 Oct; science check to confirm)

- **UVC (germicidal lamp, mainly 254 nm):** non-ionising radiation; GHS does not apply (GHS classifies chemicals). Australian control: ARPANSA, Radiation Protection Standard for Occupational Exposure to Ultraviolet Radiation (RPS 12), sets exposure limits; at 254 nm close to an unshielded germicidal lamp, the limit can be reached within seconds to minutes depending on output. Harms: photokeratitis (painful eye inflammation, appearing hours later) and erythema (skin burn). Control: purpose-built interlocked cabinet, trained staff only, lamp never on with the door open, no student exposure; if no approved cabinet, data only (pre-design). Learner copy: "it can burn skin and injure eyes after a short exposure"; "only trained staff run the interlocked cabinet, and the lamp is never on with the door open". Ozone and mercury (author note i): screen-free, teacher matters.
- **Yeast, *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* (teaching or baker's strain):** risk group 1 under AS/NZS 2243.3:2022 (current edition; Codex to confirm *S. cerevisiae* in its risk group 1 examples; WHO Laboratory Biosafety Manual also lists it as RG1) (microorganisms unlikely to cause human, plant or animal disease; physical containment level 1 laboratory); ATCC lists teaching strains at biosafety level 1 (pre-design source). Rare opportunistic infection is reported in severely immunocompromised people (not stated in learner copy; author note only). Learner copy: "a risk group one organism, unlikely to cause disease". Screen: "possible irritation or allergy in sensitive people".
- **Contaminants on incubated plates:** environmental bacteria and moulds can grow alongside the yeast; their identity is unknown, so grown plates are treated as potentially harmful: sealed (taped), counted through the lid, never opened, sterilised (autoclaved or by the school's approved method) before disposal. Learner copy as scene 13.
- **Ethanol, 70 % (bench disinfection):** ethanol's harmonised EU CLP entry is Flam. Liq. 2, H225 (highly flammable liquid and vapour); 70 % ethanol (flash point about 21 °C) remains H225; many supplier SDSs also list Eye Irrit. 2, H319. Learner copy: "flammable, so keep it away from flames".
- **Sterile saline (0.85 to 0.9 % NaCl):** not classified; no row.
- **Incubator at 30 °C:** no GHS hazard; grey row "low risk".
- **Students:** no chemical or radiation hazard in the analysis task itself; the risk table is the investigation's, for the "justify a safety control" exam pattern.
