### 2. one question for each technique
> Each technique answers a different question. Mass spectrometry asks how heavy the molecule is, and which pieces break off. Infrared asks which bonds are there, and which are missing. Carbon-13 NMR counts the kinds of carbon. Proton NMR shows the kinds of hydrogen, how many of each, and how many neighbours they have. A good order to start with is mass, then groups, then the **map**. The right structure has to agree with every one of them.

### 3. step one: the mass narrows the formula
> Step one, the mass. The molecular ion at seventy-two gives the molecular mass. But on its own, seventy-two could also be C five H twelve, with no oxygen at all. We were told each molecule has one oxygen, and that's what turns seventy-two into C four H eight O. So the mass narrows the formula, and the elements present **fix** it.
