### 3. why the predator lags
> Usually the prey. Watch the model run. The hare count climbs first, and the lynx count is still rising when the hares have already started to fall. Predators can only build up after their food becomes plentiful, because it takes time for them to breed and for their young to grow. And they only decline after the prey have already crashed. That delay is called a lag. So on a predator-prey graph, the curve that peaks first is usually the prey. The predator peak arrives late.

### 6. the fence-and-food experiment
> It can't. So ecologists in the Yukon tested it. They fenced some hare populations to keep predators out, added food to others, and did both together in a third.
