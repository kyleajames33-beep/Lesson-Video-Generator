- **Learner question kept (route).** "Why does differentiating tan x give sec squared x?" The answer, built in order: the slope of y = tan x is never less than 1 and grows towards each asymptote; measured, each slope is one more than the height squared (2); because tan x = sin x/cos x, the quotient rule splits the slope into the top's turn, cos x/cos x = 1 everywhere, and the bottom's turn, sin²x/cos²x = tan²x (3); combined over cos²x, the top is cos²x + sin²x = 1, so the derivative is 1/cos²x, which is sec²x (4).

### 3. the two turns: why the slope is one plus tan squared
> Why? Tan x is sine x over cos x, a quotient. The quotient rule video split a quotient's slope into two turns. The top's turn is the top's slope over the bottom. That's cos x over cos x, which is exactly one, **everywhere**. That's where the one comes from. Now the bottom's turn. Between zero and pi over two, cos x is shrinking, and a shrinking bottom makes a positive fraction grow, so this turn adds. It works out to sine squared over cos squared, which is tan squared x, the height **squared**. At pi over four, that's one plus one, two. At pi over three, one plus three, **four**. And near pi over two, cos x gets close to zero, so the bottom's turn grows without any limit. That's the steep **wall**.

### 4. the proof
> The syllabus asks you to show this result, so here's the proof, line by line. Tan x is sine x over cos x, and cos x can't be **zero**. The quotient rule gives cos x times cos x, minus sine x times minus sine x, all over cos squared x. The two minus signs make a plus, so the top is cos squared x plus sine squared **x**. By the Pythagorean identity, that top is **one**. So the derivative is one over cos squared x. One over cos x has its own name, sec x. So the derivative of tan x is sec squared **x**. And cos squared x is never more than one, so sec squared x is never less than one. That's why the slope never drops below **one**.

### 5. radians and branches

Tan of pi over four is one, but tan of three pi over four is minus one, even though three pi over four comes **later**. So tan x is increasing on each branch, not on its whole domain.
