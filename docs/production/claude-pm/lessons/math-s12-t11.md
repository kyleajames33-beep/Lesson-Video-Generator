### 2. three questions, in order
> Before any rule, draw the diagram from the words, with a north line at every point for bearings. You can't see a matched pair in a sentence. Then ask three questions, in order. First, is there a right angle? If so, use Pythagoras, or sine, cosine and tangent from earlier in the topic. If not, second question. Is there a matched pair, a side and the angle opposite it, both known? Then use the sine **rule**. If not, third question. Do you have two sides and the angle between them, or all three sides? Then it's the cosine **rule**. And if the question asks for an area, look for two sides with the angle between them, and use a half a b sine **C**.

### 4. the ferry: one result into the next
> Now the three questions. No right angle. No matched pair. But two sides and the angle between them, so it's the cosine rule. P R squared is twenty-four squared plus thirty-one squared, minus two times twenty-four times thirty-one times cosine ninety-four degrees, about one thousand six hundred and forty-one. So P R is about forty point five **kilometres**. Keep it in the calculator. Now there is a matched pair, P R with the ninety-four degrees it faces. So the sine rule finds the angle at P, about forty-nine point seven seven degrees. It's acute, because a triangle can only hold one obtuse **angle**. Last, the bearing. The angle at P is turned from the first leg, so add it to zero four two degrees. That's about zero nine two degrees **true**. Giving forty-nine point seven seven as the bearing is the slip. That angle is measured from P Q, not from **north**.

## Practice questions and model answers

**Attempt 1 (scene 8; original, HSC style, 3 marks suggested, not an official marking scheme).** "A survey drone flies from its base B for 2.8 km on a bearing of 060°T to a point P. Model answer: (a) At P the bearing back to B is 060° + 180° = 240°T; the bearing on is 170°T; ∠BPQ = 240° − 170° = 70°. (b) BQ² = 2.8² + 3.5² − 2(2.8)(3.5) cos 70° = 13.386; BQ = 3.66 km. Classic mistake (crossed): "∠BPQ = 170° − 60° = 110°".
