### 1. hook-board (the two types that find amounts)
> This is part two of the five types: the two where you find **amounts**. K and a **start**, asking for the settled concentrations. Or an old equilibrium, something **added**, and a new value, asking how much was added. They are the hardest two, so we take them **one step at a time**.

### 2a. tiny-k-question (type 3: K and a start; which shortcut?)
> Type three: **K and a start**, and you want the settled concentrations. You can use the quadratic formula if you like, but HSC questions are written so you shouldn't need it. Look for a **perfect square** or a **tiny K**. N two O four splits into two N O two, K is **4.0 times ten to the minus four**, and the start is 0.500 moles per litre of N two O four, with no N O two. Which shortcut fits? Find the N O two. Pause here to answer.

Visual: as G03 scene 4a. Task line with both demands: "Which shortcut fits? Find [NO2] at equilibrium."; equation strip N2O4(g) ⇌ 2NO2(g); givens "K = 4.0 x 10^-4", "start: [N2O4] = 0.500 mol/L, no NO2", tags "HSC-style question, written for HSCScience" and "a different temperature from the ICE table video, so a different K". On "quadratic formula" a small grey card "Quadratic formula: allowed. HSC questions are written so you should not need it." writes and fades. Countdown ring 10 seconds with "Pause here to answer." on screen after the speech. Nothing answer-bearing.

### 2b. tiny-k-setup (choose the shortcut)
> If x of the N two O four splits, the N two O four left is **0.500 minus x** and N O two is **two x**, so K is two x all squared, over 0.500 minus x. The bottom is **not a square**, so the square root will not help. But K is **tiny** next to the start, so **drop the x**: 0.500 minus x is about 0.500.

Visual: as G03 scene 4b (unchanged): the table fills Initial 0.500, 0; Change -x, +2x (chips "1 : 2"); Equilibrium 0.500 - x, 2x; "(2x)^2 / (0.500 - x) = 4.0 x 10^-4", tag "K: no unit written". On "not a square" the bottom is ringed and the perfect-square half greys with a cross; on "tiny" the tiny-K half ticks; on "drop the x" the "- x" slides out and the cell shades to "≈ 0.500". Unspoken crossed chip "(2x)^2 = 2x^2", correction "(2x)^2 = 4x^2".

### 2c. tiny-k-solve-and-test
> **Four x squared** over 0.500 is **eight x squared**, and that equals 4.0 times ten to the minus four. So x squared is **5.0 times ten to the minus five**, and x is **0.0071**. N O two is two x, which is **0.014**. **Test it**: the amount taken off the start, 0.0071, is **1.4 per cent** of 0.500, under about 5 per cent, so the shortcut holds.

Visual: as G03 scene 4c (unchanged): "4x^2 / 0.500 = 8x^2 = 4.0 x 10^-4", "x^2 = 5.0 x 10^-5", "x = 0.0071", "[NO2] = 2x = 0.014 mol/L"; the C6b check ruler at "1.4%" under the dashed 5 per cent line ("a school rule of thumb") with a tick; tag "exact answer 0.0140; the shortcut is about 0.7% high"; aid chip "Test it or don't use it". Hold about 2 seconds.

### 3a. huge-k-setup (huge K from products)
> A **huge** K is the mirror image. Start with sulfur trioxide alone, 0.400 moles per litre, where K for sulfur dioxide and oxygen making it is **4.0 times ten to the ten**. Almost nothing splits, so let the **oxygen** be x. Sulfur dioxide is **two x**, and sulfur trioxide is about **0.400**.

Visual: as G03 scene 5a (unchanged): task "Find [O2] at equilibrium"; 2SO2(g) + O2(g) ⇌ 2SO3(g); givens "K = 4.0 x 10^10", "start: [SO3] = 0.400 mol/L, no SO2, no O2", tags "HSC-style question, written for HSCScience" and "a different temperature again, so a different K"; table Initial 0, 0, 0.400; Change +2x, +x, -2x; Equilibrium 2x, x, 0.400 - 2x shaded to 0.400; tag "K is very large; test how little SO3 splits". Hold about 1 second.

### 3b. huge-k-solve
> In K, 0.400 squared is **0.160**, and 0.160 over four x cubed is 0.040 over x cubed. So x cubed is **one times ten to the minus twelve**, and x is **one times ten to the minus four**.

Visual: as G03 scene 5b (unchanged): "0.400^2 / ((2x)^2 x) = 4.0 x 10^10", "0.160 / 4x^3 = 0.040 / x^3 = 4.0 x 10^10", "x^3 = 0.040 / (4.0 x 10^10) = 1.0 x 10^-12", "x = 1.0 x 10^-4 mol/L"; tag "K: no unit written"; unspoken crossed chip "(2x)^2 = 2x^2".

### 3c. huge-k-test-and-reactant-start
> **Test it**: the amount taken off the start, two x, is 0.00020, a tiny part of 0.400. **Now the other way round.** If you **started with just the reactants**, in the ratio of the equation, and K was huge, nearly all of it would turn to **products** first, and you would take a small x **back** from there. An excess of one reactant would stay.

Visual: as G03 scene 5c (unchanged): the ruler with 2x = 0.00020 against 0.400 at "0.05%", tick, bar labelled "2x, the amount taken off the start"; "[O2] = 1.0 x 10^-4 mol/L, [SO2] = 2.0 x 10^-4 mol/L"; on "started with just the reactants" a coded mini diagram runs reactants to a full product bar, then a small "take x back" bar; tag "huge K from reactants in the equation's ratio: go to products first, then take x back; an excess reactant stays". Hold about 2 seconds.

### 4a. addition-setup (type 5, worked example: the amount added)
> Type five is the hardest: an **amount added**. Carbon monoxide and steam make carbon dioxide and hydrogen, K is **4.00**, and a **2.00 litre** vessel holds, at equilibrium, 0.400 moles of carbon monoxide, 0.400 of steam and 0.800 each of carbon dioxide and hydrogen. Steam is added, and the new equilibrium has **0.200 moles** of carbon monoxide. How much steam was added? First **convert** to moles per litre: 0.200, 0.200, 0.400, 0.400. The new carbon monoxide is **0.100**.

Visual: as G03 scene 7a (unchanged): task "How many moles of steam were added?"; CO(g) + H2O(g) ⇌ CO2(g) + H2(g); givens "K = 4.00 (same temperature)", "vessel: 2.00 L", "old equilibrium: CO 0.400 mol, H2O 0.400 mol, CO2 0.800 mol, H2 0.800 mol", "steam added", "new equilibrium: CO 0.200 mol"; on "Convert" the amounts divide into a mol/L column (0.200, 0.200, 0.400, 0.400) and the new CO converts to 0.100 mol/L; chip "Concentrations, not moles".

### 4b. addition-ice-table
> **Right after the steam goes in**, before anything reacts, the vessel is the old equilibrium plus the extra steam. So the unknown goes in the **initial row**: steam is the old 0.200 **plus y**, where y is the added steam in moles per litre. The new carbon monoxide, 0.100, goes in the **equilibrium row** first, so its change is 0.100 minus the old 0.200, which is **minus 0.100**, and the ratio is one to one all round. Steam also falls 0.100, because some of the added steam is **used up**, and the two products each rise 0.100.

Visual: as G03 scene 7b (unchanged): caption "the instant after adding, before anything reacts: old equilibrium + y"; Initial 0.200, "0.200 + y" (y in pen colour, "y = steam added, mol/L"), 0.400, 0.400; the pen arrow puts 0.100 into the CO Equilibrium cell; the "E - I" bracket with "0.100 - 0.200" gives "-0.100"; chips "1 : 1 : 1 : 1" give H2O -0.100, CO2 +0.100, H2 +0.100; Equilibrium "0.100 + y", 0.500, 0.500; ghost crossed chip "y in the change row"; aid "The added substance goes in the initial row".

### 4c. addition-k-equation (pause here and try it)
> At equilibrium that is 0.100, **0.100 plus y**, 0.500 and 0.500. K has not changed, because the temperature has not: it is **4.00**. So 0.500 times 0.500, all over 0.100 times the bracket 0.100 plus y, equals 4.00. Solve for y, in **moles per litre**. Pause here and try it.

Visual: as G03 scene 7c (unchanged): "0.500 x 0.500 / (0.100 x (0.100 + y)) = 4.00", tag "K: no unit written"; "y = ? mol/L" with the countdown ring for 10 seconds and "Pause here and try it." beside it. Nothing answer-bearing.

### 4d. addition-answer-and-slips
> The top is **0.250**, so 0.100 plus y is 0.250 divided by the product of 4.00 and 0.100, which is **0.625**, and y is **0.525** moles per litre. The vessel is 2.00 litres, so the steam added is **1.05 moles**. Check: 0.250 divided by the product of 0.100 and 0.625 is 4.00. The slips: 0.525 on its own forgets the **volume**. 0.425 moles per litre forgets that some steam was **used up**.

Visual: as G03 scene 7d (unchanged): "0.500 x 0.500 = 0.250", "0.100 + y = 0.250 / (4.00 x 0.100) = 0.625", "y = 0.525 mol/L", "0.525 mol/L x 2.00 L = 1.05 mol" with a tick; check "0.250 / (0.100 x 0.625) = 0.250 / 0.0625 = 4.00", teal "= K"; crossed chips "0.525 (mol/L, not mol): volume forgotten" and "0.425 mol/L: steam used up not counted"; unspoken mini line "Q right after adding steam: 0.160 / 0.145 = 1.10, below K = 4.00, so forward"; aid chip "Initial row: plus y. K stays." Hold about 2 seconds.

### 6. key-notes (reuse take: key-notes; quiet, hold about 45 seconds)
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

- A start and a K, asked for concentrations: find x. An old equilibrium, an addition and a new value: find the amount added.
- K and a start: HSC questions are written so you shouldn't need the quadratic formula (you can use it). Perfect square: take the square root. Tiny K: drop the x from "start minus x". Huge K: most of the start ends as products (reactants in the equation's ratio; an excess stays), so find the small amount left over. Test any shortcut: the amount taken off the start must be under about 5 per cent of the start (a school rule of thumb).
- Amount added: convert to concentrations first. The new value goes in the Equilibrium row first. The added substance goes in the initial row as the old equilibrium plus y, and it is also used up in the change row.
- K is unchanged at the same temperature. Solve for y in mol/L, then multiply by the volume to give moles.

Memory aids (large, on the notes screen): *Test it or don't use it.* Second line: *Adding changes Q, not K.* Condition tag: "same temperature, equation as written; 5 per cent is a rule of thumb".

## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why the square root does not apply to the N2O4 case and why the tiny K lets x be dropped, with the per cent test as the reason it is safe (2b, 2c); why a huge K means a small x, including from reactants in the equation's ratio (3a to 3c); why the new table starts from the old equilibrium plus y and why the added substance is also used up (4b); why K stays (4c); why the answer is multiplied by the volume (4d).
- **Misconceptions and slips addressed:** untested shortcut (2c, 3c); (2x)^2 as 2x^2 (2b, 3b chips); y in the change row, added substance not used, volume forgotten (4b, 4d); thinking K changes when something is added (4c).
- **Response opportunities:** two, each 10 s with the countdown and nothing answer-bearing: 2a (which shortcut, find NO2; "Pause here to answer.") and 4c (solve for y; "Pause here and try it."). There is no separate unseen type-five attempt (see Decisions: moved out for length); G03c gives unseen practice.

- **Out of scope by design:** types 1, 2 and 4 (G03a), marks allocation, colorimetry, temperature and K (C9).
