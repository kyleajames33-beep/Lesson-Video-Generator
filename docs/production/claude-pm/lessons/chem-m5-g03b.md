## Decisions

- **One main learner question:** *every HSC equilibrium calculation is one of five types. How do you tell which one you have, and how do you do the two cases you have not met yet: huge K and an amount added?*
- **Type numbering (consistent with C6a's "three types", types 1 to 3):** (1) settled concentrations, find K (C6a); (2) a start plus one equilibrium value, ICE table then K (C6a); (3) K and a start, find the concentrations, with a perfect square or a tiny K (C6b), plus the huge-K case taught here; (4) a mixture and K, compare Q with K for the direction (C8, "the Q video"); (5) an amount added, taught here. The five-card board names all five in the hook and returns as the "You see / You do" decision board.
- **What is taught here, and what is recalled:** types 1, 2 and 4 and the perfect-square and tiny-K cases of type 3 are named with where they were taught, not re-taught (no double teaching). New methods: huge K (3a to 3c) and the amount added (4a to 4d).
- **Cause chain taught, in order:** (1) what a question gives and what it asks decide the type (1, 5); (2) recall: a tiny K means hardly any reactant changes, so x is tiny next to the start and is dropped from start minus x, then tested (2); (3) a huge K is the mirror image: the reaction runs almost fully to products, so the small unknown is what is left of the reactants, including when you start from reactants in the equation's ratio (3a to 3c); (4) amounts are converted to concentrations first (4a); (5) right after an addition, before anything reacts, the vessel is the old equilibrium plus the added substance, so y sits in the initial row; the new value goes in the Equilibrium row first and gives the change; the added substance is partly used up, so the change row also has it (4b); (6) an addition changes Q and leaves K alone, so the old K is reused (4c); (7) solve for y in mol/L, then multiply by the volume (4d).
- **Entry knowledge:** C1 to C8, including C6a (the three types, ICE tables with the measured value in the Equilibrium row first), C6b (x from the coefficients, the perfect-square root, the tiny-K drop with its 5 per cent check, "multiply both sides by the bottom, then divide") and C8 (Q and the Q against K rule); C3 (the direction after adding a reactant); Year 11 (c = n / V, square and cube roots, powers of ten). Nothing about huge-K shortcuts or addition problems is assumed. G03a is not entry knowledge (dropped).
- **Start and stop:** starts at "three types in the C6 videos, two more in HSC questions, five in all"; stops after the learner can name all five types from what a question gives and asks, use the huge-K shortcut with its check (including the one-sentence reactant start), and solve an amount-added problem in a vessel that is not 1 L, answering in moles.
- **Handoff:** chem-m5-g03c (practice questions, each with a trap), said in scene 5. G03c hands off to G03d, then C9.
- **Units policy and K values:** K without a unit (tag at each substitution); mol/L in the headings; y in mol/L until the last line of 4d. 2SO2 + O2 ⇌ 2SO3 has K = 4.0 x 10^10 here (tag "a different temperature from other SO3 questions, so a different K" in 3a; Revision 2 rewords this on-screen tag, which said "again" after the cut G03a values).
- **No separate mistake scene.** Named inside each type: dropping x without a test (3c), (2x)^2 written as 2x^2 (3b chip), y in the change row, the added substance not used up, the volume forgotten (4b, 4d). The decision board carries one slip per type.
- **Owner rules applied:** I for initial, C for change, E for equilibrium (said in scene 5, shown on the type 2 card in scene 1); E row first (4b, scene 5); "multiply both sides by the bottom, then divide" (pen tag in 3b and 4d, key note 5); any question uses "Pause here to answer." with a 10 s countdown (no new question is added; the reused 4c prompt is the recorded try-it, "Pause here and try it.", with the 10 s countdown, kept verbatim as instructed); no mark-allocation talk; plain subject-action sentences; no rate algebra.
- **Quadratic line:** not needed here. The cut tiny-k-question carried it; C6b scene 10 says it, and the only type 3 case here has a huge K that passes its test. It is not repeated (author note a).
- **Reuse (no prototype components):** the C6a type cards extended to five (built for the G03 board), the C6 explainer board and ICE table with the C6a E-row-first arrow and "E - I" bracket; the C5 expression builder; the C6 algebra line; the C6b check ruler and its tiny-K thumbnail; the countdown ring; the key notes board. ### 1. hook-board (five types: what you're given and what you find)
> In the C6 videos we met **three types** of calculation. Two more turn up in HSC questions, so there are **five in all**. **Type one** gives you the settled concentrations, and you find K. **Type two** gives you a start and one equilibrium value, so you fill an ICE table, then find K. Those two are from the **first C6 video**. **Type three** gives you K and a start, and you find the concentrations, using a perfect square or a tiny K, from the **second C6 video**. And today, one more case: a **huge K**. **Type four** gives you a mixture and K, and you compare Q with K to find the direction, as in the **Q video**. **Type five** asks how much was **added**, and that's today too.

Visual: heading "What you are given and asked decides the type" writes on. On "three types" three cards slide in as a column (the C6a type cards); on "five in all" two more cards slide in below them and the column numbers 1 to 5. Each card writes its line as its cue word is said: (1) "Given: settled concentrations. Find: K." tag "C6a"; (2) "Given: a start + one equilibrium value. Do: ICE table (I initial, C change, E equilibrium), then K." tag "C6a"; on "first C6 video" cards 1 and 2 tick; (3) "Given: K + a start. Find: the concentrations (x)." with two sub-chips "perfect square" and "tiny K" tagged "C6b" (ticked on "second C6 video"), and on "huge K" a third sub-chip "huge K" lifts and lights in pen colour with the tag "today"; (4) "Given: a mixture + K. Do: Q against K, so the direction." tag "C8, the Q video", ticked on "Q video"; (5) "Given: an old equilibrium + something added + a new value. Find: how much was added (y)." On "added" card 5 lifts and lights with the tag "today". The lit huge-K chip and card 5 stay lit; the ticked cards dim slightly. Hold about 2 seconds.

### 2. type-three-recall (tiny K recalled, so huge K has its mirror)
> **Type three** first. In the second C6 video, a **tiny K** meant hardly any reactant changed, so x was tiny next to the start. We **dropped it** from start minus x, then **tested it**.

Visual: card 3 slides to the top-left and shrinks to a badge "Type 3: K + start". ### 3a. huge-k-setup (type 3, the new case: huge K from products)
> A **huge** K is the mirror image. Start with sulfur trioxide alone, 0.400 moles per litre, where K for sulfur dioxide and oxygen making it is **4.0 times ten to the ten**. Almost nothing splits, so let the **oxygen** be x. Sulfur dioxide is **two x**, and sulfur trioxide is about **0.400**.

Visual: as G03 scene 5a (one tag reworded, Revision 2): task "Find [O2] at equilibrium"; 2SO2(g) + O2(g) ⇌ 2SO3(g); givens "K = 4.0 x 10^10", "start: [SO3] = 0.400 mol/L, no SO2, no O2", tags "HSC-style question, written for HSCScience" and "a different temperature from other SO3 questions, so a different K"; table Initial 0, 0, 0.400; Change +2x, +x, -2x; Equilibrium 2x, x, 0.400 - 2x shaded to 0.400; tag "K is very large; test how little SO3 splits". Hold about 1 second.

### 3b. huge-k-solve
> In K, 0.400 squared is **0.160**, and 0.160 over four x cubed is 0.040 over x cubed. So x cubed is **one times ten to the minus twelve**, and x is **one times ten to the minus four**.

Visual: as G03 scene 5b (unchanged): "0.400^2 / ((2x)^2 x) = 4.0 x 10^10", "0.160 / 4x^3 = 0.040 / x^3 = 4.0 x 10^10", "x^3 = 0.040 / (4.0 x 10^10) = 1.0 x 10^-12", "x = 1.0 x 10^-4 mol/L"; tag "K: no unit written"; unspoken crossed chip "(2x)^2 = 2x^2". Adjusted 16 Oct: beside the x^3 line a small pen tag "multiply both sides by the bottom, then divide" writes.

### 3c. huge-k-test-and-reactant-start
> **Test it**: the amount taken off the start, two x, is 0.00020, a tiny part of 0.400. **Now the other way round.** If you **started with just the reactants**, in the ratio of the equation, and K was huge, nearly all of it would turn to **products** first, and you would take a small x **back** from there. An excess of one reactant would stay.

Visual: as G03 scene 5c (unchanged): the ruler with 2x = 0.00020 against 0.400 at "0.05%", tick, bar labelled "2x, the amount taken off the start"; "[O2] = 1.0 x 10^-4 mol/L, [SO2] = 2.0 x 10^-4 mol/L"; on "started with just the reactants" a coded mini diagram runs reactants to a full product bar, then a small "take x back" bar; tag "huge K from reactants in the equation's ratio: go to products first, then take x back; an excess reactant stays". Hold about 2 seconds.

### 4a. addition-setup (type 5, worked example: the amount added)
> Type five is the hardest: an **amount added**. Carbon monoxide and steam make carbon dioxide and hydrogen, K is **4.00**, and a **2.00 litre** vessel holds, at equilibrium, 0.400 moles of carbon monoxide, 0.400 of steam and 0.800 each of carbon dioxide and hydrogen. Steam is added, and the new equilibrium has **0.200 moles** of carbon monoxide. How much steam was added? First **convert** to moles per litre: 0.200, 0.200, 0.400, 0.400. The new carbon monoxide is **0.100**.

Visual: as G03 scene 7a (unchanged): task "How many moles of steam were added?"; CO(g) + H2O(g) ⇌ CO2(g) + H2(g); givens "K = 4.00 (same temperature)", "vessel: 2.00 L", "old equilibrium: CO 0.400 mol, H2O 0.400 mol, CO2 0.800 mol, H2 0.800 mol", "steam added", "new equilibrium: CO 0.200 mol"; on "Convert" the amounts divide into a mol/L column (0.200, 0.200, 0.400, 0.400) and the new CO converts to 0.100 mol/L; chip "Concentrations, not moles".

### 4b. addition-ice-table
> **Right after the steam goes in**, before anything reacts, the vessel is the old equilibrium plus the extra steam. So the unknown goes in the **initial row**: steam is the old 0.200 **plus y**, where y is the added steam in moles per litre. The new carbon monoxide, 0.100, goes in the **equilibrium row** first, so its change is 0.100 minus the old 0.200, which is **minus 0.100**, and the ratio is one to one all round. Steam also falls 0.100, because some of the added steam is **used up**, and the two products each rise 0.100.

Visual: as G03 scene 7b (unchanged): caption "the instant after adding, before anything reacts: old equilibrium + y"; Initial 0.200, "0.200 + y" (y in pen colour, "y = steam added, mol/L"), 0.400, 0.400; the pen arrow puts 0.100 into the CO Equilibrium cell; the "E - I" bracket with "0.100 - 0.200" gives "-0.100"; chips "1 : 1 : 1 : 1" give H2O -0.100, CO2 +0.100, H2 +0.100; Equilibrium "0.100 + y", 0.500, 0.500; ghost crossed chip "y in the change row"; aid "The added substance goes in the initial row".

### 4c. addition-k-equation (pause here and try it)
> At equilibrium that is 0.100, **0.100 plus y**, 0.500 and 0.500. K has not changed, because the temperature has not: it is **4.00**. So 0.500 times 0.500, all over 0.100 times the bracket 0.100 plus y, equals 4.00. Solve for y, in **moles per litre**. Pause here and try it.

Visual: as G03 scene 7c (unchanged): "0.500 x 0.500 / (0.100 x (0.100 + y)) = 4.00", tag "K: no unit written"; "y = ? mol/L" with the countdown ring for 10 seconds and "Pause here and try it." beside it. Nothing answer-bearing.

### 4d. addition-answer-and-slips
> The top is **0.250**, so 0.100 plus y is 0.250 divided by the product of 4.00 and 0.100, which is **0.625**, and y is **0.525** moles per litre. The vessel is 2.00 litres, so the steam added is **1.05 moles**. Check: 0.250 divided by the product of 0.100 and 0.625 is 4.00. The slips: 0.525 on its own forgets the **volume**. 0.425 moles per litre forgets that some steam was **used up**.

Visual: as G03 scene 7d (unchanged): "0.500 x 0.500 = 0.250", "0.100 + y = 0.250 / (4.00 x 0.100) = 0.625", "y = 0.525 mol/L", "0.525 mol/L x 2.00 L = 1.05 mol" with a tick; check "0.250 / (0.100 x 0.625) = 0.250 / 0.0625 = 4.00", teal "= K"; crossed chips "0.525 (mol/L, not mol): volume forgotten" and "0.425 mol/L: steam used up not counted"; unspoken mini line "Q right after adding steam: 0.160 / 0.145 = 1.10, below K = 4.00, so forward"; aid chip "Initial row: plus y. K stays." Adjusted 16 Oct: beside the "0.100 + y" line a small pen tag "multiply both sides by the bottom, then divide" writes. Hold about 2 seconds.

### 5. decision-board (all five types: You see / You do)
> So read what you're **given** and what you're **asked**. **Settled concentrations**: put them into K. **A start and one equilibrium value**: an ICE table, I for initial, C for change, E for equilibrium, with the **E row first**. **K and a start**: call the change x, look for a perfect square, a tiny K or a huge K, and **test any shortcut**. **A mixture and K**: work out Q and compare. **Something added**: y in the initial row, K unchanged, answer in moles. **Next**: practice questions, each with a **trap** in it.

### 6. key-notes (reuse take: key-notes; quiet, hold about 45 seconds)
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

- Type 1, settled concentrations: put them into K (divide moles by the volume first).
- Type 2, a start and one equilibrium value: ICE table, the measured value in the E row first, then K.
- Type 3, K and a start: call the change x; use a perfect square, a tiny K or a huge K, then test the shortcut (under about 5 per cent of the start).
- Type 4, a mixture and K: work out Q and compare. Q below K: forward. Q above K: reverse.
- Type 5, an amount added: old equilibrium plus y in the I row, the new value in the E row first, K unchanged; solve for y, then multiply by the volume for moles.

Memory aids (large, on the notes screen): *What you are given and what you are asked decide the method.* Second line: *Test it or don't use it. Adding changes Q, not K.* Condition tag: "same temperature, equation as written; 5 per cent is a rule of thumb".

## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why the type follows from what is given and asked (1, 5); why a tiny K lets x be dropped, recalled (2); why a huge K means a small x of reactant left, including from reactants in the equation's ratio (3a to 3c); why the new table starts from the old equilibrium plus y and why the added substance is also used up (4b); why K stays (4c); why the answer is multiplied by the volume (4d).
- **Misconceptions and slips addressed:** untested shortcut (3c); (2x)^2 as 2x^2 (3b chip); y in the change row, added substance not used, volume forgotten (4b, 4d); thinking K changes when something is added (4c); one slip per type on the decision board.
- **Response opportunities:** one, the recorded try-it in 4c ("Pause here and try it.", 10 s countdown, nothing answer-bearing). The tiny-K pause question is cut with its scenes. G03c and G03d give unseen practice (G03d question 3 is a tiny-K question).
- **Worked-example rule:** one stage per scene (3a set up, 3b solve, 3c test; 4a convert, 4b table, 4c K equation, 4d solve and slips). Task, equation and givens stay; earlier boards clear.
- **Avoided double teaching:** types 1, 2 and 4, the perfect square and the tiny K are named with where they were taught (C6a, C6b, C8), not re-taught.
- **Out of scope by design:** marks allocation, colorimetry, temperature and K (C9), the quadratic formula.

## Memory aid check

- **"What you are given and what you are asked decide the method."** Holds for all five types. Limit: real questions can combine types (an ICE table then Q, or K found first and then reused for an addition).
- **"Test it or don't use it."** Assumption: the 5 per cent convention on the amount taken off the start (x in scene 2, 2x in 3c).
- **"Concentrations, not moles."** Holds in 4a; the volume cancels in this K (equal gas counts) but not in the final amount (4d).
- **"Initial row: plus y. K stays." and "Adding changes Q, not K."** Assumption: constant volume and temperature, the instant-after-adding picture. Holds in 4b to 4d.
