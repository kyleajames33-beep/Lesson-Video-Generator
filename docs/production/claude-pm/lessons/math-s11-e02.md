### 1. hook: the same hours on a public holiday
> Last time ended outside this shop. It's open on a public holiday, and the roster shows the same hours as any other day. So is a public holiday shift paid the same? Usually not. Most jobs pay a higher rate for some hours, like overtime, weekends and public holidays. That higher rate is called a penalty **rate**. It pays people more for long weeks, and for working while most people are off. So how do overtime and penalty rates change a pay **packet**?

### 2. a multiplier makes the new rate
> Every penalty rate starts from the ordinary rate, the normal hourly rate. Say it's twenty-eight dollars sixty an hour. A penalty rate is the ordinary rate times a multiplier. Time and a quarter is times one point two five, thirty-five dollars seventy-five an **hour**. Time and a half is times one point five, forty-two dollars **ninety**. Double time is times two, and double time and a half is times two point five, seventy-one dollars **fifty**. The multiplier gives the whole new rate, not just the extra bit. In real jobs, an award, the legal minimum pay for that kind of work, sets the multipliers, often written as percentages, so a hundred and twenty-five per cent means times one point two five. Award rates change every first of July, and a question always tells you the **multiplier**.

### 3. overtime: each block at its own rate
> Overtime means hours worked beyond your ordinary hours, often after thirty-eight in a week. Nina earns twenty-eight dollars sixty an hour. One week she works thirty-eight ordinary hours, plus four hours of overtime at time and a half. Work each block at its own rate. Thirty-eight hours at twenty-eight sixty is one thousand and eighty-six dollars **eighty**. Four hours at forty-two ninety is a hundred and seventy-one dollars **sixty**. Add the blocks, one thousand two hundred and fifty-eight dollars forty. Think of each block as a rectangle, hours across and rate up, so its area is the pay. Putting all forty-two hours at one rate flattens the overtime step, and the pay comes out too **low**.

### 5. tiers and an allowance on a pay slip
> Some jobs pay overtime in tiers. In a Christmas rush, a warehouse pays thirty-two dollars an hour for thirty-eight ordinary hours, time and a half for the first two overtime hours, and double time after that. This week there are five overtime hours, so two at time and a half and three at double time, and an eighteen dollar meal allowance. On the pay slip, ordinary pay is one thousand two hundred and sixteen **dollars**. The first two overtime hours, at forty-eight dollars, make ninety-six. The next three, at sixty-four dollars each, make a hundred and **ninety-two**. An allowance is a flat amount for a cost of the job, like a meal or tools, so it's just added on, never multiplied. Gross pay, the total before anything is taken out, is one thousand five hundred and twenty-two **dollars**.
