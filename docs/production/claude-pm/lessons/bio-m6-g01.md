Reading on, the chain is methionine, proline, glutamate, phenylalanine, glycine.

### 2c. q1-mutants (one triplet at a time)

In mutant three, the fourth codon becomes U C U, **serine** instead of phenylalanine. That's **missense**.

### 3b. q2-align (base by base)

### 3c. q2-frame (why six and seven are normal)
> Now read in threes. The deletion **shifts the frame**, so codons two to five are regrouped, and those amino acids change. The insertion adds one base back. One removed, one added, so from codon six on, the bases fall into their **normal groups of three** again. That's why six and seven are normal. Results like this helped show that the code is read in threes.

### 4c. q3-karyotype (one chromosome missing)

### 7. key-notes (reuse take: key-notes; quiet, about 60 seconds)
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

- A template strand is paired to make mRNA (A to U, T to A, C to G, G to C); compare mutants one codon at a time and read the chart with mRNA codons.
- A substitution changes one codon: silent (same amino acid), missense (different amino acid) or nonsense (stop codon, chain cut short).
- Align an insertion or deletion base by base. A change that is not a multiple of three shifts the frame; a second change can restore it, so only the codons between them change.
- Inversion: a segment reversed on the same chromosome (it can include the centromere). Translocation needs a second, non-matching chromosome. A missing or extra whole chromosome is a change in number, from nondisjunction.
- A somatic mutation (for example, in one muscle cell) is not inherited in animals; only a germ-line mutation can be.

Memory aid (large): *Align base by base, then read in threes.*

## Practice questions and model answers

1. (Q1, 2 + 6 marks.) Template 3' TAC GGT CTT AAA CCG 5'. Mutant 1: 3' TAC GGA CTT AAA CCG 5'. Mutant 2: 3' TAC GGT ATT AAA CCG 5'. Mutant 3: 3' TAC GGT CTT AGA CCG 5'. Full codon chart. (a) Write the mRNA for the normal sequence. (b) For each mutant, name the type of point mutation and describe its effect on the polypeptide.
   Model: (a) 5' AUG CCA GAA UUU GGC 3' (Met, Pro, Glu, Phe, Gly). (b) Mutant 1: substitution (template T to A in triplet 2; codon CCA to CCU), silent; both code for proline, so the polypeptide is unchanged. Mutant 2: substitution (C to A in triplet 3; codon GAA to UAA), nonsense; UAA is a stop codon, so the polypeptide ends after Met, Pro and is likely non-functional. Mutant 3: substitution (A to G in triplet 4; codon UUU to UCU), missense; phenylalanine is replaced by serine, the rest is unchanged, and the effect depends on where that amino acid sits in the protein. Classic mistake crossed: reading the template as mRNA (UAC GGU CUU AAA CCG: Tyr Gly Leu Lys Pro, no start codon). Unspoken: "mutant 2 is missense".
2. (Q2, 2 + 3 marks.) Normal mRNA 5' AUG GCU AAC GGA UCU CAG UGG 3'; mutant 5' AUG GUA ACG GAU CUA CAG UGG 3'; the mutant still made a working protein. (a) Identify the two point mutations. (b) Explain why amino acids 6 and 7 are normal, although 2 to 5 have changed.
   Model: (a) Deletion of one base (the C, base 5, in codon 2) and insertion of one base (an A, after codon 5). (b) Codons are read in threes from the start codon. The deletion shifts the reading frame, so codons 2 to 5 are regrouped (GUA ACG GAU CUA) and amino acids 2 to 5 change (Ala Asn Gly Ser to Val Thr Asp Leu). The insertion adds one base back, so the net change is zero bases and the frame is restored from codon 6, which is read normally (CAG Gln, UGG Trp). Because only a short stretch changed, the protein still worked. Classic mistake crossed: "a frameshift always changes everything after it". Unspoken: "four separate substitutions".
3. (Q3, 2 + 3 marks.) Normal A B ● C D E F G; mutated A D C ● B E F G. (a) Name the type of chromosomal mutation and justify. (b) Turner syndrome karyotype: 45 chromosomes, 44 autosomes and one X. Identify the type of mutation and explain how it could arise during meiosis in one parent.
   Model: (a) Inversion: the segment B ● C D has been reversed (D C ● B) on the same chromosome, including the centromere; the same genes are present, none lost, added or exchanged with another chromosome. (b) A chromosomal mutation in number (one sex chromosome missing, monosomy X). Nondisjunction: in meiosis in one parent, the sex chromosomes (or sister chromatids) fail to separate, producing a gamete with no sex chromosome; fertilisation by a gamete carrying an X gives a zygote with 44 autosomes and one X (45,X). Classic mistake crossed: "translocation, because the centromere moved". Unspoken: "part b: a deletion".
4. (Q4, 3 + 2 marks.) Belgian Blue: two copies of a myostatin allele with 11 bases deleted from the coding sequence; myostatin limits muscle growth. (a) Explain how the deletion can result in no working myostatin. (b) A cow with two normal alleles develops the same deletion in one leg muscle cell. Explain whether her calves could inherit large muscles as a result.
   Model: (a) 11 is not a multiple of 3, so the deletion shifts the reading frame; every codon after it is regrouped, so the amino acids after the deletion change and a premature stop codon appears; the protein is shortened and lacks its functional part, so it cannot limit muscle growth, and the muscles grow larger (with two copies, no working myostatin is made). (b) No. The mutation is somatic: it is copied by mitosis only into that muscle cell's descendants and is not in her germ cells or eggs, so her calves cannot inherit it from this mutation. Classic mistake crossed: "11 bases deleted, so 11 amino acids lost". Unspoken: "part b: yes, because the cow now carries the allele".
