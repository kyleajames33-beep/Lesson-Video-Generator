### 15. key notes (quiet, about 60 seconds)
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

- A pathogen must leave a host, travel and enter a host it can infect; each control breaks a link. Hygiene: travel. Isolation and quarantine: leaving. Vaccination: the next host.
- Hygiene: soap lifts microbes off skin and water rinses them away; alcohol rub works less well against norovirus and bacterial spores. ## Numbers or facts to verify

Self-check (22 Oct) against the sources named. The Claude science check must confirm each before voicing.
- **Semmelweis.** Vienna General Hospital, First Clinic (medical students and doctors) and Second Clinic (midwives); admissions alternated by day. First Clinic 1846 mortality 11.4% (Semmelweis's tables; 1841 to 1846 average about 10%), Second Clinic 1846 2.8% (average about 3.9%). Chlorinated lime handwashing introduced in May 1847; monthly First Clinic mortality fell from 18.3% (April 1847) to 2.2 to 2.4% (June), 1.2% (July) and 1.9% (August); 1848 annual 1.27% (First) against 1.33% (Second) (Museum of Health Care, Kingston; Semmelweis's published tables; checked 22 Oct). Narration: "about one mother in ten", "far fewer", "deaths fell to about the midwives' level", "a chlorine solution" (chlorinated lime solution releases chlorine; site error 9). "A disease nobody could see" and the on-screen tag "germ theory not yet accepted": correct (Pasteur's and Koch's work came in the 1860s to 1880s). - **Hygiene.** Soap and water remove microbes mechanically (soap lifts oils and attached microbes; running water rinses them off); alcohol-based hand rubs kill many bacteria and enveloped viruses but are less effective against norovirus and bacterial spores such as *Clostridioides difficile*, for which soap and water is preferred (CDC hand hygiene guidance; site L15 misconception box). - **Measles.** Airborne; virus can remain infectious in the air or on surfaces for up to about two hours (NSW measles control guideline: "short survival time (less than 2 hours) in the air or on objects and surfaces"; CDC "up to 2 hours"). Infectious from 24 hours before prodromal symptoms (about 4 days before the rash) until 4 days after rash onset; isolation until 4 days after rash onset; incubation about 10 days to fever and about 14 days to rash; susceptible contacts excluded until 18 days after last contact (NSW guideline, checked 22 Oct); MMR within 72 hours or normal human immunoglobulin within 144 hours of exposure may allow return (NSW; not spoken in Revision 1). Babies in Australia get MMR at 12 months (and MMRV at 18 months) (National Immunisation Program schedule), so "babies too young for the measles vaccine": correct.
