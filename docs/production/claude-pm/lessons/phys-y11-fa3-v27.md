### 3. one turn, along the axis

### 5. the equation
> That's the equation. B equals mu nought, N I, over L. B is the field inside, in tesla. I is the current in amps, which sets how much each turn adds. N is the number of turns, and L is the coil's length in metres, so N over L is the turns per metre, how tightly the turns are packed. It's often written as a small n, so you'll also see B equals mu nought n I. Mu nought is the same constant as for the straight wire: four pi times ten to the minus seven. There's no width anywhere. And it works inside a long, empty coil, away from the **ends**.
