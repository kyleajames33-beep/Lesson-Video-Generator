- **Why zone size is not a ranking of medicines (scene 9; author note for the science check).** Diameter depends on the drug's activity against the organism, the disc content (dose), the drug's diffusion through agar (molecular size, solubility, charge), agar depth and composition, inoculum density, and incubation time and temperature. Clinical laboratories therefore compare each drug's zone with that drug's own published breakpoints for that organism (CLSI or EUCAST tables on standardised Mueller-Hinton agar), never one drug's zone with another's. Vancomycin, a large glycopeptide, diffuses slowly and gives small zones against susceptible organisms; the narration (Revision 2, science S4) says "a drug that spreads slowly through agar can make a small zone even when it works well", names no drug and makes no molecular-size ranking; the screen tag reads "diffusion differs between drugs". C's absent zone: an antibiotic with no target in the organism (for example a drug acting on a wall structure that the strain lacks, or one that cannot cross a Gram-negative outer membrane) gives no zone; the narration says "might mean this bacterium simply lacks the part C targets", which recalls b11's targets and stays conditional. Screen tag: "clinical labs compare each drug with its own cut-off, on standard agar".

### 1. hook (three discs)
> A student put four paper discs on a plate spread with bacteria. Two days later, around disc A, a clear zone twenty five millimetres across. Around disc B, fifteen. Around disc C, **nothing**. So is A the best medicine, and is C useless? The plate can't tell you **either**.

### 2. what a zone is
> Each disc holds a measured dose of an antibiotic. On the agar, the drug **diffuses** outwards, so it's strongest near the disc and weaker further out. Where it's strong enough to stop the bacteria growing, the agar stays clear. That clear ring is the **zone of inhibition**. At its edge, the drug has become too dilute, and beyond it the bacteria grow into a cloudy layer.

Visual: step rail lights "1 what a zone is". On "measured dose" `m7-antibiotic-disc-cartridge-v1.png` (new) appears small at the left (tag "commercial discs: fixed dose, printed by the supplier"). On "zone of inhibition" the gloss writes: "zone of inhibition: the clear ring where the drug stopped growth". ### 3. aim and variables
> The aim is to compare the zones of inhibition that three antibiotic discs produce on one harmless bacterium. The independent variable is the **antibiotic**. The dependent variable is the zone's **diameter**, in millimetres. Keep everything else the **same**: the bacterium and how much you spread, the agar and its depth, the disc size, and the incubation time and temperature.

### 4. the control and repeats

### 5. lawn and discs
