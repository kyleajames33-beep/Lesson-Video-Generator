### 2. a complete path
> First, the rule underneath it all. Charge only flows round a complete path: out of one terminal of the cell, through the components, and back into the other. Open a gap anywhere, with a switch or a broken filament, and charge can't cross it. It can't keep piling up at the gap either, so the drift quickly stops all the way round the loop. Watch the charges in the wires: they don't drain away, they just stop where they are. No complete path, no **current**.

### 3. series: one path
> In series, the components are joined one after another, in a single loop. There's only one path, so every coulomb passes through every globe in turn. ### 4. parallel: a path each

### 5. sharing the energy, and why homes use parallel

Every appliance gets the full supply voltage, and each one can be switched on and off by **itself**.
