- **Learner question kept (outline).** "How do displacement, velocity and acceleration connect in both directions?" The answer, built in order: velocity is how fast displacement changes, so it is the gradient of the displacement graph, v = dx/dt, and v = 0 where the particle turns (2); acceleration is how fast velocity changes, a = dv/dt = d²x/dt² = ẍ (3); whether the particle speeds up depends on the signs of v and a together, not on a alone (4); integrating velocity gives the change in displacement, while the distance travelled adds the size of every leg (5); integrating goes back up, but each integration loses the starting value, so a stated starting value fixes each constant (6); a zero displacement can hide real motion (7); the same two directions work for any quantity Q(t), with d²Q/dt² telling how the rate is changing (8).

### 2. velocity is the gradient of displacement
> Let's find out. Displacement is the particle's position measured from the origin, and it's negative on the left. Velocity is how fast displacement changes, so it's the gradient of the displacement graph: v equals d x by d t. Differentiate, and v is three t squared minus eighteen t plus twenty-four, which factors to three, t minus two, t minus **four**. So the particle is at rest at two seconds and at four seconds, where the graph is **flat**. In between, v is negative, and the particle moves left. Speed is the size of the velocity, without its sign.

### 4. speeding up or slowing down
> Now the hook. At two and a half seconds, v is minus nine quarters, and the acceleration is minus three. The velocity is already negative, and a negative acceleration pushes it further below zero, so the speed grows, from two and a quarter metres per second towards **three**. The particle is speeding up, even though its acceleration is negative. Compare one second. There, v is nine, a is minus twelve, and the particle is slowing down as it heads for rest. So when velocity and acceleration have the same sign, it speeds up. Opposite signs, it slows **down**.

### 15. key notes (quiet, hold about 60 seconds)

> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.
