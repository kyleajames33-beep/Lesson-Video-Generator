- **Cause chain taught, in order:** (1) velocity is the rate of change of displacement, so it is the gradient of the x–t graph (2); (2) the particle turns where the gradient is zero, so v = 0 there (2); (3) acceleration is the rate of change of velocity, the second derivative of displacement (3); (4) a negative acceleration makes the velocity more negative; if the velocity is already negative, its size grows, so the particle speeds up (4); (5) so speeding up depends on both signs (4); (6) velocity is a rate, so its integral is the change in displacement, while distance adds every leg as positive (5); (7) integration recovers a velocity only up to a constant, because sliding a curve keeps its gradients, so a starting value is needed for each step up (6); (8) a zero displacement can hide motion away and back (7); (9) any quantity of time has a rate dQ/dt and a rate of the rate d²Q/dt², and the quantity peaks where its rate is zero (8); (10) memory aid (9).

## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why v = dx/dt: velocity is how fast displacement changes, so it is the gradient (2). Why rest at v = 0: the particle turns where the gradient is zero, shown by flat tangents and the stopping dot (2). Why ẍ: acceleration is how fast velocity changes, so it is the derivative of a derivative (3). Why a negative acceleration can speed a particle up: it pushes an already negative velocity further from zero (4). Why ∫ v dt is not the distance: backward legs subtract in the integral, while distance counts every leg (5). ## Memory aid check
