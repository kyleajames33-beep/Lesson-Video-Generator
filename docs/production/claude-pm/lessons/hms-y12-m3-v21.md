### 2. the syllabus example, as a model
> In Year 11 you localised the goals: start from a local need, and act across the Ottawa action areas. The syllabus names one body that did this: Healthy Cities Illawarra. Treat it as a model, not a list of projects. For decades it was a community-based organisation working on the conditions that produce health, not on treating illness. It worked by convening, bringing councils, health services, schools, businesses and community groups together on local priorities. Its work maps onto the four goals. Food security, activity and social connection are SDG 3. School programs and helping people take part are SDG 4. Focusing on groups usually left out is SDG 10. And walking and cycling routes, community gardens and speaking up in planning decisions are SDG 11. One body, working on all four, because a community's problems don't arrive sorted by goal. So what transfers isn't any single project. It's having an independent, local, long-lasting body whose job is to hold the connections between sectors.

### 5. what transfers: the mechanism, not the activity
> So what can you take from another community? Use the transfer test from earlier, now for towns instead of countries. Step one, name the mechanism, not the activity. A community garden is an activity. Its mechanism might be food access, or social connection, or a reason for different groups to meet, or people feeling the space is theirs. Step two, can that mechanism work here? Is there a similar gap, a group to hold it, and a setting people use? Step three, what was local? A committed council, a long-standing organisation, one funder, one person. If it's missing here, it has to be built. Step four, check the population, and adapt it with the new community, not for them. Step five, decide what you'll measure before you start. So copy the mechanism, adapt the activity, rebuild what was local, and measure from day one.

### 6. why community-led works better, and its catch
> Here's why involving the community actually works. **Community-led** means the community sets the priorities and the design, not just comments on someone else's plan. Remember the ladder from Year 11: being consulted isn't leading. It finds the real problem: data shows a rate, but locals know the bus stops at six and the shop has closed. It's used, because people helped design it. It lasts, because the community owns it. And having control over decisions that affect your life is good for health in its own right. So the process is part of the intervention. But be honest about the catch. It's slower, it needs time and skills people may not have unpaid, and the loudest voices can take over. So it has to be community-led, properly resourced, and deliberate about including the people usually left out.
