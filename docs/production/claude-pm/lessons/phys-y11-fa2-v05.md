### 2. speed from one period
> Speed is distance travelled divided by the time taken. In one period, the wave moves on one wavelength. So the wave's speed is the wavelength divided by the **period**. For the cord, that's one point five oh metres in zero point five oh seconds, three point oh metres per **second**. For the spring, it's one point oh oh metre in the same half second, two point oh metres per second. So the cord's wave is faster, and that's why its waves are longer. Each crest gets further before the next one starts. This is the speed of the pattern, and the energy it carries. Each particle still only moves back and forth near **home**.

### 3. counting waves in one second: v = f lambda
> There's another way to get the same speed. Stand at one post, and count. Say three waves pass the post each second, and each wave is two metres long. Then six metres of wave go past every second. That's a speed of six metres per second. So the speed is the frequency, how many waves pass each second, times the wavelength, how long each one is. That's the wave **equation**: v equals f lambda. And because f is one over T, it's the same as lambda over T, the rule we just used. Check the units. Hertz means per second, so hertz times metres gives metres per **second**.

### 4. rearranging, and a radio station

### 6. the medium sets the speed
> The same speed. A wave's speed is set by what it travels through, its medium. For a cord, that's how tight it's pulled and how heavy it is. The driver only sets how often the waves are made. So doubling the frequency can't speed the crests up. Instead, each crest has only half as long to move on before the next one starts, so the wavelength **halves**, to zero point seven five metres. Four point oh times zero point seven five is still three point oh metres per second. So in one medium, frequency and wavelength trade. Double one, and the other halves. That's why a high note in air has a shorter wavelength than a low **note**.

### 7. worked example: starting from the period
> Sometimes you're given the period, not the frequency. Task: a wave on a string has a period of zero point zero four oh seconds, and a wavelength of one point five metres. Find its frequency and its speed. First, f is one over T. That's one over zero point zero four oh, twenty-five **hertz**. Then v is f lambda, twenty-five times one point five. That's thirty-seven point five, so thirty-eight metres per second to two significant **figures**. A classic slip is to multiply the period by the wavelength instead. That gives zero point zero six oh metres per second. Check the size. At that speed, the wave would take twenty-five seconds to move one wavelength. But it does it in one period, four hundredths of a **second**. The wave equation needs f, not T.

### 14. key notes (quiet, hold about 60 seconds)
Heading on screen: "Key notes: what to write in your book".
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

1. Wave speed v: how fast the wave pattern (and its energy) travels, in m s⁻¹. A wave moves one wavelength in one period, so v = λ/T.
2. Wave equation: v = fλ (f in Hz, λ in m, v in m s⁻¹). Rearranged: λ = v/f, f = v/λ. Also f = 1/T and T = 1/f.
3. Convert prefixes before substituting: k = 10³, M = 10⁶, c = 10⁻², m = 10⁻³.
4. In one medium (a cord at fixed tension, or sound in air), the speed is fixed: a higher frequency means a shorter wavelength.
5. Given T, find f = 1/T first. Check the size: the wave moves one λ in one T.

## Practice questions and model answers

**Attempt 1 (scene 9; original wording, HSC style, 3 marks suggested, not an official marking scheme).** "A siren sounds a note of frequency 1.7 kHz. The speed of sound in the air is 340 m s⁻¹. (a) Calculate the wavelength of the sound. (b) Calculate the period of the sound wave."
Model answer: (a) f = 1.7 kHz = 1.7 × 10³ Hz = 1700 Hz; λ = v/f = 340 ÷ 1700 = 0.20 m. (b) T = 1/f = 1 ÷ 1700 = 5.9 × 10⁻⁴ s (0.59 ms). Classic mistake (crossed): "λ = 340 ÷ 1.7 = 200 m" (the kilo prefix not converted).

**Attempt 2 (scene 11; original wording, HSC style, 4 marks suggested).** "A longitudinal wave travels along a stretched spring. One coil takes 0.25 s to complete each oscillation, and the compressions are 0.60 m apart. (a) Calculate the frequency and the speed of the wave. (b) The frequency of the driver is raised to 8.0 Hz, on the same spring. State the new speed of the wave and calculate its new wavelength."
Model answer: (a) The period is 0.25 s, so f = 1/T = 1 ÷ 0.25 = 4.0 Hz. The wavelength is the distance between compressions, 0.60 m, so v = fλ = 4.0 × 0.60 = 2.4 m s⁻¹. (b) The speed is set by the spring, which is unchanged, so v = 2.4 m s⁻¹. λ = v/f = 2.4 ÷ 8.0 = 0.30 m (doubling the frequency halves the wavelength). Classic mistake (crossed): "v = 4.8 m s⁻¹" (the speed doubled with the frequency).
