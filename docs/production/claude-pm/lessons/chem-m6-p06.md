- **Author notes (not learner copy):** (a) Standard: M(Na₂CO₃) = 2(22.99) + 12.01 + 3(16.00) = 105.99 g/mol (HSC data sheet values; C12 and the site used 106.0, which gives the same four-figure results here). n = 1.3656 ÷ 105.99 = 1.2884 × 10⁻² mol; c = 1.2884 × 10⁻² ÷ 0.2500 = 0.051537 → 0.05154 mol/L (four significant figures, from 250.0 mL). Drying: anhydrous Na₂CO₃ takes up some water from air (C12 new correction 4); school methods dry it at about 270 to 300 °C for an hour and cool it in a desiccator. Weighing into a tared beaker and dissolving in that beaker avoids losses from a weighing boat. (c) Titres: rough 24.9 mL (recorded to 0.1 mL, overshot to red); run 1 0.40 → 24.75 = 24.35; run 2 0.15 → 24.80 = 24.65 (bubble noted; excluded); run 3 0.20 → 24.50 = 24.30; run 4 1.05 → 25.40 = 24.35. Concordant: 24.35, 24.30, 24.35 (spread 0.05 mL, within 0.10). Mean 24.3333 → 24.33 mL (burette precision). c(HCl) = 2.577 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.02433 = 0.10592 → 0.1059 mol/L; with the unrounded standard and mean: 0.10590 → 0.1059 (the same, chosen for that reason). Run 4 starts at 1.05 mL to show that a start reading need not be zero, and needs a burette refill margin: 25.40 mL is within a 50 mL burette. (d) Bench B: rinsed with distilled water before each fill (said in scene 11), so the dilution is similar each time; titres 24.80, 24.85, 24.80, mean 24.8167 → 24.82 mL, about 2.0% larger than bench A's. A water film of roughly 1 mL left in a 50 mL burette and mixed into about 50 mL of acid dilutes it by about 2%, so the size is realistic. c = 2.577 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.02482 = 0.10383 → 0.1038 mol/L, 2.0% low. If bench B had rinsed only once before the first fill, the later fills would be less diluted and the titres would drift; the narration's "before each fill" keeps them concordant, which is the point. (e) Methyl orange: yellow in the alkaline carbonate solution, red in excess acid; the endpoint is the first permanent orange (transition range about pH 3.1 to 4.4). At this endpoint both protons have been taken up by carbonate, so the 1 : 2 ratio applies (C12 author note e). Dissolved CO₂ near the endpoint can make it fade back; some methods boil the solution briefly near the endpoint. This video uses "first permanent orange, after swirling" and leaves boiling out (not needed for a school method at this precision; C13 and C15 territory). No justification of the indicator is given (C13). (f) Variables: HSC questions sometimes ask for the independent and dependent variables of a titration. A titration is a measurement, not a test of one variable's effect on another; the titre is the measured quantity, and the method's quality comes from what is held the same. The narration says "nothing is being changed to test its effect", then lists the controlled conditions. If a reviewer prefers the common school framing, the fallback screen line is "measured: the titre; controlled: aliquot, standard, indicator and amount, endpoint colour, temperature". (g) Bubble: a bubble in the tip that leaves during a run makes the burette level fall by the bubble's volume without that volume of acid entering the flask, so the recorded titre is too large (24.65 against 24.33, 0.32 mL, a plausible tip bubble). If its cause is known, the run is excluded and repeated; c = 2.577 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.02465 = 0.1045 mol/L would be 1.3% low (not shown). (h) Parallax: the burette scale numbers increase downwards; with the eye above the meniscus the sight line meets a higher mark, so the reading is too low (site lesson 14 worked example 3(d)). Scene 7 shows the sight line tilt from above (coded reading too low) and level. Error direction for parallax is not traced (G04). (i) Pipette: a volumetric pipette is calibrated to deliver its volume with the small drop left in the tip after it drains and touches the wall. (j) Concentrations for the safety rows: 0.05154 mol/L Na₂CO₃ = 5.46 g/L, about 0.55%; 0.1059 mol/L HCl = 3.86 g/L, about 0.39%. (k) Significant figures: mass 1.3656 g (five), M 105.99 (five), volumes 250.0, 25.00 mL and titres to 0.01 mL (four), so concentrations to four significant figures. (l) Pronunciation: "pipette" as "pi-PET", "burette" as "byoo-RET", "aliquot" as "AL-i-kwot", "titre" as "TY-ter", "meniscus" as "men-IS-kus", "desiccator" as "DESS-i-kay-ter", "concordant" as "con-CORD-ant"; numbers spoken as written; formulas not spoken.

### 1. hook (concordant, but which is right?)
> Two lab benches titrate the same hydrochloric acid against the same standard. Each bench's titres agree within zero point zero five millilitres. But bench A's average is **twenty four point three three**, and bench B's is **twenty four point eight two**. So how do you run a titration you can trust, and spot what went **wrong**?

### 2. the plan
> The unknown is hydrochloric acid, roughly zero point one moles per litre. The known is a **sodium carbonate** standard, made from the solid. A pipette puts an aliquot of it in the flask, and the acid goes in the **burette**. As in the titration video, carbonate takes **two** hydrochloric acid.

Visual: the board clears. Centre: the burette plate (coded fill amber, label "unknown HCl, about 0.1 mol/L") over the conical flask (coded label "Na₂CO₃ standard, 25.00 mL", navy). Hold about 1 second.

### 3. make the standard: dry, weigh, rinse it all in
> Start with sodium carbonate dried in an oven and cooled in a **desiccator**, a sealed jar that keeps moisture out, so its mass is all carbonate. Weigh it into a beaker and record the exact mass: **one point three six five six** grams. Dissolve it, pour it through a funnel into a two hundred and fifty millilitre volumetric flask, then **rinse** the beaker and funnel in, so every grain ends up inside.

Visual: right zone, step card "1 Make the standard" with a grey tag "desiccator: sealed jar with a drying agent". Centre: `m6-analytical-balance-v1.png` (new plate) with `m6-beaker-v1.png` small on its pan; on "one point three six five six" the coded readout settles "1.3656 g" (navy), with a grey tag "analytical balance: reads to 0.0001 g" and a chip "record every digit". ### 4. make up to the mark, and its concentration
> Add water until the bottom of the meniscus sits on the **mark**, at eye level, then stopper and **invert** it many times, so every aliquot is the same. n equals m over M gives zero point zero one two eight eight four moles, and over zero point two five zero zero litres, that's **zero point zero five one five four** moles per litre.

Visual: P04's fill-to-mark motion: the coded level rises, slows near the neck mark, and the last drops land singly; on "mark" a magnifier ring opens on the neck with the curved surface touching the line and a tag "meniscus: the curved surface; its bottom on the mark". On "invert" the flask plate rotates whole through 180° and back three times (coded motion, no hand), tag "mixed: every aliquot the same". Then the right zone becomes the calculation card. Hold about 1 second.

### 5. the aliquot: rinse, fill, drain, leave the last drop
> Now the aliquot. Rinse the pipette with a little **carbonate solution**, because water left inside would dilute it. Fill it with a filler to the mark, drain it into the conical flask, and touch the tip to the glass, but leave the last **drop**: the pipette is calibrated for that. The flask can be wet with **distilled water**, because water adds no carbonate. Add three drops of methyl orange: **yellow**.

### 6. the burette: rinse, fill, take the funnel out, no bubble
> Next, the burette. Rinse it twice with a little **hydrochloric acid**, running each rinse out through the tap. Fill it through a funnel, with its top below eye level, then take the funnel **out**, because drips could change the reading. Open the tap to fill the tip, with no **air bubble** left.
