### 2d. q1-carbon (count environments, not carbons)
> Part c. Pentan-3-ol is symmetrical about its middle carbon. The two end carbons swap, and so do the two next to the middle, so five carbons give just three **peaks**. In 2-methylbutan-2-ol, only the two methyl groups on carbon two swap, so it gives **four**. Three peaks or four tells you which is which. Counting five peaks for both is the classic **slip**. Count environments, not carbons.

### 6. key-notes (reuse take: key-notes; quiet, about 60 seconds)
Heading on screen: "Key notes: what to write in your book".
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

1. Tests rule groups out: no fizz (no COOH), no fade (no C=C), dichromate stays orange (no 1° or 2° alcohol and no aldehyde). Use a fresh portion for each test.

## Practice questions and model answers

1. (Q1.) Pentan-3-ol, CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CH₂CH₃, and 2-methylbutan-2-ol, (CH₃)₂C(OH)CH₂CH₃, are isomers with the molecular formula C₅H₁₂O. (a) Explain why the infrared absorption ranges on the data sheet cannot be used to distinguish these two compounds. (b) Describe a chemical test that would distinguish them. Include the expected observation for each compound. (c) Predict the number of peaks in the ¹³C NMR spectrum of each compound, and explain how this distinguishes them.
   Model: (a) Both contain the same types of bond (O–H, C–H, C–O, C–C), so both show a broad O–H absorption (3230 to 3550 cm⁻¹), C–H absorptions near 3000 cm⁻¹ and a C–O absorption (1000 to 1300 cm⁻¹); every absorption lies in the same data sheet range. (b) Warm a fresh portion of each with acidified potassium dichromate. Pentan-3-ol is a secondary alcohol and is oxidised: orange to green. 2-methylbutan-2-ol is a tertiary alcohol (no H on the C–OH carbon) and is not oxidised: it stays orange. (c) Pentan-3-ol: 3 peaks (the molecule is symmetrical about C3, so the two CH₃ carbons are equivalent and the two CH₂ carbons are equivalent). 2-methylbutan-2-ol: 4 peaks (only the two CH₃ groups on C2 are equivalent).
