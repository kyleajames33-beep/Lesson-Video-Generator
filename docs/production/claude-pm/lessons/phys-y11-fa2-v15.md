- **Why bone stops X-rays (learner copy, with its cause).** "Soft material is mostly light atoms, like hydrogen, carbon and oxygen, so most X-ray photons get through. Bone contains calcium, a much heavier atom, and it's denser, so it absorbs far more of them." Chalk is chosen for the test block because it contains calcium, like bone (said once). The site says only "denser bone"; density alone undersells the cause (author note f).
- **Ionising, said with its threshold (site correction 3 of v14 applies).** "Ionising" is glossed "able to knock electrons out of atoms" and attached to X-rays, gamma rays and only the shortest ultraviolet. Ultraviolet's use and hazard are both "breaks bonds in DNA", without "ionise". X-rays: "Their photons are ionising, so they can damage cells. That's why X-ray exposures are kept short, and the people running the machine stand behind shielding." (said; on screen a coded lead screen symbol beside the machine, no person).
- **Microwave oven mechanism (site correction 4).** Said: "Water molecules have a slightly positive end and a slightly negative end. The microwave's electric field swings back and forth billions of times a second, twisting the molecules one way, then the other. They jostle their neighbours, and the food heats up." The site's "frequency strongly absorbed by water molecules" suggests a special water frequency; water absorbs well over a broad range, and ovens use 2.45 GHz partly so the waves soak a few centimetres into food (author note d).
- **Remote control (site correction 5).** Infrared is used in remotes because it is invisible, harmless at these levels, cheap to make with a small infrared LED, and walls stop it, so a remote only works in its own room. The site's "Infrared is emitted by warm objects and felt as heat, so it is used in TV remotes" links the wrong property.

- **Author notes (not learner copy):**
  (a) AM broadcast 1.0 × 10⁶ Hz: λ = 300 m. FM about 1.0 × 10⁸ Hz: 3.0 m. Microwave point-to-point links about 10¹⁰ Hz: 3.0 cm. Diffraction becomes significant when the wavelength is comparable with or larger than the obstacle or gap; a hill hundreds of metres across diffracts 300 m waves appreciably (ground-wave propagation also helps AM follow the terrain) and casts a sharp shadow for 3 cm waves. Walls: radio passes through most non-metal walls with some loss.
  (b) Oven: f = 2.45 × 10⁹ Hz, λ = 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 2.45 × 10⁹ = 0.122 m (12.2 cm; standing waves in the cavity have half-wavelength spacing of about 6 cm, not mentioned). Door mesh holes about 1 to 2 mm: about 60 to 120 times smaller than λ, so the metal mesh reflects microwaves (leakage is tiny); visible light at 5 × 10⁻⁷ m is about 4000 times smaller than a 2 mm hole and passes through.
  (c) Microwave photon at 2.45 GHz: E = 1.6 × 10⁻²⁴ J (about 10⁻⁵ eV), far too small to break bonds or ionise; the heating is from the oscillating field acting on many molecules (dielectric heating). Said as "twisting the molecules", which is the accepted school description.
  (d) Water's dielectric loss is broad (peaking near 20 GHz at room temperature); 2.45 GHz is an allocated industrial band and gives a penetration depth of roughly 1 to 2 cm in water-rich food, so the outer few centimetres heat directly and the centre by conduction. The video says "a few centimetres".
  (e) Infrared: a cup at about 60 °C emits most strongly near 8.7 μm (Wien: 2.898 × 10⁻³ ÷ 333 K); thermal cameras detect about 8 to 14 μm. Remotes use near infrared around 940 nm (photon about 1.3 eV); walls and opaque objects block it; it reflects off pale walls within a room (not mentioned).
  (f) X-ray contrast: at diagnostic energies photoelectric absorption rises steeply with atomic number (roughly Z³) as well as with density; calcium (Z = 20) and phosphorus (Z = 15) in bone absorb far more than hydrogen, carbon, nitrogen and oxygen (Z = 1 to 8) in soft tissue. Chalk (calcium carbonate) inside gelatin or agar gel is a fair stand-in for bone in soft tissue (tag "a test object"; medical physicists call such objects phantoms: not named on screen).
  (g) Ultraviolet: germicidal UV-C at 254 nm, photon energy 7.8 × 10⁻¹⁹ J (4.9 eV), is strongly absorbed by DNA (peak near 260 nm) and forms faulty bonds between neighbouring bases (thymine dimers), stopping the microbe from copying its DNA. Not ionising (ionisation of biological molecules needs roughly 10 eV or more). UV-C is absorbed by most plastics and by glass, and penetrates only the outer layers of skin; hence "surfaces only". Sunburn is caused mainly by UVB (280 to 315 nm). The cabinet plate's lamp glows violet; real UV-C is invisible, the faint violet is the lamp's visible output (fine as drawn).

## Practice questions and model answers

**Attempt 1 (scene 12; original wording, HSC style, 4 marks suggested, not an official marking scheme).** "An AM radio station broadcasts at 1.0 × 10⁶ Hz. A microwave communication link uses a frequency of 1.0 × 10¹⁰ Hz. Take c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹. (a) Calculate the wavelength of each. (b) Explain why the AM broadcast can be received in a house behind a hill, while the microwave link needs a clear line of sight."
Model answer: (a) λ = c / f: AM 3.00 × 10⁸ / 1.0 × 10⁶ = 3.0 × 10² m (300 m); microwave 3.00 × 10⁸ / 1.0 × 10¹⁰ = 3.0 × 10⁻² m (3 cm). (b) Waves diffract (spread around obstacles) most when their wavelength is comparable with the obstacle's size; 300 m waves are comparable with a hill, so they spread around it and reach the house. 3 cm waves are tiny compared with the hill and barely diffract, so the hill casts a shadow and the link needs a clear line of sight. Classic mistake (crossed): "radio waves travel faster, so they get round the hill".

**Attempt 2 (scene 14; original wording, HSC style, 3 marks suggested).** "A company needs to sterilise plastic syringes that are already sealed in their packets. It can use an ultraviolet lamp or a gamma-ray source. Choose one, and justify your choice by referring to the properties of each radiation."
Model answer: Gamma rays. Gamma photons carry enough energy to destroy microbes (damaging their DNA), and they pass through the plastic packaging, so they reach the microbes inside the sealed packets; no heating is needed and the packets stay sealed, so the syringes stay sterile. Ultraviolet photons also carry enough energy to kill microbes, but ultraviolet is absorbed by the packaging's surface, so it cannot reach the syringes. Classic mistake (crossed): "UV, because its photons carry plenty of energy".
