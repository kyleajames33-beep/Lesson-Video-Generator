> An **infectious** disease is caused by a **pathogen**, an organism or agent that lives in or on another organism, its **host**, and harms it. ## Numbers or facts to verify

Self-check (21 Oct) against standard texts (Campbell Biology; OpenStax Microbiology, chapters 3, 5 and 6) and the sources named. The Claude science check must confirm each before voicing.
- **Fleming, 1928; penicillin.** Fleming observed lysis of staphylococcal colonies around a *Penicillium* contaminant in September 1928 and named the substance penicillin in 1929 (Fleming 1929, *British Journal of Experimental Pathology* 10: 226). (A "saved millions of lives" clause was cut for length.) Penicillin has no effect on influenza (virus), tinea (fungus), malaria (protozoan) or tapeworms (helminth): correct.
- **Tuberculosis and type 2 diabetes.** TB is caused by *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* and spreads by airborne droplets between people; type 2 diabetes arises from genetic and lifestyle factors and is not transmissible. Correct. Infectious is not the same as communicable (tetanus is infectious but is not passed between hosts), so the screen definition, key note 1 and scene 12 say "most can pass between hosts" or test only for a pathogen.
- **Gut bacteria.** The revised estimate is about 38 trillion bacteria in a reference adult, mostly in the colon (Sender, Fuchs and Milo 2016, *PLoS Biology* 14: e1002533). "Your gut carries trillions of bacteria, and most do no harm, or even help": correct and not over-precise.
- **Bacteria.** Prokaryotic; nucleoid, no nucleus; peptidoglycan in the cell wall; binary fission. *M. tuberculosis* rods are about 2 to 4 micrometres long and 0.2 to 0.5 wide, and non-motile (no flagella), so the new TB plate has no flagellum (the existing `m7-bacterium-rod-coccus-v1.png` and `b6a-bacterium-v1.png` both show a flagellum and are not used to stand for TB). Mycobacterial walls contain peptidoglycan with mycolic acids outside it. "A rod a few thousandths of a millimetre long": correct. "DNA lying in the cytoplasm" (Revision 2) matches b06a, which says the nucleoid DNA is organised, not floating loose. Mycobacteria are intrinsically resistant to most beta-lactams, so scene 9 shows penicillin's target on a generic bacterium glyph, not the TB rod.
- **Fungi.** Eukaryotic; cell walls containing chitin (most of the wall is glucans and glycoproteins, so the narration says "containing"); hyphae or yeast forms; spores. Tinea is caused by dermatophyte fungi (*Trichophyton*, *Microsporum*, *Epidermophyton*). Wheat stem rust: *Puccinia graminis*. Correct.
- **Protozoa.** Single-celled eukaryotes without cell walls (some have a pellicle). *Plasmodium* invades liver cells, then red blood cells. "Lives inside red blood cells" describes the blood stage shown on the plate: acceptable; the liver stage is b13 detail. Red blood cells are about 7 to 8 micrometres across. Scene 4's true-scale pairing (a 3 micrometre rod against a 7.5 micrometre cell): correct.
- **Helminths and ectoparasites.** Adult *Taenia solium* attaches with a scolex bearing hooks and suckers and usually reaches 2 to 3 m, reported up to about 7 m (WHO taeniasis/cysticercosis fact sheet; CDC). "Can grow to several metres long": correct. The paralysis tick sentence was cut for length (not voiced). Ticks are a few millimetres long unfed (adult female about 3 to 4 mm). Size is not the defining test: root-knot nematodes and scabies mites are below or near the limit of the unaided eye, and fungal colonies are visible, so scene 3 says "each too small to see on its own" and "usually big enough to see", and the sorting question is "many-celled animal?". Scene 5's "ticks and lice" as macroorganisms follows the site.
- **Viruses.** Nucleic acid (DNA or RNA) in a protein capsid, some with a lipid envelope from a host membrane; no ribosomes or metabolism; replicate only inside host cells. SARS-CoV-2 particles are about 60 to 140 nm across (Zhu et al. 2020, *New England Journal of Medicine* 382: 727), so "about a ten-thousandth of a millimetre" (100 nm) is correct; viruses carry DNA or RNA, so the scene 10 question is "RNA only, protein only, or genes in a coat?" (Revision 2) and does not misfile DNA viruses; about 20 to 40 would fit along a 2 to 4 micrometre rod, so "dozens" is correct. Tobacco mosaic virus is a rigid rod about 300 nm long and 18 nm wide, single-stranded RNA in a helical protein coat (OpenStax Microbiology 6.1). Correct.
- **Viroids.** Small circular single-stranded RNA, no protein coat, no protein-coding genes; potato spindle tuber viroid is 359 nucleotides and causes elongated, spindle-shaped tubers (Diener 1971; OpenStax Microbiology 6.4). All known viroids infect plants (hepatitis delta virus in humans is a viroid-like satellite but has a protein coat from hepatitis B and is classed as a virus). "One makes potato tubers grow long and spindle-shaped" and "the known viroids infect plants": correct. The native PSTVd molecule is a rod-like structure tens of nanometres long; scene 7 shows it as a fraction of a virus's width: plausible, Codex to check the length.
- **Prions.** Misfolded forms (PrPSc) of the normal prion protein (PrPC), which is found on many cells, mostly nerve cells; PrPSc contains no nucleic acid and templates misfolding of PrPC (Prusiner 1998, *PNAS* 95: 13363). BSE in cattle; CJD and kuru in humans; scrapie in sheep. No prion disease of plants is known. No cure exists (CDC prion disease pages). "A misfolded copy of a normal protein found especially on nerve cells", "makes them fold the same wrong way", "the known prions infect animals", "no known cure": correct. The normal form is rich in alpha helices; the misfolded form is rich in beta sheets, which is how the plate pair is drawn (inspected: the existing `m7-prion-folded-protein-v1.png` is drawn mostly as blue flat arrows, a beta-rich ribbon, so it serves as the misfolded form).
