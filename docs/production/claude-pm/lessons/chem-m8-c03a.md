### 1. hook (eight bottles, no labels)
> A prep room shelf holds eight bottles of solutions, and the labels have washed off. Each one holds a different metal ion: barium, calcium, magnesium, lead, silver, copper, and iron in two forms, iron two and iron three. A sample from one bottle is in this tube. A blue tint might hint at copper, but a hint isn't proof. One test won't settle eight suspects. We need a sequence of tests, where each result rules some ions **out**.

### 2. three kinds of evidence
> We've got three kinds of evidence. A precipitate test adds an anion that makes an insoluble solid with only some cations, using the solubility rules from Module 5. A flame test heats the sample and reads the colour of the flame. And a complexation test adds molecules or ions that bond around the metal ion. That makes a **complex ion**, which often has a striking colour, or can pull a solid back into solution. The plan: use precipitates to sort, then flames and complexes to **confirm**.

Visual: three small panels write left to right, one per sentence: "precipitate" (`m5x-ppt-agcl-white-v1.png`, small) with a grey chip "c13: NAGSAG"; "flame" (`m5x-bunsen-burner-v1.png`, small); "complex" (the complex wrap inset: NH₃ tokens cluster around a grey cation dot). On "complex ion" a tag writes under the third panel: "complex ion: a metal ion with molecules or ions bonded around it" (bright). On "confirm" the first panel gets a chip "sort" and the other two a shared chip "confirm"; the panels shrink to small icons at the right edge (they return as step colours). Earlier text dims; at most three bright items.

### 3. step 1: chloride
> Start with a fresh portion of the sample, and add dilute hydrochloric acid. That brings in chloride ions. Chlorides dissolve, except with silver and lead, so a white precipitate here means silver or lead, and nothing else on our list. No precipitate usually rules both of them **out**. One catch: lead chloride is only slightly soluble, so a dilute lead sample can slip through to the next **step**.
