### 2. lifting stores energy
> The winch raises the box at a steady speed, so the forces balance. The winch pulls up with a force equal to the weight, m g. Over a height change delta h, the winch does work m g times delta h. Gravity does the same amount of negative **work**, so the net work is zero, and the kinetic energy doesn't **change**. So where did the winch's energy go? It's stored as gravitational potential energy, the energy an object has because of its height in Earth's gravity. Its change is delta U equals m g delta **h**. It works because near Earth's surface the field is uniform: g is the same at every height in the problem, so the weight stays the same all the way **up**.

### 3. only the change in height counts
> Now the puzzle. From the floor, the box rises two metres, so delta U is two times nine point eight times two, thirty-nine point two **joules**. You can measure height from the floor or from the table, and the change is the same. The path doesn't matter either, because gravity is conservative. And going down, delta h is negative, so delta U is **negative**.

### 5. why speed is squared
> Now kinetic energy, half m v squared, the work needed to bring a mass up to speed. Why is the speed squared? Pull the five kilogram trolley with forty newtons again. It reaches four metres per second after one **metre**. To reach eight, twice the speed, it needs four **metres**. Twelve needs nine. Here's why. With a steady force, the speed rises by the same amount each second. Twice the speed takes twice as long, and the trolley moves twice as fast on average, so it covers four times the distance. Same force, four times the distance, so four times the work, and four times the kinetic **energy**. Double the mass at the same speed, though, and the kinetic energy only **doubles**.

### 8. the rock's speed
> Now drop the rock from rest. As it falls, gravity pulls in the direction of motion, so it does positive work, m g times the drop, seven hundred and eighty-four joules. Ignoring air resistance, that's the net work, so the rock gains seven hundred and eighty-four joules of kinetic **energy**. So v squared is three hundred and ninety-two, and v is nineteen point eight, about twenty metres per **second**. A rock ten times heavier gets ten times the work, but has ten times the mass to speed up, so it lands at the same **speed**. The height gave up exactly what the speed **gained**.

### 16. key notes (quiet, hold about 60 seconds)

> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

1. Gravitational potential energy U: energy an object has because of its height in Earth's gravity. ΔU = mgΔh, in a uniform field (g = 9.80 m s⁻² at every height in the problem).
2. Only the change in height counts: Δh = final height − initial height (negative going down). The zero level and the path don't change ΔU.
3. Kinetic energy KE = ½mv². Double the speed: four times the KE. Double the mass: twice the KE.
4. ΔKE = ½mv² − ½mu²: square each speed, then subtract (not ½m(Δv)²). Speed from KE: v = √(2KE ÷ m).

## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why lifting stores energy: the winch does work but KE doesn't change, because gravity's equal negative work makes the net work zero (2, with bars).
