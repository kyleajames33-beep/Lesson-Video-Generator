### 11. semi-conservative
> One. Each new molecule keeps one old strand, which acted as its template, and has one new strand. This is called semi-conservative replication: semi means half, and each copy conserves, or keeps, one old strand. Half a hand-me-down, half brand new. And because every new strand was built against an old template, base by base, the copies come out very accurate.

## Numbers or facts to verify

Self-check (2 Nov). The Codex science check must confirm each before voicing.
- **"Six billion base pairs ... in about eight hours" (scene 1).** A diploid human cell has about 6.4 billion base pairs; S phase typically lasts about 6 to 8 hours in dividing human cells. "about" covers it. Correct.
- **Helicase (scene 3).** Separates the paired bases (breaking the bonds between them, with base stacking also disrupted) and unwinds the helix at the fork; does not break the backbone. Correct. (It uses ATP; not mentioned.)
- **Free nucleotides (scene 3).** Strictly, the incoming units are nucleoside triphosphates; "free nucleotides" is the standard school term (site wording). Correct at Year 11.
- **Primase and primers (scene 4).** Primase synthesises a short RNA primer; DNA polymerase cannot start a chain de novo and needs a free 3′ end. Correct.
- **Proofreading (scene 4).** Replicative DNA polymerases have 3′ to 5′ exonuclease proofreading that removes a mispaired nucleotide. Correct.
- **Direction (scenes 5 to 7).** Polymerase adds only to the 3′ end (synthesis 5′ to 3′); the new strand is antiparallel to its template. Leading strand continuous in the direction of fork movement, one primer (per origin, per fork); lagging strand discontinuous, built away from the fork, each fragment primed. Fork geometry in Decisions checked: top template arrow left to right, so its new strand runs right to left and grows leftwards (away from a fork moving right): lagging. Correct.
- **"Made at the same time" (scene 7).** Leading and lagging synthesis are coordinated at the same fork. Correct.
- **Primer replacement (scene 8).** In bacteria DNA polymerase I removes RNA primers and fills with DNA; in eukaryotes primers are removed by nucleases (RNase H, FEN1) with polymerase delta filling the gap. Revision 2 (brief 150): spoken as "a team of enzymes, including DNA polymerase", since human primer processing involves polymerase delta with other enzymes (FEN1, ligase I). Correct.
- **Ligase (scene 8).** Forms the phosphodiester (covalent) bond that seals nicks in the backbone. Correct.
- **Semi-conservative (scenes 10, 11, attempt 2).** Correct; two rounds give two hybrid and two all-new molecules (Meselson and Stahl logic). Correct.
