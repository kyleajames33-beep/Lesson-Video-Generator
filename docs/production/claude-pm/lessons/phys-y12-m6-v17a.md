### 2. why the balance reads the force
> It's the wire. By the right-hand palm rule, the field pushes the current in the wire upwards. And by Newton's third law, the wire pushes down on the magnet just as **hard**. So the balance weighs the motor effect. Change grams to kilograms and multiply by g, and that's about zero point zero zero seven five **newtons**. The wire crosses the field at ninety degrees, so F equals l I B sine theta becomes F equals B I l, and the force should be proportional to the **current**.

### 3. a fair test
> The current is the independent variable. We change it with a rheostat and read it on the ammeter. The dependent variable is the change in the balance reading. Everything else stays fixed: the same magnet in the same spot on the pan, so B stays the same, the same wire through the same stretch of the gap, so the length in the field stays the same, and the wire clamped square to the magnet, so it can't sag or twist, and the angle stays at ninety **degrees**. That makes it a **valid** test: it measures the current's effect alone.

## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why the balance changes: the third-law partner of the force on the wire acts on the magnet (2). Why F ∝ I is expected: F = BIl at 90° with B and l fixed (2, 3). Why switch off between readings: heating raises resistance and drifts the current (4). Why reversing helps: a zero drift adds the same to both readings, so averaging the sizes removes it (4). Why bigger readings are better: the same 0.01 g is a smaller share (6). Why the gradient is Bl (7). Why the rig cannot test sin θ: the slanted wire's extra length cancels the smaller sine (8). Why the motor reverses: reversing I or B reverses the side forces (9). Why it rocks without a split ring: the current in the coil never flips, so the torque reverses after face on (10). Why the held-still current gives R: a still coil makes no back emf (11).
- **Misconceptions addressed:** the balance reading is the wire's weight or heat (1, 2); a straight line proves the whole formula (8); the angle can be tested by turning the magnet (8); the split ring changes the supply current (v16 aid recalled, 10); a motor's running current gives its resistance (crossed in 16); grams used as kilograms (crossed in 14).

## Practice questions and model answers

**Attempt 1 (scene 13; original wording, HSC style, 3 marks suggested, not an official marking scheme).** "A straight wire carrying a current of 2.5 A passes at right angles through the uniform field of a magnet. Model answer: The force on the magnet equals the force on the wire (Newton's third law): F = Δm g = 0.00049 kg × 9.8 m s⁻² = 0.0048 N. With θ = 90°, F = BIl, so B = F ÷ (Il) = 0.0048 ÷ (2.5 × 0.040) = 0.048 T. Classic mistake (crossed): using 0.49 as kilograms, F = 4.8 N and B = 48 T.

**Scene 2. why the balance reads the force (full new text).**

Narration: It's the wire. By the right-hand palm rule, the field pushes the current in the wire upwards. And by Newton's third law, the wire pushes down on the magnet just as **hard**.
