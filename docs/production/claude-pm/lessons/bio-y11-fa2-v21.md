## Numbers or facts to verify

Self-check (2 Nov). The Codex science check must confirm each before voicing.
- **One circuit about a minute (1).** At rest the whole blood volume (about 5 L) circulates about once a minute (cardiac output about 5 L per minute); blood from a leg muscle returns through the right heart, lungs and left heart before reaching the gut. Correct as "about a minute" (site error 1).
- **Muscle (2).** Working skeletal muscle greatly increases oxygen and glucose uptake and carbon dioxide release; resting muscle extracts much less. Urea unchanged. Lactic acid rises in blood during intense exercise (grey tag only). Correct.
- **Every organ respires (2).** All tissues respire; venous blood from each organ has less oxygen and more carbon dioxide than arterial blood; the lungs are the exception for blood as a whole (pulmonary venous blood is oxygenated). Correct; "the lung tissue itself respires" stated; lung tissue is also supplied by bronchial vessels (author note).
- **Lungs (3).** Oxygen and carbon dioxide cross by diffusion; urea unchanged. Correct.
- **Small intestine (4).** Glucose (and galactose) are absorbed by sodium-linked secondary active transport (SGLT1) and leave the cell by facilitated diffusion; amino acids by several co-transporters; most fat enters lacteals as chylomicrons. "Much of that glucose is moved by active transport, using energy": acceptable at Year 11 (matches v18 and FA1 v14's author-note convention for secondary active transport). Gut wall respires. Correct.
- **Hepatic portal vein (5).** Carries blood from the intestines (and stomach, spleen, pancreas) to the liver before it reaches the general circulation; the liver also receives oxygenated blood in the hepatic artery and drains to the hepatic veins. "It goes first to the liver": correct for blood from the intestines.
- **Liver glucose (5, 6).** After a meal the liver takes up glucose and stores glycogen (insulin-promoted; FA2 v29); between meals it breaks glycogen down (and makes new glucose) and releases glucose. Correct. The scene 5 line chart is illustrative.
- **Deamination and urea (6).** The liver removes the amino group from excess amino acids, forming ammonia, which the urea cycle converts to urea; urea is made essentially only in the liver. "Strips off the part that contains nitrogen, and turns it into urea": correct simplification. Revision 2 uses root decision 22's fallback, "urea rises in blood leaving the liver", tied to the liver processing spare amino acids.
- **Large intestine (7).** Absorbs most of the remaining water (about 1 to 1.5 L a day) and salts (sodium, chloride); some vitamins made by gut bacteria (vitamin K, some B vitamins) are absorbed (grey tag). Correct.
- **Kidneys (8).** Remove much of the urea (about half of filtered urea is excreted), adjust water and salt; glucose is normally fully reabsorbed (Revision 2 learner copy: "filtered glucose is reabsorbed rather than lost in urine"); kidney cells respire (renal oxygen extraction is relatively low). Correct as worded ("much of that urea", "glucose is normally kept").
- **Item 10a.** A liver between meals, after absorption has ended, releasing glucose and producing urea is standard. Data are comparisons only, no values.
