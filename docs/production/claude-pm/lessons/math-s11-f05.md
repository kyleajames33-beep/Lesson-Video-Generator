### 1. hook: zero for L and P plates
> Alcohol slows reactions, so it stretches the stopping distance. That's why NSW measures alcohol in drivers, as blood alcohol content, or BAC. So how do these formulas estimate BAC, and what does the answer **mean**?

### 2. what BAC measures
> First, what's being measured. A BAC of zero point zero five means zero point zero five grams of alcohol in every hundred millilitres of blood. In NSW, learner and P-plate drivers must have zero. Full licence holders must be under zero point zero **five**. Drinks are counted in standard drinks. One standard drink holds ten grams of pure alcohol, and a large glass can be two or more standard drinks. Drink containers in Australia show how many standard drinks they **hold**.

### 3. two formulas, and what they mean
> Here are the two formulas. For males, BAC equals ten N minus seven point five H, all over six point eight M. For females, it's the same top, over five point five M. N is the number of standard drinks, H is the number of hours of drinking, and M is the person's weight in **kilograms**. The top estimates the alcohol still in the body. That's ten grams for each drink, minus seven point five for each hour of drinking, an allowance for what the body clears as time passes. The bottom spreads it through the body, so a heavier person gets a smaller **estimate**.

### 4. worked example: an invented adult man
> Here's the method, with an invented example. An adult man who weighs eighty kilograms has four standard drinks over three hours. Estimate his BAC. Work out the top first. Ten times four is forty, minus seven point five times three, twenty-two point five, leaves seventeen point **five**. Now the bottom. Six point eight times eighty is five hundred and forty-four. Divide, and seventeen point five over five hundred and forty-four is about zero point zero three **two**, rounded to three decimal places at the end. That's above zero, so on L or P plates, he must not drive. On a full licence it's under the limit, but it's only an estimate, not a safe **number**. Typing it in with no brackets is the slip. The calculator then divides only part of the top, and gives minus two hundred and twenty-four, which is **impossible**.

### 5. the female formula
> The female formula divides by five point five M, instead of six point eight M. For the same drinks, hours and weight, the bottom is smaller, so the estimate is higher. These formulas are averages for typical body types, and the next video looks at why any estimate like this is **rough**.

### 6. when the answer is negative
> Sometimes the top comes out negative. For a seventy-five kilogram man, one standard drink over two hours gives ten minus fifteen on top, minus five.
