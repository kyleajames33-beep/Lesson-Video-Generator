- **Learner question kept (route).** "How do both factors contribute to a product derivative?" The answer, built in order: when x changes, each factor changes, and each change is multiplied by the other factor's current value. So the slope of uv is u′v + uv′: u's turn plus v's turn. On y = eˣ sin x the two turns are visible as two readout rows; at x = π/2 one turn is zero, and at x = 3π/4 the two turns cancel.
- **Recall, not re-teach (route recommendation and root decision).** The product rule is named as the Year 11 rule in one sentence (scene 2) with a grey chip. The rectangle is shown once (about 40 s) as the picture behind the rule, because it answers the learner question directly ("both factors contribute"), and is then reused as the memory aid. No first-principles limit is written.
- **The hook surprise (graph driven).** y = eˣ sin x, with both factors from the new families (d01's eˣ and d05's sin x). The tempting guess "multiply the two derivatives" gives eˣ cos x, which is 0 at x = π/2, so it predicts a flat tangent there. The graph is climbing steeply at x = π/2 (true slope e^(π/2) ≈ 4.81). One fair comparison, at one point; the guess line and the true tangent are drawn through the same point.
- **Why eˣ sin x and not the site's x² sin x.** The site's first mix-and-match example x² sin x shows the same failure at π/2 (guess 0, slope π) but has only one new family, and its level point solves tan x = −2/x, which has no exact form. eˣ sin x levels off exactly at x = 3π/4, where the two turns are ±(√2/2)e^(3π/4): an exact, graph-driven contrast with sin x, which levels off at π/2. Site examples are used where the route assigns them (`we-1`, scene 6) and in the attempts' crossed mistakes.
- **The two turns, said as slope times height (author note a).** Each term is one factor's slope times the other factor's height: u′v is "eˣ's slope times the height of sin x", uv′ is "the height of eˣ times the slope of sin x". The guess multiplies a slope by a slope. For eˣ sin x the guess eˣ cos x happens to equal uv′, because (eˣ)′ = eˣ; the narration never equates them and never says "the guess keeps one turn" (that would be a property of eˣ only). The attempt 1 crossed mistake (2xeˣ for x²eˣ) shows the guess failing without that coincidence.
- **The level point is not classified (route stop).** Scene 4 shows the tangent is flat at x = 3π/4 because the two turns cancel, and says the curve "levels off" there. It does not call it a maximum, test signs or solve sin x + cos x = 0 in general: classification is d09. The point x = 3π/4 is checked by substitution (sin = √2/2, cos = −√2/2), not found by solving.
- **Worked example 1 (apply and factor one new-family product, route stop).** y = eˣ sin x: u = eˣ, u′ = eˣ; v = sin x, v′ = cos x (x in radians, d05); y′ = eˣ sin x + eˣ cos x = eˣ(sin x + cos x). Check at π/2: e^(π/2)(1 + 0) = e^(π/2), the slope seen in scene 3. Task, equation and grouped givens separated; `ProductContributions` shows each term keeping the unchanged other factor.
- **A log factor (scene 6, site section 05).** y = eˣ ln x: the same two turns, with the domain x > 0 carried from the factor ln x to the answer eˣ(ln x + 1/x), x > 0. This covers the third new family (d03) in one short scene and prepares d08, where the domain is the main theme. No tangent is drawn; the thumbnail shows only the curve on its domain.
- **Worked example 2 (route primary `we-1`; transfer to a chain rule factor).** y = x³ sin(2x): the outside operation is multiplication (product rule); v = sin(2x) has an inside, 2x, so v′ = 2cos(2x) by d06's chain rule. y′ = 3x² sin(2x) + 2x³ cos(2x) = x²(3 sin(2x) + 2x cos(2x)). Site numbers kept exactly.
- **The inner 2 checked on the graph (scene 8).** At x = π/2: sin π = 0, cos π = −1, so y′(π/2) = 2(π/2)³(−1) = −π³/4 ≈ −7.75. Leaving out the inner 2 gives −π³/8 ≈ −3.88, half the slope; the dashed wrong tangent is visibly too shallow against the curve (author note c). This is the site's "chain rule inside the product" common error, shown rather than told.
- **Attempts (pack question 1 kept; pack question 2 replaced).** (A1) f(x) = x²eˣ, fully factorised: f′(x) = 2xeˣ + x²eˣ = xeˣ(x + 2) (pack Q1; cross-source DC01 `we-2` value). ### 2. the rectangle behind the rule
> Here's a way to see it. Think of the product as the area of a rectangle, with width e to the x and height sine x. At x equals one, it's about two point seven two wide, and point eight four high. Now nudge x a little. The width grows, and that adds a strip down the **side**: the change in width, times the height. The height grows too, adding a strip along the **top**: the change in height, times the width. The corner is a small change times a small change, so it vanishes as the nudge **shrinks**. So the area changes in two ways, one factor changing while the other stays. Divide by the nudge, and that's the product rule from Year 11: u prime v, plus u v **prime**.

### 4. where the turns cancel
> Keep sliding. Past pi over two, sine x starts to fall, so its slope is negative, and its turn pulls the slope **down**, while e to the x's turn still pushes it up. That's why this curve levels off at three pi over four, not at pi over two, where sine x levels off. E to the x is still growing, and it carries the product further.

### 5. worked example: e to the x sine x
> Now the algebra. Differentiate y equals e to the x sine x. Label the factors first. u is e to the x, so u prime is e to the **x**. v is sine x, so v prime is cos **x**. Check pi over two. That's e to the pi over two, times one plus zero, about four point eight one, the slope we **saw**.

### 6. a log factor keeps its domain
> The same rule works with a log. Differentiate y equals e to the x, times the natural log of x. The natural log needs x positive, so this function only exists for x greater than **zero**. u is e to the x, and so is u prime. v is the natural log of x, so v prime is one over **x**. That gives e to the x times the natural log of x, plus e to the x over x. Take out e to the x, and you get e to the x, times the natural log of x plus one over x, for positive **x**. Every new family works the same way: two turns, then look for a common factor.

### 9. memory aid
> Here's a way to remember it. Each factor takes a turn, and the other one stays. In the rectangle, the width changes while the height stays, then the height changes while the width stays. That's why there are two terms, and why each term keeps one of the original **factors**. Multiplying the two derivatives keeps neither factor, so it isn't the rule. And because the two turns are added, it doesn't matter which factor you call u. That won't be true for a **quotient**.

Why the curve levels off at 3π/4 and not π/2: the falling sin x's turn cancels the still-growing eˣ's turn (4). Why sin 2x gives an extra 2: its inside 2x changes twice as fast as x (7, recalled from d06), checked on the tangent (8). Why eˣ ln x needs x > 0: its factor ln x does (6).

## Memory aid check

- **"Each factor takes a turn; the other one stays."** The site's "the other one stays", made into a full sentence.
