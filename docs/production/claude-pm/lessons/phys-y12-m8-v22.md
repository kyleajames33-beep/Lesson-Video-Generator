## Decisions

- **Learner question (route wording kept).** "How do accelerators create and test evidence about particles?" The answer: they give particles enormous kinetic energy using electric fields, many times over (3, 4, 6), and steer and focus them with magnetic fields so they can be reused and collided (5 to 9); in a collision that energy becomes new, heavy particles (E = mc²) or probes tiny structure (2); layered detectors read what comes out (10, 11); the theory predicts what should appear, at what energy, so a result could have failed it (12), within limits (13).
- **Hook (v21's handoff).** 3 s prediction chips for "How do you make a Higgs boson?": "heat something", "smash particles together very fast", "find one in rocks". Answer: smash particles together with enough energy.
- **Why high energy (site card 1; m7-v16 and v20 recalled).** Two reasons, both from the site: creating a heavy particle needs at least its mc² in the collision (Higgs 125 GeV, top 173 GeV; Revision 2, brief 193: this is a necessary minimum, not a sufficient threshold, because conservation laws usually require other particles to be made too; the scene 2 slider is a rest-energy comparison, not cells "switching on"); seeing small structure needs short wavelength (v20). "Collision energy decides which particles and processes are possible" (route).
- **Electric acceleration (m6-v03; site "Analyse 7 marks" step 2).** W = qV: a proton across 1 MV gains 1 MeV; 6.8 TeV in one step would need 6.8 × 10¹² V, impossible, so accelerators give many small pushes. Said "almost seven million million volts".
- **Linac (site).** A row of gaps; the field in each gap flips in step with the particle's arrival, so it is always pushed forwards and shielded when the field is backwards (drift tubes, named on screen). Limited by length: SLAC 3 km, 50 GeV electrons (site). The v20 linac plate reused. The Australian Synchrotron's own linac (100 MeV) is named in scene 7's chain (sourced).
- **Ring (site "Analyse 7 marks" steps 1, 4).** Magnetic force is perpendicular to the velocity, so it does no work and only steers (m6-v05 recalled); a ring brings the particle back to the same accelerating gap each lap. "Electric fields push, magnetic fields steer" is the organising contrast. Focusing: other magnets squeeze the beam so it stays narrow (quadrupoles named on screen; route "focusing"; no engineering).
- **Synchrotron condition (site steps 3, 5; m6-v05 rearranged).** Revision 2 (brief 193): the narration starts from r = p/(qB), with p the momentum ("At low speeds that's m v"), so for a fixed radius B = p/(qr): as the momentum (and energy) grows, the field must grow in step, and the accelerating field's timing too; "that's where the name comes from" (site step 3). For particles near the speed of light the momentum is given in the question (the relativistic p is beyond this video; author note a). Momentum and energy, not speed alone, are the ramped quantities.
- **Worked example (Australian; sourced).** Australian Synchrotron, Clayton, Melbourne (ANSTO, operating since 2007): a 100 MeV linac feeds a 130 m booster synchrotron that takes electrons to 3.0 GeV, then a 216 m storage ring. Booster at 3.0 GeV: p = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁸ kg m s⁻¹, r = 8.0 m, B = p/(qr) = 1.25 T (matches the published booster dipole field 1.25 T); at injection (0.1 GeV), B = 0.042 T, so the field ramps about 30 times during each cycle. Sources: World Nuclear Association, https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/appendices/australian-research-reactors (3 GeV, 216 m, 130 m, since 2007); ANSTO, https://www.ansto.gov.au/accelerator-systems-overview (100 MeV linac, booster); booster dipole 1.25 T and 8.25° bends of 1.15 m (bending radius 8.0 m): EPAC/PAC booster comparison, https://epaper.kek.jp/p07/PAPERS/TUPMN001.PDF . Brief 193 confirmed the strong-dipole 1.25 T and 7.99 m bending radius from the booster design paper, tables 1 and 2 (https://proceedings.jacow.org/p05/PAPERS/RPAE084.PDF); the 8 m is the bend radius, not the ring's average radius (open decision 2 closed).
- **Synchrotron light (site step 6).** A charge forced round a curve radiates; electrons, being light, radiate strongly; the Australian Synchrotron keeps electrons circling in its storage ring precisely to make that light (intense X-rays for studying materials and proteins); the same loss is why the highest-energy collider uses protons. Named and explained in one scene; the E⁴/m⁴ scaling is author note b.
- **LHC and colliders (site).** 27 km ring at CERN; protons at 6.8 TeV per beam; about 8 T superconducting dipoles (author note c); two beams head-on, so 13.6 TeV is available (site misconception: beam energy against collision energy). Fixed target: much of the energy is wasted carrying products forwards (site); the formula and 113 GeV appear as a grey "extension" tag only.
- **Detectors (site `read-the-detector-layers`, marked extension by the site but in the route's sections).** One detector chosen (CMS, as the image-bank brief requires: "Choose one, not a hybrid"): from the beam outwards: tracker inside a strong magnetic field (curved tracks: radius gives momentum, direction of bending gives the sign of charge; neutral particles leave no track), electromagnetic calorimeter (stops electrons and photons), hadron calorimeter (stops protons, neutrons, pions), muon chambers (muons reach them and leave outer tracks). Neutrinos escape; Revision 2 (brief 193): missing transverse momentum shows that something unseen carried momentum, with a neutrino as one physical source, not a unique identification ("such as a neutrino"); muons are the charged particles that leave tracks in the outer chambers (not "only muons get through", since neutrinos also pass through unseen).
- **Testing the Standard Model (site "Assess 6 marks").** The Higgs: predicted in 1964 (v21), sought for decades, found in 2012 by two separate detectors as an excess of photon pairs with a combined (invariant) mass of 125 GeV (site: H → γγ, H → ZZ* → 4ℓ). The W and Z found at CERN in 1983 at the predicted masses (site lesson 18). Falsifiability: "if it hadn't turned up at all, the theory would have been in trouble". Limits (site step 5): energy reach; indirect evidence from decay products; gravity outside the Standard Model. Revision 2 (brief 193): the narration no longer says "no accelerator can test gravity" (accelerator-made antihydrogen was used by ALPHA to test gravitational free fall); it says gravity sits outside the Standard Model, so testing the model says nothing about it. Bounded verdict (site step 6).

### 2. why so much energy?
> There are two reasons. First, to make a heavy particle, the collision must supply at least its mass as energy, E equals m c squared. A Higgs boson needs at least a hundred and twenty-five GeV. ### 3. pushing with electric fields

### 6. a field that keeps up
