### 2b. q1-denominator (each group out of itself)
> Start with the denominator. The question asks about each lunch group, so divide by the number who ate each lunch, not by the whole camp. Sixteen of the **twenty-eight** who ate the sandwiches fell ill. That's about **fifty-seven per cent**. None of the **fourteen** who ate salad rolls fell ill. That's **zero**. Now the two groups compare fairly, because each number is out of the people who had that food.

### 2d. q1-mistake (the classic mistake)
> The classic mistake is dividing by sixty. Sixteen out of sixty is about twenty-seven per cent, but that mixes in students who never ate the sandwiches, so it can't show which food was the problem. Always divide by the people who could have caught it.

### 3b. q2-calc (two measures, two denominators)
> Incidence first. That's new cases in a year, divided by the people at risk, here the **whole population**. For twenty eighteen, four thousand eight hundred, divided by four hundred thousand, times a thousand, is **twelve** per thousand. For twenty twenty-three, eight hundred and forty, divided by four hundred and twenty thousand, times a thousand, is **two**. Prevalence is the share infected at one time, so its denominator is the **people tested**. Fifty of two thousand is **two and a half per cent**. Six of two thousand is **zero point three**.

### 3c. q2-explain (the tap and the tank)
> Now link each measure to the tap or the tank. Bed nets mean fewer bites by Anopheles, which bite mostly at night, so fewer people get new infections. That turns down the **tap**, so incidence falls. Same-day testing and treatment clears each infection sooner, so people stay infected for less time. That drains the **tank** faster, so prevalence falls. And with fewer infected people, mosquitoes pick up fewer parasites, so **new cases** fall too.

### 6. key-notes (reuse take: key-notes; quiet, about 60 seconds)
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

- Before you compare, find the denominator: divide by the people who could have caught it (each exposure group, or the whole population at risk), or by the people tested in a survey.
- Food outbreak: compare the percentage ill among people who ate a food with those who did not. Most ill people eating a food is not enough.
- Epidemic curve: one or two sharp clusters a set time after a shared meal or source = common source; rising waves over days = person-to-person spread. Check the timing against the incubation period.
- Incidence = new cases in a set time ÷ population at risk (× 1000 or × 100 000). Prevalence = people infected at one time ÷ people surveyed (× 100). Fewer new infections lowers incidence; shorter infections lower prevalence.
- Justify a control: name the route, the link the measure breaks, and why other measures miss it (for example, people with no symptoms who still spread it).
