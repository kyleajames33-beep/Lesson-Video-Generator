### 3. energy in kilowatt hours

A kilowatt hour is the energy a one kilowatt appliance uses in one hour. So in one hour, the two point four kilowatt heater uses two point four kilowatt hours. In five hours, it uses five times as much, two point four times five, twelve kilowatt **hours**. The name says it: kilowatts times hours. Multiplying the watts, two thousand four hundred, by five is the slip. That gives twelve thousand watt hours, a thousand times too big, so convert to kilowatts **first**.

### 5. minutes and days

### 6. worked example: two heaters
> Here's a surprise. Heater A is rated at two point four kilowatts and runs five hours a day. Heater B is rated at only one point eight kilowatts, but runs seven hours a day. Electricity costs thirty-three cents per kilowatt hour. Which costs more to run over thirty days, and by how much? Heater A uses two point four times five, twelve kilowatt hours a day, so three hundred and sixty in thirty **days**. Heater B uses one point eight times seven, twelve point six a day, so three hundred and seventy-eight in thirty **days**. Picture each heater's day as a rectangle, power up and hours along. Its area is the energy, and B's is bigger. At thirty-three cents, that's a hundred and eighteen dollars eighty, against a hundred and twenty-four dollars **seventy-four**. So the less powerful heater costs five dollars ninety-four more, because it's on for longer. Choosing heater A, just because its power is higher, is the **slip**.
