### 2. the gradient of the gradient
> The derivative f prime is a function in its own right. It gives the gradient at every x. So we can differentiate it again. The result is called the second derivative. It's written f double prime of x, or d two y by d x squared, or y double prime. For our curve, f prime of x is three x squared, minus twelve x, plus nine. Differentiate again, and f double prime of x is six x minus **twelve**. It tells us how fast the gradient is changing, and in which **direction**.

### 3. how the gradient changes the bend
> Watch the tangent ride along the curve, with the gradient graph underneath. On the left, the gradient is falling, so the tangent keeps turning clockwise, and the curve bends **down**. That's called concave down. The curve sits below its tangents, like a cap. And concave down isn't the same as decreasing. This part rises, then falls, and it bends down the whole **time**. On the right, the gradient is rising, so the tangent turns anticlockwise, and the curve bends **up**. That's concave up. The curve sits above its tangents, like a cup. Now, f double prime is the gradient of this gradient graph. Where f prime is falling, f double prime is negative. Where f prime is rising, it's **positive**. So f double prime greater than zero means concave up, and less than zero means concave **down**.

## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why the bend depends on the change in the gradient: the same gradient sits on a bending side and a straight side (1). Why f″ > 0 means concave up: the gradient rises, so the tangent turns anticlockwise and the curve bends up, staying above its tangents; f″ is the gradient of f′ (3). Why an inflection is where f′ turns: the gradient switches from falling to rising there (4). Why f″ = 0 is not enough: f″ can touch zero without changing sign, as for x⁴ (5). Why the second derivative test works: a curve bending down sits below its flat tangent, so the flat point is the highest nearby (6). Why the test fails at f″ = 0: three curves share f′(0) = f″(0) = 0 with three different outcomes (7).
- **Misconceptions addressed:** "f″ = 0 means an inflection" (5; 12, crossed); "f″ > 0 means a maximum" (8; 10, crossed); "f″ = 0 at a stationary point means a horizontal inflection" (7, refuted by x⁴ and −x⁴); "an inflection must be a stationary point" (4); "concavity is about increasing or decreasing" (3: the cubic is concave down on parts where it increases and where it decreases).
