## Decisions

- **Main teaching move: a fair test lets only one suspect remain.** The hook shows three tubes that foam differently and then plants extra suspects (disc size, peroxide volume), so the student feels why one difference at a time matters before the terms are named. The fixed frame on screen, "Change / Measure / Keep the same", is the independent, dependent and controlled variables in plain words; it fills for temperature in full (scenes 2 to 7), then refills briefly for pH and substrate concentration (scene 8, the route's coverage note).
- **The selected enzyme: catalase in potato discs (site walk-through).** Potato is used rather than liver: equal discs cut with a cork borer give a controllable amount of enzyme (a visible controlled variable), and raw meat adds a hygiene risk. v18 showed liver for the hook; potato appeared in v18's attempt 1, so it is not new.
- **Rate measure (scene 3).** The volume of oxygen in a gas syringe after exactly 60 seconds (the site's preferred measure and its Assess walk-through's main criticism of bubble counting: bubbles vary in size). Foam height in a measuring cylinder after a set time is named as the simpler option (route stop: "oxygen volume or foam height"). Volume in 60 s is read directly as millilitres per minute, so no rate calculation is needed.
- **Equilibration (scene 4; the site's "validity mark").** The flask of peroxide and the discs stand separately in the bath for five minutes before mixing, with the reason spoken: cold discs would spend the measured minute warming up, so the enzyme would never be at the stated temperature. Attempt 1's first flaw tests it.
- **Control (scene 5).** Boiled potato discs at the same temperature: almost no oxygen shows the gas comes from active catalase, not from peroxide breaking down on its own (site card 3 and Q2 model). One sentence separates "a control" from "controlled variables", recalling v10 rather than re-teaching it. The discs are boiled beforehand by the teacher (teacher note).
- **The prediction beat (scene 5).** "Will the boiled discs make oxygen?" with a 3 s countdown (root decision 7). It uses v20's denaturation, so the answer is reasoned, not guessed.
- **Repeats, anomaly and reliability (scene 6).** Three trials per temperature and a mean; one illustrative anomaly at 20 °C (14, 15 and 23 mL; the 23 is explained by a recorded fault (disc cut too big), kept on record and repeated, giving 13; mean of the valid trials 14). The plotted means give v20's curve, cited, not re-explained (route stop). "Reliable" is defined as "consistent when repeated" (site vocabulary).
- **Validity and improvements (scene 7).** "Valid" is defined as "really tests what it claims to" (site vocabulary), justified by one variable changed and a direct measure of rate (site walk-through). Two improvements: more temperatures between 30 and 50 °C (the gaps can't locate the optimum; site Q2), and a thermometer check of each bath (site: "uneven water-bath temperature").
- **The other two factors (scene 8; route coverage note).** pH: equal volumes of buffer solutions from pH 3 to 11, every tube in one bath at the same temperature. Substrate concentration: the 3% peroxide diluted with distilled water (0.5% to 3%), the total volume kept the same. Both "Change" lines write into the frame; "Keep the same" updates. Each is tied to its v20 curve by a thumbnail only.
- **Risk table (scene 9).** Three spoken rows, each hazard, harm and control: hydrogen peroxide 3% (irritates eyes and skin: safety glasses, wipe up spills, wash skin); hot water baths at 50 and 70 °C (scalds: tongs or heat-proof gloves); the cork borer (cuts: press straight down onto a tile). Teacher-note rows, not shown: boiling the control discs; the glass syringe (breakage; don't force the plunger); disposal of potato and dilute peroxide; the bung not forced so tight that it can't be removed. Shown table: only the row being spoken; earlier rows collapse to one-word grey chips (v05's rule). Codex to confirm against NSW Department of Education Chemical Safety in Schools guidance, including the 3% concentration and the site's conflicting "irritant" and "corrosive" wording (site finding 1).

- **Site alignment (L17, kept in substance).** The Before You Read question (change one thing, measure, keep two the same) as the frame (1, 2); the vocabulary for independent, dependent and controlled variables, validity and reliability (2, 6, 7); the misconception "change two variables at once" and its fix (1, 2, crossed in 10b); card 2's plan ("change the independent variable (e.g. temperature using water baths), measure the dependent variable (rate of oxygen produced, bubbles per minute, gas volume, or height of foam), and keep controlled variables constant") and "repeat each level (at least 3 trials) and average" (2 to 6); card 2's safety box (9); card 3's control ("a tube with boiled, denatured enzyme"), "a gas syringe gives a more accurate volume than counting bubbles by eye" and "uneven water-bath temperature" (3, 5, 7); the Propose walk-through's levels (10, 20, 37, 50 and 70 °C), its equilibration step, 60 s collection and three trials (2, 4, 6); the Assess walk-through's bubble criticism and single-trial criticism (3, 6, 10a); short answer 2's "too coarse to locate an optimum" (7, 10a); short answer 3's substrate dilution (8). - **Attempt systems (unseen, every condition visible, HSC wording, original).**

### 2. one change at a time: the variables
> The fix is a fair test: change one thing, and keep everything else the same. The thing you change is the independent variable. Here, that's temperature, using water baths at ten, twenty, thirty-seven, fifty and seventy **degrees**. What you measure is the dependent variable: the rate of the **reaction**. And everything you keep the same are the controlled variables: the size and number of potato discs, the volume and concentration of hydrogen peroxide, the pH, and the **time**. Change one thing. Two suspects means no **conviction**.

### 3. measuring the rate
> How do you measure the rate? Catalase breaks hydrogen peroxide down into water and oxygen, so measure the **oxygen**. Better: collect the gas in a gas syringe, and read the volume after exactly sixty **seconds**. That's the average rate of oxygen production in the first minute.

### 4. the method
> Here's the method. Cut potato discs with a cork borer, all the same size, and use five in each **trial**. Put ten millilitres of three percent hydrogen peroxide in the flask, with the discs in a small tube hanging above it. Stand the flask in the water bath for five **minutes**. Why warm both? If the discs went in cold, they'd spend the whole minute warming up, so the enzyme would never really be at the temperature you **recorded**. Then seal the flask to the syringe, tip the discs into the peroxide, and start the **stopwatch**.

### 5. a control, and a quick prediction
> At each temperature, one more tube is the control: discs that were boiled beforehand, in the same peroxide at that temperature. Quick prediction: will the boiled discs make oxygen? Hardly any. Boiling denatures the catalase. So if the boiled discs make almost no gas, the oxygen in the other tubes most likely came from active enzyme, not from the peroxide breaking down by **itself**. A control isn't a controlled variable: it's an extra set-up, here one without active **enzyme**.

### 7. valid, and how to improve it
> Is the test valid? Valid means it really tests what it claims to. Only the temperature is meant to change, and oxygen made in the first minute measures the rate. But leaks, differences between discs and baths that drift can still weaken it, so improve **it**. With gaps of up to twenty degrees, you can't pin down the optimum, so add more temperatures between thirty and **fifty**. And check each water bath with a thermometer, because a bath that drifts changes the very thing you're **testing**.

### 12. key notes (quiet, hold about 60 seconds)

> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

1. Fair test: change one independent variable (temperature: water baths at 10, 20, 37, 50, 70 °C); measure the dependent variable (average rate: volume of oxygen in a gas syringe in the first 60 s, or foam height); keep the controlled variables the same (disc size and number, volume and concentration of H₂O₂, pH, time).

## Memory aid check

- **"Change one thing. Two suspects means no conviction."** (Site hook, l17.)
