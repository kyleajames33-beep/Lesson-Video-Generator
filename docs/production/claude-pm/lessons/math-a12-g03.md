- **Worked problem 1 (site lesson 13 Problem 2, numbers unchanged): the maximum loan.** A buyer can afford $2,800 a month; 4.8% p.a. compounded monthly; 25 years. Model: a reducing-balance loan (equal repayments, interest on the balance), so the balance after the last repayment is zero; the unknown is P. r = 0.048/12 = 0.004 and n = 300 (per month). A₃₀₀ = P(1.004)³⁰⁰ − 2800[(1.004)³⁰⁰ − 1]/0.004 = 0, so P = 2800[(1.004)³⁰⁰ − 1]/[0.004(1.004)³⁰⁰] ≈ $488,658.79 to the nearest cent. Rounded down to stay within the budget: $488,658.78 (with $488,658.79 the balance after 300 repayments of $2,800 would be about 1 cent short of zero; with .78 it is about 2 cents in credit). Grey note: "another form you may see: P = M[1 − (1 + r)^(−n)]/r" (site's transposed form; decision 47). The words "can afford $2,800 a month" make M the given and P the unknown; the crossed slip is "P = 2,800 × 300 = $840,000" (ignores interest; that is the total repaid).
- **Worked problem 2 (original): same numbers, two models.** "A delivery takes between 40 and 60 minutes. Find the probability that it takes more than 58 minutes" under two different givens: (a) "every time in that range is equally likely": uniform on [40, 60], f(x) = 1/20, P(X > 58) = 2/20 = 0.1; (b) "delivery times are normally distributed with mean 50 minutes and standard deviation 4 minutes": 58 is 2 standard deviations above the mean, so P(X > 58) is about 2.5% by the empirical rule (about 0.025). The cause: the model sets the shape; a flat density puts as much probability in the last 2 minutes as anywhere else, while a normal curve piles probability near the mean and leaves thin tails. Scope: (b) does not claim all times lie in [40, 60]; the normal model only says almost all (about 95%) lie within 42 to 58. The exact normal value 0.0228 is not given (s11's tables own it); "about 2.5%" is the empirical-rule answer (s10 convention).

### 2. finance words and their models
> Here are the finance phrases to look for. The same amount added each period means an arithmetic model, a straight line. The same percentage of the current amount means a geometric model, multiplying by one plus r each period. Equal end-of-period deposits into an account that starts at zero add up to a geometric series, so that's an annuity, with future value M times one plus r to the n, minus one, all over **r**. And equal repayments on a loan, where interest is charged on the balance, give the loan model, with the balance zero when it's paid off. In every one, r is the rate for one **period**, and n counts the periods. Which model does "can afford a repayment each month" point to? Think for three **seconds**. It's a loan with equal repayments.

### 4. probability words and their models
> Probability questions work the same way. If the question lists the values with their probabilities, it's a discrete distribution, and the probabilities add to **one**. If it gives a density function, probabilities are areas under it, and the total area is one. If equal-width parts of an interval are equally likely, the density is flat, one over the width of the interval. And if it says normally distributed, with a mean and a standard deviation, the curve is a bell, and the empirical rule or z-scores give the **probabilities**. Here's a test. A question asks how likely a delivery is to take more than fifty-eight minutes. Which model fits? Think for three **seconds**. You can't tell yet. The question has to say which shape it **means**.

### 5. worked problem 2: same numbers, two models
> First, suppose the time is uniform from forty to sixty minutes, so equal stretches of time are equally likely. The density is flat, at one over twenty. More than fifty-eight minutes is the last two minutes of the twenty, so the probability is two twentieths, which is one in **ten**. Now suppose instead the times are normally distributed, with mean fifty and standard deviation four. Fifty-eight is two standard deviations above the mean. About ninety-five per cent of times are within two standard deviations, so about five per cent are outside, and half of that is above. That's about two point five per **cent**. Same threshold, different shape, so a different answer. And the normal model isn't even limited to forty to sixty. The normal curve piles its probability near the mean, and leaves only a thin tail out **there**.

### 6. worked problem 3: a list against a density
> One more pair. In the first question, a spinner lands on one, two, three or four, with probability k x. In the second, X is a choc chip's distance from the centre of a cookie, in centimetres, and it has density k x, from zero to four. Same rule, but is k the **same**? For the list, the four probabilities add to one. That's k plus two k plus three k plus four k, which is ten k, so k is one **tenth**. For the density, the area under k x from zero to four is one. That's a triangle, half of four times four k, which is eight k, so k is one **eighth**, the cookie's density from the earlier video. A sum of bars and an area under a line are different totals, so they give different values of **k**.

## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why one phrase changes the answer: simple interest adds the same amount, compound multiplies (hook, with the bending dots). Why the annuity is a geometric series: each deposit earns compound interest for a different number of periods (2; recalled from f13). Why the maximum loan uses Aₙ = 0: the loan is repaid exactly at the last payment, and the unknown is P (3). Why round down: a larger P would leave money owing (3).
