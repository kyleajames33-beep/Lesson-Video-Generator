### 2. the boundary: membrane, cytoplasm and wall
> Start at the edge. The **cell membrane** is a very thin double layer of fatty molecules called phospholipids, with proteins set into it. It's partially permeable, which means it lets some substances through and blocks others, so it controls what enters and leaves. Inside is the **cytoplasm**, a jelly-like fluid where many reactions happen and the organelles sit. Plant cells add a **cell wall** outside the membrane. It's made of cellulose, a tough fibre, and it's rigid, so it gives the cell its shape and support. Animal cells don't have one.

### 8. mitochondria

So a mitochondrion doesn't make energy. It's a money changer, not a mint: it converts, it never prints.

### 11. chloroplasts

> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

## Numbers or facts to verify

Self-check (1 Nov) against standard references (Campbell Biology; Alberts, Molecular Biology of the Cell). The Codex science check must confirm each before voicing.
- **Cell membrane (scene 2).** Phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins; selectively (partially) permeable. Correct.
- **Cell wall.** Plant cell walls are mainly cellulose, outside the membrane; rigid, give shape and support (and resist bursting, not spoken). Correct.
- **Nucleus (scene 3).** "usually the largest organelle" (true for most animal cells; a plant's central vacuole can be larger, hence "usually"). Nuclear envelope = two membranes with nuclear pores; mRNA leaves through the pores (spoken "copies of those instructions"). Correct.
- **Ribosomes (scene 4).** Two subunits of RNA and protein, not membrane-bound; free or bound to the ER; they join amino acids into a polypeptide by reading mRNA. "many sit on a membrane network beside the nucleus" (the rough ER). Correct.
- **ER (scene 5).** Network of flattened sacs (cisternae) and tubules, continuous with the outer nuclear membrane. Proteins made by bound ribosomes enter the rough ER lumen, fold and are modified there, and leave in vesicles. Smooth ER: lipid synthesis and detoxification (spoken "breaks down some toxins"). Correct. Site error 1 concerns attribution of synthesis.
- **Golgi body (scene 6).** Stack of flattened membrane sacs; receives vesicles from the ER, modifies proteins (for example adds or trims carbohydrate groups, glycosylation; spoken "adding sugar groups"), sorts and packages them into vesicles for secretion, the membrane or lysosomes. Correct.
- **Export (scene 7).** Secretory vesicles fuse with the plasma membrane and release their contents (exocytosis; not named here, v13). Correct.
- **Mitochondria (scene 8).** Double membrane; inner membrane folded into cristae, increasing surface area for the electron transport chain; site of aerobic respiration (the first stage, glycolysis, happens in the cytoplasm; "the reactions of aerobic respiration" and "Here, energy stored in glucose is transferred into ATP" follow the site and Year 11 level, with v16 owning the stages). Codex to judge whether "Here" needs softening to "Mitochondria are where most of ..." (about 20 characters more). Muscle cells have many mitochondria. Correct.
- **Lysosomes (scene 9).** Single membrane; contain hydrolytic (digestive) enzymes that work best in acidic conditions; break down worn-out organelles (autophagy), waste and engulfed material. Their membrane keeps the enzymes from the cytoplasm. Correct at this level (lysosomes are characteristic of animal cells; plant cells use the vacuole for much of this, not spoken; v03's concern).
- **Vacuoles (scene 10).** Membrane-bound (the tonoplast, not named); store water, ions, nutrients and wastes; a large central vacuole in most mature plant cells presses the cytoplasm against the wall (turgor). "Animal cells have small ones, if any." Correct.
- **Chloroplasts (scene 11).** Two outer membranes (an envelope) and internal thylakoid membranes stacked into grana, holding chlorophyll; site of photosynthesis; in plant and algal cells. Correct.
- **"most are wrapped in membrane" (scene 11).** Seven of the eleven are membrane-bound organelles (nucleus, ER, Golgi body, mitochondria, lysosomes, vacuoles, chloroplasts); the cell membrane is itself a membrane; ribosomes, cytoplasm and the cell wall are not membrane-bound. "most" holds. Correct.

- **Attempt 2.** A hypothetical block at Golgi exit. Real agents block other steps (for example brefeldin A blocks ER to Golgi transport); the item names no drug. Correct as a reasoning item.
