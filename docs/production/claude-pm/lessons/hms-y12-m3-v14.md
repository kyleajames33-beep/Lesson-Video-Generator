### 3. privatisation, in degrees
> The syllabus names **privatisation**, moving ownership, delivery or funding from the public sector to the private sector. It isn't one event. It comes in degrees. Most GP, specialist and pathology care is already privately delivered and publicly funded. Public hospitals contract out cleaning or scans. Private firms build or run hospitals under contract. Private hospitals treat public patients to shorten waiting lists. And the private sector grows, helped by support for private insurance. The case for: it adds capacity without public money upfront, it can be efficient for clear, measurable services, and it gives people choice. The case against: profit isn't always aligned with health, contracts are hard to write and monitor, private capacity goes where paying demand is, not need, and both sectors draw on the same workers, so growth in one can drain the other. So the sharper question isn't public or private. It's who carries the risk, and who's accountable when it goes wrong.
