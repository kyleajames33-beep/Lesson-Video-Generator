## Decisions

- **Site source used:** `site-extracts/chem-m5-l12.md` (read) and `site-corrections.md` (lesson 12 items C1, C11 and the lesson 10 cross-reference). Site order reused: K says where a reaction is heading, Q says where it is now; the same expression with current concentrations; Q < K right, Q > K left, Q = K no net shift; the five steps (write the expression, identify the current concentrations, calculate Q, compare with K, state the direction); adding a reactant or removing a product lowers Q and shifts right, and the reverse raises Q and shifts left; Q starts at 0 from pure reactants and off the scale from pure products, then converges on K. The site's misconceptions are all addressed: different formulas (scene 2), "Q and K are equal when the same concentrations are used" (scene 8: Q equals K only when settled), "Q < K shifts left" (scenes 5, 11 and the aid), and "removing the product raises K" (scene 9: only Q moves).
- **Site numbers replaced, and why:** the site's examples are SO2/O2/SO3 (K = 270 against 280 at 700 C, a confirmed inconsistency, correction C11), N2 + 3H2 ⇌ 2NH3 (K = 0.500, Q = 4.17) and H2 + I2 (K = 54.3). The first is not used at all (so the 270 and 280 are not repeated). The CO + 3H2 ⇌ CH4 + H2O short answer with K = 5.67 x 10^6 (correction C1; the real value is about 8 x 10^24) is not used. The H2 + I2 tube keeps K = 49 at about 450 C (C5 policy, not the site's 54). The ammonia equation and K = 0.50 at 400 C are kept from the site's worked example 1 for the attempt, with a different mixture so it is unseen (the site's mixture 0.200, 0.300, 0.150 mol/L is not used).
- **Cause chain taught, in order:** (1) a mixture that has not settled will change, and we want to know which way before it does (hook, 1); (2) Q is the K fraction with the values in the tube now, so K needs settled values and Q takes any values (2); (3) calculating Q is arithmetic on the expression (3); (4) Q is below K, so this mixture has less product, relative to reactant, than a settled one would (4); (5) why that gives a direction: in the rate model from C5 the forward rate is a fixed number times [H2][I2] and the reverse rate a fixed number times [HI]^2, so forward / reverse = (ratio of the fixed numbers) x [H2][I2] / [HI]^2 = K x (1 / Q) = K / Q, which is above 1 when Q < K (5); (6) as the faster reaction runs, Q moves toward K, because making product raises Q and using it up lowers Q, and it stops when the rates are equal, which is Q = K ("Q chases K"; the C2 aid "the faster rate falls, the slower rate rises" supplies the movement) (6); (7) the same thing from the other side: Q above K means too much product, reverse faster, and a tube that looks mostly products can still need to calculate (7); (8) Q = K means equal rates, not equal concentrations, and the two ends of the scale are the pure-reactant and pure-product starts met in C2 (8); (9) a disturbance changes Q and leaves K alone, which turns the concentration half of Le Chatelier into a Q against K comparison, shown on the settled H2/I2/HI tube with extra hydrogen (9); (10) the attempt (10); (11) feedback names the likely wrong answers (11).
- **Entry knowledge:** C1 to C6 and Year 11. The learner knows dynamic equilibrium with equal rates (C1), the C2 flasks and the faster-falls, slower-rises picture including the products-only start, the C4b H2 + I2 tube (dots 9, 9, 0 settling at 2, 2, 14) and the C5 rate model for H2 + I2 (forward = 0.05 x [H2][I2], reverse = (0.05 / 49) x [HI]^2, so K = 0.05 / (0.05 / 49) = 49), the K expression and rules (C5), and the C6 methods: the settled tube 0.10, 0.10, 0.70 mol/L with K = 49 (0.70^2 / (0.10 x 0.10) = 49), the rule "substitute equilibrium values into K, never initial ones", and concentration in mol/L. Nothing about Q, direction prediction from numbers, or the K/Q rate ratio is assumed.
- **Numbers and arithmetic shown:** all in "Numbers to verify" in the final reply and repeated below. The H2 + I2 mixtures use mol/L, one dot = 0.05 mol/L so the dots match the C4b and C6 tube. Q is reported to the figures of the data (6.25 and 256 exactly; 16.3 to 3 s.f.; 0.80 to 2 s.f.). Q is written without a unit (C5 and C6 policy, see open decisions).
- **Learner-visible definitions:** reaction quotient, Q (the K fraction worked out with the concentrations in the mixture right now); "has not settled" (the rates are not yet equal and the amounts are still changing); "too much product for this temperature" (Q above K) and "too little" (Q below K); forward (left to right as the equation is written) and reverse.
- **Two worked mixtures that end in the same tube (the central design choice):** Mixture A (0.20 mol/L H2, 0.20 I2, 0.50 HI; dots 4, 4, 10) has Q = 6.25 < 49. Mixture B (0.05, 0.05, 0.80; dots 1, 1, 16) has Q = 256 > 49. Both hold the same total hydrogen as the tube in the ICE table video (H2 + HI / 2 = 0.45 mol/L: 0.20 + 0.25, 0.05 + 0.40, 0.10 + 0.35) and the same total iodine, so both settle at 0.10, 0.10, 0.70 (dots 2, 2, 14): A by using up 0.10 mol/L of each reactant, B by making 0.05 mol/L of each. This shows Q chasing K from below and from above, ties to the C2 "same destination from either side" idea, and keeps one tube across C4b, C6 and C8. The settled values are given as an outcome, not solved (solving needs the quadratic, g03).
- **Attempt system (unseen, every condition visible):** N2(g) + 3H2(g) ⇌ 2NH3(g) in a sealed rigid vessel with an iron catalyst at a constant 400 C, K = 0.50 for this equation, mixture just made up: [N2] = 0.40, [H2] = 0.50, [NH3] = 0.20 mol/L. Demands: (1) write Q and calculate it; (2) will the mixture make more ammonia or less, with a reason. Answer: Q = 0.040 / (0.40 x 0.125) = 0.80 > 0.50, so the reverse reaction is faster and ammonia falls. The numbers are chosen so that forgetting the cube gives 0.20 (below K), which reverses the conclusion, so the power error is visible in the direction and not only in the value. About 100 seconds (write the expression, cube, multiply, divide, compare). The mixture appears pale and mixed (no extra tint cue). The first-use ICE scaffold of C6 is not used because no ICE table is needed.
- **Mistake scene:** none separate. The wrong instincts (flipping the inequality, forgetting the powers, thinking only settled values can go in, thinking Q = K means equal concentrations, thinking an added substance changes K, reading a mostly-product tube as Q < K) are corrected in scenes 2, 5, 7, 8, 9 and 11, with a named wrong answer for the first two in the feedback.
- **Reuse:** the C4b/C6 hot H2 + I2 tube with 0.05 mol/L dots; the C2 forward (blue) and reverse (amber) rate bars and the teal "equal" bracket; the C5 expression builder (fraction bar, bracket chips, coefficient-to-power flight); the C6 givens card, task line and board zones; the countdown ring; key notes board. New in C8: a coded log number line with a fixed K marker and a sliding Q marker (the Q marker is linked to the rate bars so the bar heights follow K / Q), a rate-ratio algebra line (a division that cancels the fixed numbers and leaves K over Q), and a small tube-to-number-line link (dots change while the marker slides). No prototype components.
- **Author notes (not learner copy):** (a) Forward / reverse = K / Q is exact for elementary reactions and holds in the C5 teaching rate models; for a multi-step real mechanism the relation between net rate and Q / K is still true in sign (Q < K means the net reaction is forward, by thermodynamics) but the ratio need not equal K / Q. The learner copy says "in this model" for the ratio (as C5 does) and states the direction rule as general. (b) Q chasing K without overshoot holds for a single reaction in a closed vessel at constant temperature, which is the condition tag. (c) Q for a reaction with a changed equation: if the equation is reversed both Q and K become their reciprocals, so Q < K becomes Q' > K' and the physical direction is unchanged. The video teaches "same equation for Q and K" as a condition and does not show the reversal. (d) "Off the scale" for a products-only start: the denominator is zero, so Q is undefined as a number and is treated as larger than any K; the learner copy says "off the scale". (e) The settled values 0.10, 0.10, 0.70 for A and B are exact consequences of K = 49 and the shared totals (A: x = 0.10; B: y = 0.05). (f) The attempt K (0.50 at 400 C) is the classic textbook Kc for ammonia synthesis in L^2 mol^-2 (Kp about 1.6 x 10^-4 atm^-2 gives Kc = Kp x (RT)^2 = about 0.49); the mixture totals 1.10 mol/L, which at 673 K is about 61 atm, a realistic Haber pressure. Codex to confirm. (g) Pronunciation: "Q" as the letter, "K" as the letter, "reaction quotient" (quoh-shunt), "H two", "I two", "H I", "N two", "N H three"; say "nought point" rather than "point"; say "ammonia squared" and "hydrogen cubed". Check that "0.040" and "0.0025" are clear in the voiced preview ("four hundredths", "twenty-five ten-thousandths" are fallbacks). (h) The industrial link (condensing ammonia out so Q falls below K) is left to c07 and g03; it is one clause the learner can add after scene 9. (i) The site's "effect of dilution on Q" and Qsp are out of scope.

Main question: *Given a mixture that has not settled, and the K for that temperature, how can you tell which way it will run before anything changes?*
Entry knowledge (C1 to C6 and Year 11): dynamic equilibrium and equal rates; the faster rate falls and the slower rate rises (C2); the C4b/C6 H2 + I2 tubes; K as a products-over-reactants fraction with coefficient powers (C5); the C6 settled tube 0.10, 0.10, 0.70 with K = 49 and "equilibrium values only for K"; concentration in mol/L; the rate model with fixed numbers (C5).
Stops after: writing Q for an equation and calculating it from supplied concentrations; comparing Q with K at the same temperature and stating the direction with the rate reason; saying that Q = K means equal rates and no net change; saying that a disturbance changes Q and not K; using the five steps on an unseen mixture. No how-far calculation, no ICE table, no quadratic, no temperature dependence, no Qsp.
Handoff: chem-m5-g03, harder ICE and Q decisions (the route lists g03 next at playlist order 12, then c09). If the playlist runs c09 first, change the line and chip in scene 12.

### 2. Q is the K fraction with the numbers from now
> So far K has always taken the settled values. Now take the same fraction, hydrogen iodide squared over hydrogen times iodine, and put in the concentrations **in the tube right now**, settled or not. That number is the **reaction quotient**, Q. It follows the same rules as K, with powers for coefficients, and solids and the solvent left out. At equilibrium Q is K. At any other moment, Q can be any value.

Visual: on "same fraction" the C5 builder writes "K = [HI]^2 / ([H2][I2])" and a tag "settled values only" under it. On "in the tube right now" a second copy writes beside it as "Q = [HI]^2 / ([H2][I2])" with the tag "values right now" and a pen arrow from the dots in the tube to the brackets. ### 3. calculate Q

### 5. why a small Q means the forward reaction wins (the rate link)
> Remember the rate model from the expressions video: joining is a fixed number times hydrogen times iodine, and splitting is another fixed number times hydrogen iodide squared. **Divide the two.** The two fixed numbers divide to give **K**. The concentrations divide to give the **Q fraction upside down**, hydrogen times iodine over hydrogen iodide squared. So in this model forward over reverse is K times one over Q, which is **K over Q**. That is forty-nine over 6.25, about **eight**, so the forward reaction runs about eight times faster, and hydrogen iodide is made.

### 6. Q chases K
> As hydrogen iodide forms, the top of Q grows and the bottom shrinks, so Q **climbs**. The forward rate falls, the reverse rate rises, just as in the flask videos, until the two are **equal**, because K over Q is then one. That happens exactly when **Q reaches K**, here at **0.10, 0.10 and 0.70**, the settled tube from the ICE table video, where Q is forty-nine. Q chases K.

### 8. Q equals K, and the two ends of the scale
> If Q equals K, as in the settled tube, forward and reverse run at the same rate and nothing changes overall. That does **not** mean equal concentrations: this tube has 0.10, 0.10 and 0.70. And if a flask holds **only reactants**, like the P C L five flask at the start, the top of Q is zero, so Q is below any K and the reaction runs forward. If it holds **only products**, the bottom is zero, Q is **off the scale**, above any K, and it runs in reverse.

### 11. feedback
> Write Q as ammonia squared over nitrogen times **hydrogen cubed**. Ammonia squared is **0.040**. Hydrogen cubed is 0.50 times 0.50 times 0.50, **0.125**, and times the nitrogen that is **0.050**. So Q is 0.040 over 0.050, which is **0.80**. Q is above K, 0.50, so the mixture holds too much ammonia for this temperature. The reverse reaction is faster and ammonia **breaks down**, so it makes less. If you got **0.20**, you left the cube off the hydrogen, and that wrong number sits below K, so you would have said more ammonia. If you found 0.80 and still said more ammonia, you **flipped the rule**: a Q above K means too much product, not too little.

### 12. decision rule and handoff
> So when a question gives you a mixture and a K, **write the expression, put in the concentrations from now, calculate Q, and compare it with K** at the same temperature. Q below K, the reaction runs forward. Q above K, it runs back. Equal, nothing changes overall. That gives you the direction, not how far it goes. Finding how far uses an ICE table and K, and the harder cases come **next**.

### 13. key notes (quiet, hold about 90 seconds)
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

- The reaction quotient, Q, is the equilibrium expression worked out with the concentrations at any moment: products over reactants, each concentration raised to the power of its coefficient, with solids and the solvent left out. K uses settled values only. At equilibrium Q equals K.
- Compare Q with K for the same equation, written the same way, at the same temperature. Q below K: too little product, the forward reaction is faster and the mixture runs right. Q above K: too much product, the reverse reaction is faster and the mixture runs left. Q equals K: equal rates, no net change.
- Why: in a simple model where each rate is a fixed number times the concentrations, forward rate divided by reverse rate equals K divided by Q. Real reactions can have more steps, but the direction rule still holds. As the faster reaction runs, Q moves toward K and stops there. Equal rates do not mean equal concentrations.
- Adding or removing a substance (at the same volume and temperature) changes Q, not K. Only temperature changes K (a later video). Q gives the direction only, not how far or how fast.
- Method: write the expression, find the concentrations now, calculate Q, compare with K, state the direction with the reason.

Memory aid (large, on the notes screen): *Q chases K.* Second line: *Q too small, make more products. Q too big, make more reactants.* Condition tag: "same temperature, same equation as written."

## Memory aid check

"Q chases K." From the bank entry "Q chases K" (Reaction quotient Q section; the bank calls it an insight): Q is calculated like K with the current concentrations, and the reaction always moves so that Q moves toward K (Q < K forward, Q > K reverse, Q = K at equilibrium).
