## Decisions

- **Main question:** *A class prepares pentyl ethanoate, the banana-smelling ester, and gets about 60% of the most it could make. Is that a failed practical? How do you prepare an ester safely, calculate its yield, explain what limits it, and design a fair test of whether excess acid helps?* Heading on screen: "Making an ester: is 60% a fail?"
- **Why pentyl ethanoate (and pentan-1-ol):** c10 used ethyl ethanoate throughout, so a new ester keeps the practical from being a replay while recalling the same steps. Pentyl ethanoate is a common school preparation (site L15 lists it, and c10 names it as the banana ester), its alcohol and ester are only flammable (H226), not highly flammable (H225) like ethanol and ethyl ethanoate, and the ester barely dissolves in water (about 0.2 g per 100 mL), so the layers separate cleanly with the ester on top (density 0.876 g/mL). The mixture's boiling point is well above 100 °C, so reflux needs an electric heating mantle; a boiling water bath cannot bring it to reflux. This replaces the route review's "water bath for ester reflux" (written with ethanol in mind), which the narration addresses in one clause (author note b). The hook links to c10's fruit plate (banana).
- **Why an investigation, not just a recipe:** the dotpoint says "investigate". A yield comparison between a 1:1 and a 2:1 ratio of acid to alcohol gives the practical an aim, an independent variable, controlled variables and data to judge, and it tests c10's Le Chatelier prediction in the lab. The alcohol sets the theoretical maximum in both (limiting at 2:1; at 1:1 both reagents set the same maximum), so both yields are measured against the same amount of alcohol.
- **Numbers (author note a; Numbers to verify):** worked group (2:1): 4.41 g pentan-1-ol (about 5.4 mL) → 0.0500 mol; 5.7 mL glacial ethanoic acid (0.0996 mol, 1.99:1; Revision 2, science S4); maximum 0.0500 mol pentyl ethanoate × 130.18 g/mol = 6.51 g; collected 3.90 g (vial 12.36 g, vial + ester 16.26 g; screen only) → 59.9%, said "about sixty per cent". Class data (screen and scene 8): 1:1 runs 41, 38, 44% (mean 41%); 2:1 runs 60, 57, 63% (mean 60%). Revision 2: no numerical ceilings (science S2: K about 4 for ethanol is not a verified value for this system at reflux), and no equal-loss argument. All yield data are labelled "example data".
- **Site sources used (site lesson 15):** the conditions panel ("REAGENT: Carboxylic acid + alcohol (at least one in excess for higher yield)"; "CONDITIONS: Heat under reflux"; "YIELD: <100% at equilibrium"); the Safety note (concentrated sulfuric acid "highly corrosive and reacts violently and exothermically with water, add it dropwise to the cooled mixture, wear goggles and gloves"; "heat with an electric heating mantle or water bath rather than a naked flame, add anti-bumping granules, and never seal the apparatus"; "work in a well-ventilated area"; scenes 3 and 11); the Isolation Procedure (cold water in the separating funnel; "Identify the aqueous layer from density data or a drop test rather than assuming it is always lower"; the carbonate wash with frequent venting "away from people"; anhydrous Na₂SO₄ or MgSO₄ that "clumps as it hydrates"; "Collect the fraction at the known boiling point"; scenes 5 and 10); Worked Example 2 (use an excess; reflux without a naked flame; reflux "improves rate while retaining volatile components"; scenes 2 and 3); the common esters list and Q6 (pentyl ethanoate, banana). Q8's yield question uses "55% yield" without a calculation; this video adds the calculation the site does not show.
- **Wash reagent:** the site and c10 use sodium carbonate solution. This design uses sodium hydrogen carbonate solution (about 5%), which does the same job (turns leftover carboxylic acid into a salt in the water layer, releasing CO₂), is the c09 fizz-test reagent students already know, and is not classified as an eye irritant (sodium carbonate is H319). The key notes say "NaHCO₃ (or Na₂CO₃) solution" so the two do not look contradictory (Open decisions).
- **Main teaching move (Revision 2):** the yield is limited twice. Equilibrium sets a ceiling below 100% (not measured here); the work-up decides how close you get. A correctly weighed dry, pure product gives a correct isolated yield even when it is low; repeats show how much isolated yields vary but cannot recover lost ester. Water or other impurities make the mass too high, so a yield above 100% is a red flag, not a better reaction. The class data support a higher isolated yield with excess acid under the tested conditions; blaming equilibrium alone would also need evidence on reaction progress, purity and recovery, shown as evaluation questions.

- **Attempt systems (unseen, every condition visible, HSC wording, original):** (A1) "A student heated 3.70 g of butan-1-ol with excess ethanoic acid and a few drops of concentrated sulfuric acid under reflux. After washing and drying the product, she collected 3.19 g of butyl ethanoate. (a) Calculate the percentage yield of butyl ethanoate. (b) Explain TWO reasons why the yield is less than 100%." (4 marks.) Molar masses on the card: butan-1-ol 74.12 g mol⁻¹; butyl ethanoate 116.16 g mol⁻¹. New alcohol and ester. Answer: n(butan-1-ol) = 3.70 ÷ 74.12 = 0.0499 mol; 1:1, so the maximum n(ester) = 0.0499 mol; maximum mass = 0.0499 × 116.16 = 5.80 g; yield = 3.19 ÷ 5.80 × 100 = 55.0%. Reasons: the reaction is reversible and reaches equilibrium, so not all the alcohol reacts; ester is lost during transfers, washing and drying. Crossed mistake: "3.19 ÷ 3.70 × 100 = 86%" (dividing masses directly, the moles step skipped). (A2) "A student prepared propyl ethanoate from propan-1-ol and excess ethanoic acid. She ran off the water layer from the separating funnel and weighed the ester layer straight away, without washing or drying it. She calculated a percentage yield of 104%. Explain why her result is inaccurate, and describe TWO changes to her method that would give a more accurate yield." (3 marks.) Answer: more than 100% means the weighed liquid contained substances other than ester (water, and probably leftover ethanoic acid and propan-1-ol), so the mass and yield are too high; wash with sodium hydrogen carbonate solution (then water), dry with anhydrous magnesium sulfate, and if possible distil, collecting near the ester's boiling point, before weighing. Crossed mistake: "Over 100%, so the reaction went to completion" (even complete conversion caps the yield at 100%, and esterification stops at equilibrium).

From the esters video, you'd predict the excess pushes the equilibrium **right**.

### 3. the reflux set-up
> Measure the pentan-1-ol and the ethanoic acid into a round-bottom flask, and add a few anti-bumping granules. Your teacher then adds about ten drops of concentrated sulfuric acid, the **catalyst**. Fit the condenser upright, and leave its top **open**. This mixture boils well above a hundred degrees, so a water bath isn't hot enough. Heat it with an electric heating mantle for about forty minutes. The condenser sends the vapours back, so they don't **escape**.

### 4. the variables
> What changes, and what stays the same? The independent variable is the mole ratio of acid to alcohol: one to one, or two to one. The dependent variable is the percentage **yield**. Keep the amount of pentan-1-ol, the catalyst, the heating time, and every **work-up** step the same.
