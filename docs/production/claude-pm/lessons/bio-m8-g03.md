### 2b. q1-build (five parts, in order)
> Build it in order, and take each part from the stem. The **stimulus** is the change in the variable: blood pressure falls. The **receptors** are the baroreceptors. The **control centre** is the cardiovascular centre in the medulla. The **effectors** are the heart and the arterioles. Now the **response**, which is where answers often fall short. A faster, stronger heartbeat pumps more blood each minute, and narrower arterioles hold the pressure up, so blood pressure **rises back** towards normal. Then close the loop with an arrow back to the stimulus, marked **minus**, because the response opposes the fall.

### 4b. q3-identify (one test for every process)
> Test each process with one question: does the response **oppose** the change, or **strengthen** it? In P, water content was falling, and closing the stomata slows the loss. That opposes the change. In R, ethylene speeds ripening, ripening releases more ethylene, and that speeds ripening further. The response strengthens the change, so R is **positive feedback**. ### 6. key-notes (reuse take: key-notes; quiet, about 60 seconds)
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

- Build a loop in order: stimulus (the change in the variable) → receptor → control centre → effector → response (what happens to the variable), with a minus on the arrow back to the stimulus.
- The response is not the effector's action: "the heart beats faster" is the effector; "blood pressure rises back towards normal" is the response.
- Glucose after a meal: glucose peaks first and insulin follows, because the rise is the stimulus. Insulin makes liver, muscle and fat cells take up glucose (stored as glycogen), so glucose and then insulin fall. Below the usual level: glucagon, the liver releases glucose from glycogen.
- Hormones are signals. Name the target cells (effectors) that change the variable.
- Negative feedback: the response opposes the change, whether the variable goes up or down. Positive feedback: the response strengthens the change, until an end point (ripening with ethylene, blood clotting).

Memory aid (large): *Compare the response with the change. Opposes: negative. Adds to it: positive.*

   Model: (a) Blood glucose peaked at 30 minutes (7.6 mmol/L) and insulin at 45 minutes (62 mU/L) (1 mark).
