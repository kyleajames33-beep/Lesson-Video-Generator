## Decisions

- **Main teaching move: a demonstration turns an invisible particle process into a change you can see or measure, and the particle model explains that change.** Every set-up is taught in the same fixed frame on screen, "Set up / Measure / Means", introduced in the hook. Students who keep that frame can outline any diffusion or osmosis demonstration; the site's Activity 1 asks exactly this (method, what you would measure, expected result).
- **The hook's surprise (scene 1).** A bag of sugar water in plain water gets heavier although nothing was poured in; colour climbs through still water with nobody stirring. Both are changes with no visible cause, which is the problem the video solves. The v09 definitions are not restated as a separate scene: each is applied where its demonstration is explained (scenes 4 and 6), with v09's title on a grey tag.
- **Diffusion demonstration: potassium permanganate in still water (scenes 2 to 4).** The site's method. The video adds the condition that makes it evidence for diffusion: the water must be still and at room temperature, because stirring or currents of warm water rising would carry the colour by themselves. Stated as a cause in plain words, not as the word convection. The route's "or dyed agar" alternative is a screen-only grey tag with its reason (jelly can't swirl), cut from the narration for length; agar cube calculations stay in FA2 (route stop).
- **What to measure for diffusion (scene 3).** The distance the coloured edge has moved from the crystal at timed intervals (every two minutes, against a ruler behind the beaker), or the time until the beaker is evenly coloured. No diffusion numbers are spoken; the coded ruler marks the edge on each painted stage.
- **Particle explanation (scene 4).** A coded zoom into the gradient: random motion, more particles near the crystal, so more move away than back; net movement down the gradient; at even colour the particles still move, but there is no net movement. A recall of v09 applied to the beaker, not re-taught.
- **Osmosis demonstration: Visking tubing (scenes 5 and 6).** The site's method: tubing filled with concentrated sugar solution, tied, weighed, placed in distilled water, reweighed after 30 minutes. The video names the sugar as sucrose (table sugar) and adds the details a student needs to write the method: soaking the tubing, knotting both ends, rinsed and blotted the same way before every weighing.
- **Science correction carried: sugar and Visking tubing (scene 5; site finding 1 below).** The site's short-answer model says "water molecules are small enough to cross but sugar molecules are not". Standard dialysis tubing lets glucose through (the classic starch and glucose Visking demonstration depends on it) and lets sucrose through slowly. Saying "sugar can't cross" would teach a wrong idea about real apparatus, so the narration says sucrose molecules are much bigger and "barely get through in the time of the demo". This keeps the cause (water crosses far faster) without a false absolute. For the science check.
- **The prediction beat (scene 6).** Before the bag is reweighed, students predict heavier or lighter. 3 s countdown with a "Predict" chip (root decision 7; a quick check, not a practice question).
- **Liquid level as the second measure (scene 7; route stop "or liquid level").** A filled bag tied around a narrow glass tube, clamped upright: the level rises as water enters. Reason given for the narrow tube (a small volume makes a big rise). Measured at timed intervals.
- **Fair test (scene 8).** The site's control (a bag of distilled water, same water, same time) and what it rules out (the tubing itself, clinging water). The video separates **a control** (an extra set-up without the factor being tested) from **controlled variables** (things kept the same), because students starting Year 11 often merge them; attempt 2's crossed mistake depends on it. Repeats and a mean, with the reason ("one leaky bag can't decide the result"). The words validity and accuracy are not taught (exam skills series); "reliability" appears only as a screen word with its plain reason.
- **Percentage change in mass (scene 9).** Bags start at different masses, so the fair comparison is percentage change. One worked case with illustrative numbers (15.0 g to 17.4 g, +16%; control under 1%), laid out as the v06 calculation stack. This stays inside the route stop ("state what to measure ... change in mass"), is the standard way school osmosis results are compared, and its sign rule (positive means water in, negative means water out) is what attempt 1 tests.
- **Potato cylinders (attempt 1 only).** The site's second osmosis example ("potato cylinders in concentrated sugar solution lose mass") is the unseen transfer case, with the necessary condition supplied on the card ("the solution inside potato cells is more dilute than the sucrose solution"). The words hypotonic, hypertonic, isotonic, turgid, flaccid and plasmolysed are not used anywhere (v11's).
- **Risk table (scene 10).** Three spoken rows, each hazard, harm and control for this practical: potassium permanganate (oxidiser, harmful if swallowed, stains: forceps, gloves, safety glasses, one small crystal, dispose as the teacher directs); glass beakers and the narrow tube (cuts: clamp gently, report breakages); spilt sugar solution (slips: wipe up at once). Teacher-note rows, not shown: storing permanganate away from combustibles and reducing agents; disposal of permanganate solution (aquatic toxicity) under the school's procedure. Shown table: only the row being spoken; earlier rows collapse to one-word grey chips (v05's rule). Codex to confirm against NSW Department of Education Chemical Safety in Schools guidance, including whether students may handle the crystal.
- **Memory aid (author-written).** "**Diffusion you can see; osmosis you can weigh.**" Each half names the measurement and carries its reason: a coloured solute spreading is visible; water entering or leaving a closed bag changes its mass. Second line on the notes screen: "Add a control. Repeat. Explain with particles." Shown in scene 12 after both demonstrations are explained. The site's L08 memory hook ("A door is not an engine") is about facilitated diffusion, so it belongs to v09. Assumptions under "Memory aid check".
- **Site alignment (L08, kept in substance).** The two demonstrations and their evidence (colour spread; Visking tubing gaining mass as water moves in); "Measuring mass change before and after is the quantitative evidence for osmosis"; the control "tubing in water vs water in water" and repeats; short answer 2 (predict the mass change after 30 minutes and explain it, carried with the correction above); short answer 3 (one control and one repeat measure, and what each contributes; turned into attempt 2 with a new scenario). Not used here: the definition cards and the explain and compare walk-throughs (v09's); facilitated diffusion (v09's); the salty-chips application (root to place); tonicity (v11's).
- **Site errors and gaps found (this read, 2 Nov).** For `../site-corrections-bio-y11.md` (root forwards to Codex; not repeats of logged items).
  1. L08 short answer 2 model answer and its mark guide: "water molecules are small enough to cross but sugar molecules are not, so the sugar stays inside". Standard Visking (dialysis) tubing lets glucose pass and lets sucrose pass slowly; the school demonstration works because water crosses much faster than sucrose. Proposed: "Water molecules cross the tubing much faster than the larger sucrose molecules, so over 30 minutes there is a net movement of water into the tubing and only a little sugar leaks out." **For the science check, not yet a confirmed error.**
  2. L08 card 3, diffusion demo: "spreads colour outward over time with no stirring". A gap, not an error: the demonstration is evidence for diffusion only when the water is still and at an even temperature, because currents also spread colour. Proposed addition: "Use still water at room temperature and don't bump the beaker, so currents don't spread the colour."
- **Cause chain taught, in order:** (1) the problem: changes with no visible cause, and the three questions; (2) the diffusion set-up and why the water must be still; (3) what you see and what to measure; (4) the particle explanation, including no net movement at even colour; (5) Visking tubing, why water crosses and sucrose barely does, and the method with reasons; (6) prediction, the result and its explanation; (7) the liquid level measure and why the tube is narrow; (8) a control versus controlled variables, and repeats; (9) percentage change and its sign; (10) risk; attempts; decision rule and handoff; key notes.
- **Learner-visible vocabulary (each with a plain meaning at first use):** potassium permanganate (scene 2, on screen "a purple crystal that dissolves in water"); Visking tubing and dialysis tubing (scene 5, "a thin, clear film with tiny pores"); partially permeable (scene 5, recalled, on screen "lets some particles through but not others"); sucrose (scene 5, "ordinary table sugar"); distilled water (scene 5, on screen "pure water"); control (scene 8, "an extra set-up without the factor you're testing"); controlled variables (scene 8, "kept the same"); percentage change in mass (scene 9, worked). Recalled from v09: diffusion, osmosis, concentration gradient, net movement.
- **Attempt systems (unseen, every condition visible, HSC wording, original).**
  - (A1, calculate and explain, 4 marks) A potato cylinder in concentrated sucrose solution for 60 minutes, blotted and weighed before and after: 5.00 g to 4.40 g; the card states that the solution inside potato cells is more dilute than the sucrose solution. (a) Calculate the percentage change in mass. (b) Explain the change using osmosis. Model: (a) (4.40 − 5.00) ÷ 5.00 × 100 = −12% (a 12% decrease); (b) water concentration is higher inside the potato cells than in the sucrose solution, so there is a net movement of water out of the cells, across their partially permeable membranes, into the solution, and the cylinder loses mass. Crossed mistake: dividing by the final mass (−0.60 ÷ 4.40 × 100 ≈ −13.6%). Unseen: a new tissue, a decrease rather than an increase, the condition supplied on the card.
  - (A2, propose and explain, 4 marks) One Visking bag of concentrated sucrose solution, weighed, left in distilled water for 30 minutes, weighed straight out of the water without drying; the student concludes osmosis caused the gain. Propose two changes that make the result stronger evidence for osmosis and explain how each helps. Model (any two): a control bag of distilled water in the same water for the same time (shows the gain needs a concentration difference, not the tubing or clinging water); blot each bag dry the same way before every weighing (clinging water isn't counted as gain); several bags and a mean (one leaky or badly dried bag can't decide the result). Crossed mistake: "Control: keep the temperature the same" (a controlled variable, not a control). Unseen: a new flawed method, not the site's question.
- **Mistake scene:** none separate. Misconceptions addressed: "colour spreading always means diffusion" (2: stirring or warm currents also spread it); "particles stop moving when the colour is even" (4); "sugar can't cross Visking tubing at all" (5: it barely gets through in the time of the demo); "water moves only one way in osmosis" (6: both ways, more inward); "a control means keeping things the same" (8, A2); "compare raw mass changes between bags" (9); "divide by the final mass" (A1).
- **Legacy logic ported:** none. **Reuse** (no prototype components): the explainer kit (`Plate`, `Callout`, `Mark`, `Countdown`, `HandText`, `DrawPath`, `scribbleEllipse`, `Enter`, `Cam`, `NumberTicker`); v05's step rail, method card, risk table (row at a time), attempt cards and answer table; v06's calculation stack (task, equation card, grouped givens with units, one working line at a time); B6a's `WrongChip`, `GreyTag`, `Cross`. New coded items (lesson-local): the **particle lens** (a circular `Cam` lens on the beaker with violet particles in random walk, densest near the crystal; two counters, away versus back); the **water and sugar lens** on the tubing wall (blue water dots crossing both ways through gaps, more inward; larger amber sucrose dots nearly all staying inside); the **coded ruler** beside the beaker (marks only, positions read from the painted stages); the **balance readout** (coded digits in the blank display of the new balance plate, ticking to each mass); the **liquid column** (a coded blue column inside the painted narrow tube, rising, with a level mark and a coded scale); the **swirl arrows** (terracotta curved arrows for stirring and warm currents); the **percentage stack** with its sign chips.

### 4. diffusion: what it means
> Zoom in, and the particle model explains it. Each purple particle moves randomly, but there are far more of them near the crystal, so more move away from it than move back **towards** it. That's a net movement down the concentration **gradient**. When the colour is even, particles still move, but as many go one way as the other, so there's no net movement any **more**.

### 5. osmosis set-up: Visking tubing

## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why a demonstration counts as evidence (1: a measurable change explained by particles). Why still water (2: stirring or warm currents spread colour by themselves). Why the colour fades outward (3: it is the gradient). Why the colour spreads and why it stops changing (4: more particles leave the crowded region than return; at even colour as many go each way). Why water crosses Visking tubing and sucrose barely does (5: tiny pores; much bigger sucrose molecules). Why rinse and blot (5, 6: weigh only the bag). Why the bag gains mass (6: fewer water molecules in each drop inside; more cross inward; the sugar stays, so the difference stays). Why a narrow tube (7: small volume, big rise).
