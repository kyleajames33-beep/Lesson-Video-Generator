### 2. rebuild the plan: performance and learning
> Group A did **blocked practice**: one skill repeated many times before the next. Group B did **random practice**: several skills mixed in an unpredictable order. In blocked practice, each hit is a small tweak of the last, so it looks smooth. In random practice, the other strokes push the plan out of mind, so on every hit she rebuilds it from scratch. That rebuilding is hard work, makes each hit worse, and is exactly what a match demands. So we need two words. **Performance** is how well you do it right now. **Learning** is a lasting change, and you only see it later, in a **retention test** after a delay. A coach at training sees performance, and hopes for learning. Here they moved in opposite directions. One catch: a beginner has no plan to rebuild yet. So block to acquire, then shuffle to retain.

### 3. two more choices with rules
> Two more choices have clear rules. **Massed practice** has little rest. **Distributed practice** has real rest between attempts or sessions. A beginner usually needs distributed practice, because the movement uses all her attention, and a tired beginner practises errors. Practice doesn't make perfect, it makes permanent. Massed practice suits short, easy skills and experienced learners. **Whole practice** does the full skill, and **part practice** splits it. The scale from last time decides. A continuous skill stays whole. A serial skill can be split, then joined two parts at a time, then done whole.

### 4. performance elements: deciding, planning, adjusting
> The syllabus also names performance elements, the parts of an open skill that aren't the movement. **Decision-making** is choosing the right response, under time pressure, with incomplete information. A drill with one right answer can't train it. Small-sided games and drills with two real options can. A coach calling out a shuffled order isn't a decision, either. The player has to choose from what she reads. **Strategy** is the plan made before a contest, like pressing high. **Tactics** are changes made during it, like whether to keep pressing when you're two goals down. Strategy is planned by studying the opposition, then rehearsed in training. Tactics grow from game situations and questions. Before, or during: that's the difference.

### 5. feedback: three questions
> The syllabus names six types of feedback, but they're really three questions, and every comment answers all three. Where did it come from? **Task-intrinsic** feedback comes from doing the skill: what you feel, see and hear. **Augmented** feedback is added from outside, by a coach, a video or a timing gate. When did it arrive? **Concurrent** feedback comes during the movement, and **delayed** feedback after it. Concurrent feedback only helps when the movement is slow enough to act on, like a long swim, not a half-second shot. And what is it about? **Knowledge of results** is the outcome: it went wide. **Knowledge of performance** is the movement that caused it: your shoulders opened early. A player who's just missed already knows the result. What she needs is the reason.

### 6. too much feedback
> And here's the same twist again. Frequent, instant, detailed feedback makes a session look better, and learning worse. The learner stops attending to her own feel, because a better source is always there. Then in a match, her own feel is all she has, and she's never learnt to read it. So fade it: less often, a little later, and ask, how did that feel, before you tell her.

## Numbers or facts to verify

Self-check (24 Nov). The Codex science check must confirm each before voicing.
- **Blocked and random (2).** The contextual interference effect: blocked practice better during acquisition, random practice better on delayed retention and transfer (Shea and Morgan, 1979; later reviews find smaller, less consistent effects in applied sport settings, which v19 addresses); blocked practice suiting early acquisition.
- **Performance and learning (2).** Performance as temporary and observable; learning as relatively permanent, inferred from retention or transfer tests.
- **Massed and distributed, whole and part (3).** Distributed practice for demanding skills and beginners; "practice makes permanent"; part practice for serial skills with separable parts, whole for continuous ones; progressive joining.
- **Performance elements (4).** Decision-making trained through games with genuine choices; strategy before, tactics during; site correction 24 (random order is not a decision).
- **Feedback (5, 6).** The six types and their pairing; the guidance hypothesis (frequent augmented feedback helps performance and harms retention); fading, delaying and self-assessment first.
- **Numbers used.** Thirty of each stroke, ninety hits, six sessions, two weeks (site's hypothetical); curves illustrative (blocked 40 to 80, retention 58; random 30 to 60, retention 70); attempt: eight weeks.
