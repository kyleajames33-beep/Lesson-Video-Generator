### 2. four single bonds: tetrahedral
> Start with shape, and why chains zigzag. A carbon with four single bonds has four groups of bonding electrons around it, and those groups push each other as far apart as they can. In three dimensions, that's a **tetrahedral** shape, with about **one hundred and nine point five** degrees between the bonds. So alkane carbons, like those in **ethane**, are tetrahedral, and a chain of them zigzags.

### 3. a double bond: trigonal planar
> A double bond changes that. Each carbon in ethene has only **three** bonding directions: two to hydrogens, and one to the other carbon. The double bond counts as one direction, because both of its bonds point at the same atom. Three directions spread out **flat**, about one hundred and twenty degrees apart, so the shape is trigonal planar. The double bond's **second** bond sits above and below the line between the carbons, and it's weaker than the first.

### 4. a triple bond: linear, and shape carbon by carbon
> In ethyne, with a triple bond, each carbon has just **two** directions, so they point opposite ways: a straight line, one hundred and eighty degrees. That's linear. Shape belongs to each carbon, not to the whole molecule. In **propene**, the two carbons of the double bond are trigonal planar, but the C H three carbon on the end is still **tetrahedral**.

### 5. a homologous series and its general formula
> Line up the alkanes, from methane up to **octane**. Each one has one more **C H two** than the last. A family like that is called a homologous series: the same functional group, with members that differ by C H two. One general formula gives every member. For alkanes, it's **C n H two n plus two**. For octane, n is eight, so that's C eight H eighteen.

Visual: the board clears. Task line "Build a series". ### 6. alkenes and alkynes, and what a series shares
> Alkenes form a series too, and their functional group is the C double bond C. Making one double bond takes away **two** hydrogens, so alkenes are C n H two n. A triple bond takes away two more, so alkynes are **C n H two n minus two**. Alkanes have no functional group. Members of a series share a group, so they **react** alike, while their physical properties change gradually.

### 8. inside molecules and between them
> Now, properties. Inside each molecule, strong covalent bonds hold the atoms together. That's **intramolecular** bonding.
