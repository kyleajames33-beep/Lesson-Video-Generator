### 1. hook: words first
> Here's the theatre's problem. Adult tickets cost twenty-two dollars, and child tickets twelve dollars. So how do we build simultaneous equations from a real situation, and make sense of the **answer**?

### 3. step two: two facts, two equations
> Step two. Look for two facts in the words, because each fact becomes one equation. The first fact is a count. Three hundred and fifty tickets were sold, so adult tickets plus child tickets makes three hundred and fifty. That's a plus c equals three hundred and **fifty**. The second fact is a value, the money. Each adult ticket brings in twenty-two dollars, so a adult tickets bring in twenty-two a. Child tickets bring in twelve c. Together that's the takings, so twenty-two a plus twelve c equals five thousand nine **hundred**. Here's a quick test. The count equation has no prices in it. Putting the prices into the count equation is the **slip**. The prices only go in the money equation.

### 4. step three: solve it on a graph
> Step three, solve. Every student can do this with a graph. Draw both equations as lines, with adult tickets across and child tickets up. To draw a line, two points are enough. If every ticket sold were an adult's, that's three hundred and fifty adults, and if every one were a child's, three hundred and fifty children. That's the count **line**. For the money line, five thousand nine hundred dollars would buy about two hundred and sixty-eight adult tickets on their own, or about four hundred and ninety-two child **tickets**. Rule both lines, or type them into a graphing app, and they cross at one hundred and seventy, one hundred and **eighty**. That crossing is the only pair of ticket numbers that fits both facts. Slide along the count line, and every point has the right number of tickets, but the wrong amount of money, except right at the **crossing**.

### 5. step three, by algebra
> If you're doing Standard 2, you can also solve it by algebra. From the count equation, a equals three hundred and fifty minus c. Swap that into the money equation. Twenty-two lots of three hundred and fifty, minus twenty-two c, plus twelve c, equals five thousand nine hundred. That's seven thousand seven hundred, minus ten c, equals five thousand nine hundred. So ten c is eighteen hundred, and c equals one hundred and **eighty**. Then a is three hundred and fifty minus one hundred and eighty, which is one hundred and **seventy**. The algebra lands on exactly the point the graph **showed**.

### 6. step four: say it, and check it makes sense
> Step four. Answer the question in a sentence, with units. One hundred and seventy adult tickets, and one hundred and eighty child tickets, were **sold**. Then check it makes sense. Thirty minutes is half an hour, so that's four point five hours, not four point three. On a graph, the lines cross at two, two point **five**.
