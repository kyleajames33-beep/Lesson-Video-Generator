### 2. why the waves slow down
> They bunch closer. Here's why. In deep water, a wave hardly feels the bottom. But once the water is shallow compared with the wavelength, the water moving under each crest reaches the seabed, and the seabed gets in the way. So the wave slows down, and the shallower the water, the slower the **wave**.

### 3. same frequency, shorter wavelength
> But the frequency doesn't change. Out at sea, one crest arrives every ten seconds. Crests can't pile up or vanish on the way in, so near the shore, one still arrives every ten seconds. Now use v equals f lambda. If v falls and f stays the same, lambda must fall too. In each ten seconds, a slower crest covers less ground before the next one arrives behind it. Slower waves crowd **closer**.

### 5. from speed to depth: a model
> So the spacing tells you about the speed, and the speed tells you about the depth. In shallow water, physicists model the wave speed as v equals the square root of g d, where d is the depth. That g is the same nine point eight as for falling objects: gravity pulls the raised water back down, and that keeps the wave going. Square both sides, and d equals v squared over **g**. This only works where the water is shallow compared with the wavelength, which is exactly where reading the depth **matters**.
