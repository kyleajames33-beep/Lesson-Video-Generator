### 2b. ex1-method (the five-line chain, built one line at a time)
> Build the answer in the order things happen. **One, the change.** Water vapour is removed, so its concentration falls. **Two, collisions.** Fewer water molecules means fewer collisions per second between water and carbon monoxide, so the reverse rate falls. The reaction is the whole change; the rate is how fast it happens. **Three, the other rate.** The forward rate has not changed yet, so it is now bigger. **Four, the net result.** More carbon monoxide is made than used, so its concentration ends higher than at the first equilibrium. **Five, the level.** The rates move together until they are equal again.

## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** The collision-theory answer is built from the C3 mechanism (one rate changes first, the other is unchanged at that instant, the faster rate falls and the slower rises), and the same frame is used for the temperature example (with both rates rising) and attempt 1. The graph lines come from the squeeze (all concentrations double because concentration is amount over volume) separate from the reaction (curves), and the amount graph has no jump because nothing is added or removed. The fingerprints are split by axis (4a, 4b) so no rule contradicts the amount graph, and they state direction (up or down), what else leaves a graph flat, and the second clue. The no-shift test is "does Q still equal K?", linked to C8, with the argon collision question answered.
- **Does it earn attention?** The crossed slip opening, the six-card board with ticks and "you try two" give a reason to keep watching. Each worked example starts from a realistic question with the command word and data underlined, and each ends with a crossed-out slip the learner may recognise in their own writing. The unusual cases (named species is not the added one; no jump on the amount graph; argon does not raise the temperature) are the surprises.
- **Misconceptions addressed:** Le Chatelier's principle as the cause (with its condition); "forward reaction" for "forward rate"; the answer that never names the species; mol axis read as concentration; jumps in untouched species; levelling off at the wrong value; curves not following the equation ratio; pressure from argon shifts the mixture; extra gas means extra relevant collisions; pressure raises the temperature; activation energy changes on heating; "catalyst speeds it up" with no cause; an equal-gas-count squeeze shifts the mixture because the colour darkens; gas escaping "shifts it right" as a reason; a no-jump amount graph always meaning temperature.

## Numbers to verify (all arithmetic, repeated for Codex)

- Example B: 2NO2(g) ⇌ N2O4(g), 1.00 L: [NO2] = 0.200, [N2O4] = 0.300 mol/L, K = 0.300 / 0.200^2 = 0.300 / 0.0400 = 7.50 (made-up; not a real room-temperature value).
