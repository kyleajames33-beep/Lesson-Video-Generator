### 2b. q1-chain (the five-line chain, one line at a time)
> Build the answer in the order things happen. **One, the change.** Water vapour is removed, so its concentration falls. **Two, collisions.** Fewer water molecules means fewer collisions per second between water and carbon monoxide, so the reverse rate falls. The reaction is the whole change; the rate is how fast it happens. **Three, the other rate.** The forward rate has not changed yet, so it is now bigger. **Four, the net result.** More carbon monoxide is made than used, so its concentration ends higher than at the first equilibrium. **Five, the level.** The rates move together until they are equal again.

Visual: the vessel stays on the right with the C3 rate bars beside it (forward blue, reverse amber, equal teal bracket at the start). The answer builds on the lower left as five numbered lines in handwriting, one per cue, the keyword of each ringed as it lands and the previous line dimming: (1) "Water vapour is removed, so [H2O] falls." (2) "Fewer H2O and CO molecules collide per second, so the reverse rate falls." (3) "The forward rate is unchanged at that instant, so forward rate > reverse rate." (4) "Net forward reaction makes more CO, so [CO] is higher than at the first equilibrium." (5) "[H2] and [CO2] fall, [CO] rises, so the forward rate falls and the reverse rate rises until they are equal: new equilibrium." On line 1 the water dots lift out and the H2O reading drops. On line 2 the amber bar drops with a ring on "reverse". On line 3 the blue bar stays and a ">" sign lights between the bars. On line 4 the CO dots rise by two and a rising arrow lands on "CO". ### 2c. q1-mistake (three slips crossed out)
> Three classic slips. Writing only that **Le Chatelier's principle says it shifts right**. When the question asks for collision theory, or for why, that only repeats what happens. Use the principle to predict the direction, then give the rate reason. Writing that the **forward reaction increases**, when you mean the forward rate. And stopping at **shifts right** without **naming carbon monoxide**. Finish on the species the question asked about.

Visual: three chips write on beside the finished answer, each crossed out in muted red as it is said, with a green correction next to it: "LCP says it shifts right" crossed, green "predict the direction with it, then: fewer collisions, so the reverse rate falls" and a small condition tag "when the question asks for collisions or why"; "the forward reaction increases" crossed, green "the forward rate ..."; "so it shifts right." crossed, green "... so [CO] increases".
