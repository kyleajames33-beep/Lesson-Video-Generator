### 2. a strip of rate is an amount
> Less, because the air conditioner only draws four kilowatts at two o'clock. Here's how to get it exactly. Take a thin strip of time, a tiny part of an hour. Over that strip, the power barely changes, so the energy is power times time, in kilowatts times hours, which is kilowatt **hours**. On the graph, that's the strip's area. Add every strip, make them thinner, and you get the area under the power graph. That's the definite integral of power over the day. The same idea works for any rate. Integrate a rate over an interval, and you get the amount it **adds up** to. The area's units are the graph's units up, times its units **across**.

### 3. evaluate the energy, and the model's domain
> So the energy is the integral from zero to twelve, of four sine of pi t over twelve. The inside is linear, with rate pi over twelve, so the primitive divides by it. That gives minus forty-eight over pi, times cos of pi t over twelve. At twelve, cos pi is minus one. At zero, cos zero is one. So the energy is ninety-six over pi, about thirty point six kilowatt **hours**. That's less than forty-eight, as predicted. Notice the domain, too. After eight at night this formula goes negative, but an air conditioner can't use negative power. So the model only holds from zero to **twelve**.

### 4. integrate a rate, not a total
> Units also stop a common mistake. A factory finds that each extra item costs fifty minus zero point two x dollars to make, when it's already making x items. That's a rate, in dollars per item. Raising production from one hundred to two hundred items costs the integral of that rate, from one hundred to two hundred. The primitive is fifty x minus zero point one x squared, and the answer is two thousand **dollars**. Dollars per item, times items, gives dollars. But integrating a total, like a profit in dollars, gives dollars times items, which doesn't measure anything useful. So integrate a rate, and you get an **amount**.

### 5. a rate below the axis: net change or total?
> Some rates go negative. Water moves through a gate into a dam at a rate of eight minus two t megalitres per day, for six days. After day four the rate is negative, so water flows back out through the gate. The integral from zero to six is twelve, and that's the net change: the dam ends up with twelve megalitres more than it **started** with. But the water that passed through the gate is the total area, sixteen in plus four out, which is twenty **megalitres**. So the question decides. If it asks how much the amount changed, use the integral. If it asks how much flowed in total, use the area.

### 6. a region against the y-axis: a channel
> Now a region bounded by the y-axis. A drainage channel has sides shaped like y equals x squared, in metres, with y measured up from the bottom. The water is one metre deep. What area of the cross-section, the shape you'd see if you cut straight across, does the water fill? Put the y-axis down the middle of the channel. Then the right half of the water is bounded by the curve, the y-axis and the water surface. Cut it into thin horizontal strips, like layers of water. At height y, a layer reaches from the y-axis out to x equals root y. So integrate root y with respect to y, from zero to **one**, which gives two thirds. The channel is even about the y-axis, so double it: four thirds of a square **metre**.

## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why peak × time overcounts: the peak lasts an instant (1, 2). Why area is energy: over a thin strip power barely changes, so power × time is the energy, and that is the strip's area (2). Why the integral: adding thinner strips (2). Why the units name the answer: area is height times width, so units up times units across (2, 8). Why divide by π/12: the linear inside (3). Why the domain: the formula goes negative where the situation cannot (3). Why integrate a rate, not a total: a total times an input has no useful meaning (4). Why the integral is a net change when the rate goes negative: outflow strips subtract, while the total flow counts every strip as positive (5). Why horizontal strips and dy: the region is against the y-axis and each layer's length is a function of y (6). Why double: the channel is even about its centre line (6). Why depth 2 m gives nearly three times the area: the channel widens with height (7).

## Memory aid check

- **"Units up × units across = what you've found."** Meaning, as said in scene 8: a strip's area is its height times its width, so the integral's units are the vertical axis's units times the horizontal axis's units, and those units say what the integral measures. Assumptions checked: the graph's axes carry units (every practical graph here does); for a region in the plane both are lengths, giving area (m × m = m²); for a rate graph the vertical unit is "amount per unit across", so the product is an amount. It helps a new case: a graph of flow in L/min against minutes gives litres; a graph of a total in $ against items gives $ × items, a sign of the wrong integrand. Introduced after the cause (2 to 7), with the four modelling steps.
- **Considered, not used:** "area = amount" as a bare slogan (false for a total integrated across its input, the scene 4 contrast); "rate × time" alone (true only for a constant rate, the hook's error).
