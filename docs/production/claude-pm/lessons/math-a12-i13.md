### 2. a strip of rate is an amount
> Less, because the panel only reaches four kilowatts at noon. Here's how to get it exactly. Take a thin strip of time, a tiny part of an hour. Over that strip, the power barely changes, so the energy is power times time, in kilowatts times hours, which is kilowatt **hours**. On the graph, that's the strip's area. Add every strip, make them thinner, and you get the area under the power graph. That's the definite integral of power over the day. The same idea works for any rate. Integrate a rate over an interval, and you get the amount it **adds up** to. The area's units are the graph's units up, times its units **across**.

### 4. integrate a rate, not a total
> Units also stop a common mistake. A factory finds that each extra item costs fifty minus zero point two x dollars to make, when it's already making x items. That's a rate, in dollars per item. Raising production from one hundred to two hundred items costs the integral of that rate, from one hundred to two hundred. The primitive is fifty x minus zero point one x squared, and the answer is two thousand **dollars**. Dollars per item, times items, gives dollars. But integrating a total, like a profit in dollars, gives dollars times items, which doesn't measure anything useful. So integrate a rate, and you get an **amount**.

### 5. a rate below the axis: net change or total?
> Some rates go negative. Water moves through a gate into a dam at a rate of eight minus two t megalitres per day, for six days. After day four the rate is negative, so water flows back out through the gate. The integral from zero to six is twelve, and that's the net change: the dam ends up with twelve megalitres more than it **started** with. But the water that passed through the gate is the total area, sixteen in plus four out, which is twenty **megalitres**. So the question decides. If it asks how much the amount changed, use the integral. If it asks how much flowed in total, use the area.

> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why peak × time overcounts: the peak lasts an instant (1, 2). Why area is energy: over a thin strip power barely changes, so power × time is the energy, and that is the strip's area (2). Why the integral: adding thinner strips (2). Why the units name the answer: area is height times width, so units up times units across (2, 8). Why divide by π/12: the linear inside (3). Why the domain: the formula goes negative where the situation cannot (3). Why integrate a rate, not a total: a total times an input has no useful meaning (4). Why the integral is a net change when the rate goes negative: outflow strips subtract, while the total flow counts every strip as positive (5). ## Memory aid check
