### 6. HbA1c: is the plan working?
> How do you know the plan's working? One glucose reading only shows that moment. Glucose slowly sticks to haemoglobin in red blood cells, and red cells last about four months. It's the evidence for keeping, changing or stepping up the plan.

### 13. key notes (quiet, about 60 seconds)
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

   Model: (a) HbA1c is the proportion of haemoglobin with glucose attached; because red cells last about four months, it reflects average blood glucose over the previous two to three months (1 mark). 2. (11c, evaluate, 5 marks.) Randomised, placebo-controlled trial of a weekly injection; 600 adults; 26 weeks; HbA1c fell 1.4 against 0.3 points; 12% stopped because of nausea; not subsidised.
   Model: Strength: random allocation with a placebo means other differences between groups should even out, so the larger fall is likely due to the drug (1 mark). The difference of 1.1 percentage points is clinically large, and 600 participants gives a reasonably precise estimate (1 mark).
