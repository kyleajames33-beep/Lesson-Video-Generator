### 2. two crests meet
> Let's watch two crests first, each four centimetres high. As they overlap, the spring rises higher than either one alone. At every point, the spring's displacement is the first pulse's displacement plus the second's. When the peaks line up, four plus four gives eight centimetres. ### 8. in phase and out of phase
> The same rule works for whole trains of waves. Take two identical waves along the same line. Shift it by anything else, and you get something in **between**.

## Practice questions and model answers

**Attempt 1 (scene 14; original wording, HSC style, 3 marks suggested, not an official marking scheme).** "Two pulses travel towards each other along a stretched rope. At its peak, pulse P has a displacement of +5 cm and pulse Q has a displacement of −2 cm. (a) Calculate the displacement of the rope at the instant the two peaks coincide. (b) Name the type of interference that occurs at that instant. (c) Describe what happens to the two pulses immediately afterwards."
Model answer: (a) By the principle of superposition, y = y_P + y_Q = (+5) + (−2) = +3 cm. (b) Destructive interference (partial): the displacements have opposite signs, so the resultant is smaller than pulse P's displacement. **Attempt 2 (scene 16; original wording, HSC style, 4 marks suggested).** "Two waves with the same frequency and wavelength travel in the same direction along the same string. Model answer: (a) Largest: 3.0 + 2.0 = 5.0 cm. Smallest: 3.0 − 2.0 = 1.0 cm. The cancellation is not complete because the amplitudes are unequal. Classic mistake (crossed): "destructive interference always gives zero".
