### 2. rain as a very flat box
> A rain gauge is just a tube that catches the rain, and here the water in it stands twenty-five millimetres deep. Let's use that on something simpler, a house roof, twelve metres by eight metres, seen from above. Twenty-five millimetres of rain means this. Its volume is the area times the depth. The syllabus writes this as V equals A h, where A is the area and h is the depth of rain. Careful, in the last video h was the strip width. Here it's the depth of **rain**.

### 3. units first, then the volume
> The area is in square metres, so the depth must be in metres too. A millimetre is a thousandth of a metre, so divide by a thousand. Twenty-five millimetres is zero point zero two five **metres**. The roof's area is twelve times eight, ninety-six square metres. So the volume is ninety-six times zero point zero two five, two point four cubic **metres**. A cubic metre is a box one metre each way, and it holds a thousand litres. So that's two thousand four hundred **litres**, from one storm, on one roof. Writing twenty-five millimetres as zero point two five metres is the classic slip. That divides by a hundred, and it makes ten times too much **water**.

The tank holds five thousand, so it overflows by one thousand four hundred and eighty **litres**. Comparing six point four eight with five thousand is the slip.
