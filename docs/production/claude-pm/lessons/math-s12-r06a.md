### 1. hook: price it without visiting
> Here's the floor plan of a small unit, drawn at one to a hundred. The kitchen bench is three point six centimetres long on the plan, and benchtop costs three hundred and twenty dollars a metre. Can you price it without ever visiting? At one to a hundred, each centimetre on the plan is a hundred real centimetres. So three point six centimetres is three hundred and sixty centimetres, which is three point six **metres**. At three hundred and twenty dollars a metre, that's three point six times three hundred and twenty, one thousand one hundred and fifty-two **dollars**. Builders and renovators price whole houses like this. So how do building plans let us estimate materials and **costs**?

### 2. reading a plan
> A building plan is a scale drawing of a floor, seen from above. It uses a few common symbols. Thick lines are **walls**. A gap in a wall with a quarter circle is a door, and the curve shows which way it **swings**. A thin triple line in a wall is a **window**. Two overlapping lines across a gap is a sliding **door**. An arrow marked N points **north**. And a line with arrows at both ends and a number on it is a dimension line, which gives a **length**.

## Practice questions and model answers

**Attempt 1 (scene 8; original, HSC style, 3 marks suggested, not an official marking scheme).** "On a plan drawn at 1 : 50, a bathroom floor measures 5.6 cm by 4.0 cm. Floor tiles are sold in boxes that cover 1.44 m², at $95 a box. The tiler adds 10% to the floor area for cuts and breakages. (a) Find the real floor area. (b) How many boxes are needed, and what do they cost?"
Model answer: (a) 5.6 × 50 = 280 cm = 2.8 m; 4.0 × 50 = 200 cm = 2.0 m; 5.6 m². (b) 5.6 × 1.1 = 6.16 m²; 6.16 ÷ 1.44 ≈ 4.28, so 5 boxes; 5 × $95 = $475. Classic mistake (crossed): "4 boxes".

**Attempt 2 (scene 10; original, HSC style, 3 marks suggested).** "A living room on a 1 : 100 plan measures 5.0 cm by 4.2 cm. Option A: carpet at $38/m², laid. Option B: vinyl planks in boxes covering 2.2 m² at $99 a box, adding 5% to the area for waste. Which option is cheaper, and by how much?"
