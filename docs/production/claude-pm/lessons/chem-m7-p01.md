Heating heptane, octane or nonane to boiling would put flammable vapour next to a heat source (octane boils at about 126 °C and nonane at about 151 °C, above a water bath). Confounds of the method (learner copy names only air flow, in scene 5, and the liquid on the paper, in scene 12): (1) air flow (fume cupboard face velocity, sash height, probe position): more flow gives a bigger drop for every member; controlled in scene 5 by a fixed sash height and probe position. (2) The amount of liquid the paper holds: the most volatile member (heptane here) can run dry before it reaches a steady minimum, so its ΔT depends more on the liquid held; this compresses, but does not reverse, the ranking; scene 12 names it as scatter. (3) The sample's start temperature: stock from a cool flammables cabinet gives a smaller drop; controlled by the 30 s soak and one start temperature. (4) The enthalpy of vaporisation per mole rises with chain length and works against the trend, but the vapour pressure falls far faster (note d). (5) Water condensing on the cold sleeve below the dew point (about 11 to 15 °C at 22 °C and 50 to 65 % humidity) releases heat and limits the largest drop; with heptane's minimum near 15.8 °C this matters only on a humid day. (6) Probe response time: a slow probe misses the true minimum of a fast run; logging every 2 s is enough. (7) Probe resolution: 0.1 °C is about one seventh of nonane's 0.7 °C drop, so a single nonane reading is coarse; the repeats and the ranking carry the conclusion. The video says "fewer molecules have enough energy to break free each second" and does not discuss enthalpy of vaporisation. (e) Why the sleeve is soaked for 30 s first: the paper and probe tip reach the liquid's temperature, so the logged start is the sample temperature and the drop is caused by evaporation after lifting. ## Safety facts check (self-check, 22 Oct; science check to confirm)

- **n-Heptane** (CLP Annex VI harmonised, 601-008-00-2): Flam. Liq. 2, H225 (highly flammable liquid and vapour); Asp. Tox. 1, H304 (may be fatal if swallowed and enters airways); Skin Irrit. 2, H315; STOT SE 3, H336 (may cause drowsiness or dizziness); Aquatic Acute 1, H400; Aquatic Chronic 1, H410 (very toxic to aquatic life with long-lasting effects). Flash point about −4 °C; boils at 98.4 °C.
- **n-Octane** (harmonised, 601-009-00-8): H225, H304, H315, H336, H400 and H410. Flash point about 13 °C; boils at 125.6 °C.
- **n-Nonane** (no Annex VI entry; supplier SDSs, Sigma-Aldrich and Merck): Flam. Liq. 3, H226 (flammable liquid and vapour); H304; H315; H336; H400; H410. No reproductive or repeated-exposure class. Flash point about 31 °C; boils at about 151 °C (150.8).
- **n-Hexane** (not used in the practical; scene 13's substitution chip, A2 and scene 9's data only): harmonised entry 601-037-00-0 as amended by the 22nd ATP (Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/2564, applying from 1 May 2026): STOT RE 1, H372 (causes damage to the nervous system through prolonged or repeated exposure), upgraded from STOT RE 2, H373; also H225, H304, H315, H336, Repr. 2 H361f (suspected of damaging fertility) and H411. Older SDSs still show H373. The neurotoxicity is specific to n-hexane (its metabolite 2,5-hexanedione); heptane and pentane did not cause it in the comparison studies (science check sources).
- **n-Pentane** (harmonised; scene 2 and A2 only): Flam. Liq. 1, H224 (extremely flammable liquid and vapour); H304; H336; H411; EUH066. Boils at 36.1 °C; flash point about −49 °C.
- **Learner copy:** "highly flammable" for heptane and octane (H225), "flammable" for nonane (H226), "extremely flammable" for pentane (H224), "can make you drowsy" (H336), "repeated exposure can damage nerves" for hexane only (H372, scene 13 and key note 6), "can be fatal if it enters the airways" (H304, table only), "harms aquatic life" (table only). No hazard code appears on screen without a plain harm beside it. No claim that any of them is harmless.
- **Ignition:** the vapours are heavier than air and can travel along a bench to an ignition source; hot plates are ignition sources for hydrocarbon vapour (heptane autoignition about 204 °C, nonane about 205 °C). Nonane's flash point (about 31 °C) is above room temperature, but a flame still ignites it. The route rule "no flames near volatile organics" is kept, and hot plates are added because the method needs no heating at all.
- **Substitution:** the hierarchy of control puts substituting a less hazardous chemical above ventilation and protective equipment (Safe Work Australia, model Code of Practice: Managing risks of hazardous chemicals in the workplace); scene 13 models it with hexane. n-Hexane is not prohibited in Australian schools (it is not on the Queensland Department of Education prohibited or high-risk list), so the video says "we swapped out hexane", not that it is banned.
- **Disposal:** unused alkanes and residues into the labelled organic (non-halogenated) waste container; paper sleeves left in the fume cupboard until dry, then into the solid waste as the school directs; nothing down the sink (H410, and flammable vapour in drains).
- **Eye protection:** safety glasses; gloves as the school directs (nitrile gives short-term splash protection against alkanes).
