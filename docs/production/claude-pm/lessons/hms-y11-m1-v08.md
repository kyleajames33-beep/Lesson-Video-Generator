### 2. the paradox
> Death rates alone would say there's little to do. That's wrong, for three reasons. First, the burden is mostly non-fatal. **Burden of disease** adds years lost to early death to years lived in ill health, and for young people, mental health conditions and injury dominate it. Second, the main causes, like road trauma and self-harm, are events with social causes, not slow diseases, so they're largely preventable. And third, the habits that shape the next sixty years are set now. Young people are low in illness, but high in leverage: what changes now pays off for decades.

### 6. determinants shape behaviours
> Take physical activity, which looks like the purest personal choice there is. Whether a young person is active depends on whether sport is affordable, whether there's somewhere safe and lit to go, whether they can get there without a car, whether paid work fills their afternoons, and whether their family and friends are active. That's five conditions and one decision, and the decision comes last in the chain. Help-seeking works the same way. It depends on whether a service exists, is confidential, can be reached after school, and won't judge you.

## Practice question and feedback

1. (10a, 3 marks.) "Teenagers won't go to the doctor about their mental health."
   Model: a chain of at least three links with a determinant group at each (for example, services concentrated away from where young people live and open only in school hours (environmental); travel and missed class cost time and money (socioeconomic); fear of judgement delays help-seeking (behaviour)) (2 marks); one protective factor that interrupts it, with who provides it (a confidential, youth-friendly service open after hours; a trusted adult) (1 mark).
