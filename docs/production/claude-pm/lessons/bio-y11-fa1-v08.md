### 4. the oily core is the filter (answer one)
> So the middle of the membrane is an oily layer, called the hydrophobic core, and that core is the filter. Small molecules with no charge, like oxygen and carbon dioxide, dissolve into it and pass straight through. Ions like sodium carry an electric charge, so they're strongly attracted to water and can't enter the oily core. Large polar molecules, which are slightly charged at each end, like glucose, are blocked too. That's what makes the membrane partially permeable.

### 5. fluid
> Now the first half of the name: fluid. The phospholipids aren't locked in place. They drift sideways within their own layer, constantly swapping places with their neighbours, so the bilayer behaves like a thin film of liquid. In 1970, scientists fused a mouse cell and a human cell, after tagging each cell's membrane proteins with a different coloured dye. Within about forty minutes, the colours had mixed all round the fused cell. A solid sheet couldn't do that.

### 6. mosaic, and integral proteins
> The second half: mosaic. Proteins are scattered through the bilayer like tiles in a mosaic, and they do jobs the lipids can't. Integral proteins sit embedded in the bilayer, and the ones shown span it from side to side. Their middle section is water-avoiding, so it sits comfortably among the tails and keeps the protein in the membrane.
