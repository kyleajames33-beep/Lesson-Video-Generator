### 4. the oily core is the filter (answer one)
> So the middle of the membrane is an oily layer, called the hydrophobic core, and that core is the filter. Small molecules with no charge, like oxygen and carbon dioxide, dissolve into it and pass straight through. Ions like sodium carry an electric charge, so they're strongly attracted to water and can't enter the oily core. Large polar molecules, which are slightly charged at each end, like glucose, are blocked too. That's what makes the membrane partially permeable.

### 10. cholesterol
> Last, cholesterol. In animal cells, small cholesterol molecules are wedged between the phospholipid tails, and they act as a fluidity buffer. When it's warm, the tails jiggle faster, and the membrane could get too runny and leaky. Cholesterol gets in the way and holds them back. When it's cold, the tails would pack tightly and turn stiff. Cholesterol keeps them apart, so the membrane stays flexible.

> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

- Phospholipid: hydrophilic (water-loving) phosphate head, two hydrophobic (water-avoiding) fatty acid tails. In water they form a bilayer: heads out, tails in. ## Numbers or facts to verify

Self-check (2 Nov). The Codex science check must confirm each before voicing.
- **Membrane thickness (scene 1).** The plasma membrane is about 7 to 10 nm thick (bilayer core about 4 to 5 nm). Spoken "about seven nanometres". Correct (v07 says "under ten").
- **Sodium ion size (scene 1, 4, 12b).** Na⁺ ionic radius about 0.1 nm, smaller than an O₂ molecule (about 0.3 nm across); its hydration shell and charge, not size, keep it out of the core. Spoken "A sodium ion is tiny". Correct.
- **Phospholipid structure (scene 2).** Head with phosphate (and glycerol plus a small group such as choline); two fatty acid tails. Correct at this level.
- **Self-assembly and self-sealing (scene 3).** Driven by the hydrophobic effect: water molecules hydrogen-bond with each other and with polar heads, and non-polar tails are excluded and cluster. The spoken "the tails get pushed together out of the way" is a simplification of an entropy-driven effect; acceptable at Year 11 (flag for the science check). Bilayers reseal small disruptions. Correct.
- **Permeability (scene 4).** Small non-polar molecules (O₂, CO₂) cross the bilayer readily; ions and large polar molecules (glucose) are effectively blocked; water crosses slowly (and through aquaporins), told in v09 (screen tag here). Correct.
- **Lateral diffusion (scene 5).** Phospholipids exchange places with neighbours about 10 million times a second; "constantly swapping places" is safe. Frye and Edidin (1970): mouse and human cells fused (Sendai virus), surface proteins labelled with green and red fluorescent antibodies, about 50 per cent of cells fully mixed by 40 minutes at 37 °C. Spoken "Within about forty minutes, the colours had mixed". Correct.
- **Integral proteins and carriers (scene 6).** Span the bilayer with hydrophobic middle regions; carriers bind a specific solute and change conformation. Correct.
- **Channels (scene 7).** Water-filled hydrophilic pores; selective (voltage-gated sodium channels pass Na⁺ about 10 times more readily than K⁺: "blocks most others" is safe). Correct.
- **Peripheral proteins (scene 8).** Bound to heads or to integral proteins on one face; roles include links to the cytoskeleton (for example spectrin in red blood cells), enzymes and signalling. Correct.
- **Glycoproteins (scene 9).** Carbohydrate chains on the outer face; cell recognition, immune self recognition, receptors (many hormone receptors are glycoproteins). ABO antigens are carbohydrates on glycoproteins and glycolipids: spoken "Your blood group depends on sugar chains like these". Correct.
- **Cholesterol (scene 10).** In animal cell membranes; reduces phospholipid movement at higher temperatures and prevents tight packing at lower temperatures. Plants use phytosterols (grey tag). Correct.
- **Attempt 2 plausibility.** Cholesterol depletion of cultured cells (for example with methyl-β-cyclodextrin) is a standard method; the predicted fluidity changes follow the model. 40 °C and 10 °C are tolerable short-term test temperatures for mammalian cells. Correct.
