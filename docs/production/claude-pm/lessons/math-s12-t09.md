### 2. the height from the sine
> Take one fence, side a, as the base. Drop the height from the far corner straight down to it. That makes a right-angled triangle, with the other fence, side b, as its hypotenuse, and the angle C at its corner. Earlier in this topic, opposite equals hypotenuse times sine. So the height is b times sine **C**. Put that into half the base times the height, and the area is a half, times a, times b sine C. In symbols, A equals a half a b sine **C**. Here a and b are two sides, and C is the angle between them, the included angle from last **time**. That's why it has to be the angle between. With any other angle, side b wouldn't be the hypotenuse of that right-angled triangle, and the height would come out wrong.

### 5. worked example: the angle first
> Here's an exam-style one. A triangular block of land has sides of forty-five, sixty and eighty metres. Find its area, to the nearest square metre. There's no angle given, so find one first, with the cosine rule. Take the angle between the forty-five and sixty metre sides. It faces the eighty metre side, so eighty is the one you subtract. Cosine C is forty-five squared plus sixty squared minus eighty squared, over two times forty-five times sixty. That's about minus zero point one four, so C is about ninety-eight point two five **degrees**. Keep that in your calculator. Now the area. A half times forty-five times sixty is thirteen fifty, and times sine C, the area is about one thousand three hundred and thirty-six square **metres**. Using eighty metres with that angle is the slip. The angle must sit between the two sides you **use**.

## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why sine appears: the height is the opposite side of a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse b (2). Why the angle must be between: only then is b the hypotenuse of that right-angled triangle on base a (2). Why an obtuse angle is used as it is: past 90° the height falls outside, at 180° − C (70° for the paddock), and sin 110° = sin 70°, so b sin C is still the height (3, 4). Why the answer is under ½ab: the height b sin C is at most b, at 90° (4). Why the cosine rule first: the area rule needs the angle between, and three sides give it (5).
