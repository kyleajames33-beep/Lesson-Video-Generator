## Decisions

- **Learner question rewritten (route placeholder).** "How can a model eye on an optical bench show that spectacles work by moving the focus back onto the retina?" It is v25's handoff chip, it is causal (spectacles work by moving the focus), and the investigation answers it with measured positions (open decision 1). The answer, built in order: a model eye with a movable retina mark (1, 2); controls make the correcting lens the only change (3); a fair way to find the sharpest image (4); the healthy model's image sits on its mark (5); the too-long model's image falls short; the diverging lens moves it back onto the mark (6, 7); the converging lens makes it worse (8); the too-short model's image lands long; the converging lens moves it forward onto the mark (9); the results plot and conclusion (10); what this says about real spectacles, and the model's limits (11); limitations (12); risks (13).

- **The model eye (`ci49f242d5`; site card 3, made measurable).** The site's model (a converging lens focusing in front of, or behind, a screen) is kept, with one change: the eye lens and its retina mark stay fixed, and a second job of the screen is to search for the sharp image. A strip of tape on the bench marks the retina; the screen slides to find where the sharp image actually forms; the distance from the eye lens to that position is recorded and compared with the mark. "Too long" and "too short" are made by moving the tape mark, not the lens, which mirrors v25's "the eyeball is a little too long". The screen parked on the mark shows what the "retina" sees: a sharp filament image, or a blur patch.
- **Distant object.** A filament lamp at the far end of the lab, 5.0 m from the eye lens (v13's plate), so its light arrives nearly parallel (v25: distant light is nearly parallel). The sharp image is a small upside-down picture of the glowing filament, which makes "sharp or blurred" easy to judge. Not the Sun or a window (risk row 2).
- **Illustrative data (labelled; generating model stated).** Model (author note b): eye lens focal length 9.80 cm, so a lamp 5.0 m away images at 10.0 cm; correcting lenses touching the eye lens, labelled "−50 cm" (diverging) and "+40 cm" (converging) on the carriers (v25's "minus means diverging"). Three readings per condition, each the screen position judged sharpest, approached alternately from the near and far side: healthy (no lens) 9.8, 10.1, 10.0 cm, mean 10.0 cm, range 0.3 cm; too-long model, no lens: the same 10.0 cm image (2.5 cm in front of the 12.5 cm mark); too-long with −50 cm: 12.3, 12.7, 12.5, mean 12.5, range 0.4; too-long with +40 cm (the wrong lens): 7.8, 8.1, 8.0, mean 8.0, range 0.3 (4.5 cm in front of the mark); too-short model, no lens: the image at 10.0 cm (2.0 cm behind the 8.0 cm mark); too-short with +40 cm: 7.9, 8.2, 8.0, mean 8.0 (8.03), range 0.3. Said aloud in scene 5: "These numbers are illustrative, made up to show the method, not real results." Every table, plot and readout carries the grey tag "illustrative data".
- **Variables and controls, with reasons (`ci9ce164ec` applied).** IV: the correcting lens (none, diverging, converging) for each model eye. DV: the distance from the eye lens to the sharpest image, compared with the retina mark. Controlled, each with its reason: the same eye lens (the same "eye"); the lamp at the same 5.0 m (a closer lamp would push the image back; author note c); the correcting lens touching the eye lens every time (moving it changes how much it adds; author note d, A2); all lens centres at the same height on the bench (a tilted or off-centre lens smears the image). Gloss "model: a simpler thing that behaves like the real thing in the way we're testing".
- **Finding the sharpest image (`ci752a2a2f`, `ci6c11c587`, partial).** The image looks sharp over a small range of screen positions, so the screen is slid in from the near side until the filament first looks sharp, then out from the far side, and the middle is the reading; three readings per condition, alternating sides; the half-range is the uncertainty (fa1-v15, v24 recalled in a phrase). A coded "sharp band" (pale gold) on the bench shows the range.
- **Prediction 1 (3 s, scene 6).** "The too-long model's image forms two and a half centimetres in front of its mark. Which lens should move it onto the mark?" Chips "the converging lens?", "the diverging lens?". Answered in 7 (diverging).
- **Prediction 2 (3 s, scene 8; site misconception 2 from v25).** "Some people think short sight needs a lens that helps you see further, a converging one. Try it on the too-long model. Where will the image go?" Chips "onto the mark?", "further from it?". Answered in 8 (further in front: 8.0 cm).
- **Results plot (`ci0fca96de` applied; `ErrorBarPlot`).** fa1-v24's and v24's `ErrorBarPlot` in a one-axis form: a horizontal axis "distance from the eye lens (cm)", 6 to 14; three rows (healthy, too long, too short); each row's tape mark as a teal vertical tick; each measured mean as a dot with its half-range bar; the "no lens" positions hollow, the corrected positions filled; the wrong-lens result as a hollow muted red dot. The corrected dots sit on their marks with the marks inside the bars.
- **Conclusion (`ci8856aeb0`, partial).** "In each model, the right lens moved the image onto the retina mark, within the spread of our readings. The diverging lens moved it back two and a half centimetres; the converging lens moved it forward two. The wrong lens moved it the wrong way." This supports v25's prediction; "supports, not proves" (fa1-v24) recalled in a phrase.
- **The discussion (`ci49f242d5`'s "discuss"; `cibe345fb1`, `cibc9c30ac`, partial).** How spectacles assist people: a lens changes the light before it reaches the eye, so the eye's own focusing puts the image on the retina; the spectacle lens doesn't form the image (v25). Where the model differs from a real eye, each said with its effect: a real eye can fatten its lens (v25), and long sight matters most for near objects, which our distant lamp didn't test (improvement: repeat with the lamp 25 cm away); real spectacles sit about a centimetre or two in front of the eye, not touching (so the needed lens is a little different; author note d); a real eye is filled with fluid and has two focusing parts (cornea and lens), our model has one lens in air.
- **Limitations and improvements (`ci7e38e278` applied).** (1) Judging sharpness: a range of positions looks sharp; improve: approach from both sides (done), use a sharper object such as a cross-wire slide on a lamp box, and dim the room. (2) The lamp is 5.0 m away, not infinitely far, so its light is not perfectly parallel (the image sits about 0.2 cm further back than for a truly distant object; author note c); improve: keep the distance fixed (done) or use a more distant object, never the Sun. (3) The correcting lens's position: improve: a double holder that clamps both lenses together. (4) Only distant objects tested (from 11).
- **Risk assessment (`cif90164e3`, `ci6e7b9681` partial).** Three spoken rows, hazard, harm and control: (1) the hot lamp (burns: don't touch the bulb or holder while it's on; let it cool before packing away); (2) focused sunlight (a converging lens can focus sunlight onto skin, paper or an eye: never aim a lens at the Sun or look at the Sun through it; keep lenses out of direct sunlight); (3) the darkened room (trips: bags under the bench and the floor clear before the lights go down). Teacher notes, not shown: chipped lens edges (cuts: check before use); the lamp's mains lead routed away from walkways. Only the spoken row is shown; earlier rows collapse to one-word grey chips (v20, v24).

- **Author notes (not learner copy):**
  (a) Apparatus: a 1 m optical bench with sliding carriers; a converging lens of focal length 9.8 cm (sold as "10 cm"; the model uses the measured image distance, 10.0 cm, as the healthy eye); weak lenses of focal length −50 cm and +40 cm in a carrier slot touching the eye lens; a white card screen on a carrier; a 12 V filament lamp (or a mains lamp box) 5.0 m away at the far end of a darkened lab; masking tape for the retina mark.
  (b) Model: 1/f₁ = 1/10.0 + 1/500, f₁ = 9.804 cm; thin lenses in contact add powers: with −50 cm, 1/v = 1/9.804 − 1/50 − 1/500, v = 12.50 cm; with +40 cm, v = 8.00 cm (on either model, so the wrong lens on the too-long model also gives 8.0 cm: 4.5 cm in front of the 12.5 cm mark). Lenses needed exactly: too long (12.5 cm) −50.0 cm; too short (8.0 cm) +40.0 cm; A1 (15.0 cm) −30.0 cm.
  (c) Lamp distance: for an object at infinity, v = 9.80 cm; at 5.0 m, 10.00 cm: a 0.2 cm shift (limitation 2).
  (d) Gap: the −50 cm lens 10 cm in front of the eye lens: it makes a virtual image of the lamp 45.5 cm in front of itself, so 55.5 cm from the eye lens; 1/v = 1/9.804 − 1/55.45, v = 11.9 cm (against 12.5 cm touching), a 0.6 cm shift, larger than the 0.4 cm range. ## Practice questions and model answers

**Attempt 1 (scene 14; original wording, HSC style, 4 marks suggested, not an official marking scheme).** "A model eye is set up on an optical bench. The retina mark is 15.0 cm from the eye lens. Model answer: (a) Short sight (myopia): without a correcting lens the image of a distant object forms 5.0 cm in front of the retina mark, as in an eye that is too long. (b) Mean = (14.6 + 14.9 + 15.2) ÷ 3 = 14.9 cm; range = 15.2 − 14.6 = 0.6 cm. (c) The retina mark (15.0 cm) lies within the readings (14.6 to 15.2 cm; 14.9 ± 0.3 cm), so the results are consistent with the diverging lens moving the image back onto the retina mark, within the spread of the measurements (they cannot show an exact focus on the mark). **Attempt 2 (scene 16; original wording, HSC style, 3 marks suggested).** "In a repeat of the investigation, the diverging lens was placed touching the eye lens in some trials, but 10 cm in front of the eye lens in others. Model answer: (a) The position of the diverging lens affects how much the light has spread by the time it reaches the eye lens, so the image position changes with the lens position as well as with the lens type. (b) Keep the correcting lens in the same position in every trial (touching the eye lens, or at one measured gap), for example with a clamp that holds both lenses. Classic mistake (crossed): "only the type of lens matters, not where it sits".

## Safety facts check (self-check, 3 Nov; Codex to confirm)

- **Hot lamp.** A filament lamp's glass and holder become hot enough to burn within minutes. Controls: don't touch the bulb or holder while on; switch off and let it cool before moving or packing away. Learner copy as scene 13.
- **Focused sunlight.** A converging lens of a few centimetres' diameter concentrates sunlight enough to burn skin and ignite paper; looking at the Sun through a lens (or at all) can permanently damage the retina. Controls: never aim a lens at the Sun or look at the Sun through it; store lenses out of direct sunlight (a lens left on a sunny sill can start a fire). Learner copy as scene 13.
- **Darkened room.** Trips over bags and leads, knocked apparatus. Controls: bags under the bench, floor and bench clear before the lights go down. Learner copy as scene 13.
- **Lens edges (teacher note).** Chipped glass lenses can cut; check before use. Not in learner copy.
