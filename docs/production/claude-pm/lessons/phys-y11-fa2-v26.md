"Too long" and "too short" are made by moving the tape mark, not the lens, which mirrors v25's "the eyeball is a little too long". The screen parked on the mark shows what the "retina" sees: a sharp filament image, or a blur patch.

The sharp image is a small upside-down picture of the glowing filament, which makes "sharp or blurred" easy to judge. Not the Sun or a window (risk row 2).

- **Results plot (`ci0fca96de` applied; `ErrorBarPlot`).** fa1-v24's and v24's `ErrorBarPlot` in a one-axis form: a horizontal axis "distance from the eye lens (cm)", 6 to 14; three rows (healthy, too long, too short); each row's tape mark as a teal vertical tick; each measured mean as a dot with its half-range bar; the "no lens" positions hollow, the corrected positions filled; the wrong-lens result as a hollow muted red dot. - **Conclusion (`ci8856aeb0`, partial).** "In each model, the right lens moved the image onto the retina mark, within the spread of our readings. The diverging lens moved it back two and a half centimetres; the converging lens moved it forward two. The wrong lens moved it the wrong way." - **The discussion (`ci49f242d5`'s "discuss"; `cibe345fb1`, `cibc9c30ac`, partial).** How spectacles assist people: a lens changes the light before it reaches the eye, so the eye's own focusing puts the image on the retina; the spectacle lens doesn't form the image (v25). Where the model differs from a real eye, each said with its effect: a real eye can fatten its lens (v25), and long sight matters most for near objects, which our distant lamp didn't test (improvement: repeat with the lamp 25 cm away); real spectacles sit about a centimetre or two in front of the eye, not touching (so the needed lens is a little different; author note d); a real eye is filled with fluid and has two focusing parts (cornea and lens), our model has one lens in air.
- **Limitations and improvements (`ci7e38e278` applied).** (1) Judging sharpness: a range of positions looks sharp; improve: approach from both sides (done), use a sharper object such as a cross-wire slide on a lamp box, and dim the room. (2) The lamp is 5.0 m away, not infinitely far, so its light is not perfectly parallel (the image sits about 0.2 cm further back than for a truly distant object; author note c); improve: keep the distance fixed (done) or use a more distant object, never the Sun. (3) The correcting lens's position: improve: a double holder that clamps both lenses together. (4) Only distant objects tested (from 11).
- **Risk assessment (`cif90164e3`, `ci6e7b9681` partial).** Three spoken rows, hazard, harm and control: (1) the hot lamp (burns: don't touch the bulb or holder while it's on; let it cool before packing away); (2) focused sunlight (a converging lens can focus sunlight onto skin, paper or an eye: never aim a lens at the Sun or look at the Sun through it; keep lenses out of direct sunlight); (3) the darkened room (trips: bags under the bench and the floor clear before the lights go down). Teacher notes, not shown: chipped lens edges (cuts: check before use); the lamp's mains lead routed away from walkways. Only the spoken row is shown; earlier rows collapse to one-word grey chips (v20, v24).

## Safety facts check (self-check, 3 Nov; Codex to confirm)

- **Hot lamp.** A filament lamp's glass and holder become hot enough to burn within minutes. Controls: don't touch the bulb or holder while on; switch off and let it cool before moving or packing away. Learner copy as scene 13.
- **Focused sunlight.** A converging lens of a few centimetres' diameter concentrates sunlight enough to burn skin and ignite paper; looking at the Sun through a lens (or at all) can permanently damage the retina. Controls: never aim a lens at the Sun or look at the Sun through it; store lenses out of direct sunlight (a lens left on a sunny sill can start a fire). Learner copy as scene 13.
- **Darkened room.** Trips over bags and leads, knocked apparatus. Controls: bags under the bench, floor and bench clear before the lights go down. Learner copy as scene 13.
- **Lens edges (teacher note).** Chipped glass lenses can cut; check before use. Not in learner copy.
