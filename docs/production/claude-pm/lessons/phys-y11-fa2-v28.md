## Decisions

- **Learner question kept (route).** "How does opening size relative to wavelength affect spreading?" It is v27's handoff chip and already an answerable investigation question (open decision 1). The answer, built in order: the plan and the measurement (1 to 3); narrowing the gap at fixed λ widens the spread, to semicircles at one wavelength (4, 5); lengthening λ at a fixed gap does the same, and a matched ratio gives a matched spread (6, 7); one outlier explained (8); the depth control explained (9, 10); both runs on one curve against w/λ (11); obstacles follow the same rule (12); so the ratio, not either size alone, decides (13).
- **The hook (v27's frame, made a plan).** "Last time we watched a model. Today we measure it." No prediction in the hook; the two in-teaching predictions are the matched ratio (6) and the tilted tank (9).

- **Author notes (not learner copy):**
  (a) Apparatus: a ripple tank with a lamp above and white paper (or a screen) below; a straight vibrating bar on a variable-speed motor powered from a low-voltage supply; two straight barrier pieces (metal or plastic strips) to set the gap; a post (a 1.5 cm wide block standing in the water) and a 12 cm block for run 3; a ruler; a phone on a tall tripod above the paper; a protractor; water about 5 mm deep, the tray levelled with its adjusting feet; sloping foam edges on the tray.
  (b) Tank scale: the gaps are drawn at tank scale (9.0 cm = 6λ at λ = 1.5 cm); the photo cards show the projected pattern.
  (c) Data model: the first minimum of a single gap is at sin θ = λ/w: w/λ 6: 9.59°; 4: 14.48°; 3: 19.47°; 2: 30.0°; 1: 90° (no minimum in the forward half; near semicircles). Not taught (Year 12). Small-angle approximation θ ≈ 57.3°/(w/λ): 28.6, 19.1, 14.3, 9.5; the readings happen to sit close to inverse for w/λ 2 to 6, which is why no proportionality is claimed (at w/λ = 1 the inverse rule would give 57° or 60°, against semicircles).
  (d) Outlier: λ 1.7 cm in a 6.0 cm gap gives sin⁻¹(1.7 ÷ 6.0) = 16.5°, shown 16°. Six crests (five wavelengths) across 8.5 cm.
  (e) Ripple speed depends a little on frequency (surface tension matters at these wavelengths), so real wavelengths will not scale exactly as 1/f; measuring λ directly removes this. The illustrative frequencies (15, 10, 7.5, 5.0 Hz) are chosen with v = 0.15 m s⁻¹ throughout for simplicity and carry the tag.
  (f) The lamp projects the pattern magnified by the same factor in every direction, so angles on the paper equal angles in the water; the ruler is in the water, so its shadow is magnified with the crests and the λ readings are true (as v20 note f).

## Practice questions and model answers

**Attempt 1 (scene 16; original wording, HSC style, 4 marks suggested, not an official marking scheme).** "In a ripple tank investigation, straight waves of wavelength 2.0 cm were sent through gaps of different widths, and the spread angle beyond each gap was measured from photographs (illustrative data). Gap width: 4.0 cm, 8.0 cm, 12 cm. Mean spread angle: 30°, 14°, 10°. (a) Identify the independent variable, the dependent variable and one controlled variable, giving a reason for controlling it. (b) Calculate the ratio of gap width to wavelength for each gap, and describe the relationship between this ratio and the spread angle."
Model answer: (a) Independent variable: the gap width. Dependent variable: the spread angle. Controlled: the frequency of the vibrating bar, because it sets the wavelength (or the water depth, kept the same by levelling the tank, because it sets the wave speed and so the wavelength). (b) w/λ = 4.0 ÷ 2.0 = 2; 8.0 ÷ 2.0 = 4; 12 ÷ 2.0 = 6. As the ratio of gap width to wavelength increases, the spread angle decreases (30° to 14° to 10°): the narrower the gap compared with the wavelength, the more the waves diffract. Classic mistake (crossed): "the amount of spreading is the independent variable".

**Attempt 2 (scene 18; original wording, HSC style, 4 marks suggested).** "In a ripple tank, water waves of wavelength 1.2 cm pass through a gap 2.4 cm wide and spread out at a spread angle of about 30°. A sound of frequency 680 Hz, travelling at 340 m s⁻¹, passes through an open window 1.0 m wide. (a) Calculate the wavelength of the sound. (b) Use the ripple tank result to predict the spreading of the sound beyond the window. Justify your prediction."
Model answer: (a) λ = v/f = 340 ÷ 680 = 0.50 m. (b) The amount of diffraction depends on the ratio of the gap width to the wavelength. For the window, w/λ = 1.0 ÷ 0.50 = 2.0; for the tank, w/λ = 2.4 ÷ 1.2 = 2.0. The ratios are equal, so the sound should spread beyond the window by about the same angle as the water waves, about 30° on each side of straight ahead. Classic mistake (crossed): "a window is far too wide for sound to spread".
