**Attempt 1 (scene 16; original wording, HSC style, 4 marks suggested, not an official marking scheme).** "In a ripple tank investigation, straight waves of wavelength 2.0 cm were sent through gaps of different widths, and the spread angle beyond each gap was measured from photographs (illustrative data). (b) Calculate the ratio of gap width to wavelength for each gap, and describe the relationship between this ratio and the spread angle."
Model answer: (a) Independent variable: the gap width. Dependent variable: the spread angle. Controlled: the frequency of the vibrating bar, because it sets the wavelength (or the water depth, kept the same by levelling the tank, because it sets the wave speed and so the wavelength). (b) w/λ = 4.0 ÷ 2.0 = 2; 8.0 ÷ 2.0 = 4; 12 ÷ 2.0 = 6. As the ratio of gap width to wavelength increases, the spread angle decreases (30° to 14° to 10°): the narrower the gap compared with the wavelength, the more the waves diffract. Classic mistake (crossed): "the amount of spreading is the independent variable".

**Attempt 2 (scene 18; original wording, HSC style, 4 marks suggested).** "In a ripple tank, water waves of wavelength 1.2 cm pass through a gap 2.4 cm wide and spread out at a spread angle of about 30°. (b) Use the ripple tank result to predict the spreading of the sound beyond the window. Justify your prediction."
Model answer: (a) λ = v/f = 340 ÷ 680 = 0.50 m. (b) The amount of diffraction depends on the ratio of the gap width to the wavelength. For the window, w/λ = 1.0 ÷ 0.50 = 2.0; for the tank, w/λ = 2.4 ÷ 1.2 = 2.0. The ratios are equal, so the sound should spread beyond the window by about the same angle as the water waves, about 30° on each side of straight ahead.
