## Decisions

- **Learner question kept (route).** "How do the function and its two derivative graphs constrain a sketch?" The answer, built in order: three marked points allow many curves (hook); the sign of f′ on each interval fixes the direction, so a curve that rises where f′ < 0 is ruled out (2); the sign of f″ fixes the bend, so a straight or wrongly bent join is ruled out (3); symmetry, intercepts and end behaviour fix the rest, and the checklist is the list of these rules (4, 5); an excluded value can split the graph, carry an asymptote and stop a concavity change from being an inflection (6, 7); read in reverse, the gradient of each graph draws the next one down (8, 9).
- **Opening frame and hook (d16 handoff; the wrong-curve contrast is original).** d16 (Revision 1, read 1 Nov) shows three stacked panels for its worked cubic x³ − 6x² + 9x, with the chip "Next: sketch a curve from f, f′ and f″", then holds its key notes. d17 opens on the key-notes last frame, which clears to the panels in the first second, says in one sentence that the three graphs will now be used to sketch, and changes to y = x³ − 3x (site `we-l19-1`, a cubic students met in d09 and d10). x³ − 3x is kept rather than d16's cubic because it is the site's sketching example and it is odd, so the symmetry check has something to find; d16's cubic would repeat d16's own final stack in scene 8. ### 1. hook (three points are not a sketch)
> Last video ended here, with a cubic and its two derivative graphs. Now let's sketch. Take a cubic you've met before, y equals x cubed minus three x. It has a local maximum at minus one, two, a local minimum at one, minus two, and a point of inflection at the origin. If you only had those three points, could you draw the curve? This wiggly curve goes through all three **points**. So does this one, made of straight **lines**. Both are wrong. What rules them out isn't the points. It's what the derivatives say between them. Each one is a constraint, a rule the curve has to obey.

### 2. direction from f prime
> Start with f prime. f prime of x is three, times x minus one, times x plus one. It's positive when x is less than minus one, negative between minus one and one, and positive again after **one**. So the curve must rise, then fall, then rise. Now look at the wiggly curve. Near zero it climbs, where f prime says it must **fall**. So it's out. Each sign of f prime is a rule for a whole interval, not just a point.

Visual: right column `AlignedAlgebra` row "f′(x) = 3x² − 3 = 3(x − 1)(x + 1)". `SignChart` under the graph with boundaries x = −1 and x = 1 (kind `zero`): on "one" the three signs and arrows write in order "+ rising", "− falling", "+ rising".
