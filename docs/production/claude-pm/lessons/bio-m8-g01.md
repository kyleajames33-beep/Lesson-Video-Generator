### 2b. q1-calc (rates, one row at a time)
> Rates first, laid out as equation, substitute, solve, sense check. Incidence is new cases over the population, times a hundred thousand. For P, six thousand six hundred over one point two million, times a hundred thousand, is **five hundred and fifty** per hundred thousand per year. For Q, one thousand and fifty over a hundred and fifty thousand gives **seven hundred**. Mortality puts deaths on top. Three hundred over one point two million gives **twenty-five**, and fifty-four over a hundred and fifty thousand gives **thirty-six**. Sense check: Q is the smaller region, yet both its rates are higher.

### 3b. q2-graph (build the graph, one stage at a time)
> Set up the graph before you plot anything. Year goes on the **x axis**, because it's the independent variable. The rate goes on the **y axis**, with its unit, per hundred thousand people per year. Both rates share that unit, so one axis works. Look at the years: they jump by ten, then by **five** at the end, so space them to scale. For the y axis, choose an even scale that fits the largest value, fifty-two: **zero to sixty** in tens. Plot each point, then join each set with its own ruled line, because each value is a whole year's rate, and add a **key** and a **title**. That's TAILS: title, axes, intervals, labels, scale.

### 6. key-notes (reuse take: key-notes; quiet, about 60 seconds)
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

- Incidence = new cases ÷ population × 100 000 (per year). Mortality rate = deaths ÷ population × 100 000 (per year). Prevalence = existing cases ÷ group × 100 (%).
- Compare rates, not raw counts. Before inferring higher risk, check age structure (use age-standardised rates).
- Graph two rates with the same unit on one set of axes: independent variable (year) on x, spaced to scale; y labelled with quantity and unit; an even scale that fits every value; a key and a title (TAILS).
