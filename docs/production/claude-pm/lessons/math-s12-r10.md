### 2. change the bottom unit
> Write the rate like a fraction, zero point three litres on top, one minute underneath. An hour is sixty minutes, so in an hour the tap runs sixty times as long, and loses sixty times as much water. Multiply the top and the bottom by sixty. That's eighteen litres in sixty minutes, eighteen litres per **hour**. A day is twenty-four hours, so multiply the top and the bottom by twenty-four. That's four hundred and thirty-two litres per **day**. A year is three hundred and sixty-five days, so that's about a hundred and fifty-eight thousand litres a **year**. That would fill a bath more than eight hundred **times**.

### 3. change both units
> Sometimes both units have to change. A fast bowler sends the ball down at a hundred and forty-four kilometres per hour. The pitch is about twenty metres long, so metres per second would be more useful. Change one unit at a time. First the top. A kilometre is a thousand metres, so that's a hundred and forty-four thousand **metres** per hour. Now the bottom. An hour is three thousand six hundred seconds, so it's a hundred and forty-four thousand metres in three thousand six hundred seconds. Divide, and that's forty metres per **second**. Times a thousand, then divide by three thousand six hundred, is the same as dividing by three point **six**, which is a handy shortcut. At forty metres per second, the ball covers the twenty point one two metre pitch in about half a **second**. Changing only the top, and calling it a hundred and forty-four thousand metres per second, is the slip. Change both **units**.

### 4. turn it upside down
> A rate can also be turned upside down. Say someone runs a five kilometre fun run in twenty-five minutes. Put the minutes on top, and that's twenty-five minutes for five kilometres, which is five minutes per **kilometre**. Runners call that their pace. Put the distance on top instead, and in sixty minutes they'd cover twelve kilometres, so it's twelve kilometres per **hour**. It's the same run, said two ways. The units tell you which one you've **got**.

### 5. using a rate
> Once you have a rate, you can use it two ways. A pump empties a two thousand four hundred litre tank in forty minutes. That's two thousand four hundred divided by forty, sixty litres per **minute**. How long would a three thousand six hundred litre tank take? Each minute removes sixty litres, so divide the amount by the rate. Three thousand six hundred divided by sixty is sixty minutes, one **hour**. So to find an amount, multiply the rate by the time. To find a time, divide the amount by the **rate**.

### 6. worked example: hours and minutes
> Here's one where the time comes in hours and minutes. A car travels two hundred and seventy kilometres in three hours and fifteen minutes. Find its average speed in kilometres per hour. Speed is distance divided by time, so two hundred and seventy divided by three point two five is about eighty-three point one kilometres per **hour**. Writing three hours fifteen as three point one five is the slip. Fifteen minutes is a quarter of an hour, not fifteen **hundredths**.

## Memory aid check
