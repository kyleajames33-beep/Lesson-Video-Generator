### 1. a key, a lock, and a lock that moves (opening)
> Last time, we saw that only the right substrate fits an enzyme's active site. But how does an enzyme pick out its own substrate from all the molecules around it? So how do these two models explain why an enzyme works on its own substrate?

### 3. the lock-and-key model
> The first model is the lock-and-key model, from the chemist Emil Fischer. In it, the active site is rigid, with a fixed shape, like a lock. Only a substrate with exactly the complementary shape can slot in. When it does, the two form an enzyme-substrate complex, which just means the enzyme and substrate bound together. The reaction happens, the products leave, and the active site is exactly the same shape as before, ready for the next substrate. In this drawing, a molecule with a different shape doesn't fit.

### 5. a flexible protein, and induced fit
