### 2. three jobs, three meanings of prevent
> Genetic technologies do three different jobs. Screening finds a risk or a condition, but it doesn't change any DNA. Embryo testing chooses between embryos that already exist. Genetic engineering changes the genetic material itself. So for any method, ask which job it does, and what exactly it prevents: symptoms in a person, the birth of an affected child, or the faulty gene itself.

### 3. PKU: the worked case
> Start with the heel prick. If the test finds it early, the baby starts a low-phenylalanine diet straightaway, the level stays low, and the brain develops normally.

### 7. changing genes in body cells
> The third job is changing genes. A change in a body cell stays with that person. **Gene therapy** works in body cells. For spinal muscular atrophy, a modified virus carries a working copy of a missing gene into nerve cells, and it has to be given early, before many of those cells are lost. For sickle cell disease, CRISPR can edit a patient's own blood stem cells outside the body. Both treat one person. Their children's risk doesn't change.

### 13. key notes (quiet, about 60 seconds)
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

- Genetic technology does three jobs: screening finds risk, embryo testing (PGT) chooses an embryo, genetic engineering changes genes. Ask what is prevented: symptoms, an affected birth, or the gene itself.
- Newborn screening for PKU: found early, a low-phenylalanine diet prevents brain damage. The harmful phenotype is prevented; the alleles are unchanged and still heritable. Screening only helps when an early treatment exists.
- Two carriers of a recessive allele: 1 in 4 chance of an affected child each pregnancy. Carrier screening plus non-directive counselling lets couples choose; PGT selects an unaffected IVF embryo without changing DNA.
