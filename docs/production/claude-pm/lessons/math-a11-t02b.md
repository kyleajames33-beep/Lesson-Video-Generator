### 2. compass bearings into true bearings
> A compass bearing starts at north or south, then turns towards east or west. North forty degrees east starts at north and turns forty degrees towards east. That's clockwise, so it's zero four zero degrees again. For a southerly one, start from south, one eight zero degrees. South thirty-two degrees west turns from south towards west. That's still clockwise, so add: two one two **degrees**. South twenty-five degrees east turns from south towards east, which is back anticlockwise. So subtract: one eight zero minus twenty-five, one five five **degrees**. And north sixty-five degrees west turns back anticlockwise from north, so it's three sixty minus sixty-five, two nine five **degrees**. Subtracting for south thirty-two degrees west, to get one four eight, is the **slip**. One four eight degrees is south thirty-two degrees east.

## Practice questions and model answers

**Attempt 1 (scene 7; lesson09 Q3's bearing with a return part added, re-worded, HSC style, 2 marks suggested, not an official marking scheme).** "A yacht sails on a compass bearing of S48°E. (a) Write this as a true bearing. (b) Give the true bearing of the return journey."
Model answer: (a) S48°E starts at south (180°) and turns 48° towards east, which is anticlockwise, so 180° − 48° = 132°. (b) The return is half a turn away: 132° + 180° = 312°. Classic mistake (crossed): "228°" (180° + 48°, which is S48°W).

**Attempt 2 (scene 9; pack question 2, original, HSC style, 4 marks suggested).** "A hiker walks 5 km on a true bearing of 070°, then 12 km on a true bearing of 160°. (a) Explain why the path turns through a right angle. (b) Find the hiker's distance from the start and the true bearing of the finishing point from the start, to the nearest degree."
Model answer: (a) at the turn, the reverse bearing of the first leg is 250°, and both bearings are measured from the same north line there, so the angle between the legs is 250° − 160° = 90°, a right angle. (b) d = √(5² + 12²) = 13 km. At the start, tan α = 12/5, α ≈ 67.4°, clockwise from the first leg, so the bearing is 070° + 67.4° ≈ 137°. Classic mistake (crossed): "067°".

## Memory aid check

- **"Bearings: from north, clockwise, three digits; a new north at every turn."** Original wording of standard conventions. Meaning, as said in scene 6: a true bearing is the clockwise angle from north, written 000° to 359° with three digits, and at every turning point the next bearing is measured from a new north line there. Assumptions checked: true bearings only (compass bearings are converted first, scene 2); north lines are parallel over the distances in these problems (a flat map). - **Second line: "Reverse: add or subtract 180°."** Meaning: the bearing straight back is half a turn away; add 180° if the bearing is under 180°, subtract if it is 180° or more, so the answer stays between 000° and 360°. Checked on 212° → 032° (3) and 132° → 312° (A1).
