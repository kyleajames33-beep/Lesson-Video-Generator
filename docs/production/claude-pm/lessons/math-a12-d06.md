- **Learner question kept (route).** "How do we apply the chain rule to exponential, log and trig functions?" The answer, built in order: when a function has an inside part, the outside's derivative, taken at the inside, gives only part of the slope; the measured slope is that times the inside part's derivative, because a small change in x is multiplied first by the inside's rate and then by the outside's rate. With the derivatives from d01 to d05 as the outsides, this gives e^(f(x)) → f′(x) e^(f(x)), ln(f(x)) → f′(x)/f(x) for f(x) > 0, sin(f(x)) → f′(x) cos(f(x)) and cos(f(x)) → −f′(x) sin(f(x)).

- **Why multiply: rates along a chain (cause).** Three linked number strips at x = 1: x moves by 0.01; the inside u = x² moves by 0.0201, about 2 × 0.01, because its rate there is 2x = 2; y = sin u moves by about 0.0107, about cos(1) × 0.02 ≈ 0.0108, because sine's rate at u = 1 is cos 1 ≈ 0.540. So dy/dx ≈ 1.07, close to the exact 2 cos 1 ≈ 1.081 (author note b). The narration says "rates along a chain multiply: dy/du times du/dx", Year 11's form.

### 4. why the rates multiply
> Why multiply? Follow a small step at x equals one. Move x by point zero one. The inside, x squared, moves about twice as far, about point zero **two**, because its rate there is two x, which is two. Then sine reacts to its own input at a rate of cos one, about point five **four**. So y moves about point five four times point zero two, which is about point zero one **one**. That's why it's called a chain: each link passes its change on to the next, multiplied by its own rate. Rates along a chain multiply: d y by d u, times d u by d **x**.

### 5. the four outsides
