## Decisions

- **Learner question kept (route).** "How do we apply the chain rule to exponential, log and trig functions?" The answer, built in order: when a function has an inside part, the outside's derivative, taken at the inside, gives only part of the slope; the measured slope is that times the inside part's derivative, because a small change in x is multiplied first by the inside's rate and then by the outside's rate. With the derivatives from d01 to d05 as the outsides, this gives e^(f(x)) → f′(x) e^(f(x)), ln(f(x)) → f′(x)/f(x) for f(x) > 0, sin(f(x)) → f′(x) cos(f(x)) and cos(f(x)) → −f′(x) sin(f(x)).
- **A recall, not a first teaching (route root decision 2; route recommendation).** Year 11 `ci00fa8fac` is recalled in one scene with a picture students have already seen in d01 (the sideways squash multiplies slopes by the coefficient of x), then extended. The words "inside part" and "outside" are d01's and d03's spoken habit ("copy e to that power, then multiply by the inside part's derivative").

### 2. recall: the squash and the chain rule agree
> You've met the reason before. Sine two x is the sine curve squashed sideways by a half, so every slope, at matching points, **doubles**. The chain rule from Year 11 says the same thing. Two x is the inside part, the expression sine is applied to, and sine is the outside. Differentiate the outside, and keep the inside as it is: cos two **x**. Then multiply by the inside part's derivative, which is **two**. So the derivative is two cos two x, and at zero, that's **two**.

### 4. why the rates multiply
> Why multiply? Follow a small step at x equals one. Move x by point zero one. The inside, x squared, moves about twice as far, about point zero **two**, because its rate there is two x, which is two. Then sine reacts to its own input at a rate of cos one, about point five **four**. So y moves about point five four times point zero two, which is about point zero one **one**. That's why it's called a chain: each link passes its change on to the next, multiplied by its own rate. Rates along a chain multiply: d y by d u, times d u by d **x**.

### 5. the four outsides

For sine of an inside part, it's cos of the same inside, times the inside's **derivative**. And for cos, it's minus sine of the same inside, times the inside's **derivative**. Each time, the inside stays **unchanged**. For instance, e to the x squared has derivative two x, times e to the x squared.
