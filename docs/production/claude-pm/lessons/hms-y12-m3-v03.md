### 1. hook (same condition, same day, 800 km apart)
> So, why do the same causes keep appearing? Two people have the same treatable condition, diagnosed on the same day. One lives in a capital city. The other lives eight hundred kilometres from the nearest specialist. Think of three things that will differ that year. For each, is it a difference in the condition, or in the circumstances? There's a third kind of difference, and it's where the word inequity lives.

### 4. the mechanism: say "because"
> Now the mechanism, and the key word is because. Distance means a longer trip to care, and for trauma, heart attack and stroke, the treatment is **time-critical**, so distance turns directly into deaths. The workforce is thinner, so a service can close when one doctor leaves. Incomes are lower, more jobs carry injury risk, and every trip for care costs fuel and a day's pay. Now let them interact. Distance plus a thin workforce means care is delayed. Delay plus risky work means more injuries arrive late. Low income plus travel cost means follow-up is skipped. Each alone is manageable. Together, they hold the gradient in place.

### 8. one population, cut five ways
