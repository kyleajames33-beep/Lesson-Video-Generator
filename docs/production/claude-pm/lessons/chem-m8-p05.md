## Decisions

- **Main question:** *Four bottles, A to D, each hold one unknown organic liquid. How do you plan and run three quick tests, with the right controls and safely around chromium(VI), so that each claim you make about a functional group would stand up?* Heading on screen: "Unknown organics: which claims can you back?"
- **Dichromate as a teacher demonstration (root decision, matches chem-m7-p04 Revision 3).** Every dichromate step is a teacher demonstration in a fume cupboard; students record. Small volumes (2 mL of about 0.1 mol/L K₂Cr₂O₇ in about 1 mol/L H₂SO₄ per tube, five drops of sample), as route review change 5 asks ("dichromate as a Cr(VI) oxidant used in small volumes"). The design does not claim this is a rule for every school.
- **Students run the fizz test and the bromine water test.** Sodium hydrogen carbonate is the lowest-hazard reagent here (not classified on supplier SDSs). Bromine water follows p02's method and controls: the teacher dispenses it in the fume cupboard, students wear glasses and gloves.
- **Candidate classes stated in the aim (c08 Decisions, kept).** "Your teacher tells you each unknown is a hydrocarbon, an alcohol or a carboxylic acid." Without it a green dichromate tube could be an aldehyde and a fade could be a phenol or an aldehyde, so leaving it out would teach a false inference. It is a necessary condition, so it stays in learner copy (scene 2) and in key note 1.
- **The unknowns (teacher's key; author note a):** A = dilute ethanoic acid (1 mol/L); B = propan-1-ol; C = cyclohexene; D = 2-methylpropan-2-ol. Low-hazard, available in school labs, all used in earlier designs (p02, p04, c09). D is chosen on purpose: it changes nothing in any test, so the honest claim is "no C=C, no –COOH and no 1° or 2° alcohol detected", and the key then shows an OH group was there all along. This makes "not detected is not absent" concrete, and it pays off c08 scene 10's limit. No unsaturated alcohol or other two-group compound is used as a class sample (allyl alcohol is very toxic, c08 author note e); attempt 2 carries the two-group case on paper.
- **Knowns:** ethanoic acid (fizz), cyclohexene (fade; p02's known), ethanol (green). A known can be the same compound as an unknown, as in p03 (known Mg²⁺ against unknown C). The blank for every test is distilled water.
- **What a changed blank means (scene 3).** Said in general terms ("the tube, the water or the reagent") so that attempt 1's ethanol-rinse case stays unseen.
- **Dichromate demonstration in two batches (scenes 6 and 7).** Batch 1 uses the four-tube water-bath pair from chem-m7-p04 (`m7-oxidation-water-bath-before-v1.png`, `-after-v1.png`: tubes 1 and 2 green, 3 and 4 orange): tubes "ethanol (known) | B | D | water (blank)". Batch 2 reuses the "before" plate as an end state, because after five minutes it is honestly unchanged: tubes "A | C | D repeat | water (blank)", all orange. The D repeat is the reliability example in scene 10. Cyclohexene in batch 2 depends on c08 author note (c) (alkenes usually leave warm acidified dichromate orange); fallback in Open decisions.
- **Claims language (scenes 9 and 12; author-written memory aid).** "Supported, not detected, can't tell." Supported: a positive with a clean blank and a working known. Not detected: a negative, which is not proof of absence (p03's "no visible change means not detected", c08's "orange ≠ no OH"). Can't tell: two possibilities give the same results (D: an alkane or a tertiary alcohol). This answers the route stop "infer supported groups" in the student's own words.
- **Accuracy for a claim about groups (s07).** s07 defines accuracy as "how close is the result to the true value". Here the result is a claim about groups, so accuracy means the claim matches the true structure; the teacher's key checks it. D's claim was accurate because it claimed only what the tests showed, which is the point of scene 9.
- **Smell.** The site's esterification test uses a smell, and its safety box says never smell an unknown until hazards are controlled. This practical asks for no smell at all, and scene 11 says "Never smell an unknown to identify it."
- **Site sources used (site lesson 11):** the safety box on smelling (scene 11, risk table); card 5's safety controls (bromine water in a fume cupboard with gloves and eye protection, away from strong light; dichromate with gloves and minimal quantities), with its wording corrected (corrections 5 and 6); card 5's "always state both the positive and the negative observation, because an absence of change is also evidence" (scene 8); card 4's worked example ("Report the groups the evidence supports and what remains unresolved, rather than overstating the identification"), which is scene 9's rule; card 3's limewater confirmation. The site gives no method quantities; volumes come from p02 and p04 (author note b).

- **Attempt systems (unseen, every condition visible, HSC wording, original):** (A1) "In a teacher demonstration, four test tubes were rinsed with ethanol and drained to help them dry. 2 mL of acidified potassium dichromate solution was added to each tube, followed by five drops of water, ethanol, unknown S and unknown T respectively. The tubes were warmed in a water bath at 60 °C for five minutes. All four mixtures turned from orange to green. A student concluded that S and T both contain a primary or secondary alcohol. Evaluate the student's conclusion, and propose one improvement to the method." Answer: not valid; the water blank turned green, so something other than the samples reduced the dichromate (ethanol left from the rinse), and S and T's results can't be trusted; use clean tubes rinsed with distilled water and dried (not with ethanol) and repeat with a blank that stays orange. Crossed mistake: "repeat the test" (the same ethanol residue gives the same green; s07 word, feature, fix). (A2) "Unknown E is a hydrocarbon, an alcohol or a carboxylic acid. Fresh portions of E were tested, with a water blank for each test. Solid sodium hydrogen carbonate: brisk effervescence; the gas turned limewater milky. State which functional groups the results support, which were not detected, and one limitation of these tests." Answer: a carboxylic acid group and a C=C are supported (one molecule can carry both); no primary or secondary alcohol was detected; a limitation: a tertiary OH can't be ruled out, or the tests don't show where the C=C sits or the full structure.
