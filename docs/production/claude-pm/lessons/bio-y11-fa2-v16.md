### 3. why diffusion can't do it
> So why can't diffusion do the job in a body like yours? Three reasons. First, you're big. Remember the cubes: as something grows, its surface can't keep up with its volume. Second, distance. Diffusion is fast over tiny distances and painfully slow over long ones. Oxygen crosses ten micrometres in a fraction of a second, but diffusing through five centimetres of tissue would take days. And third, you're active. Your cells respire fast, so they need supplies quickly and their wastes cleared quickly.

## Numbers or facts to verify

Self-check (2 Nov). The Codex science check must confirm each before voicing.
- **Muscle cell distance (1).** Skeletal muscle fibres in the thigh lie up to several centimetres below the skin (the quadriceps is several centimetres thick under skin and fat). "centimetres from your skin" is correct. "metres of tubing away from the food you ate": the gut is several metres long (site hook). Correct.
- **Amoeba (2).** Amoeba proteus is about 0.25 to 0.75 mm long; it exchanges gases and wastes across its membrane by diffusion and takes in food by phagocytosis (FA1 v13). "manages all four needs straight across its own membrane, by diffusion": food intake is by phagocytosis, not diffusion. The site's Before You Read says the same ("meets all its needs by diffusion"). Flag for the science check: if judged misleading, change "by diffusion" to "mostly by diffusion" (length +7). Water is gained by osmosis and excess pumped out by a contractile vacuole (author note).
- **Diffusion times (3).** Order-of-magnitude estimate t ≈ x² ÷ 2D, with D for oxygen in water about 2 × 10⁻⁹ m² s⁻¹ (about 3 × 10⁻⁹ at 37 °C; lower in tissue). 10 µm: (10⁻⁵)² ÷ (4 × 10⁻⁹) = 0.025 s ("a fraction of a second"; readout "about 0.03 s"). 5 cm: (0.05)² ÷ (4 × 10⁻⁹) = 625,000 s ≈ 7 days (at D = 3 × 10⁻⁹, about 5 days; slower in tissue). "would take days" is safe. Calculated by script (2 Nov). The 1 cm value (about 7 hours) is not spoken.
- **Organ systems and organs (5 to 8).** Digestive system: mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small and large intestine (with rectum and anus), accessory organs liver, pancreas (and gall bladder, salivary glands). Most absorption occurs in the small intestine. Respiratory: trachea, bronchi, bronchioles, alveoli in the lungs; fish use gills. Excretory (urinary) system: kidneys, ureters, bladder (and urethra). Correct. Correct.
- **Urea (7).** Excess amino acids are deaminated in the liver; the amino groups become ammonia, converted to urea in the urea cycle; urea is carried in the blood and removed by the kidneys. "harms cells if it builds up": urea itself is relatively low in toxicity, but high blood urea (with the other retained wastes in kidney failure) causes harm (uraemia). Acceptable at Year 11; flag for the science check. "the liver turns the spare nitrogen into urea" is correct.
- **Water (8, 11).** Water is absorbed mainly in the small intestine (and the large intestine) into the blood; the kidneys adjust the volume and concentration of urine (by ADH, not named; Module 8 and FA2 v26 to v33 territory). Drinking 1 L of water produces a large volume of dilute urine within about one to two hours (a standard water-load response). Correct.
- **Circulatory pathway (9).** Glucose absorbed in the small intestine enters the blood (hepatic portal vein to the liver, then on to the heart: v21 owns this; "The heart pumps it through the vessels" skips the liver deliberately, author note), reaches muscle capillaries and enters the cell by facilitated diffusion (GLUT4; "diffuses into the muscle cell" is acceptable, FA1 v14). Carbon dioxide diffuses into the blood, returns in veins to the right side of the heart and is pumped to the lungs, where it diffuses into the alveoli and is exhaled. Correct.
- **Kidney failure (10).** Kidney failure causes retention of urea and other nitrogenous wastes, salts (sodium, potassium) and water, with whole-body symptoms (fatigue, nausea, swelling, confusion); dialysis (haemodialysis or peritoneal) replaces the filtering function. Correct.
- **Flatworm and rat (11c).** Flatworms (Platyhelminthes) have no respiratory or circulatory system; their flattened bodies (many species well under 1 mm thick) keep cells close to the surface, and the branched gut reaches close to all cells; gas exchange is by diffusion across the body surface. Rats are endotherms with a high metabolic rate and a body several centimetres thick. Correct.
