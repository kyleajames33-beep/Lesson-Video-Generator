## Decisions

- **Main teaching move: say what you see before you say what it does.** The evidence frame ("I see / So it can / How sure?") fills for each sample, so every function claim is attached to a visible feature, and the third line keeps structure apart from movement (route stop: "a section alone does not prove flow direction"). Scene 8 states that rule outright, and attempt 2 tests it.
- **Three samples, three jobs (site order, route boundary).** Leaf for gas exchange (3 to 5), stem for water and sugar transport (6), root for uptake (7). Each sample has one preparation students can do or watch safely, and a prepared slide where hand-cutting is not realistic (route: "Use prepared sections where needed").
- **Leaf surface by nail-varnish print (scene 3), not an epidermal peel.** Painting clear varnish on the lower surface, lifting it with clear tape and sticking it to a slide is the standard safe school method, needs no blade and gives the stomata count. The existing matched plates `m8-stomata-field-lower-v1.png` (20 stomata in the field, counted 2 Nov) and `m8-stomata-field-upper-v1.png` (3) give a real, countable observation and the scene's prediction. The site's "peel the lower epidermis" is named as an alternative in a grey tag.
- **Leaf section from a prepared slide (scene 5).** A leaf is too thin to section well by hand at Year 11; the painted `m8-leaf-cross-section-v1.png` stands for the prepared slide's view (tag "prepared slide, illustration"), showing the spongy mesophyll's air spaces opening onto a stoma, and the vein.
- **Stem: the celery from v09, now as the students' own slide (scene 6; no double teaching).** v09 taught xylem from the dye result and the stained micrograph. Here the dye stalk is the practical: the teacher cuts thin slices with a blade (thin enough for light to pass through), students view on low power, and the stained xylem beside unstained phloem becomes evidence for two tissues. v09's plates are reused; nothing in v09's explanation is repeated beyond one clause.
- **Root: radish seedlings for root hairs, a prepared slide for the centre (scene 7).** Seeds germinated on damp paper show root hairs on low power without any cutting (route "root-hair context"); a prepared root cross-section shows the xylem in the centre, unlike the stem's ring of bundles. v12's `y11-root-hair-v1.png` is recalled small for the cell-level view.
- **Structure, not movement (scene 8).** A slide is a still picture; a hollow tube suggests liquid flow but cannot show direction. Direction needs extra evidence, for example dye appearing higher up the stalk over time (attempt 2) or, for phloem, v11's aphid evidence (grey tag). Observation and inference are named here with plain meanings.
- **Recording (scene 9).** A labelled line drawing (or annotated photo) with the sample, the surface and the magnification; then one results table: structure, where seen, job, evidence (the site's Activity 1 columns plus "evidence"). The site's hooks-file mnemonic closes the scene: "Hole for gas, hollow pipe for water, living tube for sugar, hair for soaking."
- **The prediction beat (scene 3).** "On the upper surface, will you find more stomata, or fewer?" with a 3 s countdown, reasoned from v13 (the underside is shaded and cooler).
- **Risk table (scene 10).** Three spoken rows, each hazard, harm and control: blades (cuts: the teacher does any fine slicing, down onto a tile, away from the body); nail varnish (flammable, strong fumes: no flames, a little at a time, an aired room); glass slides and coverslips (cuts: hold by the edges, report breakages; broken glass in the glass bin). Teacher-note rows, not shown: food dye stains clothes and skin; the microscope lamp gets warm; seedlings and plant waste disposed of as green waste; any stain used on prepared material handled with gloves. Codex to confirm against current NSW Department of Education guidance, including whether students may hand-section with a single-edged blade.
- **Memory aid.** The site's l12 hook, "**Hole for gas, hollow pipe for water, living tube for sugar, hair for soaking.**" (scene 9, after all four structures are observed, and the notes; checked below).
- **Site alignment (L12, kept in substance).** The hook ("A leaf must let carbon dioxide in but not let too much water out; a root must soak up water ... how can a slice under a microscope reveal the answer?", 1); the Before You Read celery (6, attempt 2); the vocabulary for stomata, mesophyll, xylem, phloem and root hair cell (3 to 7); the misconception box ("Plants get all their gases through their roots", crossed in 11b); card 1's method ("cut very thin cross-sections of a stem and root (or use prepared slides) ... Proposed: "a stain such as methylene blue or toluidine blue makes cell walls stand out (iodine shows starch grains)". Minor.
  2. L12 short answer 3 model: phloem "explains an energy-requiring transport of dissolved sugars rather than passive flow".
