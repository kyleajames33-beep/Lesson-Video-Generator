### 1. hook: no right angle
> Here are the two towers, eight kilometres apart. Tower A sees the smoke at sixty-five degrees from the line between the towers, and tower B sees it at seventy degrees. The towers and the fire make a triangle, but none of its angles is ninety degrees, so we can't use sine, cosine and tangent as side ratios directly. Here's the trick. Drop a perpendicular, a line meeting the opposite side at right angles, and the triangle splits into two right-angled **ones**. So why does the sine rule work, and when does the area formula **apply**?

### 2. one height, written two ways
> Label any triangle's corners A, B and C. Each side takes the small letter of the corner opposite it, so side a faces angle A. Now drop a perpendicular from B down to side b, and call its length h. In the left triangle, sine A is h over c, so h equals c sine **A**. In the right triangle, sine C is h over a, so h equals a sine **C**. It's the same h, so c sine A equals a sine C. Divide both sides by sine A times sine C, and a over sine A equals c over sine **C**. Drop the perpendicular from another corner, and b over sine B joins in. That's the sine rule. Each side, divided by the sine of the angle facing it, gives the same **number**.

### 4. finding an angle, in degrees and minutes
> The rule finds angles too, with the sines on top. In triangle ABC, a is nine, b is seven, and A is sixty-three degrees. Find B, to the nearest minute. Sine B over seven equals sine sixty-three degrees over nine, so sine B is seven sine sixty-three degrees over nine, about zero point six nine three **zero**. Inverse sine gives about forty-three point eight seven degrees. In any triangle, the shorter side faces the smaller angle. B faces seven, which is shorter than nine, so B is smaller than sixty-three degrees, and must be acute. A minute is a sixtieth of a degree, so zero point eight seven of a degree is about fifty-two minutes. Your calculator's degrees and minutes key does the same conversion. B is forty-three degrees fifty-two **minutes**. Going the other way, thirty minutes is half a degree, so thirty-seven degrees thirty minutes is thirty-seven point five degrees. Writing forty-three degrees eighty-seven minutes is the **slip**.

### 5. where the area formula comes from
> Now the area. You know the area of a triangle is a half, times base, times height. Take side b as the base. The height is our perpendicular h, and from the right half, h equals a sine C. So the area is a half, times b, times a sine C, which is a half a b sine **C**. Notice which angle it uses. C sits between sides a and b. It's the included angle, the angle between the two sides you **use**. With sides b and c, you'd use angle A instead.
