## Decisions

- **Site sources used:** lesson 06: the Watson and Crick model (sugar, phosphate and base; A with T, C with G; two hydrogen bonds for A-T and three for C-G; covalent backbone; antiparallel strands), the "Build and Pair" activity (template A T C G A gives T A G C T, used as the pairing rule, not re-taught), the replication fork activity ("Why must the original strands separate?"), and the Explain walkthrough's three causes of accuracy (complementarity removes ambiguity, weak hydrogen bonds allow intact separation, each daughter keeps an original strand). The "Beyond the syllabus" box (5' to 3' synthesis, Okazaki fragments, ligase, primase are extension) sets the limit on enzyme detail: enzymes appear only as "jobs" in the limitations scene. Lesson 07: replication happens before division and makes two identical sister chromatids (one sentence in scene 6 linking the model to mitosis).
- **Site gap and route scope.** The site lessons 06 and 07 have no hands-on modelling method; the syllabus verb is "model" (B5-IQ2-01) and the site maps it to Explain in exams. This design supplies a standard school paper-card method (cut nucleotide cards, two colours for original and new strands), which is consistent with the site's DNA Builder and pairing activities. The route title and stop also name **synthesis** models (B5-IQ3-02, "model the process of polypeptide synthesis"), and the route lists site lessons 09 to 11 as candidates. The same card kit models transcription and translation, so both models share one kit and one colour key. **Division** modelling (route stop "replication/division") is not built here: P02 builds chromosomes from pop-beads and runs meiosis, and the same kit runs mitosis; scene 6 links the replicated molecule to sister chromatids only. **DNA forms** (prokaryote and eukaryote representations, B5-IQ3-01) are B6a's; not modelled here. Flag for root: confirm this split (doubt 1).
- **Main teaching move:** one kit, two models, one assessment habit. Build a twelve-base-pair DNA stretch from cards whose base tabs only fit their partner; copy it with a second colour; read the result. Then use the same stretch as a gene: build mRNA on the template strand, run it through a paper ribosome with tRNA cards carrying pop-bead amino acids, and record a codon table. Then assess both models: what they represent well, what they leave out or distort, and one improvement for each limit named.
- **Cause chain taught, in order:** (1) building a model makes you follow the real rules, which is why it is worth doing (1); (2) a fixed colour key lets you track old and new strands (2); (3) tab shapes that only fit the partner model complementary pairing, with two or three notches for the number of hydrogen bonds (3); (4) taped backbones and touching tabs model strong covalent bonds and weak hydrogen bonds, so strands separate without breaking (4, 5); (5) each template fixes its new partner, so both copies match the original and each keeps one original strand (5, 6); (6) the template strand alone is read to make mRNA, with U for T, and the gene is unchanged (7); (7) a ribosome reads three bases at a time and each tRNA links one codon to one amino acid, so the order of codons sets the order of beads (8, 9); (8) a model is assessed by what it represents accurately, what it leaves out or distorts, and how to improve it (10 to 12).
- **Entry knowledge:** B4 (nucleotide = sugar, phosphate, base; A-T and C-G; hydrogen bonds between bases, covalent backbone; antiparallel 5' and 3' ends; template; semi-conservative replication; original blue and new green), B5a (copied chromosome = two sister chromatids; mitosis separates them), B6b (template strand; mRNA built with U opposite A; gene unchanged), B6c (codons of three; ribosome; tRNA with an anticodon and one amino acid; AUG start; stop codons; codon chart; a polypeptide must fold). Year 11 Working Scientifically: models represent part of reality and have limitations.
- **Learner-visible vocabulary (plain meaning at first use):** model (a simplified representation used to explain or predict); represent (stand for); limitation (something the model leaves out or gets wrong). All biology terms are recalled, not introduced.
- **Model sequence (on screen, computed):** coding strand 5' A T G G C A T T T T G A 3'; template strand 3' T A C C G T A A A A C T 5'. mRNA (built on the template) 5' A U G G C A U U U U G A 3'. Codons AUG (Met, start), GCA (Ala), UUU (Phe), UGA (stop). Anticodons (3' to 5'): UAC, CGU, AAA. Product: Met-Ala-Phe (three amino acids). This sequence differs from B6c's worked and attempt sequences.
- **Attempt system (unseen, every condition visible):** a replication model built with one colour of card for both original and new strands; both daughter molecules match the original. Demand: explain why the model cannot show semi-conservative replication, and suggest one change. Answer: old and new strands look identical, so you cannot see that each daughter keeps one original strand; use two colours (original and new). Crossed mistake: "the model is fine because the sequences match" (matching shows accurate copying, not which strand is old).
- **Mistake scene:** none separate. Misconceptions blocked: replication makes two all-new molecules (5, 6, attempt); the original strand is discarded (5); both DNA strands are used to make mRNA (7); the gene is used up (7); a bead chain is a working protein (11); a model is "wrong" because it is simplified (10 to 12: limits are named, not used to dismiss the model).
- **Reuse:** B4's coded `BasePiece` jigsaw tiles, `Strand` ribbon with 5' and 3' tags and `HBonds` (two or three dotted lines), restyled as paper cards on the explainer paper grain (`public/assets/explainer-paper-grain.png`): card body tinted by strand (B4 blue original and green new; yellow RNA and orange tRNA are kit colours for this practical), base letter always printed, tab shape by pair family (B4 rule). B6c's codon chart excerpt and anticodon chip style. `b5x-zoom-helix-v1.png` (real helix, scene 11 comparison) and `m5x-ribosome-v1.png` (real ribosome, scene 10 and 11 comparison). B5a's `b5x-chromosome-duplicated-v1.png` (scene 6 link, small). The explainer kit (`Plate`, `Callout`, `Mark`, `Countdown`, `HandText`, `DrawPath`, `scribbleEllipse`, `Enter`), B6a's `WrongChip`, `GreyTag`, `Cross`, `Cam`. New coded items: the colour-key chip, the method step rail (step 1 to 5, one lit at a time), the record sheet (sketch plus labels), the codon results table, the assessment card (three columns: represents well / leaves out or distorts / improvement), the attempt card. New painted plates: see the Codex asset brief.
- **Why the working cards are coded, not painted:** each card carries a letter and is moved, paired, peeled and taped many times; B4 already taught the jigsaw tile look, and students should recognise the same pieces. Real kit objects (card sheets, a pile of cut cards, scissors, tape, pencil, the paper ribosome, tRNA cards, pop-beads) are painted plates. The paper ribosome and tRNA card plates are blank; code writes their letters.
- **No human figures:** every step is shown as cards moving on a bench by themselves (slide, flip, snap), tape strips appearing, scissors resting beside cut cards. No hands.
- **Safety:** one clause (scissors). No chemical or biological hazard.
- **Author notes (not learner copy):** (a) Notch rule: A tabs carry two bumps, T tabs two matching slots; C tabs three bumps, G tabs three matching slots, spaced so two bumps cannot seat in three slots. In real DNA, pairing depends on hydrogen-bond donor and acceptor positions and on purine-pyrimidine width; the notch count models the bond number, not the chemistry. (b) "Only add new cards at the 3' end" (scene 12) models polymerase direction; the lagging strand and Okazaki fragments stay out (site: extension). (c) Enzyme jobs in scene 11: helicase unzips, DNA polymerase builds new DNA, RNA polymerase builds RNA, aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases load tRNAs. Names are not spoken (B4 and B6c did not require them); a grey tag lists "helicase, DNA polymerase, RNA polymerase" small. (d) "A real gene can be thousands of bases long": typical human protein-coding genes span thousands to tens of thousands of base pairs including introns; coding sequences average about 1,000 to 1,500 bases. Correct as stated. (e) In cells, RNA polymerase opens only a short bubble of about 10 to 20 base pairs at a time; the model unzips the whole gene at once (a further limit, screen tag only). (f) The model's paper ribosome shows one codon at a time; a real ribosome has A, P and E sites and can hold up to three tRNAs at once (corrected 18 Oct, Codex check 73; RCSB PDB 5DOY shows three bound tRNAs). Screen tag only: "real ribosome: up to three tRNAs (A, P and E sites)". (g) Pronunciation: "methionine" meh-THY-oh-neen, "alanine" AL-uh-neen, "phenylalanine" fen-il-AL-uh-neen, "semi-conservative" as written, "five prime", "three prime". Base letters spoken as capitals.

Main question: *How do you build a model of DNA copying and of making a polypeptide, and how do you judge what each model shows and what it hides?*
Entry knowledge: B4, B5a, B6b and B6c as above; Year 11 idea that models have limitations.
Stops after: stating what each piece and colour represents; building a paired, antiparallel DNA stretch whose tabs only fit A-T and C-G; modelling replication by separating at the tabs and adding new-colour cards, and recording two molecules each with one original strand; linking the two copies to sister chromatids; modelling transcription on the template strand with U for T and the gene unchanged; modelling translation codon by codon with tRNA cards and beads to a stop codon; recording a codon, anticodon and amino acid table; assessing each model by what it represents well, what it leaves out or distorts, and one improvement per limit; applying this to an unseen one-colour replication model. No enzyme names required, no Okazaki fragments, no folding mechanism (B7).
Handoff: how a chain of amino acids folds into a protein and becomes a trait (route entry bio-m5-b07).

### 1. hook (a model on the bench)
> This strip of paper cards is a model of twelve base pairs of DNA. It can't do anything a real cell does. So why build it? Because moving every piece yourself makes you follow the same rules the cell follows. In this practical you'll build two models, one of **DNA copying** and one of **making a polypeptide**, and then judge what each one gets right and what it gets wrong.

Visual: the bench strip draws across the centre. The finished coded card ladder (twelve pairs, blue backbones, jigsaw tabs, letters printed) slides on and settles. On "can't do anything a real cell does" the real helix plate (`b5x-zoom-helix-v1.png`) fades in small at the right with a grey "?" between it and the cards. On "follow the same rules" one A card lifts and drops back onto its T partner with a soft snap. On "DNA copying" a small icon of two ladders appears above the bench; on "making a polypeptide" a small bead chain icon appears beside it. The question writes on at the top left: "What does a model show, and what does it hide?" Hold about 2 seconds.

### 2. aim and kit
> Here's the aim: build models of DNA replication and polypeptide synthesis, and assess how well they represent the real processes. You need card in four colours, scissors, sticky tape, a pencil, a handful of pop-beads and three small paper cups. **Blue** card is original DNA, **green** is new DNA, **yellow** is RNA, and **orange** cards are transfer RNAs. The beads are amino acids. Keep the colours the same all the way through, because they let you track old and new strands. The only hazard is the scissors, so cut on the desk.

### 3. step 1: cut nucleotides that only fit one partner
> Step one: make the nucleotides. Each card is one nucleotide: draw a **phosphate** and a **sugar** on the strip, and the tab is the base. Cut the tabs so that **A only fits T**, and **C only fits G**. That shape rule is the heart of the model, because in real DNA only those partners fit and hold together with hydrogen bonds. So give A and T tabs **two** notches, and C and G tabs **three**, one notch per hydrogen bond.

Visual: step rail lights "1. Make nucleotides". The painted pile of cut cards (`m5x-card-pile-v1.png`, item 5) sits at the left of the bench. One coded blank card enlarges at centre; on "phosphate" a small pencil circle draws at the strip's end (tag "phosphate"); on "sugar" a pencil pentagon draws beside it (tag "sugar"); on "the tab is the base" the tab outlines (tag "base"); a bracket over circle and pentagon reads "backbone part". On "A only fits T" an A card and a T card slide together and seat (snap); a C card tries the T and does not seat (small grey bounce, tag "doesn't fit"). On "C only fits G" C and G seat. On "two" two bumps on the A tab and two slots on the T tab pulse, with B4's `HBonds` two dotted lines between; on "three" the C-G tabs pulse with three dotted lines. Tag: "notches = hydrogen bonds: A-T two, C-G three" (B4 recall, grey tag "B4"). Hold about 1 second.

### 3b. make the pieces for the second model
> While you're cutting, make the pieces for the second model too. Cut **yellow** RNA cards exactly like the DNA cards, but write U wherever you'd write T. A U tab gets the same two notches as T, so it fits A. For each transfer RNA, cut an **orange** T-shaped card with **three base tabs** along its bottom edge. Those three tabs are its anticodon. Glue a paper cup on top to hold one bead. You need three: U A C with a red bead, C G U with a gold bead, and A A A with a green bead, matched using a codon chart. Last, cut a **ribosome** shape from card, with a window exactly three tabs wide, so it shows one codon at a time.

Visual: step rail lights "1b. Make RNA, tRNA and ribosome". No hands: the painted scissors (`m5x-scissors-v1.png`) rest beside each piece as it separates along a coded dashed cut line. On "yellow" a yellow sheet (from the card sheets plate) gives up a row of coded yellow cards; one U card enlarges beside a blue T card, their tabs outlined side by side (tag "U: same notches as T"), and the U seats onto an A (snap). On "orange" the painted tRNA card (`m5x-trna-card-v1.png`, Codex item 7) cuts free; on "three base tabs" its three blank tabs outline and coded letters write on them, "U A C", with a small direction tag "3' to 5'" and the bracket "anticodon". On "paper cup" the cup on the plate pulses; on "red bead" a red pop-bead (`m5x-popbead-red-v1.png`) drops into the cup. On "C G U" and "A A A" two more cards slide in beside it with gold and green beads; a small codon chart excerpt (B6c style) rings AUG for Met, GCA for Ala and UUU for Phe as each card is filled (tag "bead = the amino acid for the codon this card fits"). On "ribosome" the painted card ribosome (`m5x-paper-ribosome-v1.png`, item 6) cuts free and three coded yellow tabs slide into its window and fit exactly (bracket "three tabs: one codon"). Hold about 1 second.

### 4. step 2: build the starting molecule
> Step two: build the starting molecule. Tape twelve blue cards into a row, backbone to backbone, in this order. Then pair a second blue strand against it, tab to tab. Draw a five prime to three prime arrow along each strand, and **point the second arrow the other way**, because DNA is antiparallel. The tape holds each backbone together, like strong covalent bonds, while the tabs only **touch**, like the weaker hydrogen bonds between bases.

Visual: step rail "2. Build the molecule". Twelve blue cards slide into a row on the bench, tape strips appearing between backbones (coded tape pieces matching the painted roll); the sequence prints in the tabs: A T G G C A T T T T G A. On "pair a second blue strand" the partner cards slide in one by one and seat: T A C C G T A A A A C T. On "Draw a five prime to three prime arrow" a pencil arrow draws along the top strand, left to right, with "5'" and "3'" tags at its ends (B4's `Strand` arrows); on "point the second arrow the other way" a second arrow draws along the partner strand right to left, tags "3'" left and "5'" right, and the two arrows pulse together (tag "antiparallel: arrows opposite"). On "covalent bonds" the tape strips glow with the tag "tape: strong backbone bonds"; on "touch" the tab joins show dotted `HBonds` with "tabs: weak hydrogen bonds". Hold about 1 second.

### 5. step 3: model replication
> Step three: copy it. Pull the two strands **apart** at the tabs. The tape stays intact, just as the backbones do when the hydrogen bonds break. Now each blue strand is a **template**. Add green cards one at a time, each opposite its only matching partner, and tape the new backbone. You end up with **two molecules**. Each one has one blue strand and one green strand.

Visual: step rail "3. Copy (replicate)". On "apart" the ladder opens like a zip from the left (B4's `Fork` motion), the dotted bonds vanishing pair by pair while the tape strips stay (tag "tape unbroken"). On "template" both blue strands get the tag "template". Green cards slide in from a small green pile, one per base on the template, each seating with a snap; a coded tape strip joins each new backbone. On "two molecules" the two ladders separate along the bench, each with a bracket: "1 blue + 1 green". The colour key's blue and green rows pulse. Hold about 1 second.

### 6. record and check
> Now record what the model shows. Draw both molecules and label the old and new strands. Then read each one against the original. They **match base for base**, because every new card was set by its template. Try forcing a wrong card in: it won't sit flat, so a mismatch stands out. One original strand in each molecule is the **semi-conservative** result. In a dividing cell, these two copies stay joined as the **sister chromatids** that mitosis later separates.

Visual: the record sheet (painted clipboard `m5x-clipboard-sheet-v1.png`, item 12, with a coded sketch drawing on it) in the right zone: two small ladders sketched, labels "old (blue)" and "new (green)" writing on. On "forcing a wrong card in" a green C card tries an A on the template and tilts, not seated (grey tag "mismatch: doesn't fit"; small grey tag "cells also proofread: B4"). On "semi-conservative" the term writes on under the sketch: "each copy keeps one original strand". On "sister chromatids" the two ladders shrink and slide into the two arms of `b5x-chromosome-duplicated-v1.png` (small, grey tag "B5a"), each arm tagged "one copy". Hold about 2 seconds.

### 7. step 4: model transcription
> Step four switches to the second model: making a polypeptide. Unzip the whole gene, and use only the **template strand**. Build an RNA strand from yellow cards, pairing as before, except that RNA has **U instead of T**, so a yellow U sits opposite every A. Then peel the yellow strand off and close the DNA back up. That yellow strip is your **messenger RNA**, and the gene itself is **unchanged**.

Visual: step rail "4. Transcribe". One of the two molecules returns to centre; the colour key's yellow row pulses. On "messenger RNA" the yellow strip gets the tag "mRNA"; on "unchanged" the DNA gets a teal tick "gene unchanged, can be read again". Grey tag, unspoken: "a cell opens only a short stretch at a time". Hold about 1 second.

### 8. step 5: model translation
> Step five: translation. Slide the messenger RNA through a paper ribosome, which shows three bases at a time, one **codon**. Each orange card carries one bead, its amino acid, and three tabs, its **anticodon**, that fit its codon. Match the first card to the start codon, A, U, G. Then match the next, and **clip its bead** to the first. Keep going until a **stop codon**, where no card fits. Then the bead chain comes off.

On "start codon" it drops and seats over A U G (snap, tag "start: methionine"). On "clip its bead" the strip moves one codon left, a second card (gold bead, anticodon C G U) seats over G C A, and the gold bead clips to the red one (small coded peg joint, tag "peptide bond"). The first card lifts away empty. The third card (green bead, anticodon A A A) seats over U U U and its bead clips on. Grey tag, unspoken: "real ribosome: up to three tRNAs (A, P and E sites)". Hold about 1 second.

### 9. results table
> Record the results in a table: codon, anticodon and amino acid, using a codon chart. This gene gives A, U, G, then G, C, A, then U, U, U, then the stop codon U, G, A. That's **methionine, alanine and phenylalanine**: three amino acids joined in order. Three bases chose each bead, so changing **one card** in the DNA can change a bead in the chain.

### 10. assess: what the models represent well
> Now assess the models. Start with what they **represent well**. The tab shapes show why A pairs with T and C with G. The colours show that each new molecule keeps one original strand. The ribosome window shows that the code is read in **threes**, and the orange cards show why transfer RNAs matter: each one links a codon to its amino acid.

Visual: the assessment card writes in the right zone with three column headings: "Represents well", "Leaves out or distorts", "Improvement". On each cue a row writes in the first column with a small pen link to the matching piece on the bench: "pairing rule (tab shapes)" to a seated A-T tab; "one original strand kept (colours)" to the two blue-green ladders; "code read in threes (window)" to the ribosome window, with `m5x-ribosome-v1.png` small beside the paper ribosome (tag "real ribosome"); "tRNA links codon to amino acid" to an orange card with its bead. Hold about 1 second.

### 11. assess: limitations
> Then the limits. Your hands did every job, so the model hides the **enzymes** that unzip DNA, build new strands and RNA, and load each transfer RNA. It's **flat**, so it hides the twist that has to unwind before copying. It's **tiny**, so it hides how long copying takes: real genes run to thousands of bases. Nothing makes new strands grow in the right **direction**, so it hides the rule that cells build only one way. And it never shows a bead chain **folding** into a working protein, so it hides why the order of amino acids matters.

Visual: the second column fills, one row per cue: "no enzymes: hides who does each job" with a small grey tag "helicase, DNA polymerase, RNA polymerase"; "flat: hides the twist that must unwind" with `b5x-zoom-helix-v1.png` sliding in beside the flat ladder; "12 base pairs: hides how long copying takes" with a scale bar that stretches far off screen; "direction not enforced: hides one-way building" with a green card sliding on at the wrong end and a grey "?"; "no folding: hides why order matters" with the bead chain and a dotted fold outline (grey tag "next video"). Hold about 2 seconds (reading hold).

### 12. improving the model
> A good assessment ends with **improvements**. Add new cards only at the three prime end, following each strand's arrow, as cells do. Add labelled cards for each enzyme job. And **twist** the finished ladder to show the helix. Each change fixes one limit you named, which is what makes it an improvement, not just decoration.

Visual: the third column fills opposite its limit, one per cue: "add cards at the 3' end only" (the scene 4 arrows pulse, and a green card is only allowed at the 3' end of a growing strand, tick); "enzyme job cards" (three small blank coded cards labelled "unzip", "build DNA", "build RNA" slide near the fork); "twist into a helix" (the card ladder twists a half turn on the bench). On "fixes one limit" pen links join each improvement to its limit across the card. A crossed chip: "the model is wrong because it's simplified". Hold about 1 second.

### 13. attempt (countdown, 10 seconds)

1. Key: blue = original DNA, green = new DNA, yellow = RNA, orange = tRNA, bead = amino acid. Tabs fit only A-T (two notches) and C-G (three notches): complementary pairing and hydrogen bonds. ## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why build a model: it forces the real rules (1). Why a fixed colour key: tracking old and new (2, attempt). Why tab shapes: only partners fit, as in DNA (3). Why tape and touching tabs: strong backbone bonds against weak hydrogen bonds, so strands separate intact (4, 5). Why both copies match: each template fixes its new strand (6). Why only the template strand and U: transcription pairs with one strand, RNA has U (7). Why the chain has that order: each codon selects one tRNA and its amino acid (8, 9). Why each improvement counts: it fixes a named limit (12).
