1. (13a, calculate and respond, 4 marks.) Six quadrats, each 0.5 m × 0.5 m, at random coordinates in a 900 m² paddock; capeweed counts 3, 6, 2, 5, 4, 1. (a) Estimate the total population, showing working. (b) Respond to the claim that 1 m × 1 m quadrats would make the estimate four times as good.
   Model: (a) Mean = (3 + 6 + 2 + 5 + 4 + 1) ÷ 6 = 21 ÷ 6 = 3.5 per quadrat (1 mark). Quadrat area = 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25 m², so area ratio = 900 ÷ 0.25 = 3600 (1 mark). Estimate = 3.5 × 3600 = about 12 600 capeweed plants (check: density 3.5 ÷ 0.25 = 14 per m², × 900 = 12 600) (1 mark). (b) The claim is wrong: a 1 m² quadrat would hold about four times as many plants, but the area ratio would be four times smaller, so the expected estimate stays about the same; it does not guarantee four times the precision, because quadrat size, placement, number and the plants' patchiness all affect precision (1 mark). Classic mistake crossed out: "quadrat area = 0.5 m²" (gives 6300). Other wrong chains: dividing the counts by the area before taking the mean; multiplying by 900 alone (gives 3150, treating each quadrat as 1 m²).
2. (13c, evaluate, 3 marks.) Native ground orchid, 3000 m² reserve; four 1 m × 1 m quadrats placed wherever orchids were seen, along the track; every orchid touching the frame counted; mean 6 per quadrat; reported 18 000.
   Model (problems with linked improvements, then a judgement): Quadrats were placed where orchids were visible and only along the track, so the sample is biased towards dense, accessible patches and over-estimates abundance; place quadrats at random coordinates across the whole reserve (1 mark). Four quadrats is too few for a patchy species, so the mean is unreliable; use many more quadrats until the mean settles (1 mark). Counting every orchid touching the frame over-counts edge plants; use a fixed edge rule (for example top and left sides only). Judgement: the method is not valid for estimating the reserve's population, and 18 000 is likely an over-estimate (1 mark; any two problems with improvements plus the judgement for full marks). Classic mistake crossed out: "use a bigger quadrat" (does not fix bias or number). Other weak answers: "be more careful counting" (no specific change); "count all the orchids" (impractical and not the method asked about; accept only as a comment that a small reserve could be fully searched).
