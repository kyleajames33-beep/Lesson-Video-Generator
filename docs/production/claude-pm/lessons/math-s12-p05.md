### 2. relative frequency
> Each flip is called a trial, one go of the experiment. The relative frequency of heads is how often heads happened, as a fraction of the trials. So it's the number of times it happened, seven, over the number of trials, **ten**. That's zero point seven. The theoretical probability, worked out from equally likely outcomes, is a half. So the relative frequency isn't the probability. It's an estimate of it, a value from real results that should be close, but isn't exact. And with only ten flips, it can easily be **off**.

### 3. more trials: the line settles
> So let's watch what a fair coin does. A computer flips one a thousand times, and we plot the relative frequency of heads after every flip. In the first ten flips, the line jumps around, because each flip is a big share of so few. After ten, it's at zero point seven, just like our coin. After a hundred flips, it's zero point five four. After a thousand, it's zero point five zero seven, hugging the dashed line at a **half**. Each new flip now changes the fraction only a tiny bit, so the line **settles**. And if you flipped ten thousand times, you might get five thousand and thirty-seven heads, zero point five zero three seven. That's why a large number of trials is far more trustworthy than **ten**.

### 6. is this die fair?
> Here's an exam-style question. A die is rolled a hundred and twenty times, and the results are in the table. Find the relative frequency of a three, and of a six, and compare them with the theoretical probability. The relative frequency of a three is twenty-four over a hundred and twenty, which is zero point **two**. A six is sixteen over a hundred and twenty, about zero point one three **three**. The theoretical probability is one sixth, about zero point one six seven. All six faces land within about zero point zero three of one sixth. A sixth of a hundred and twenty is twenty, and every face came up between sixteen and twenty-four times, only a few either side. With only a hundred and twenty rolls, that's normal variation, so there's no strong evidence the die is **biased**. Dividing twenty-four by six faces, and getting four, is the slip. Divide by the number of trials, a hundred and **twenty**.
