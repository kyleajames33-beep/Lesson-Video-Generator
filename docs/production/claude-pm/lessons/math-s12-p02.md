### 2. favourable over total
> Every marble is the same size and feel, so each one is equally likely to come out. Nothing makes one marble come up more than another. That makes the ten marbles ten equally likely outcomes. The whole chance, one, is **shared** equally between ten marbles, so each marble has one tenth of it. Five of the marbles are blue, so blue gets five **tenths**. An event is the result we're asking about, here, blue. The favourable outcomes are the ones that make the event happen, the five blue marbles. So the probability of blue is the number of favourable outcomes, five, over the total number of outcomes, **ten**. Five tenths simplifies to one half, which is zero point five, or fifty per **cent**. On the chance line, that's right at even **chance**.

### 3. the three-colours trap
> So why isn't it one in three? Because the three colours aren't equally likely. There are five blue marbles but only two yellow ones, so blue comes out more often. The formula only works when you count equally likely outcomes, and here those are the marbles, not the colour **names**. So red is three out of ten, blue is five out of ten, and yellow is two out of ten. Together, that's ten tenths, which is **one**. That's why the first step is always the same. Check that the outcomes you're counting are equally likely, then count them.

### 4. impossible and certain
> What's the chance of a green marble? There are no green marbles, so there are zero favourable outcomes. Zero over ten is zero, so it's impossible. And the chance it's red, blue or yellow? All ten marbles fit, so ten over ten is one, which is **certain**. Every probability sits from zero to one. Close to zero means unlikely, and close to one means likely. So a probability can never be negative, and it can never be bigger than one. Writing thirty, or one point two, for a probability is always a **slip**.

### 5. the complement: not blue
> Now turn the question around. What's the chance the marble is not blue? "Not blue" is called the complement of blue. It means every outcome that isn't blue, here the three red and two yellow **marbles**. That's five out of ten, a half. Blue and not blue never happen together, and between them they cover every marble. So their probabilities add to one. That gives a shortcut. The chance an event doesn't happen is one, take away the chance it does **happen**. One take away a half is a **half**. It works for any event. On a fair die, the chance of a six is one sixth, so the chance of not rolling a six is one take away a sixth, which is five **sixths**.

### 6. when the shortcut saves time
> Here's the shortcut in an exam question. A rideshare app has twelve cars nearby, and it sends one of them at random. Three of the twelve are electric. What's the probability the car is not electric? Each car is equally likely, so first find the chance it is electric, three over twelve, which is one **quarter**. Then the chance it's not electric is one take away a quarter, three quarters, or seventy-five per **cent**. Counting the nine other cars gives nine twelfths, the same three **quarters**. When a question says not, think of the complement first. Writing one take away three, and getting minus two, is the slip. Take away the probability, not the **count**.
