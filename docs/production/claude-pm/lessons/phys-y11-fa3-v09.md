### 2. gravity first
> To answer that, we need to know where the energy comes from. Think about gravity first. Lift a ball off the floor, and you do work against gravity. That work isn't lost. It's stored as gravitational potential energy: energy the ball has because of its height. Let the ball go, and gravity pulls it down. The stored energy turns into kinetic energy as it **falls**.

Visual: board change: `phys-y11-steel-ball-v1.png` resting on `phys-y11-surface-floor-v1.png` in the left half; an amber weight arrow "W = mg" down. On "Let the ball go" the outside-push arrow vanishes; the ball drops; on "turns into kinetic energy" the green bar drains into a blue "KE" bar, a dashed total outline staying the same height.

### 3. a charge in a field works the same way
> A charge in a field works the same way. Take a positive charge near the negative plate, and push it towards the positive plate, against the field. The field pushes back, so you have to do work. That work is stored as electric potential energy: energy the charge has because of where it sits in the field. Now let it go. The field pushes it back towards the negative plate, and that stored energy turns into kinetic energy. So for a positive charge, moving against the field is like going uphill, and moving with the field is like going **downhill**.

### 4. how much? W equals q E d
> How much energy? Work is force times the distance moved along the force. The force on the charge is q E. So the work is q E d, where d is the distance the charge moves along the field. Push a positive charge against the field, and your work, q E d, is stored as potential energy. Let the field move it, and the field does the work, so its potential energy goes down by the same **amount**.

### 5. worked example: the work to push a charge
> Let's try one. Task: a charge of plus two point oh times ten to the minus six coulombs is pushed three point oh centimetres against a uniform field of four point oh times ten to the four newtons per coulomb. Find the work done, and the change in its potential energy. The givens: q is two point oh times ten to the minus six coulombs, E is four point oh times ten to the four newtons per coulomb, and d is point oh three oh metres. So W is q E d: two point four times ten to the minus three joules. It moved against the field, so its potential energy goes up by that **much**.

### 6. W equals q V, and the puzzle solved
> Now back to our puzzle. For a charge crossing the whole gap, E is V over d. So q E d becomes q, times V over d, times d. The d's cancel, and the work is just q V. Pull the plates twice as far apart, and the force halves, but it acts over twice the distance. ### 18. key notes (quiet, hold about 60 seconds)
Heading on screen: "Key notes: what to write in your book".
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

1. Electric potential energy (U) is energy a charge has because of its position in a field. A positive charge gains U when moved against the field (uphill) and loses U when the field moves it (downhill). For a negative charge the directions reverse.
2. Work in a uniform field: W = qEd, where d is the distance moved along the field. Across the whole gap, E = V/d, so W = qV.
3. Moving at right angles to the field (parallel to the plates): no work, no change in U.
4. Energy transformations: a charge released from rest moves to lower U; U lost = KE gained (W = ΔKE), and the total is conserved. Moving against its force, its KE turns back into U.
