## Decisions

- **Anchor: the middle half (d14's frame).** The median splits the 20 values in two; splitting each half again gives four quarters, and the middle two quarters are the "middle half" the frame asks about. The bracket's "?" becomes the IQR, 1.5 hours.
- **One method: halve, then halve again (site lesson13 card 05).** Order; find the median; Q1 is the median of the lower half, Q3 the median of the upper half; for odd n, the median is left out of both halves. This matches common calculators' statistics mode (scene 6 shows Q1 and Q3 on the results screen). A one-line caution: some spreadsheets and apps use a slightly different rule, so their quartiles can differ a little; in exams, use the halves method.
- **Even n first (sleep, 20), then odd n (Batter A, 15) from a stem-and-leaf plot (graphical form).** The odd case's leave-the-median-out step is shown happening: the 8th value (24) lifts out before the halves are halved.
- **The IQR as a width, not an interval.** IQR = Q3 − Q1 = 8 − 6.5 = 1.5 hours: "the middle half of the students' sleep spans one and a half hours". The slip "IQR = 6.5 to 8" is crossed.
- **Range against IQR (`cib3d54f04`).** The range (6.5 h) depends on the two most extreme values, including the 3 h night; the IQR (1.5 h) ignores the top and bottom quarters, so it resists extreme values. Simulation: slide the 3 out to 1 or in to 5.5: the range changes, the IQR doesn't. The contrast: the range is quick and uses the whole span; the IQR describes the typical spread and suits skewed data (the same logic as median against mean, d12).
- **Plain meanings at first use.** Quartiles ("the values that split ordered data into four quarters"); lower quartile, Q1 ("a quarter of the values are at or below it"); upper quartile, Q3 ("three quarters are at or below it"); five-number summary ("minimum, Q1, median, Q3, maximum"); interquartile range, IQR ("Q3 minus Q1: the spread of the middle half").

### 2. halve, then halve again
> Quartiles are the values that split ordered data into four quarters. To find them, halve, then halve again. The median has already split the twenty values into a lower half of ten and an upper half of ten. Now find the middle of each half. The lower half's middle is between its fifth and sixth values, six and a half and six and a half, so the lower quartile, Q one, is six point **five**. A quarter of the values are at or below it. The upper half's middle is between the fifteenth and sixteenth values, eight and eight, so the upper quartile, Q three, is **eight**. Three quarters of the values are at or below it. And the median is sometimes called Q two.

### 3. odd numbers, from a stem-and-leaf plot

That score is the middle, so it belongs to neither half. Leave it out. That leaves seven scores below it and seven above. The middle of the lower seven is the fourth, twelve, so Q one is twelve. The middle of the upper seven is forty-one, so Q three is forty-one. The five-number summary is nought, twelve, twenty-four, forty-one and eighty-**seven**. Including the median in both halves is a common slip, and it shifts both quartiles.

### 4. the five-number summary and the IQR
> Those five values are the five-number summary. The minimum, Q one, the median, Q three, and the maximum. For our sleep data, three, six and a half, seven and a half, eight, and nine and a half. Together they show the centre, the spread, and even the shape. Always write them in that order, smallest to largest. Now the middle half. It runs from Q one to Q three, six and a half to eight hours. Its width is the interquartile range, the IQR, which is Q three minus Q one. Eight minus six and a half, one and a half **hours**. So the middle half of our students' sleep spans just an hour and a half. The IQR is a width, a single number. Writing it as six and a half to eight is a **slip**.

### 5. range against IQR
> So we have two measures of spread from the five numbers. The range is nine and a half minus three, six and a half hours. The IQR is one and a half. Why so different? The range uses only the two most extreme values, and one of them is that three hour night. The IQR ignores the top quarter and the bottom quarter, so extreme values barely touch it. Watch. Slide the shortest night down to one hour, and the range grows to eight and a half. Slide it up to five and a half, and the range shrinks to four. The IQR stays at one and a half the whole **time**. So the range is quick, and shows the full span. The IQR shows how spread out the typical values are, which is why it suits skewed data, just like the median.
