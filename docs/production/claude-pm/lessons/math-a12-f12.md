### 2. what makes an annuity
> An annuity is a run of equal payments made at regular times, with interest compounding on the balance every period. Compounding means the interest is added to the balance, so next period it earns interest too. There are two kinds. In the first, you make equal contributions into an investment account, and interest is added at the end of each **period**. In the second, you invest one single sum, then make equal withdrawals from it. Same size, same gap, the same rate each **period**. That's the pattern to look for.

### 3. what isn't an annuity

But two hundred dollars at the end of every quarter, with interest each quarter, **is**.

### 4. inside one period: the savings account
> Now look inside the savings account. Say it earns six per cent a year, compounded monthly, which is zero point five per cent a month. So each month the balance is multiplied by one point zero zero five, and then the two hundred dollar deposit goes in. Month one starts with nothing, so there's no interest, and the balance is just two **hundred**. In month two, the two hundred earns one dollar, then the next deposit arrives, giving four hundred and one dollars. Month three gives six hundred and three dollars and one **cent**. It's one rule, used again and again. The next balance is one plus r, times this balance, plus **M**. The first deposit earns nothing in its own month, because it arrives at the end, after the interest.

## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why the two accounts are the same kind: both have equal payments at regular times with interest every period (2). Description: Full lesson, notes and practice questions: https://hscscience.com.au/subjects/maths-advanced/year12/financial-mathematics/lesson06.sa.html

A savings account that grows by $200 a month and a $100,000 fund that pays out $1,050.91 a month look opposite, but both are annuities. See what they share: equal payments at regular times, with interest compounding every period. Run the savings account month by month with Aₙ₊₁ = (1 + r)Aₙ + M, see why the first deposit earns nothing in its first month, read a cash-flow diagram, and see why a withdrawal fund follows the same rule as a loan. Match the rate and the number of payments to the payment period. Two exam-style questions at the end.
