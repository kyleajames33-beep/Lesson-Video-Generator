### 2. five jobs, and only one is movement
> The skeleton has five jobs, and only one of them is movement. It supports the body. It protects: the cranium around the brain, the vertebrae around the spinal cord, and the ribs around the heart and lungs. It makes movement possible, because bones act as levers and joints act as fulcrums, the pivots they turn on. It makes blood cells in red marrow. And it stores minerals, mainly calcium. Those last two show that bone is alive. It rebuilds to match its load, which is called **Wolff's law**. Load a bone and it gets denser. Remove the load, as in months in space, and it thins.

### 4. one trade-off, three classes of joint
> Now the joints, where bones meet. Every joint sits on one trade-off: mobility, how far it can move, against stability, how well it resists being forced out of place. No joint can have the most of both. **Fibrous joints**, like the sutures in your cranium, are knitted together by tough tissue and don't move at all, which is what you want around a brain. **Cartilaginous joints**, like the discs between vertebrae, have a pad of cartilage. Each moves only a little, but twenty-three of them in a row let your spine bend and still guard the spinal cord. **Synovial joints** have a fluid-filled gap, so they move freely.

### 6. inside a synovial joint
> Every synovial joint has the same six parts. **Articular cartilage** caps each bone end, so friction is tiny and shocks are absorbed. It has no blood supply, so it heals poorly. The **joint capsule** is a tough sleeve around the joint, and its lining, the **synovial membrane**, makes **synovial fluid**, which oils the joint and feeds the cartilage. **Ligaments** join bone to bone and stop movement in unsafe directions. Tendons, which join muscle to bone, are different. And **bursae** are small fluid sacs that cushion where a tendon slides over bone.

### 7. six shapes, and the hook settled
> Synovial joints come in six shapes, and the shape of the bone ends decides which movements exist. A **hinge**, like the elbow, knee or ankle, bends and straightens in one direction. A **ball-and-socket**, like the hip, moves every way, including rotation. A **pivot** turns around one axis, like the radius rolling over the ulna. **Gliding** joints, between the small bones inside the wrist and the ankle, slide a little. A **condyloid** joint, like the wrist joint itself, where the radius meets those small bones, moves four ways but doesn't rotate. And a **saddle** joint, at the base of the thumb, adds the thumb's reach. So your hip turns because it's a ball and socket, and your knee barely does because it's a hinge.

### 8. the words for movement
> Now the words for movement. Every action is described from the **anatomical position**: upright, facing forward, arms by your sides, palms forward. **Flexion** decreases the angle at a joint, and **extension** increases it. **Abduction** moves a limb away from the midline, and adduction brings it back. Rotation turns a limb around its long axis. Pronation turns the palm down, supination turns it up. **Dorsiflexion** pulls the toes up towards the shin, and plantar flexion points them down. Circumduction traces a cone, like bowling a cricket ball.

### 9. the squat, described properly
