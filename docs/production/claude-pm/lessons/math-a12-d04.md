- **Anchor: music and sound, two log scales you hear (existing painted plates).** Log bases are not abstract here: pitch and loudness are heard on log scales with different bases.
  - **Octaves (base 2), the guitar.** Double a note's frequency and it rises one octave. With x = the frequency ratio (new frequency ÷ the open string's), the octaves climbed are log₂ x. On the A string (110 Hz), x = 2 is 220 Hz (one octave up) and x = 4 is 440 Hz, concert A (two octaves up).
  - **Bels (base 10), the sound level meter.** Make a sound ten times as powerful and its sound level rises by one bel, ten decibels. With x = the power ratio, the rise in level in bels is log₁₀ x.
  - **Ratio 1 is the shared point (1, 0):** the same note climbs 0 octaves, the same power rises 0 bels.
  - The slopes mean something too: on log₂ x the slope is how fast the pitch climbs (octaves per unit of frequency ratio). At x = 4 it is a quarter of its value at x = 1, so each extra hertz near concert A raises the pitch a quarter as much as it does near the open string. That is 1/x at work, and the factor 1/ln 2 sets the scale.
  The anchor appears in scene 1 (narrated), labels the curves and readouts in scenes 2, 3, 6 and 7 (screen tags), and returns, narrated, when the worked example answers its question in scene 9 (log₂ x at x = 4, concert A).

- **Learner question kept (route).** "How does changing the log base change its derivative?" The answer, built in order: every base-a log curve is the natural log curve scaled vertically by the factor 1/ln a (the change of base rule), and a vertical scale by a factor multiplies every slope at the same x by that factor. So d/dx(log_a x) = (1/ln a) × (1/x) = 1/(x ln a). The base sets one number, 1/ln a, and that number explains the steepness (a > e flatter than ln x, 1 < a < e steeper), the sign (0 < a < 1 falling) and the excluded base (a = 1 divides by zero).

## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why the slopes differ at (1, 0): each base-a log is ln x multiplied by its own factor, and a multiplier changes the steepness but not the shared zero (1 to 4). Why the slope ratio is constant: the heights are in a constant ratio 1/ln 2 at every x, and a vertical scale multiplies every rise at the same x by the factor (3, 4).
