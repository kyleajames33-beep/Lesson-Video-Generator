### 3. more extreme, and how often
> A result is more extreme when it's further from the mean. Think about z equals two. Beyond two standard deviations, the rule leaves five percent outside, two point five percent in each tail. So the chance a loaf is at least two standard deviations heavier than the mean is about two point five **percent**. At least two standard deviations away, heavier or lighter, means either tail, so about five **percent**. Less extreme means closer to the mean, so the chance of a loaf less extreme than that, within two standard deviations either way, is the other ninety-five **percent**. Beyond three standard deviations it's tiny, zero point one five percent on one side, or zero point three percent either **side**.

### 4. where the line goes
> So here's the usual line. A result more than two standard deviations from the mean is called unusual, and more than three is very unusual. ## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why further out is rarer: the tails are thin (3). Why less extreme is the rest: the whole area is 100% (3). Why 2 and 3 are the lines: beyond them the rule leaves 5% and 0.3% (3, 4). Why a negative z can still be ordinary: unusual is about distance, not direction (A1). ## Practice questions and model answers

**Attempt 1 (scene 8; pack question 1, original, HSC style, 3 marks suggested, not an official marking scheme).** "A café's espresso shots take a time that is normally distributed with a mean of 28 seconds and a standard deviation of 2 seconds. The owner checks any shot that is unusual (more than 2 standard deviations from the mean). (a) Is a 33-second shot unusual? (b) Is a 25-second shot unusual? Justify each answer with a z-score."
Model answer: (a) z = (33 − 28)/2 = 2.5; more than 2 standard deviations above the mean, so unusual (the owner checks it). (b) z = (25 − 28)/2 = −1.5; within 2 standard deviations of the mean, so not unusual. Classic mistake (crossed): "−1.5 is negative, so unusual".

**Attempt 2 (scene 10; pack question 2, original, HSC style, 2 marks suggested).** "A café's espresso shots take a time that is normally distributed with a mean of 28 seconds and a standard deviation of 2 seconds. (a) What percentage of shots take longer than 32 seconds? (b) What percentage of shots are at least 4 seconds away from the mean, either faster or slower?"
Model answer: (a) 32 s is z = 2; one tail: (100% − 95%) ÷ 2 = 2.5%. (b) At least 4 s away is z ≤ −2 or z ≥ 2, both tails: 5%. Classic mistake (crossed): "(b) 2.5%".
