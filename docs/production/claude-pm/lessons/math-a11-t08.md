### 2. fractions of a turn: the pizza
> First, a fact that holds whatever unit you use. Here's a pizza with a radius of twenty centimetres, cut into eight equal slices. Each slice's angle is forty-five degrees, one eighth of a full turn, or one eighth of a revolution. So a slice gets one eighth of the crust, the curved edge we call an arc. And it gets one eighth of the topping, the shape we call a sector. The whole crust is two pi times twenty, forty pi, so one slice's crust is five pi, about fifteen point seven **centimetres**. The whole area is four hundred pi, so one slice is fifty pi square **centimetres**. In general, arc length over circumference, and sector area over circle area, both equal the angle over one full **revolution**.

### 3. the radian
> Now measure the angle itself with the radius. The size of an angle theta in radians is the arc it cuts off, divided by the **radius**. In other words, how many radius-lengths of arc it cuts off. When the arc is exactly as long as the radius, the angle is one **radian**. That's the meaning of a radian: an arc of length equal to the radius. Since it's a length divided by a length, a radian measure is a pure number. We often say radians to be clear, but the number doesn't depend on centimetres or metres. So it's the same angle on a cake tin, a pizza or a dinner plate. A bigger circle gives a longer arc, but the same **ratio**.

### 4. a full turn is two pi radians
> Now a full turn. Its arc is the whole circumference, two pi r. Divide by the radius r, and a full turn is two pi **radians**. So three hundred and sixty degrees is two pi radians. Halve both, and a hundred and eighty degrees is pi radians. Then one radian is a hundred and eighty over pi degrees, about fifty-seven point three **degrees**. That's the angle the string made on its first lap.

### 5. converting both ways
> So converting is one multiplication. From degrees to radians, multiply by pi over a hundred and eighty. A hundred and thirty-five degrees times pi over a hundred and eighty is a hundred and thirty-five over a hundred and eighty, three quarters, so three pi over four **radians**. From radians to degrees, multiply by a hundred and eighty over pi. Five pi over six radians becomes five sixths of a hundred and eighty, a hundred and fifty **degrees**. A quick check: the number in degrees is always the bigger one. Multiplying five pi over six by pi over a hundred and eighty is the **slip**. You'd get a tiny number.

### 6. exact values in radians
> Exact values work the same way in radians. The special angles are pi over six, which is thirty degrees, pi over four, forty-five degrees, and pi over three, sixty degrees. ### 7. worked example: three quadrants in one expression
> Here's an exam-style one. Find the exact value of two sine two pi over three radians, times cosine seven pi over six radians, plus tangent three pi over four radians. Two pi over three is pi minus pi over three, the second quadrant, so sine is plus root three over **two**. Seven pi over six is pi plus pi over six, the third quadrant, so cosine is minus root three over **two**. Three pi over four is pi minus pi over four, the second quadrant, so tangent is minus **one**. Two, times root three over two, times minus root three over two, is minus three over two. Add minus one, and the answer is minus five over **two**. Making cosine seven pi over six positive is the **slip**.
