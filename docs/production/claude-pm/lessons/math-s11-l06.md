### 2. y = kx, and k
> Let C be the cost in dollars, and w the mass of apples in kilograms. Four dollars for each kilogram, and nothing to start, gives C equals four **w**. So three and a half kilograms costs fourteen dollars, and seven kilograms costs twenty-eight. Every dollar comes from the kilograms, nothing else. When one quantity is always the same number times another, we say it varies directly with it. That's direct variation, written y equals k **x**. The k is called the constant of variation, the fixed number that links the two. Here k is four, four dollars per kilogram. So k is the rate, and it's also the gradient of the **line**. It's y equals m x plus c, with m equal to k and no c at all.

### 4. spotting it
> So how do you spot direct variation? In words, look for no fixed amount, a price for each unit, or the phrase varies directly. Petrol at two dollars twenty-five a litre, yes. A gym with a joining fee, then a weekly price, **no**. In a table, divide each output by its input. If every ratio is the same, it's direct variation. In this table, nine over two, eighteen over four, and twenty-seven over six all give four point **five**, so y equals four point five x. The table doesn't have to show the zero row. But equal differences alone aren't enough, because a line with a fee has equal differences too. Check the **ratio**.

### 5. worked example: k from one pair
> Here's the method on its own. A car travels a hundred and eighty kilometres in three hours, at a constant speed. Distance varies directly with time. Find k, write the equation, and find the distance travelled in five hours. Step one, write the model, d equals k t. Step two, substitute the pair you know. A hundred and eighty equals k times three, so k is a hundred and eighty divided by three, which is **sixty**, sixty kilometres per hour. Step three, write the formula, d equals sixty **t**. Step four, use it. Sixty times five is three hundred **kilometres**. It works backwards too. Two hundred and forty kilometres takes two forty divided by sixty, which is four hours. The slip is dividing the wrong way, three divided by a hundred and eighty. k is output divided by **input**.
