### 2. short, medium and long-term goals
> Start with the goal, not the product. A short-term goal is one you can pay off within about a year, like flights or a new phone. ### 4. when the card wins
> So is a personal loan always better? No. Say you put eighteen hundred dollars of flights on the card, and pay the whole closing balance by the due date. That costs **nothing**, thanks to the interest-free days. Leave it unpaid for thirty days, and it's about thirty-one dollars thirty-three. A personal loan would charge interest from day one, and often an establishment fee to set it up. So for a short-term purchase you can clear in full, a card is the cheaper **tool**.

### 5. what each tool is built for
> Here's how they differ. A personal loan is a fixed amount, for a fixed time, with fixed repayments, at about ten to fourteen per cent. When it's paid, it's closed. A card has a changing balance and interest-free days, but about twenty-one per cent, and its limit stays open after you pay it off, so it's easy to borrow **again**. For a long-term goal like a home, neither fits. That's a home loan, at about seven per cent, usually after saving a **deposit**. And if a purchase can't be cleared in the free days, a card is the dearest common way to pay for it. Saving first, or an emergency fund, avoids interest **altogether**.
