### 5. alive, but almost empty

### 14. key notes (quiet, about 60 seconds)

> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

1. Phloem carries sap (water with dissolved sugar, mainly sucrose) up or down the plant to where it's needed. Stem: outer side of each bundle. Trunk: the inner layer of the bark.

## Numbers or facts to verify

Self-check (2 Nov). The Codex science check must confirm each before voicing.
- **Ring-barking (1, 11).** Removing a complete ring of bark (girdling) removes the phloem (the inner bark) but leaves the xylem (wood) intact; leaves stay green and keep transpiring for weeks; sugars and other substances accumulate above the girdle, which swells (callus and accumulation); the roots are starved and the tree eventually dies. "weeks" and "eventually" are hedged; time to death varies from months to more than a year. Correct.
- **Phloem position (2).** In young dicot stems the vascular bundles are collateral: phloem on the outer side, xylem on the inner side; in woody trunks secondary phloem forms the inner bark. Correct (monocot stems differ; author note in Decisions).
- **Sap contents (2).** Phloem sap is mainly sucrose in water (often 10 to 30 per cent by mass), with amino acids, hormones and ions; some families transport other sugars (raffinose-family oligosaccharides, sugar alcohols). "mostly a sugar called sucrose" is safe. Correct.
- **Direction (2, 9).** Phloem transports sugar up and down; different sieve tubes in one bundle can carry sap in opposite directions at the same time; within one sieve tube flow is one way at a time. Correct (route boundary).
- **Loading is uphill (3), Revision 2.** The narration now names active loading as one mechanism with potato as the example ("In many plants, such as potato") and says "Some plants, including many trees, skip this uphill step" (passive symplastic loaders such as willow and poplar; PMC3375970). Revision 1 note follows. In apoplastic loaders (most crop plants: sugar beet, maize, beans, potato) sucrose in the sieve element and companion cell complex is far more concentrated than in mesophyll cells, and loading is active (proton pumps and sucrose-proton co-transport, using ATP). Some trees load passively by diffusion through plasmodesmata (for example willow and poplar), and some use polymer trapping. The video's "far more concentrated" and "active transport" fit most plants and the syllabus level; the passive loaders are an author note only. Flag for the science check: "Sugar is made in the leaf cells, but the sap inside the phloem is far more concentrated" should carry no "always". It does not.
- **Membrane holds the sugar (4), Revision 2.** Sieve elements keep a functional, selectively permeable plasma membrane, which lets water cross and retains sucrose at high concentration; only a living cell maintains it. The Revision 1 contrast with xylem ("A xylem vessel has no membrane at all ... sugar would just spread out") is removed: xylem carries dissolved substances, and its walls and pit membranes constrain exchange. Confirm the new wording.
- **Mature sieve elements (5).** Lose the nucleus, vacuole (tonoplast), ribosomes and Golgi; keep the plasma membrane, some smooth ER, a few mitochondria and plastids, in a thin layer of parietal cytoplasm. "loses its nucleus and most of its organelles ... keeps its membrane and a thin layer of cytoplasm" is correct.
- **Sieve plates and vessel ends (6).** End walls with pores (modified plasmodesmata widened); sap passes through the pores; on injury, P-protein and callose block the pores within seconds to minutes (screen tag only in Revision 2). Revision 2 says xylem vessel elements' end walls "open into wide holes" (perforation plates, which may keep rims or bars; PMC59064). Correct.
- **Companion cells (7).** Revision 2 says "In active loaders like potato, it makes ATP and uses it to load the sugar". Potato is an apoplastic (active) loader (SUT1 sucrose transporter); confirm. Dense cytoplasm, a prominent nucleus, many mitochondria and ribosomes; connected to the sieve element by many branched plasmodesmata; supply proteins, ATP and other molecules; in apoplastic loaders they take up sucrose and pass it into the sieve element. "makes the ATP, and uses it to load sugar into the sieve tube" is the Year 11 form. Correct.
- **Companion cell death (8).** The sieve element depends on its companion cell; if the companion cell dies, the sieve element cannot be maintained and stops functioning (they develop from one mother cell and function as a unit). "nothing keeps the sieve tube cell alive" is acceptable at this level; flag for the science check (fallback "the sieve tube cell can't be kept working", 3 characters shorter).
- **Xylem energy (8, 12d).** Water movement in xylem is passive, driven mainly by transpiration pull (v12); v09's wording ("the pull comes from somewhere else") is followed, so no driving force is named here. Correct.
- **Direction can change (9).** Seasonal reversal: storage organs (roots, tubers, trunk) export in spring to new buds; leaves export in summer to roots, fruits and storage. Correct.

Metabolic inhibitors (for example cyanide, dinitrophenol) or anoxia applied to source leaves reduce phloem loading and export; water continues to move through xylem passively for hours (though stomata may respond). "For several hours, the leaf stays firm" is plausible; flag for the science check. Fallback stem: "a chemical that stops ATP production in leaf cells".
