Let's build it with a model you can make in class, and watch the real cell beside it.

### 10. why lining up matters
> Why line up in the middle at all? Because each chromosome sits with its chromatids facing opposite poles, a fibre from each side attaches to it. When they pull, one chromatid normally goes each way. If chromosomes were just grabbed from a heap, one cell could get two copies of a chromosome, and the other none.

### 12. what the model can't show
> How good is the model? It shows the order of events, and exactly how the chromatids are shared out. But it can't show what moves them. In your model, something outside pushes the beads. In a cell, the spindle fibres shorten. It doesn't show the coiling, the checkpoints, or the timing either. Real mitosis takes about an hour, and its phases flow smoothly into each other.

### 16. key notes (quiet, about 60 seconds)
Heading on screen: "Key notes: what to write in your book".
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

- Model: two colours (pop beads, pipe cleaners or clay) for the two sets, a long and a short chromosome of each (2n = 4); string loop for the membrane. Model S: identical chains joined at a centromere (4 centromeres = 4 chromosomes).
- Prophase: chromosomes coil and become visible; nuclear membrane breaks down; spindle forms from the poles. Metaphase: fibres attach at centromeres; chromosomes in single file on the equator, chromatids facing opposite poles.
- Anaphase: sister chromatids separate at their centromeres; fibres shorten and pull them to opposite poles (each now a chromosome). Telophase: chromosomes uncoil (DNA easier to reach); two nuclear membranes form; spindle breaks down.
- Cytokinesis (after mitosis): animal cell pinches in; plant cell builds a new wall. Two daughters that normally match the parent: each pole got one sister chromatid of every chromosome.
- Record: a labelled sketch and a chromosome count for each phase. The model shows the order and the sharing, not the force, coiling, checkpoints or timing.

## Numbers or facts to verify

- **Phase events (4 to 8, notes).** Prophase: chromatin condenses into visible chromosomes, nuclear envelope breaks down, spindle forms from centrosomes moving to the poles. Metaphase: kinetochore fibres from both poles attach at each centromere; chromosomes aligned in single file at the metaphase plate (equator), sister chromatids facing opposite poles. Telophase: chromosomes decondense, two nuclear envelopes re-form, spindle disassembles. Cytokinesis follows. Correct at Year 11.
