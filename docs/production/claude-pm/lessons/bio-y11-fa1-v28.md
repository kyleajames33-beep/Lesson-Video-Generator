## Numbers or facts to verify

Self-check (2 Nov). The Codex science check must confirm each before voicing.
- **Phase events (4 to 8, notes).** Prophase: chromatin condenses into visible chromosomes, nuclear envelope breaks down, spindle forms from centrosomes moving to the poles. Metaphase: kinetochore fibres from both poles attach at each centromere; chromosomes aligned in single file at the metaphase plate (equator), sister chromatids facing opposite poles. Anaphase: sister chromatids separate (the proteins holding them are cut; cohesin is cleaved so the sister centromeres separate; Revision 2 speaks of chromatids separating at their joined centromeres, and the site's "centromere splits" wording is logged) and are pulled to opposite poles as kinetochore fibres shorten; each chromatid is then a chromosome. Telophase: chromosomes decondense, two nuclear envelopes re-form, spindle disassembles. Cytokinesis follows. Correct at Year 11.
- **Counts (6, 11, 14).** Metaphase 4 chromosomes (8 chromatids); anaphase 8 chromosomes in one cell (4 to each pole); each daughter 4. Correct.
- **Daughters identical (9, 10).** Each daughter receives one sister chromatid of every chromosome; sister chromatids are copies made in S (rare copying errors not mentioned). Correct.
- **Why alignment matters (10).** Bipolar attachment at metaphase (one fibre from each pole per chromosome) ensures one chromatid goes to each pole; random segregation would give aneuploid cells. Correct.
- **Mitosis takes about an hour (12).** In many mammalian cells mitosis lasts about 30 to 60 minutes; "about an hour" is safe. Correct.
