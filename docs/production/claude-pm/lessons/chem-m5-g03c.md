## Decisions

- **Main question:** *In an equilibrium calculation you have not seen, where is the trap, and what do you do first so it cannot catch you?*
- **Why a practice video, and why these questions:** C6a, C6b, G03a and G03b each teach a method with one example per type. Students lose these questions on a small early step, not on the algebra (research doc, T11, T12, T14, T15 marker comments: moles used as concentrations, mole ratios mishandled, solids included, unjustified approximations, the measured value used as the change). This video gives four harder questions, the last in two parts (five answers in all), and each one hides at least one trap. Every question is solved with the same visible method, one reasoning stage per scene, then the classic mistake for that question is shown crossed out.
- **Trap coverage (learner-visible as the trap checklist, scene 1 and scene 19):**

  | Trap | Where |
  |---|---|
  | Moles given, not concentrations: divide by the volume first | Q1 (2.00 L, including the measured value), Q2, Q3, Q4a (5.00 L), Q4b |
  | A coefficient other than 1 in the change row | Q1 (2 on H2: +0.0800), Q3 (2 on NOCl and NO: 2x) |
  | A species that does not start at zero, so Change = E minus I | Q1 (methanol starts at 0.200 and ends at 0.160: change -0.0400, so the mixture runs backwards) |
  | A solid that must be left out of K | Q2 (FeO and Fe); water as the solvent named on the same card (unspoken chip) and in the decision board |
  | A reversed equation (K flips); doubled (K squares) | Q3 (K given for making NOCl, vessel starts with NOCl: K flips); doubling in the decision board and key note 4 (a C6a callback) |
  | A perfect square hidden in a K question | Q4b (x times x over (0.250 - x)(0.250 - x), which looks like a quadratic) |
  | Tiny K approximation with the 5 per cent check | Q3 (the amount taken off is 2x, 4 per cent: passes, only just); the contrast in Q2 (K = 0.50 is not tiny: dropping x would take 50 per cent) |
  | Find an equilibrium amount and convert back to moles or mass | Q2 (grams of iron), Q3 (moles of chlorine) |
  | Multi-step: find K at one point, then use it | Q4a (K from an equilibrium mixture) then Q4b (same temperature, same K, new start) |
  | Reporting x instead of what was asked | Q4b (x = 0.225 is the change; [NO2] = 0.0250) |

- **Author notes (not learner copy):** (a) **Q1 direction.** The start (Q = 0.200 / (0.100 x 0.200^2) = 50) is above K = 14.6, so the mixture runs in reverse; the video shows this with the sign of E - I and does not ask for Q. (b) **Q1 guard digits.** Rounding the bottom to 0.0110 gives K = 14.5; keeping 0.01098 gives 14.6. The narration says "keep the extra figures until the end" (route stop: retain guard digits). (c) **Q2 FeO.** Wüstite is the stable iron oxide above about 570 C, so FeO(s) and Fe(s) both present at 800 C is fair. The gas count is equal (1 to 1), so Kc = Kp. Literature reduction data (the Fe/FeO line) give CO2 / CO of about 0.4 near 1000 C and about 0.5 near 800 C; a standard-data estimate gives about 0.42 at 800 C. "About 800 C" with K = 0.50 is within that range; science check to confirm. (d) **Q3 NOCl.** A widely used textbook exercise quotes K = 1.6 x 10^-5 for 2NOCl ⇌ 2NO + Cl2 "at 35 C" with a 0.50 mol/L start; that temperature is not physically right (standard data give about 10^-8 at 35 C). A standard-data estimate (delta G 41.0 kJ/mol at 298 K, delta H +77.1 kJ/mol, Kc = Kp / RT) puts Kc = 1.6 x 10^-5 near 420 K (about 150 C). The card therefore says "at a certain temperature" with the chip "illustrative value", not 35 C. The start (2.00 mol in 4.00 L, 0.500 mol/L) gives the same core numbers as that exercise; the wording, the reversed-K twist and the moles demand are original. If the owner prefers no overlap, change the start (see open decisions). (e) **Q3 exact answer.** Solving 4x^3 = 1.6 x 10^-5 (0.500 - 2x)^2 by iteration gives x = 0.00974 mol/L, 0.0390 mol of Cl2; the shortcut (0.0400 mol) is 2.7 per cent high. The 4 per cent check passes the school rule of thumb; an unspoken tag gives the exact value so the video does not claim the shortcut is exact. (f) **Q4 NO2 + SO2.** A standard-data estimate (delta H -41.9 kJ/mol, delta S -20.7 J/(K mol)) gives K about 80 at 733 K; textbook problem sets commonly quote about 85 at 460 C. K = 81.0 from the data is realistic. The gas count is equal, so the volume cancels in Q4a: moles give the same K. This is not said aloud (it would invite skipping the division); the division is shown and the habit kept. In Q4b the volume matters, because the answer is a concentration from a start in moles. (g) **Q4b root.** The negative root, x / (0.250 - x) = -9.00, gives x = 0.28125, more than the start, so it is rejected; this is shown as an unspoken grey tag (C6b taught it). (h) **Pronunciation:** "C O" letters, "H two", "methanol" (the formula CH3OH is on screen only), "iron two oxide" for FeO, "N O C L" letters, "nitrosyl chloride" said once, "N O two", "S O two", "N O", "S O three", "ten to the four", "ten to the minus five", "ten to the minus six", "four x cubed", "sixteen x cubed", "per cent", "nought point". Check in the voiced preview that 0.01098, 0.000800 and 0.0648 are clear.

### 3. q1-initial (divide first, then the equilibrium row first)
> Everything here is in moles, so first **divide by 2.00**. The initial row is 0.100 for carbon monoxide, 0.200 for hydrogen and 0.200 for methanol. The measured methanol, 0.320 moles, is 0.160 moles per litre, and it goes in the **equilibrium row**. Change is equilibrium minus initial: 0.160 minus 0.200 is **minus 0.0400**. Methanol went **down**, so this mixture ran backwards. You did not have to guess that: the subtraction gave you the sign.

Visual: on "divide by 2.00" each start line on the givens card divides with a pen "÷ 2.00" and its result flies into the Initial row: 0.100, 0.200, 0.200 (each on its word). On "0.160 moles per litre" the equilibrium line converts on the card ("0.320 mol / 2.00 L = 0.160 mol/L"); on "equilibrium row" the C6a pen arrow carries it into the CH3OH Equilibrium cell, which ticks "0.160" in pen colour; the other two Equilibrium cells show dashed "?". ### 4. q1-change-and-k (the coefficients, then K)
> Now the coefficients. Each methanol that splits gives one carbon monoxide and **two** hydrogen, so carbon monoxide rises by 0.0400 and hydrogen rises by **0.0800**. Add each change to its start: carbon monoxide 0.140, hydrogen 0.280. K is methanol over carbon monoxide times hydrogen **squared**. 0.280 squared is 0.0784, and times 0.140 that is 0.01098. Keep the extra figures until the end. 0.160 over 0.01098 gives **K is 14.6**.

Visual: on "two" the coefficient chips "1" and "2" fly from the equation strip to the CO and H2 Change cells; CO ticks "+0.0400" and, on "0.0800", H2 ticks "+0.0800" in the made colour with a pen "x 2" arrow from the CH3OH change; the ratio chip "1 : 2 : 1" lies along the Change row. On "Add each change to its start" a pen plus draws between the Initial and Change rows and the Equilibrium row completes: "0.100 + 0.0400 = 0.140", "0.200 + 0.0800 = 0.280" (the sums fade once the row is full). On "squared" the C5 builder writes K = [CH3OH] / ([CO][H2]^2) with the 2 flying from the equation strip to the power; the Equilibrium values drop in with the tag "equilibrium values only". The working writes in beats: "0.280^2 = 0.0784", "0.140 x 0.0784 = 0.010976" (shown in full; the spoken 0.01098 is ringed), a small grey aside on "extra figures": "rounding to 0.0110 gives 14.5", then "K = 0.160 / 0.010976 = 14.6" larger, teal, with the tag "K: no unit written". Trap strip: "a coefficient in the change row" ticks.

### 5. q1-mistake (the classic mistake: the measured value used as the change)
> The classic mistake here is writing the measured 0.160 straight into the **change row**. It is not a change, it is where methanol **ended up**. Put it in the equilibrium row, and let equilibrium minus initial give you the change, sign and all.

### 7. q2-expression (leave the solids out)
> Start with the expression. Iron two oxide and iron are **solids**, so they stay out of K. That leaves K equals carbon dioxide over carbon monoxide, with no powers at all. Carbon monoxide starts at 0.600 over 2.00, which is **0.300 moles per litre**, and carbon dioxide at zero. Nothing at equilibrium is measured, so the change is **x**: carbon monoxide ends at 0.300 minus x, and carbon dioxide at x.

### 8. q2-solve (K is not tiny, and no shortcut is needed)
> So x over 0.300 minus x equals 0.50. K is not tiny, so do **not** drop the x. You do not need to: this equation is already simple. Multiply both sides by 0.300 minus x: x equals 0.150 minus 0.50 x. Add 0.50 x to both sides, so 1.50 x is 0.150, and **x is 0.100**. Check: 0.100 over 0.200 is 0.50.

Visual: the K line "x / (0.300 - x) = 0.50" with the tag "K: no unit written". ### 11. q3-flip-and-table (flip K, then the table)
> Look at which way the equation runs. K was given for **making** N O C L, but this vessel starts with N O C L **splitting**. So flip K first: one over 6.25 times ten to the four is **1.6 times ten to the minus five**, which is tiny. Now the table. The start is 2.00 over 4.00, **0.500 moles per litre**. If x of chlorine forms, then two x of N O C L splits and two x of N O forms, so the equilibrium row is **0.500 minus two x**, two x, and x.

### 12. q3-drop-and-solve (drop the two x, solve the cube)
> K is two x all squared, times x, over 0.500 minus two x, all squared. K is tiny, so very little splits: **drop the two x** from the bottom, and keep the x on top, because that is the answer. The bottom becomes 0.500 squared, **0.250**. On top, two x all squared, times x, is **four x cubed**. Dividing by 0.250 is multiplying by four, so sixteen x cubed equals 1.6 times ten to the minus five. So x cubed is **1.0 times ten to the minus six**, and x is **0.0100**.

### 20. key-notes (reuse take: key-notes; quiet, hold about 50 seconds)
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

- Moles in a vessel: divide by the volume before anything goes into the table or into K, including a measured equilibrium amount. Convert back at the end: mol/L x volume gives mol; mol x molar mass gives grams.
- ICE: the measured equilibrium value goes in the Equilibrium row first. Change = equilibrium minus initial, and its sign shows which way the mixture ran. Only the change row follows the coefficients. Equilibrium = initial + change.
- Leave pure solids, pure liquids and water as the solvent out of K. No unit is written for K.
- K belongs to the equation as written, at one temperature. Reversed: 1 / K. Doubled: K squared. Same temperature: same K, even in a new vessel.
- K and a start (HSC questions are written so you shouldn't need the quadratic formula): equal starts can give a perfect square, so take the square root and keep the positive root. A tiny K lets you drop x where it is taken from a start; check that the amount taken off is under about 5 per cent of the start. Then answer what was asked: x is the change, not always the answer.

Memory aid (large, on the notes screen): *Divide first. E row first. Convert back last.* Second line: *Flip the equation, flip K.* Condition tag: "same temperature, equation as written; 5 per cent is a rule of thumb".
