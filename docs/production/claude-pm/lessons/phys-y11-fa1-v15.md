## Decisions

- **Learner question kept (route).** "How can measured data test the separate effects of net force and mass?" The answer, built in order: the pull on the trolley is only the net force once friction is cancelled, and a nudged trolley moving at constant velocity shows it is (3); one variable changes per series, so each effect is tested on its own (4); repeats give a mean and an uncertainty (5); a straight line through the origin that passes through every scatter bar is consistent with proportionality, and its gradient gives a physical quantity to compare with an independent measurement (6, 7); conclusions are conditional: they hold within the repeat scatter, the instrument limits and the leftover-force bound that were checked, over the range tested, and the friction fix is the main limitation (8).
- **The hook (owner: a useful question).** "If your points miss the line by a little, does that disprove the law?" Four illustrative points near, not on, a line through the origin. Answered in scene 6: "Judge the line by the bars, not the dots."
- **Method (pack method adopted, with one change).** Low-friction dynamics trolley on a track; a string from a force sensor on the trolley passes over a bench pulley to a hanging mass; a motion sensor at the far end records velocity against time; acceleration is the slope of the velocity-time best-fit line (v08). The pack's "correct for measured friction" is done by **friction compensation** (tilt the track until a nudged trolley rolls at constant velocity), which uses v13 as the check and needs no subtraction. Mass measured on a top-pan electronic balance (trolley plus sensor plus carried masses).
- **Why the force sensor (author note c).** The hanging mass also accelerates, so its weight is not the string's pull; measuring the pull on the trolley directly avoids that. Not explained to learners beyond "a force sensor on the trolley measures that pull", which is all the analysis needs.
- **Series and data (pack illustrative data, unchanged means).** Series A: total trolley mass 1.00 kg, net force 0.20, 0.40, 0.60, 0.80 N (±0.02 N), mean accelerations 0.21, 0.39, 0.61, 0.79 m s⁻² (±0.05 m s⁻²). Series B: net force 0.60 N, mass 1.00, 1.50, 2.00 kg (±0.01 kg), mean accelerations 0.61, 0.40, 0.29 m s⁻². Repeats (new, consistent with the pack's means and uncertainty): one table shown, at 0.40 N: 0.34, 0.39, 0.44 (mean 0.39, range 0.10, ±0.05); others in author note a. Series B's 1.00 kg point is the same setting as series A's 0.60 N point and has the same repeats, a consistency the student can spot.
- **Graphs (owner quality bar: cause before rule).** Series A plotted as acceleration (vertical) against net force (horizontal), because the independent variable goes along the bottom; the site's Q3 and the variable-matching task use this orientation (the pack's F_net against a, gradient = mass, is not used: it puts the dependent variable on the horizontal axis). Gradient 1.0 kg⁻¹ = 1/m, giving 1.0 kg against the balance's 1.00 kg; steepest and shallowest lines through the origin that pass through every bar give 1.08 and 0.90 kg⁻¹, so masses from about 0.93 to 1.1 kg. ### 1. hook (how close is close enough?)
> In the force and mass video, every number was perfect. Double the net force, double the acceleration. Real measurements never land exactly on a line, though. If your points miss the line by a little, does that disprove Newton's second law, or not? Let's find out how close is close **enough**.

### 2. the set-up

### 3. cancelling friction (a quick prediction)
> But the track has a little friction, so the string's pull isn't the net force yet. The fix is to tilt the track slightly, so a small part of gravity cancels the friction. How will you know when the tilt is right? **Predict**. Give the trolley a gentle nudge with nothing attached. If it rolls at a constant velocity, the net force on it is close to zero, as far as this check can tell, so friction is cancelled. That's Newton's first law, used as a check. This run is the experimental control. It shows that with no pull, there's no acceleration. Now the sensor's reading is, to that accuracy, the net **force**.

Visual: method rail "2 cancel friction". The string and mass fade (detached). A small friction arrow on the trolley's wheels; on "tilt" the rail tilts by a visibly exaggerated angle (tag "tilt exaggerated") and a small gravity-component arrow along the rail appears, equal to the friction arrow (ticks). On "force" the string and mass return and the force readout gets the tag "= F_net".

### 4. change one thing at a time
> Now, change one thing at a time. In series A, we change the net force, by changing the hanging mass. That's the independent variable, the one we choose. The acceleration is the dependent variable, the one we measure. Everything else stays the same, the controlled variables. The trolley's total mass stays at one point oh oh kilograms, the tilt is rechecked for each load, and the string stays parallel to the track. In series B, the trolley's mass is the independent variable, and we choose the hanging mass so the net force stays at nought point six oh newtons. Changing only one thing keeps the test fair, which helps make it valid, so it tests what it claims to **test**.

## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why compensate friction: otherwise the pull is not the net force (3). Why the nudge test works: a constant velocity means a net force close to zero, as far as the check can tell (3, v13). Why one variable per series: changing both hides the effect (4, v14's A2). Why repeat: one run can't show the spread (5). Why half the range: it is a first estimate of the repeat scatter, judged from the spread, not the whole uncertainty (5). ## Safety facts check (self-check, 2 Nov; Codex to confirm)

- **Runaway trolley.** A school dynamics trolley (about 0.5 to 2 kg) moving at under 1 m/s can still bruise a foot if it rolls off a bench-height track, and the sensor on it can be damaged. Controls: a buffer or end stop fixed to the track; keep feet clear of the bench end; release only when the path is clear.
- **Falling hanging mass.** Slotted masses of tens to a few hundred grams fall when the trolley reaches the end stop or the string slips. Controls: keep the drop short (the mass reaches the floor or a sand tray within a short distance); sand tray or foam pad underneath; feet clear.
- **Set-up (teacher note in the footer).** Clamp the pulley and track firmly so neither can be pulled off the bench; route sensor leads so they can't be caught by the trolley. Codex to confirm against current NSW Department of Education science safety guidance for dynamics trolley investigations; record sources.
