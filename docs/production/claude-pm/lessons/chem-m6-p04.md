**Boundary:** this video prepares students to carry out and analyse the strength investigation; it does not claim that watching it is conducting it. It teaches two pH tests of strength: (1) the matched comparison: a strong and a weak acid (and a strong and a weak base) at the same concentration, 0.100 mol/L, so the pH gap comes from how completely each ionises; (2) the tenfold dilution test: dilute each solution exactly tenfold with volumetric glassware and watch how far the pH moves (strong acid about one unit, weak acid about half a unit; the bases mirror this), which does not depend on trusting the label's concentration. It also covers the variables, validity, reliability and accuracy, and the risk assessment. **Placement limits (playlist order 8):** this video comes before conjugate pairs (chem-m6-c06, order 9), pH calculation (chem-m6-c07, order 10), ICE tables (chem-m6-c08, order 12) and Ka (chem-m6-c10, order 14). So no pH is calculated from a concentration, no Ka or Kb appears, and the half-unit result for the weak acid is explained with C05's particle idea only (diluting a weak acid lets a bigger fraction of its molecules ionise), not derived. The pH meter routine (calibrate, rinse, blot, wait for steady, three readings in fresh portions) is P03's and is recalled in one scene, not re-taught. The particle model of strong against weak, the arrows and the "concentrated weak can read lower than dilute strong" surprise are C05's and are recalled, not re-taught. > Two bottles, both labelled **zero point one zero zero moles per litre**. One's hydrochloric acid, the other's ethanoic acid. You've only got a pH meter. Can you show which acid is **strong**?
