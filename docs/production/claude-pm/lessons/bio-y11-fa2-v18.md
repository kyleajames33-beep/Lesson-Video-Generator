### 6. fats take another road
> Fats take a different road. Fatty acids dissolve in fat, so they slip straight through the membrane's oily middle. Instead, they enter the lacteal, which is part of the lymph system, a second set of vessels. The lymph later drains into a large vein near the heart. So most fat does reach the blood, but not straight away.

## Numbers or facts to verify

Self-check (2 Nov). The Codex science check must confirm each before voicing.
- **Smooth tube surface (1).** Living small intestine about 3 to 5 m long, about 2.5 cm in diameter. Inner area of a smooth cylinder πdL: 3.14 × 0.025 × 4 ≈ 0.31 m² (3 m gives 0.24 m²). "about a third of a square metre" is correct for a length of about 4 m. Calculated by script (2 Nov).
- **Real lining surface (2).** Helander and Fändriks (2014, Scandinavian Journal of Gastroenterology) measured the mean total internal surface of the adult gut at about 32 m², of which the small intestine is about 30 m² (folds, villi and microvilli included). Older textbooks give about 200 to 300 m² ("a tennis court"); the 2014 estimate is now widely cited. 30 ÷ 0.31 ≈ 100 ("around a hundred times"). Skin about 1.5 to 2 m² (v04); 30 ÷ 1.8 ≈ 17 ("more than fifteen times"). Flag for the science check: a source-dependent figure; the grey tag names the date and the older figure.
- **Villi and microvilli (2, 3).** Villi are about 0.5 to 1 mm tall ("up to about a millimetre"); microvilli about 1 µm long, thousands per absorptive cell, forming the brush border. Each villus has a single layer of columnar epithelium, a capillary network and a central lacteal. Correct.
- **Gradient (3).** Blood flow removes absorbed nutrients, maintaining the concentration gradient for diffusion and facilitated diffusion. Correct.
- **Glucose and amino acid uptake (4, 5).** Glucose enters absorptive cells mainly by sodium-glucose cotransport (SGLT1), a secondary active process powered by the sodium gradient that the sodium-potassium pump maintains with ATP; GLUT2 carriers let glucose leave the cell's base into the blood by facilitated diffusion (and GLUT2 can also take part at the brush border after a large meal). Amino acids use several carriers, many sodium-coupled. Learner copy: "active transport, using energy from ATP" (FA1 root decision 14's rule). "Right after a meal ... it moves in, down its gradient": facilitated uptake when lumen glucose is high is supported (author note); flag for the science check. Absorptive cells contain many mitochondria. Correct at this level.
- **Water and ions (5).** Water is absorbed by osmosis following absorbed solutes; iron and calcium are absorbed mainly in the duodenum by carrier-mediated transport. Correct.
- **Fats (6).** Fatty acids and monoglycerides (delivered by bile-salt micelles) diffuse across the brush-border membrane; in the cell they are rebuilt into triglycerides and packaged with protein (and cholesterol, phospholipid) into chylomicrons, too large to enter blood capillaries, which pass into lacteals; lymph drains via the thoracic duct into the left subclavian vein near the heart. Short- and medium-chain fatty acids pass directly into the portal blood (author note): hence "most fat". Glycerol is water-soluble and is absorbed differently (not spoken in scene 6; the narration names fatty acids only). Correct.
- **Liver first (7).** Blood from the small intestine travels in the hepatic portal vein to the liver, which stores glucose as glycogen and processes amino acids, before blood returns to the heart. Correct (v21 owns the detail).
- **Cell functions (8).** Glucose is the respiratory substrate; ATP powers contraction and active transport. Amino acids are assembled into proteins on ribosomes, including enzymes, carrier proteins and the contractile proteins (actin, myosin) of muscle fibres. Fatty acids and glycerol form phospholipids (membranes) and triglycerides (energy store). Iron is part of haemoglobin, which carries oxygen in red blood cells. Correct.
- **Elimination and excretion (9).** Elimination (egestion) removes undigested material as faeces; excretion removes metabolic wastes (urea by the kidneys, carbon dioxide by the lungs). Faeces also carry bile pigments, an excretory product (site error 2; author note): learner copy in 10d says "mostly material that never entered your cells". Correct.
- **Coeliac disease (10a).** An immune reaction to gluten causes villous atrophy, reducing absorptive surface (and brush-border enzymes); iron, calcium, folate and fat-soluble vitamin deficiencies are common; a strict gluten-free diet allows the villi to recover. Correct.
- **Time-sensitive facts spoken.** None (the 2014 surface figure is a published measurement, dated on screen).
