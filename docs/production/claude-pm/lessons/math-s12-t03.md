### 2. Pythagoras or trigonometry?
> The height and the ground meet at a right angle, so there's a right-angled triangle. Its two shorter sides are forty and seventy-five metres. For the straight-line distance, we know two sides and want the third, and no angle is involved. That's a job for Pythagoras, from Year eleven. The hypotenuse squared is forty squared plus seventy-five squared, which is seven thousand two hundred and twenty-five. Its square root is eighty-five **metres**. Adding forty and seventy-five is the slip. Square, add, then square **root**. When an angle is given or wanted, that's when we use sine, cosine or **tangent**.

### 3. angle of elevation
> Now the angle. Stand at the pad and look straight ahead, along the level ground. That level line is the horizontal. Now tilt your gaze up to the drone. The angle you turn through, up from the horizontal, is the angle of **elevation**. So always draw the horizontal first, and measure from it, never from the vertical. Here the height is opposite and the ground distance is adjacent, so tangent. Inverse tangent of forty over seventy-five is twenty-eight point zero seven degrees, which is twenty-eight degrees four **minutes**.

### 4. angle of depression
> Now look the other way, from the drone's camera down to the pad. Draw a horizontal at the drone, too. The angle down from that horizontal to the line of sight is the angle of **depression**. Here's the useful part. The two horizontals are parallel, and the line of sight crosses both, making a Z shape. So the two angles are equal, alternate angles, and both are twenty-eight degrees four minutes. That means you can always move an angle of depression down to the bottom of the triangle, and use it **there**. Notice it sits outside the triangle at the top. The angle inside the triangle at the top is ninety degrees minus it.

### 5. worked example: from a cliff
> Here's a worked example. From the top of a cliff eighty metres high, a boat is seen at an angle of depression of twenty-four degrees. Find the horizontal distance from the foot of the cliff to the boat. First, move the twenty-four degrees down to the boat's corner. From there, the cliff is opposite and the distance is adjacent, so tangent. Tangent twenty-four degrees is eighty over d, so d is eighty divided by tangent twenty-four degrees, a hundred and seventy-nine point six eight **metres**. Leaving the angle at the top, and multiplying eighty by tangent twenty-four degrees, is the slip. That gives thirty-five point six two metres, far too **short**.
