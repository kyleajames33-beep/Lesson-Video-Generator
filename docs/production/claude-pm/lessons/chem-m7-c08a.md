### 2. dehydration: take out water, make a double bond
> First, **dehydration**, which means removing water. The O H leaves from one carbon, and a hydrogen leaves from the carbon next door. Together, they make a water molecule. The two carbons, each now one bond short, form a **double bond**. So ethanol gives ethene, and water. The conditions go on the arrow: concentrated sulfuric acid, or concentrated phosphoric acid, at about one hundred and **seventy** degrees.

### 19. key notes (quiet, hold about 60 seconds)
Heading on screen: "Key notes: what to write in your book".
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

1. Dehydration (removes H₂O; the OH and an H from the next carbon leave, a C=C forms): CH₃CH₂OH → CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O; conc. H₂SO₄ or conc. H₃PO₄, about 170 °C. It is hydration reversed (hydration: steam, H₃PO₄, about 300 °C, high pressure). Butan-2-ol gives but-2-ene (usually major) and but-1-ene.

## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why dehydration makes a C=C: the two carbons are each one bond short after OH and H leave (2). Why concentrated acid and not dilute: removing water shifts the equilibrium to the alkene, and plenty of water shifts it to the alcohol (3). Why two alkenes from butan-2-ol: the H can leave from either neighbour (4). Why water forms in HX substitution: the OH joins the H of HX (5). Why reflux: volatile chemicals drip back instead of escaping (5, c06 recall). How to tell substitution from addition: the starting molecule and whether water forms (6).
