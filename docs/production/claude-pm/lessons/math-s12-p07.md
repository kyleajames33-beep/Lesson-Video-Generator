### 2. a Venn diagram, overlap first
> Each question is an attribute, something a student either has or doesn't. A Venn diagram draws the whole group as a rectangle, with a circle for each attribute. The circles overlap, so there are four regions: lunch only, recess only, both, and neither. Start with the overlap. Twelve students bought both, so twelve goes in the **middle**. Now, the forty-five who bought lunch include those twelve. So lunch only is forty-five take away twelve, thirty-**three**. Recess only is thirty take away twelve, **eighteen**. That's sixty-three students inside the circles. Everyone else bought neither, eighty take away sixty-three, which is **seventeen**. And the four regions add back to eighty.

### 3. reading probabilities off the regions
> If one student is picked at random, each region's count over eighty is a probability. Recess only is eighteen over eighty, nine **fortieths**. Neither is seventeen **eightieths**. At least one means one or both, so all three regions inside the circles, sixty-three **eightieths**. And exactly one means one but not both, the two only regions, thirty-three plus eighteen, fifty-one **eightieths**. Mixing up at least one and exactly one is the classic trap, so ask, does both **count**?

### 4. the same counts in a two-way table
> A two-way table holds the same information as a grid. Lunch, yes or no, goes down the side, and recess, yes or no, goes across the top, with totals in the margins. You can build it straight from the survey. Put in the twelve for both, the totals forty-five, thirty and eighty, then fill each gap by **subtraction**. Lunch and no recess is forty-five take away twelve, thirty-three. No lunch is eighty take away forty-five, thirty-five. Recess and no lunch is thirty take away twelve, eighteen. And no to both is thirty-five take away eighteen, seventeen. The four inside cells are exactly the four regions of the Venn **diagram**. Every row and column adds to its total, and that's your **check**. It works the other way too. If a question gives you a table, the four inside cells give you the four regions of a Venn **diagram**.

### 5. of those who: one row only
> Here's an exam-style question. A survey asked a hundred and fifty people about exercise and diet. The table shows the four counts. Find the probability that a person chosen at random exercises regularly. Then, of the regular exercisers, what fraction eat healthily? First, fill the totals. The regular row is fifty-five plus twenty-five, eighty, so the probability is eighty over a hundred and fifty, eight **fifteenths**. Now, of the regular exercisers means we only look at that row. Fifty-five of those eighty eat healthily, so it's fifty-five over eighty, eleven **sixteenths**. That's a different answer from fifty-five over a hundred and fifty, so always check which group the question is about. Using a hundred and fifty underneath is the slip. Of those who means divide by that row's **total**.
