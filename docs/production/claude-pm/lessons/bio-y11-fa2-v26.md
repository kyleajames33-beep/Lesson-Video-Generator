### 3. the change, and the detector
> The brain. The skin felt the cold, but it didn't decide anything. Let's name the parts. The change itself, a fall in body temperature, is the stimulus. It detects the change in temperature, not what the muscles are **doing**.

### 4. the decider
> The brain region that decided is the hypothalamus, a small region at the base of the brain. It's the control centre. It receives the signals, compares them with a normal level, often called the set point, and decides whether to respond, and how strongly. That comparison is the key job. A control centre isn't just a wire passing the message on. A slight chill gets a small response. A big drop gets a big **one**.

### 6. the links

A change has to be detected before it can be judged, and judged before anything **acts**.

### 12. key notes (quiet, about 60 seconds)
Heading on screen: "Key notes: what to write in your book".
> Here are the key notes. Pause the video if you need more time to copy them.

- Stimulus: a change in a condition (body temperature falls). Receptor: a cell or structure that detects the change (temperature receptors in the skin and brain).
- Control centre: receives signals, compares them with a normal level (set point) and decides whether and how strongly to respond (hypothalamus).
- Effector: a muscle or gland that carries out the action (skeletal muscles; muscle in skin arterioles). Response: the action and its effect (shivering makes heat; narrowed vessels lose less heat).
- Links carry information (nerve impulses: fast; hormones in blood: slower). Order: detect, then decide, then act. Break any part or link and there is no response.
- Structures vary between systems (one structure can do several jobs). Describe: all five, in order, each job and its real structure.

Memory: Detect, decide, do.
