### 2. the array: one stage across, one down
> A two-stage event is one thing happening, then another. Here, a main is picked, then a drink. An array puts one stage down the side and the other across the top. The mains go down the side, and the drinks go across. Every cell is where a row meets a column, so every cell is one lunch. Three rows of four cells is twelve, with none missed and none **twice**. Because both picks are random, each lunch is equally likely, so we can count. What's the chance of a pie lunch? The pie row has four cells, so it's four out of twelve, which is one **third**.

### 4. reading "and" and "or" off an array
> Here's an exam-style example. A fair die is rolled, and a fair coin is tossed. Find the probability of a head and an even number. Then find the probability of a tail or a number less than three. The array has two rows, head and tail, and six columns, so two times six is twelve equally likely outcomes. A head and an even number means the head row, under two, four or six. That's three cells, so three twelfths, which is a **quarter**. Now, a tail or less than three. Or means either one, or both. Light the whole tail row, that's six cells, then the cells under one and two, that's four more, but two of those are already lit. So count the lit cells once. That's eight cells, eight twelfths, which is two **thirds**. Adding six and four, and writing ten twelfths, is the slip. The two tail cells under one and two were counted **twice**.

### 5. with replacement: every marble gets a name
> Back to the bag of three blue and two red marbles. Take one, note its colour, put it back, and take another. That's called with replacement, because the first marble is put back. So the second draw starts with all five marbles again. The four colour pairs aren't equally likely, but the five marbles are. So give every marble its own name, B one, B two, B three, R one and R two, and count pairs of marbles. Five first draws, each with five second draws, makes twenty-five equally likely **pairs**. Both blue fills a block of three by three, nine cells, so nine twenty-**fifths**. Both red is two by two, four twenty-fifths. That's why the four colour pairs, blue blue, blue red, red blue and red red, aren't equally likely. Blue blue is nine of twenty-five, red red is only **four**.
