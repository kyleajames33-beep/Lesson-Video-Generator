- **Author notes (not learner copy):**
  (a) Symmetry: if f is odd and integrable on [−a, a], ∫₋ₐ⁰ f(x) dx = −∫₀ᵃ f(x) dx (substitute x = −u), so the total is 0; if even, the halves are equal. ### 2. odd functions cancel

### 5. the trapezoidal rule, from shared edges
> If there's no primitive and no symmetry, we estimate, and trapezia gave the best estimates in the strips video. Each trapezium's area is half the strip width, times its two heights added together. Now add four trapezia, side by **side**. Each inside height is an edge of two trapezia, so it's counted **twice**. The two end heights belong to one trapezium each, so they're counted once. That gives the trapezoidal rule: h over two, times the first height, plus two times every inside height, plus the last **height**. Here h is the strip width, b minus a, over n, and n strips need n plus one heights.

## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why an odd integral is 0: the half-turn maps the graph to itself, so strips at ±x are equal and opposite (2). Why an even integral doubles: the mirror makes the halves equal (3). Why a mixed integrand splits: the sum rule plus each part's symmetry (4). Why the interval must be symmetric: the pairing needs a partner for every strip (4). Why inside heights count twice: two trapezia share each inside edge (5). Why five values make four strips: each strip needs two edges, and neighbours share one (5, 6). Why the estimate is too big: concave up along the whole interval puts every chord above the curve (7). Why a tool's answer needs rewriting: notation differs and constants or mod signs may be missing (8). Why a tool's beyond-course answer can be trusted: differentiating returns the integrand, and other methods agree (9).
- **Misconceptions addressed:** "odd means zero on any interval" (4); "zero integral means zero area" (4); "the whole integrand must be odd" (4; feedback 1, crossed); "five values, five strips" (6); "forget to double" (5; feedback 2, crossed); "concave up always means over" (7); "log on a tool means base 10" (8); "a tool's answer is the finished answer" (8).
- **One idea at a time:** one symmetry per scene (2, 3), then the split (4); one stage per cue in the rule's build (5); one example per scene from 6 on; scene 9 takes its two tool examples in turn and clears the first before the second.

## Memory aid check

- **"Odd cancels, even doubles (only from −a to a)."** Meaning, as said in scene 10: over a symmetric interval, an odd integrand's halves cancel and an even integrand's halves are equal, so the integral is twice one half. Assumptions checked (author note a): the interval is symmetric (stated in the aid itself); the integrand is integrable on it (all course examples are continuous); the result is a signed integral (key note 1 says so). Assumption checked: the strips have equal width h (stated with the rule). It helps a new case: any table of equally spaced readings, as in attempt 2.
