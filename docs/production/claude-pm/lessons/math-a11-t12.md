### 2. flip the circle
> Take a circle of radius r, centred at the origin, and the point x, y where the angle's ray meets it. Earlier, cosine theta was x over r, sine theta was y over r, and tangent theta was y over **x**. Flip each one. Secant theta is r over x, cosecant theta is r over y, and cotangent theta is x over **y**. For an acute angle, x is the adjacent side, y is the opposite, and r is the hypotenuse, so these match the triangle. Try the point minus three, four, on a circle of radius five. Secant is five over minus three, which is minus five over three. Cosecant is five over four, and cotangent is minus three over **four**. The radius is never negative, so each flip takes its sign from x or y, exactly like its partner. Secant has cosine's sign, and cosecant has **sine's**.

### 3. the reciprocal identities, and what they exclude
> An identity is an equation that's true for every angle where both sides exist. The angles where it fails are **excluded**, left out. Now compare the pairs. Secant is r over x, and cosine is x over r, so secant theta equals one over cosine theta. But only where cosine isn't zero. Cosine is zero where x is zero, at ninety and two hundred and seventy degrees, and every half turn from there. That's the floodlight's beam at ninety degrees, never **landing**. In the same way, cosecant theta is one over sine theta, except where sine is zero, at zero, a hundred and eighty and three hundred and sixty **degrees**. Cotangent theta is one over tangent theta, but here's the catch. One over tangent needs tangent to exist, and to not be zero. So this identity fails wherever x or y is zero, at every multiple of ninety **degrees**.

### 4. tan = sin/cos and cot = cos/sin
> Two more identities. Sine over cosine is y over r, divided by x over r. The r's cancel, leaving y over x, which is tangent. So tangent theta equals sine theta over cosine theta, except where cosine is **zero**. And cosine over sine is x over y, so cotangent theta equals cosine theta over sine theta, except where sine is **zero**. Now look at ninety degrees. The point is zero, r. Cotangent is x over y, zero over r, so cotangent ninety degrees is zero, and cosine over sine agrees. But one over tangent ninety degrees has no meaning, because tangent ninety degrees doesn't exist. So write the exclusions for each identity, not just for each ratio. Saying cotangent ninety degrees is undefined is the **slip**.
