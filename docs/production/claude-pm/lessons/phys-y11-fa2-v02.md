## Decisions

- **Learner question kept (route).** "How can a rope and spring model particle motion and energy transfer?" The answer, built in order: a claim needs measured evidence (1); the question and a hypothesis with a reason (2); the spring models both wave types and the rope models transverse waves, so each is chosen for what it shows (3); the variables (4); a fixed camera above a scale records the marked coil's position frame by frame (5); the transverse run's trace goes across and comes back (6); the longitudinal trace goes along and comes back (7, 8); in every run the largest motion lines up with the source's motion, and the final position matches home within the reading uncertainty, while the disturbance reaches the far end (9); the pulse shrinks along the spring because the floor takes energy (10); the model's limits and improvements (11); risks (12).
- **The hook (v01's claim, made testable).** "v01 said the tape comes back home. Will it, exactly?" 3 s prediction: "exactly home?", "a little forward?", "carried along?". The data answer it: home within ±1 cm in all six runs (9). The point: "it looks like it comes back" becomes a measurement with an uncertainty.
- **Method (site activity 1, extended).** A metal spring stretched to 4.00 m along a smooth floor; its far end tied low to a retort stand's rod; its near end on a wooden handle. Purple tape on the coil at the 2.00 m mark (the marked coil) and a second tape at 3.80 m (to show the disturbance arrives). A floor grid of tape at 0.10 m squares under the spring, in the same plane, as the scale. A phone fixed on a tall tripod directly above the 2.00 m mark, filming at 120 frames per second. Transverse runs: the handle moves 20 cm sideways and back, once, in about a quarter of a second. Longitudinal runs: the handle moves 20 cm along the spring and back, once. Each frame, the tape's position is read against the grid. Revision 2 (review finding 1): one camera view covers only about 1.6 m of floor, so the 3.80 m tape and the 1.00 m and 3.00 m marks are each filmed in a separate repeat run with the tripod moved directly over that mark (same height, same frame rate, same handle movement). The rope (a long cotton rope, the same length) is shown only as a model choice in scene 3: it shows transverse waves clearly, but its pieces cannot be seen squeezing, so it cannot model a longitudinal wave.
- **Illustrative data (generated, labelled).** Reading uncertainty ±1 cm (positions read to the nearest centimetre from a slightly blurred tape). Transverse, three trials: largest sideways motion 15, 14, 16 cm (mean 15 cm, range 2 cm, ±1 cm); largest along motion 0, 1, 1 cm; final offset from home 0, 1, 0 cm. Longitudinal, three trials: largest along motion 12, 13, 11 cm (mean 12 cm); largest sideways motion 1, 0, 1 cm; final offset 0, 0, 1 cm. Trend (transverse, means): largest sideways motion 17 cm at 1.00 m, 15 cm at 2.00 m and 12 cm at 3.00 m. Said aloud in scene 6: "These numbers are illustrative, made up to show the method, not real results." Every table, trace and readout carries the grey tag "illustrative data".
- **Representations (`ci0fca96de`, the "models" part left by FA1 v24).** The physical models (spring, rope) with what each can and cannot show; a path trace of the tape's positions (one dot per frame, in plan view); a results table with units in the headings. Path traces replace graphs against time, which v06 owns (open decision 2).
- **Analysis (relate to energy transfer).** In transverse runs the largest motion is across the spring, while the along motion (0 to 1 cm) is at the ±1 cm reading limit; in longitudinal runs it is along the spring, while the across motion is at the reading limit. Revision 2 (review finding 2): no ratio is claimed, because the small component is not resolved at ±1 cm (author note c). Every final offset is zero within ±1 cm, while the disturbance travels 4.00 m and the 3.80 m tape moves after it. So the tape oscillates about home, across or along the direction of energy transfer, and is not carried. The data support the hypothesis; they do not prove it for every medium (FA1 v24's "support, not prove").
- **The trend (`ci7afa7b23` applied).** The pulse's sideways motion shrinks from 17 to 12 cm between 1.00 m and 3.00 m. Cause: friction between the coils and the floor turns some of the pulse's energy into heat at each step, so less is passed on. This is also why the far end must be close enough for the pulse to arrive.
- **The model's limits (`cibc9c30ac`).** (1) The spring's coils are joined, but air molecules are not: they pass the motion on by colliding, and they also move randomly and fast all the time; the spring models the average back-and-forth motion only. (2) Floor friction drains energy (the trend). (3) A single pulse is not a continuous wave. (4) The coils are huge and slow, which is why we can see them: this is the model's strength. Improvements: hang the spring from long threads to remove floor friction; keep the camera fixed and square to the floor, with the scale in the spring's plane; use a higher frame rate to sharpen the tape.
- **Prediction 2 (3 s, scene 7).** "Push and pull the handle: what shape will the tape's trace make?" Along the spring and back (8).
- **Risk assessment (`cif90164e3`, partial; site has no risk line).** Three spoken rows, hazard, harm and control: (1) the stretched spring released (it snaps back, tangles and can strike hands or face: hold both ends, and walk the ends together before letting go); (2) a spring or rope across the floor (a trip: set up in a clear area away from doors and walkways, and walk around it, not over it); (3) the rope's moving end and the spring's handle (whipping into eyes or faces: keep movements small, and stand to the side, clear of the rope). Teacher notes, not shown: overstretching a metal spring permanently kinks it (equipment damage); only the spoken row is shown; earlier rows collapse to one-word grey chips (FA1 v24).

### 2. question and hypothesis

### 3. choosing the models

## Teaching checks

- **Does it explain causes?** Why measure: a look is not evidence (1). Why the hypothesis has a reason: the neighbours pull the coil back (2). Why the spring over the rope: only the spring can show both types (3). Why each control: so only the handle's direction changes (4). Why a fixed camera and a scale in the spring's plane: every frame read the same way (5). Why "home within ±1 cm" counts as home: the reading uncertainty (5, 8, 9). Why the pulse shrinks: friction turns energy into heat at each step (10). Why the spring is only a model: its coils are joined; air molecules collide and move randomly (11). Why each risk control: each is tied to its harm (12).
- **Misconceptions addressed:** a look is evidence (1, 5); a large motion means the coil travelled (feedback 1 crossed); a model is the real thing (11, feedback 2 crossed); a fit or a match proves a hypothesis (9, crossed); predictions reported as observations (every data item tagged illustrative; the hypothesis tagged "prediction" until the data support it).
- **One idea at a time:** the question (2), the model (3), the variables (4), the recording (5), one run (6), the second run and repeats (7, 8), the analysis (9), the trend (10), the limits (11), the risks (12).
- **Video makes it easier:** the student watches dots accumulate into a trace that goes out and comes back (6, 8), sees all six final dots land inside the uncertainty ring (9), watches the pulse's height shrink along the spring with friction marks under it (10), and watches unjoined air dots jitter beside the joined coils (11).
- **Response opportunity:** two 3 s predictions (1, 7); two unseen HSC-style tasks, 10 seconds each with the full data on the card, nothing answer-bearing. One crossed mistake each; feedback about 27 s and 26 s.
- **Avoided double teaching:** v01's wave types and comparison rule are recalled in the hypothesis; variables (v10), mean, range and half-range (v24) are recalled with a phrase each.
- **Syllabus coverage:** `cib47003d0`: conduct (3 to 8, 12), model the oscillation of particles in transverse and longitudinal waves (3, 6, 8, 11) and relate it to the direction of energy transfer (6, 9, 10, A1). FA0 as the placement table.
- **Out of scope by design:** wave properties (v03, v04); speed (v05); graphs against time (v06); standard deviation (fa0-v05).

## Safety facts check (self-check, 2 Nov; Codex to confirm)
