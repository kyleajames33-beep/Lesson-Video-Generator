## Decisions

- **Route packUpdate applied in full.** "Conduct the named-source investigation and assess mechanisms/effects and evidence quality. Classroom traces are not personal diagnosis." (1) Three named, retrievable sources, each checked by the designer on 3 Nov (see Source facts check): **S1** Diabetes Australia, "Type 1 diabetes" and "Type 2 diabetes" (two pages of one publisher, diabetesaustralia.com.au; no update date shown; treated as one source card with two pages); **S2** Australian Institute of Health and Welfare (AIHW), *Diabetes: Australian facts*, summary (aihw.gov.au, "Last updated: 27 May 2026"); **S3** The Diabetes Control and Complications Trial Research Group. "The effect of intensive treatment of diabetes on the development and progression of long-term complications in insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus." *New England Journal of Medicine* 1993; 329(14): 977-86 (peer-reviewed randomised controlled trial; PMID 8366922; abstract checked). (2) An actual **evidence table** builds through scenes 3 to 9 (criterion | type 1 | type 2 | source) and is completed in scene 11. (3) Evidence quality is assessed for each source (scene 10) and in the conclusion (11). (4) "Classroom traces are not personal diagnosis": scene 1 asks about Person B's pattern without diagnosing B ("that's the question behind diabetes"), attempt 1 asks which pattern "fits" each type using constructed data, and a one-row screen note (scene 10) says the sources describe diabetes in general, that one graph never diagnoses anyone, and that decisions are made with a person's own health team.
- **Why these three sources.** They differ in author, purpose, audience, date and kind of evidence, so the three checks give three different answers. S1 is Australian, current in its site footer but undated on the page, written for the public by a charity that also advocates (purpose); it carries the mechanisms and the signs. S2 is a government statistics agency, dated 2026, whose survey measured blood (HbA1c) rather than relying only on what people reported; it carries the scale and long-term outcomes. S3 is a randomised trial, the only source here that tests cause, but from 1993 and in type 1 only. S1 and S2 do not depend on S3, and S3's result fits S1's account of long-term damage (agreement across independent kinds of evidence).
- **What each source is used for (claims only as verified).** S1 type 1 page: "the body's own immune system is activated to destroy the beta cells in the pancreas"; glucose "cannot enter the body's cells"; signs "excessive thirst and urination, unexplained weight loss, weakness and fatigue and blurred vision"; "managed with insulin injections several times a day or the use of an insulin pump"; "not linked to modifiable lifestyle factors". S1 type 2 page: "the body becomes resistant to the normal effects of insulin"; the pancreas "responds by producing greater and greater amounts of insulin"; "develops over a long period of time (years)"; the insulin-producing cells "wear themselves out"; "Diabetes runs in the family" and modifiable risk factors (high blood pressure, being overweight, not being active enough, poor diet); "Many people with type 2 diabetes have no symptoms"; "no cure", managed "through lifestyle modifications and medication", with "oral medications and/or insulin injections" over time. S2: about 1 in 15 Australians (about 1.3 million, 6.5 per cent of adults) had diabetes in 2022 to 2024, 0.9 per cent newly diagnosed by the survey's measurement (HbA1c); about 7,000 lower limb amputations among people with type 2 diabetes in 2023 to 2024; diabetes the underlying cause of about 6,000 deaths and contributing to about 20,800 (11 per cent of all deaths) in 2024. S3: 1,441 people with insulin-dependent (type 1) diabetes randomly assigned to intensive or conventional therapy; mean follow-up 6.5 years; intensive therapy reduced the risk of developing retinopathy by 76 per cent, slowed its progression by 54 per cent, reduced albuminuria by 54 per cent and clinical neuropathy by 60 per cent; severe hypoglycaemia increased two- to threefold.
- **Main teaching move: where the loop breaks.** The site's own line, "Diabetes disrupts this control at the insulin step ... The two types fail at different points, production versus response, but converge on one outcome", becomes the investigation's first criterion, set on v29's `FeedbackLoop` (scenes 2 to 4): type 1 breaks the signal (beta cells destroyed); type 2 breaks the response (insulin resistance), and then the signal fades as beta cells wear out. Both converge on hyperglycaemia (5). The site's "detection still works and only the response has failed" is kept in substance as "the change is still there, but the loop can't answer it".
- **Hook.** v31's empty insulin panel: Person B's glucose stays high, and the question "too little insulin, or ignored insulin?" is exactly the type 1 against type 2 distinction. Attempt 1 pays it off with constructed paired data (fasting glucose and insulin for three adults).
- **Immediate effects (scenes 5, 6).** v24 already taught that very high glucose overwhelms the tubule carriers and spills into urine (recalled in one clause, not re-taught). New here: glucose left in the tubule holds water by osmosis, so more urine is made and thirst follows; cells short of glucose, so tiredness; in untreated type 1, fat and muscle are broken down, so weight loss. These are S1's listed signs, so the evidence row cites S1. Ketoacidosis is a grey tag only (an emergency; not needed for the content point; no treatment content).
- **Long-term effects (scenes 7, 8).** Vessel damage in the eyes, kidneys and nerves (small vessels) and the heart (large vessels), with S2's outcome numbers; S3 as the causal test (lowering glucose reduced eye, kidney and nerve damage). This repeats v31's rule "change the variable, watch the effect" with a real trial, and is the investigation's evidence-quality centrepiece.
- **Management limits (scene 9).** "Treatment restores control, not the loop" (the site's Q3 model answer: "the treatment restores homeostasis without restoring the mechanism"). Limits: no cure (S1); doses matched by hand to food and activity; S3's two- to threefold rise in severe low glucose with tighter control. The site's continuous glucose monitor and pump card is kept as one sentence. No drug names, doses, targets or advice.
- **Risk table.** Not used (no laboratory procedure), as v25. The one-row "using health information" note (scene 10, screen only): "These sources describe diabetes in general. One graph or reading never diagnoses anyone; a person's own care is decided with their health team."
- **Predictions (root decision 7).** Scene 2 ends "which two places in the loop could break?" (answered in 3). Scene 5 ends "what does that glucose do to the water in the tubule?" (answered in 6; v24 and FA1 v09 recall). Each with a 3 s countdown ring.

- **Site errors found (this read, 3 Nov).** For `../site-corrections-bio-y11.md` (root forwards after confirmation).

Proposed: "In type 1 the signal is missing (the beta cells are destroyed); in type 2 the effectors respond poorly to the signal. Either way, the response that lowers glucose cannot work properly."
