### 2. the related angle
> The x and y axes cut the plane into four quadrants, quarters numbered one to four, anticlockwise from the top right. For any angle that isn't a multiple of ninety degrees, the related angle is the acute angle between its ray and the x-axis. Always the x-axis, never the y-axis. Take an acute angle A, say thirty degrees, with its triangle, and reflect it in the **axes**. In the second quadrant you get a hundred and eighty minus A, a hundred and fifty degrees. In the third, a hundred and eighty plus A, two hundred and ten degrees. In the fourth, three sixty minus A, three hundred and thirty **degrees**. Each copy has the same triangle, so x and y have the same sizes. Only their **signs** change.

### 3. signs from x and y
> So the size of every ratio comes from the related angle, and the sign comes from x and y. In the first quadrant, x and y are both positive, so all three ratios are positive. In the second, x is negative and y is positive, so only sine, y over r, is positive. In the third, both are negative, so y over x is positive, and only tangent is. In the fourth, x is positive, so only cosine is. Going round from the first quadrant, that's all, sine, tangent, cosine. A, S, T, C. All Stations To **Central**.

Visual: stage change: the circle large at the left; each quadrant fills in turn with the signs "x +, y +", "x −, y +" (hand tag "x −, y +"), "x −, y −", "x +, y −". ### 4. worked examples: cosine one fifty, tangent three hundred
> Let's use it. Find the exact value of cosine a hundred and fifty degrees. It's in the second quadrant, so the related angle is a hundred and eighty minus a hundred and fifty, thirty **degrees**. Cosine thirty degrees is root three over two. In the second quadrant only sine is positive, so cosine is negative. The answer is minus root three over **two**. Now tangent three hundred degrees. That's the fourth quadrant, and the related angle is three sixty minus three hundred, sixty degrees. Tangent sixty degrees is root three, and only cosine is positive there, so tangent three hundred degrees is minus root **three**. Writing a hundred and fifty minus a hundred and eighty, to get minus thirty degrees, is the **slip**. A related angle is always acute and positive.

### 5. one ratio gives the others
> Sometimes you're given one ratio and the quadrant. Say sine theta is minus three fifths, and theta is in the fourth quadrant. Find cosine theta. Sine is y over r, so draw the triangle with y as minus three and r as five, down in the fourth quadrant. Pythagoras gives the third side, the root of twenty-five minus nine, which is **four**. In the fourth quadrant x is positive, so x is plus four, and cosine theta is four **fifths**. Pythagoras only gives the size. The quadrant picks the sign. Writing minus four fifths is the **slip**.

### 6. negative angles
> Last, negative angles. Turning thirty degrees clockwise gives minus thirty degrees. Its point is the thirty degree point reflected in the x-axis. The reflection keeps x and flips the sign of y. So cosine of minus theta equals cosine theta, sine of minus theta equals minus sine theta, and tangent of minus theta equals minus tangent **theta**. That holds for every angle. So cosine of minus forty-five degrees is root two over two, and tangent of minus sixty degrees is minus root three. Saying sine of minus thirty degrees is a half is the **slip**. It's minus a half.

## Memory aid check

- **"Size from the related angle, sign from the quadrant: All Stations To Central."** "All Stations To Central" is a common Australian expansion of ASTC (a Sydney train announcement); the letters are introduced only after the signs of x and y are worked out in scene 3. Meaning: for an angle not on an axis, |sin θ|, |cos θ| and |tan θ| equal the ratios of the related angle; the quadrant's letter names which ratios are positive (A: all, S: sin, T: tan, C: cos, in quadrants 1 to 4). Assumptions checked: angles in standard position from the positive x-axis, anticlockwise numbering of quadrants (scene 2); angles on the axes are excluded (the syllabus's "excluding multiples of 90°"). It helps a new case: cos 225° is in quadrant 3 (T), so cosine is negative: −cos 45° = −√2/2.
- **Second line: "−θ mirrors in the x-axis: cos keeps its sign, sin and tan flip."** Holds for every θ (the reflection keeps x and negates y).
