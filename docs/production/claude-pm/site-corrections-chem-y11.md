# Year 11 Chemistry site corrections (2017 course)

Brief 167, 5 November 2026. Website read only. The old text below is quoted from the named current source. Corrections are research findings for Claude, not silent edits or recording approval.

## 1. module1/lesson02.sa.html, we-chem-y11-m1-l02-1

Old: "Sample C melts sharply at −78°C and sublimes (goes directly from solid to gas)."

Correction: Sample C sublimes at about minus 78 degrees C at atmospheric pressure.

Reason: Melting and sublimation are different transitions; this observation must not assert both. NIST carbon-dioxide phase data place the ordinary-pressure solid/gas transition below the triple-point temperature.

Check/source: https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/cbook.cgi?ID=C124389&Mask=17.

## 2. module1/lesson02.sa.html, we-chem-y11-m1-l02-1

Old: "This immediately rules out mixture."

Correction: A sharp transition is consistent with a pure substance, but one transition observation alone does not establish purity.

Reason: Some mixtures have sharp transitions. This is also inconsistent with the later site ethanol/water azeotrope explanation.

Check/source: module1/lesson04.sa.html, we-chem-y11-m1-l04-1.

## 3. module1/lesson02.sa.html, we-chem-y11-m1-l02-1

Old: "Variable melting behaviour is chemical evidence of a mixture"

Correction: A melting range is physical evidence consistent with a mixture

Reason: A phase change is a physical observation, not by itself evidence of chemical change.

Check/source: NESA C1-IQ1-01; same-page physical-property definitions.

## 4. module1/lesson02.sa.html, we-chem-y11-m1-l02-2

Old: "The Na⁺ ion has no reactivity, it is inert."

Correction: Sodium ions have different chemical properties from sodium metal; the reaction of sodium metal with water does not describe dissolved sodium ions.

Reason: A sodium ion is not universally chemically inert. The useful distinction is between the element and its ions.

Check/source: NESA C1-IQ4-04 and C1-IQ4-05.

## 5. module1/lesson02.sa.html, we-chem-y11-m1-l02-2

Old: "they cannot be predicted or inferred from the constituent elements"

Correction: they cannot be obtained by simply averaging the properties of the constituent elements; bonding and structure help explain and predict them

Reason: The course explicitly uses structure to explain and predict properties.

Check/source: NESA C1-IQ4-05.

## 6. module1/lesson03.sa.html, we-chem-y11-m1-l03-2

Old: "CuSO₄ molecules (or ions) fit neatly into the crystal lattice"

Correction: The appropriate ions and water molecules become arranged in the hydrated crystal lattice

Reason: Copper sulfate is ionic; its blue crystals are hydrated, not a lattice of discrete CuSO4 molecules.

Check/source: NESA C1-IQ4-04.

## 7. module1/lesson04.sa.html, we-chem-y11-m1-l04-2

Old: "If calculated and reference Rf values match, the substances are the same (within experimental error)."

Correction: Matching Rf values under the same conditions support the proposed identity; other compounds may share that value.

Reason: A single chromatographic match is not a unique identification.

Check/source: https://www2.chem.wisc.edu/deptfiles/genchem/lab/labdocs/modules/tlc/tlcinterp.htm.

## 8. module1/lesson05.sa.html, we-chem-y11-m1-l05-2

Old: "NaCl's solubility decreases on cooling, so heat the filtrate to concentrate it, cool slowly to crystallise NaCl, then filter crystals and dry."

Correction: Concentrate the solution and use checked fractional-crystallisation conditions; verify purity before claiming pure NaCl from a solution that also contains MgCl2.

Reason: The starting mixture contains a second dissolved salt. Cooling and filtration alone do not guarantee the identity/purity claimed, and NaCl has only a modest solubility change on cooling.

Check/source: The stated multi-solute givens and separation selectivity.

## 9. module1/lesson13.sa.html, l13-atomic-emission-spectra-and-the-bohr-model-title

Old: "or a paste of the solid salt with a drop of concentrated HCl"

Correction: use teacher-approved ionic solutions and the school-reviewed flame-test method

Reason: Do not copy an unreviewed concentrated-acid paste alternative into a student method. RSC supplies a school flame-test method with teacher/technician safety notes; school review still decides the exact chemicals and controls.

Check/source: https://edu.rsc.org/download?ac=511946.

## 10. module1/lesson13.sa.html, l13-atomic-emission-spectra-and-the-bohr-model-title

Correction: (CH11-8; C1-IQ2-04)

## 11. module1/lesson16.sa.html, we-chem-y11-m1-l16-2

Old: "All 3 3p orbitals singly occupied, waiting for a 4th electron"

Correction: For 3p5, two 3p orbitals are paired and one is singly occupied.

Reason: The corrected chlorine configuration has five electrons in 3p, not three.

Check/source: Electron count and Hund filling for the stated configuration.

## 12. module1/lesson07.sa.html, we-chem-y11-m1-l07-1

Old: "MgO requires ~4× more energy to break apart compared to NaCl. This is reflected in the ~3.5× higher MP"

Correction: The higher ionic charges contribute to stronger attraction, which helps explain the much higher melting point.

Reason: A charge-product comparison at assumed equal distance does not establish a lattice-energy or melting-point ratio; ratios of Celsius temperatures are not physical absolute-temperature ratios. Keep the given 2852 and 801 degrees C as comparison observations.

Check/source: Coulomb-law assumptions and temperature-scale arithmetic.
