## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Tension, The Pull from Transpiration: When water evaporates from the leaves it leaves behind a pulling force in the xylem, because the escaping molecules tug on the ones still attached below them.
2. Cohesion, Why the Column Doesn't Break: Water molecules attract one another strongly enough that a column of water in a narrow tube behaves like a single thread that can be pulled without breaking.
3. Osmotic Pressure and the Whole Theory: Water enters the root by osmosis, is held together by cohesion, and is pulled up by the tension of transpiration, three forces acting as one continuous system.
4. Why air bubbles can kill a drought-stressed tree: Because xylem water is under tension, literally being stretched, it is vulnerable.

## 4. Key terms

- Transpiration: The evaporation of water from the leaves, mainly through the stomata, which drives water movement up the plant.
- Transpiration stream: The continuous flow of water from roots to leaves through the xylem, pulled by transpiration.
- Cohesion: The attraction between water molecules (hydrogen bonding) that holds them together in an unbroken column.
- Tension: The negative pressure (pull) created in the xylem as water evaporates from the leaves.
- Osmotic pressure: The pressure resulting from osmosis that draws water from the soil into the root hair cells.
