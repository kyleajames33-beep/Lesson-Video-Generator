## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Synthesis and Decomposition: These two families are mirror images: synthesis joins simple reactants into one product, while decomposition splits one compound back into simpler pieces.
2. Combustion: Combustion is a rapid reaction of a fuel with oxygen that releases energy.
3. Precipitation and Neutralisation: Both of these happen when aqueous solutions are mixed, and both swap partners between the dissolved species, the difference is what the products turn out to be.

## 4. Key terms

- Synthesis (combination): Two or more reactants join to form a single product: A + B → AB.
- Decomposition: A single compound breaks down into two or more simpler products: AB → A + B.
- Combustion: A substance reacts rapidly with oxygen, releasing energy; for hydrocarbons the products are carbon dioxide and water.
- Precipitation: Two aqueous solutions mix and form an insoluble solid (a precipitate).
- Neutralisation: An acid reacts with a base to produce a salt and water.
