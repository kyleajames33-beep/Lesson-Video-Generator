## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Two Kinds of Pressure: Every selective pressure comes from the environment, and the environment splits cleanly into two halves: the things that are alive and the things that are not.
2. Abiotic Pressures at Work: An abiotic pressure changes which variants survive by setting a physical challenge that some individuals are better built to meet.
3. Biotic Pressures at Work: Living things in the environment pressure a population by competing with it, eating it, or infecting it, and each interaction favours particular variants.
4. Why this matters: predicting climate winners and losers: Conservation biologists use the abiotic/biotic framework to predict which species will cope with climate change.

## 4. Key terms

- Abiotic factor: A non-living component of the environment, e.g. temperature, water, light, salinity, soil pH.
- Biotic factor: A living component of the environment, e.g. predators, prey, competitors, parasites, mates.
- Selective pressure: Any factor that affects which individuals survive and reproduce, thereby driving natural selection.
- Population: A group of individuals of the same species living in the same area at the same time.
- Predation: A biotic interaction in which one organism (the predator) kills and eats another (the prey).
