## 3. Key ideas in order

1. A dihybrid cross tracks two genes at once; each AaBb parent produces four gamete types: AB, Ab, aB, ab.
2. The 4x4 Punnett square has 16 equally likely boxes.
3. The boxes group into four phenotypic classes: A_B_ (9), A_bb (3), aaB_ (3) and aabb (1), a 9:3:3:1 ratio.
4. The ratio comes from independent assortment: random orientation of bivalents at metaphase I of meiosis I.
5. Mendel's 556 F2 seeds were 315 yellow round, 101 yellow wrinkled, 108 green round and 32 green wrinkled.
6. Linked genes (on the same chromosome) do not give 9:3:3:1; parental combinations appear more often.

## 4. Key terms

- Dihybrid cross: A genetic cross that tracks the inheritance of two different genes simultaneously.
- Independent assortment: Mendel's second law; genes on different chromosomes segregate independently during meiosis.
- 9:3:3:1 ratio: The expected phenotypic ratio from an AaBb x AaBb cross when both genes assort independently.
- Linked genes: Genes located on the same chromosome that tend to be inherited together, violating independent assortment.

## 5. Worked examples

- Mendel's pea cross: P generation YYRR x yyrr; F1 all YyRr; F2 gives 9 yellow round : 3 yellow wrinkled : 3 green round : 1 green wrinkled.
- Activity 2: 320 offspring from two YyRr plants give 9/16 x 320 = 180 yellow round; 3/16 x 320 = 60 yellow wrinkled; 3/16 x 320 = 60 green round; 1/16 x 320 = 20 green wrinkled. Check: 180 + 60 + 60 + 20 = 320.

- A cross that does not give 9:3:3:1 is "most likely" caused by linked genes (Reasoning box and MC option).
