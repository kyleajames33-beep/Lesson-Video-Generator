## 3. Key ideas in order

1. A gene is a DNA sequence whose base sequence codes for a functional product, usually a polypeptide.
2. RNA polymerase binds the promoter and builds mRNA from one DNA template strand.
3. RNA uses uracil in place of thymine, so adenine pairs with uracil.
4. DNA stays in the nucleus; mRNA is a temporary, portable copy that carries the message out and allows many protein copies from one gene.
5. mRNA is read in three-base codons; DNA uses A, T, C and G, mRNA uses A, U, C and G.
6. The lesson stops at mRNA and codons; translation is the next lesson.
7. CFTR example: a change in the DNA sequence can change the mRNA codons before translation begins.

## 4. Key terms

- Gene: A section of DNA that contains coded information for a product, usually a polypeptide.
- Template strand: The DNA strand (antisense strand) read by RNA polymerase to determine the complementary mRNA sequence.
- Coding strand: The DNA strand (sense strand) not read during transcription; its base sequence matches the mRNA, with T in place of U.
- Promoter: A control sequence just before a gene where RNA polymerase binds to begin transcription.
- Codon: A three-base sequence on mRNA that carries transferable coded information.

## 5. Worked examples

- Activity 1: DNA template strand "T A C C G A A T T"; mRNA formed "A U G G C U U A A".
- Four-step model (CFTR gene): 1, the DNA region unwinds and exposes the template strand; 2, RNA nucleotides pair with exposed bases (A-U, C-G); 3, the mRNA strand separates as a temporary copy; 4, the mRNA carries codons out of the nucleus for translation.
- Activity 2 answer: a DNA change means "the codons carried by the mRNA may differ before translation even begins".

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- "One gene codes for exactly one protein and has exactly one function." Page: alternative splicing lets one gene give several transcripts; "one gene, one protein" is "a significant oversimplification".
- Do not say the cell moves DNA to the ribosome, or that both DNA strands are copied into one mRNA.
- True/false (false): "After transcription, the mRNA permanently replaces the cell's DNA."
