## 3. Key ideas in order

1. The c = n/V Relationship and Its Units: Concentration just answers one question: how crowded is the solute?
2. Concentration from a Mass of Solute: Most real problems start at the balance, so the work is a two-step chain: turn the measured mass into moles, then turn those moles into a concentration.
3. Rearranging to Find Moles or Volume: There is only one equation here; the two rearrangements just isolate the unknown you want.

## 4. Key terms

- Molar concentration (molarity): The amount of solute, in moles, dissolved per litre of solution. Symbol $c$, units mol L -1.
- $c = \dfrac{n}{V}$: The defining equation: concentration equals moles of solute divided by volume of solution in litres.
- mol L -1: The unit of molar concentration, read as "moles per litre". A solution that is 1 mol L -1 is often written as 1 M.
- Solution vs solvent: The solution is the solute plus solvent together; $V$ in $c = n/V$ is the total volume of solution, not the volume of solvent added.
