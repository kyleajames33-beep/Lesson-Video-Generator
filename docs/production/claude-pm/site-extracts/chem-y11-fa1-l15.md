## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Group Sets Valence Electrons and Ion Charge: An element's group is the single most useful clue: it tells you how many valence electrons the atom has, and from that you can predict the charge of the ion it normally forms.
2. Period and Group Set Atomic Size: Atomic radius follows two reliable patterns: it gets smaller as you move left-to-right across a period, and larger as you move down a group.
3. Position Predicts Metallic Character and Reactivity: Metallic character increases down and to the left, because larger atoms hold their outer electrons loosely and give them up easily, the heart of metallic behaviour.

## 4. Key terms

- Group: A vertical column of the periodic table; elements in a group share the same number of valence electrons.
- Period: A horizontal row; the period number equals the number of occupied electron shells.
- Block: A region (s, p, d or f) named for the sublevel its valence electrons occupy; sets the broad type of element.
- Valence electrons: The outer-shell electrons that take part in bonding and decide the ion an atom forms.
- Atomic radius: A measure of atom size; it decreases left-to-right across a period and increases down a group.

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: A bigger atomic number always means a bigger atom. ✓ Right: Across a period the atomic number rises but the radius shrinks, because added protons pull the same shell in more tightly.
