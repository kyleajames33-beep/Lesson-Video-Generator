## 3. Key ideas in order

1. What a Covalent Bond Is: When two non-metal atoms of similar electronegativity meet, neither can pull electrons off the other, so they reach a stable full outer shell by sharing electrons.
2. Discrete Molecules vs Covalent Networks: Covalent bonding produces two very different kinds of substance depending on how far the bonding extends.
3. Constructing Electron Dot Diagrams: An electron dot diagram shows each atom's valence electrons as dots, with shared pairs between atoms forming the bonds and the remaining electrons drawn as lone pairs.

## 4. Key terms

- Covalent bond: A bond formed when two atoms share one or more pairs of electrons, usually between non-metals.
- Lone pair: A pair of valence electrons on an atom that is not shared in a bond.
- Electron dot (Lewis) diagram: A drawing that shows the valence electrons of each atom as dots, with shared pairs representing the covalent bonds.
- Covalent network: A giant lattice in which atoms are joined by a continuous 3D framework of covalent bonds (e.g. diamond, SiO 2 ), rather than separate molecules.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: A covalent bond means one atom gives an electron to the other. ✓ Right: A covalent bond is a shared pair, both electrons are counted by both atoms at once, which is how each reaches a full outer shell. ✗ Wrong: An electron dot diagram only needs to show the bonding pairs. ✓ Right: You must show every valence electron, the shared (bonding) pairs and the non-bonding lone pairs, so the dots around each atom add up correctly.
