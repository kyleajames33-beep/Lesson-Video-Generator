## 1. Title and main question

Titration & Indicator Mastery, Curves, Selection & Curve Interpretation. How can a student interpret a titration curve, choose an indicator and diagnose common errors?

## 2. Syllabus content covered

Consolidates “conduct practical investigations to analyse the concentration of an unknown acid or base by titration” and “investigate titration curves and conductivity graphs to analyse data to indicate characteristic reaction profiles.”

## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Four students interpret one weak acid/strong base curve; the page compares their equivalence point, indicator and pKa decisions.
2. A six-question framework asks for acid/base type, starting concentration and volume, equivalence volume, equivalence pH, pKa and indicator.
3. Read all four curve types using the initial pH, buffer region, jump and salt hydrolysis.
4. Keep experimental endpoint separate from stoichiometric equivalence point.
5. Diagnose five mistakes: forcing equivalence to pH 7, confusing endpoint and equivalence, putting the buffer after equivalence, misreading pKa and choosing phenolphthalein automatically.

## 4. Key terms

- Equivalence point: calculated stoichiometric point at the curve's steepest region.
- Endpoint: observed indicator colour change.
- pKa: pH at half-equivalence volume for a weak acid curve.
- Indicator range: pH interval of colour change, chosen to overlap the steep region.
- Buffer region: part of a weak-acid curve before equivalence where HA and A⁻ coexist.

## 5. Worked examples

- Adding 0.0500 mol/L NaOH to 40.00 mL HX gives a jump centred at 20.00 mL. The page identifies a strong acid, gives c(HX) 0.0250 mol/L, equivalence pH about 7 and accepts phenolphthalein.
- 25.00 mL propanoic acid (Ka 1.3 × 10⁻⁵) titrated with 0.100 mol/L NaOH: equivalence at 25.00 mL, pH 8.57; half-equivalence pH 4.89; theoretical starting pH 2.94. Methyl orange at 6.25 mL reports 0.0250 instead of 0.1000 mol/L, a 75% underestimate.
- Equal 20.00 mL portions of 0.100 mol/L HCl and HF (Ka 6.8 × 10⁻⁴), each titrated with 0.100 mol/L NaOH: starting pH 1.00 and 2.10; both equivalence volumes 20.00 mL; equivalence pH 7.00 and 7.93. HF has half-equivalence pH 3.17.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Equivalence always occurs at pH 7 or at the curve's maximum pH.
- Indicator colour change directly proves stoichiometric equivalence.
- pKa is the starting, equivalence or neutral pH rather than half-equivalence pH.
- Methyl orange is suitable for a weak acid titration because its colour is vivid.
- A buffer region appears after equivalence in a weak acid/strong base titration.

## 7. Practical or real-world examples used

HCl, HF, propanoic and lactic acid curves; NaOH and ammonia titrants; methyl orange, bromothymol blue and phenolphthalein; four-student curve diagnosis.

## 8. Activities and questions

Error diagnosis, data-table interpretation, step sorting, multiple choice and extended comparison of two curves. Representative question: “A student examining a titration curve for CH₃COOH + NaOH marks the equivalence point where the curve crosses pH 7. Diagnose this error.”
