## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Metal Reactivity and Ionisation Energy: A metal reacts by losing electrons to form a cation, so the easier it is to pull an electron off the atom, the more reactive the metal will be.
2. Atomic Radius and Electronegativity: A larger atomic radius means the outer electron sits further from the nucleus, so the pull holding it is weaker and it is easier to lose, making the metal more reactive.
3. Pulling It Together: Explaining the Trends: The three properties are not independent: a highly reactive metal is one that combines a low ionisation energy, a large atomic radius and a low electronegativity, because all three describe the same loosely held outer electron.

## 4. Key terms

- First ionisation energy: The energy needed to remove the most loosely held electron from one mole of gaseous atoms.
- Atomic radius: A measure of the size of an atom, from the nucleus to the outermost electrons. It increases down a group.
- Electronegativity: A measure of how strongly an atom attracts the electrons in a bond. Metals have low electronegativity.
- Cation: A positive ion formed when an atom loses one or more electrons, for example Na + or Mg 2+.
