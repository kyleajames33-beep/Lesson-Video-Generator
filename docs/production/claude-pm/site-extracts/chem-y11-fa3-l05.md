## 3. Key ideas in order

1. What Is a Displacement Reaction: A metal displacement reaction is a swap: a more reactive metal goes into solution as ions and pushes a less reactive metal out, depositing it as a solid.
2. Observations and Method: A displacement practical is mostly observation: place a clean metal strip in a salt solution and watch for colour changes, a coating forming and a temperature rise.
3. Interpreting Results to Compare Reactivity: Every displacement is a head-to-head contest: if metal A displaces metal B, then A is more reactive than B, and stringing those results together ranks the metals.

## 4. Key terms

- Displacement reaction: A reaction in which a more reactive metal takes the place of a less reactive metal in its salt solution.
- Salt solution: An aqueous solution of an ionic compound, e.g. copper sulfate (CuSO 4 ) or silver nitrate (AgNO 3 ).
- Reactivity: How readily a metal loses electrons to form ions; the more reactive metal is the better at displacing others.
- Qualitative observation: A recorded description of what is seen, felt or measured by description, colour change, a coating forming, the tube warming.
- Activity series: A list of metals arranged in order of reactivity, which displacement results help to build.
