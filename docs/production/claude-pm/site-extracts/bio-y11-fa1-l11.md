## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Gases: Oxygen and Carbon Dioxide: A substance's characteristics decide its route, and small non-polar gases have the easiest path of all.
2. Glucose and Salts: Needing a Protein: Polar molecules and charged ions are repelled by the hydrophobic core, so they must use membrane proteins, and the direction of their gradient decides whether energy is needed.
3. Wastes Out, and the General Rule: Wastes build up inside a cell, so they move out down their gradient, and the same two-factor logic explains every exchange.
4. Gradients in the lungs and kidneys: Every breath you take depends on these rules.

## 4. Key terms

- Non-polar molecule: A molecule with no charged regions (e.g. O₂, CO₂), dissolves in the lipid core, so it diffuses straight through.
- Polar molecule: A molecule with uneven charge (e.g. glucose), repelled by the lipid core, so it needs a protein.
- Ion: A charged particle (e.g. Na⁺, Cl⁻ "salts"), cannot cross the lipid core; uses channels or active transport.
- Metabolic waste: A by-product of cell reactions (e.g. CO₂, urea) that must leave the cell down its gradient.
- Gradient direction: Set by the cell's activity: respiration uses O₂/glucose (low inside) and makes CO₂/waste (high inside).
