## 3. Key ideas in order

1. The NAGSAG rules (Nitrates, Ammonium, Group 1, Sulfates, Acetates, Group 17 halides, everything else) predict solubility, and the first three override the rest.
2. To predict precipitation, list the four ions, find the two possible products, and apply NAGSAG to both.
3. Only the insoluble product forms a precipitate.
4. Molecular, full ionic and net ionic equations describe the same event at three levels of detail; spectator ions cancel in the net ionic equation.
5. The three NESA reactions are KCl + AgNO₃ (white AgCl), KI + Pb(NO₃)₂ (bright yellow PbI₂) and Na₂SO₄ + Ba(NO₃)₂ (white BaSO₄).
6. A water-treatment precipitant must form an insoluble compound with the target metal and soluble compounds with the other ions present.

## 4. Key terms

- Solubility rules: "Empirical guidelines predicting whether an ionic compound is soluble or insoluble in water."
- Precipitate: "A sparingly soluble solid that forms and separates out when two aqueous solutions of ions are mixed."
- Net ionic equation: "An equation showing only the ions that participate in a reaction, omitting spectator ions."
- Spectator ions: "Ions present in solution that do not participate in the net ionic reaction."

## 5. Worked examples

**Worked Example 1 (Apply/Analyse).** (a) Na₂CO₃(aq) + CaCl₂(aq); (b) KNO₃(aq) + NaCl(aq); (c) FeCl₃(aq) + NaOH(aq).
Answer: (a) CaCO₃(s), white; Ca²⁺ + CO₃²⁻ → CaCO₃(s). (b) No precipitate, both possible products soluble. (c) Fe(OH)₃(s), rust-brown; Fe³⁺ + 3OH⁻ → Fe(OH)₃(s).

**Worked Example 2 (Analyse/Evaluate).** A water sample contains Pb²⁺ (5.0 × 10⁻³ mol/L) with Na⁺, Ca²⁺, Cl⁻ and NO₃⁻. (a) Identify two anions that precipitate Pb²⁺. (b) Evaluate which is preferable. (c) Write the net ionic equation for the preferred reaction.
Answer: (a) SO₄²⁻ (PbSO₄) and CO₃²⁻ (PbCO₃). (b) Na₂CO₃ is preferred for Pb²⁺ removal; Na₂SO₄ risks CaSO₄ co-precipitation. (c) Pb²⁺(aq) + CO₃²⁻(aq) → PbCO₃(s).

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- "All ionic compounds dissolve in water because water is polar." Page: AgCl and BaSO₄ are insoluble despite water's polarity.
- Checking only one product. Page: "Both products must be assessed, only one may be insoluble."
- Recording "no reaction" instead of "no precipitate." Page: every ion is then a spectator.
- Writing the precipitate as separate ions in the full ionic equation. Page: it "must be written as the formula unit with state symbol (s)."
- Writing PbI as the formula. Page: "Pb²⁺ requires two I⁻ to balance charge → PbI₂."

Possible site errors: the summary says "PbCl₂/PbBr₂/PbI₂ all insoluble", but PbCl₂ is noticeably soluble in hot water and the page's own investigation says PbCl₂ and PbI₂ behave differently in warm water, so the absolute wording oversimplifies; the Think First gives Talbot's "first photographic fixing process" as 1856, which is not sourced on the page and conflicts with the usual credit of thiosulfate fixing to Herschel in 1819 (verify).
