## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Griffith's 1928 transformation showed that something passed from dead smooth bacteria to live rough ones; Avery, MacLeod and McCarty identified it as DNA in 1944.
2. Both prokaryotes and eukaryotes use DNA; the difference is how it is stored, packaged and organised.
3. Prokaryotes usually have one main circular DNA molecule in the nucleoid, which has no membrane.
4. Many prokaryotes also carry plasmids, small separate circular DNA molecules, often with genes such as antibiotic resistance.
5. Eukaryotes have multiple linear chromosomes in a membrane-bound nucleus, wound around histones as nucleosomes that form chromatin.
6. Mitochondria, and chloroplasts in plants, each carry their own small circular DNA.
7. A plasmid can be isolated and used as a vector; a human chromosome cannot be treated the same way.

## 4. Key terms

- Prokaryote: An organism whose cells lack a membrane-bound nucleus, such as bacteria.
- Nucleoid: The region in a prokaryotic cell where the main DNA is located.
- Plasmid: A small circular DNA molecule in many prokaryotes that is separate from the main chromosome.
- Histone: A packaging protein that eukaryotic DNA wraps around; DNA plus a histone core forms a nucleosome.
- Chromatin: DNA wound around histone proteins as nucleosomes in eukaryotic cells, before full chromosome condensation.
