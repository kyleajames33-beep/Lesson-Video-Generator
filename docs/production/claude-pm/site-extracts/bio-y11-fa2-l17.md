## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Plasma, The Transport Fluid: About 55% of blood is plasma, and its structure as a mostly-water liquid is exactly what makes it a universal transport medium.
2. The Three Cellular Components: Each cellular component has been shaped, sometimes literally, for one specialised role: carrying oxygen, defending against disease, or sealing wounds.
3. Why "Structure Fits Function" Matters Here: The dot point asks you to explain the relationship between structure and function, so the real skill is justifying why a given feature exists, not just naming parts.
4. Anaemia: too little haemoglobin: In anaemia, a person has too few red blood cells or too little haemoglobin, so the blood cannot carry enough oxygen.

## 4. Key terms

- Plasma: The pale yellow liquid part of blood (mostly water) that transports dissolved substances and cells.
- Red blood cell (erythrocyte): A biconcave, anucleate cell packed with haemoglobin that carries oxygen.
- Haemoglobin: The iron-containing protein in red cells that binds and carries oxygen.
- White blood cell (leukocyte): A nucleated cell of the immune system that defends against pathogens.
- Platelet: A small cell fragment (not a whole cell) that triggers blood clotting at wounds.
