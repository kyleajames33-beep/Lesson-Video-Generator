## 3. Key ideas in order

1. What Half-Life Tells You: Half-life is simply the time for half of the radioactive nuclei in a sample to decay, and it is fixed for each radioisotope, no matter how much you start with.
2. Radiation Type and Half-Life Decide the Use: A radioisotope is chosen by asking two questions: can its radiation reach where it is needed without doing the wrong damage, and does it last for the right length of time?
3. Justifying a Radioisotope Choice: Every good justification names the radiation type and the half-life, then links both to what the application actually requires.

## 4. Key terms

- Radioisotope: An isotope of an element with an unstable nucleus that decays, emitting radiation.
- Half-life: The time taken for half the radioactive nuclei in a sample to decay.
- Penetrating power: How far a type of radiation travels through matter: α stops at paper or skin, β at thin metal, γ needs thick lead or concrete.
- Tracer: A radioisotope introduced into a system so its emitted radiation can be followed from outside.
