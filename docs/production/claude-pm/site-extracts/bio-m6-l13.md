## 1. Title and main question

Current Genetic Technologies That Induce Genetic Change. What does each current technology change, and what is its advantage?

## 2. Syllabus content covered

“investigate the uses and advantages of current genetic technologies that induce genetic change”

## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Artificial insemination and pollination control which gametes combine without normally changing DNA sequence.
2. Whole-organism cloning aims to copy a donor's nuclear genotype; gene cloning makes many copies of a selected sequence.
3. Recombinant DNA technology joins selected DNA to a vector, and transgenic organisms contain introduced DNA.
4. Reproductive control, copying and direct insertion have different uses and limits.

## 4. Key terms

- Genetic technology: “A technology used to analyse, manipulate or direct inheritance and genetic change.”
- Reproductive technology: “A technology that controls how gametes or reproductive material are brought together.”
- Cloning: “Producing a genetically identical copy of DNA, a cell or sometimes a whole organism.”
- Recombinant DNA: “DNA formed by combining genetic material from different sources.”
- Transgenic organism: “An organism that contains inserted DNA originating from another species or external source.”

## 5. Worked examples

No numerical worked example. A technology map compares artificial insemination, artificial pollination, whole-organism cloning, gene cloning and recombinant DNA by what changes, where each acts and why it is used.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Every genetic technology directly edits DNA. Reproductive technologies usually direct parental contribution.
- Gene cloning makes a whole organism. It copies a DNA sequence.
- Clones must have identical phenotypes. Environment and development can differ.

## 7. Practical or real-world examples used

Multiple ovulation and embryo transfer (MOET); human insulin made with bacteria; Bt cotton carrying a gene from *Bacillus thuringiensis*; selected livestock and crop breeding.

## 8. Activities and questions

Technology classification, multiple choice, comparisons and three short answers. Representative question: “Compare one reproductive technology with one DNA-level technology in terms of mechanism and outcome.”
