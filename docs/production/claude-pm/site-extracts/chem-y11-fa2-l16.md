## 3. Key ideas in order

1. The Equation and Its Variables: The ideal gas law states that for an ideal gas the pressure times the volume equals the number of moles times the gas constant times the absolute temperature: $pV = nRT$.
2. Rearranging and Solving: Because $pV = nRT$ is a single equation, you can rearrange it to solve for whichever quantity is unknown, as long as the other three are known in consistent units.
3. Standard Temperature and Pressure: Standard temperature and pressure (STP) is an agreed reference condition so that gas measurements can be compared fairly.

## 4. Key terms

- Ideal gas law: The relationship $pV = nRT$ linking pressure, volume, amount of gas and temperature for an ideal gas.
- Gas constant ($R$): A fixed constant, $8.314$ J K$^{-1}$ mol$^{-1}$, used in $pV = nRT$ when $p$ is in pascals (Pa) and $V$ is in cubic metres (m$^{3}$).
- Kelvin (K): The absolute temperature scale. Convert from Celsius by adding 273.15 (often rounded to 273).
- Standard temperature and pressure (STP): NESA 2025 reference conditions: a pressure of 100 kPa and a temperature of 273.15 K (0 °C).
