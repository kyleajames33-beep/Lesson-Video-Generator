## 3. Key ideas in order

1. A point mutation changes one or a few DNA bases by substitution, insertion or deletion.
2. A substitution may be silent, missense or nonsense, depending on the resulting codon.
3. An insertion or deletion that is not a multiple of three can shift the downstream reading frame.
4. Protein and phenotype effects depend on mutation type, position and the role of the affected sequence.
5. The page traces DNA change to codon, amino acid, protein function and possible phenotype change.

## 4. Key terms

- Point mutation: “A mutation affecting one base pair or a very small number of bases in the DNA sequence.”
- Substitution: One base replaced by another.
- Insertion: One or more bases added.
- Deletion: One or more bases removed.
- Frameshift: Codon reading frame shifted by insertion or deletion not in multiples of three.
- Silent, missense, nonsense: A changed codon specifies the same amino acid, a different amino acid or a stop codon respectively.

## 5. Worked examples

The sickle-cell example changes the sixth β-haemoglobin DNA codon from GAG to GTG, replacing glutamic acid with valine. The page links this to haemoglobin polymerisation under low oxygen and red-cell sickling. A sequence example shows normal `ATG | AAA | CCT | GGA | TTT` and a deletion regrouping downstream codons as `ATG | AAC | CTG | GAT | ...`. Its interactive uses a 13-codon HBB excerpt: deleting base 5 introduces an early stop; inserting at position 8 restores the downstream frame while residues between edits remain changed. GAA to GAG remains glutamic acid, so is silent.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Point mutation means small biological effect. A single base change can have major effects.
- Every insertion or deletion affects only one amino acid. A non-triplet change shifts downstream codons.
- Every mutation destroys a protein. Some substitutions are silent or have limited effects.
