## 3. Key ideas in order

1. The Outer Layers: A bacterium is protected and bounded by up to three concentric layers, working from the outside in.
2. Inside: DNA, Plasmids and Ribosomes: Without a nucleus or organelles, a prokaryote runs its genetics and protein synthesis directly in the cytoplasm.
3. Moving and Connecting: Flagella and Pili: Two kinds of projection reach beyond the cell wall, one for movement, one for attachment and gene transfer.
4. How antibiotic resistance spreads: Antibiotic resistance is one of the biggest threats in modern medicine, and prokaryotic structures explain it.

## 4. Key terms

- Capsule: A slimy outer layer outside the cell wall that protects the bacterium and helps it stick to surfaces.
- Nucleoid: The region where the single circular chromosome sits, not enclosed by a membrane.
- Plasmid: A small, separate ring of DNA carrying extra genes (e.g. antibiotic resistance) that can be shared between cells.
- Flagellum: A long whip-like tail that rotates to move the cell (plural: flagella).
- Pili: Short hair-like projections used for attachment and for transferring DNA between cells (singular: pilus).
