## 3. Key ideas in order

1. A Punnett square predicts the chance of each genotype per child, not a locked-in family outcome.
2. "Dominant" describes expression in a heterozygote; it does not mean the allele is more common.
3. Autosomal dominant traits usually appear in every generation; an affected child usually has an affected parent.
4. Autosomal recessive traits can skip generations; two carriers (Aa x Aa) give a 1:2:1 genotype ratio and 3:1 phenotype ratio.
5. X-linked recessive: males have one X, so one recessive allele is expressed; a carrier mother gives each son a 50% chance of being affected.
6. Pedigree clues: autosomal recessive shows unaffected parents with an affected child; X-linked recessive shows more affected males and no father-to-son transmission.

## 4. Key terms

- Autosomal: A gene located on a non-sex chromosome.
- Dominant: An allele expressed in the phenotype when present in a heterozygous genotype.
- Sex-linked: A trait controlled by a gene on a sex chromosome, usually the X chromosome at HSC level.

## 5. Worked examples

- Garrod (1902): 4 affected children across 2 consanguineous families with alkaptonuria, "consistent with inheritance of two copies of a recessive allele".
- Short answer 1 model: Aa x aa gives "50% Aa, 50% aa".
- Short answer 3 model: parents XHXh and XHY; "(a) affected son = XhY = 25% of all children, or 50% of sons; (b) unaffected son = XHY = 25% ...; (c) carrier daughter = XHXh = 25% of all children, or 50% of daughters."
- Haemophilia Interpret model: an affected female with an unaffected father rules out X linkage; an unaffected sibling of an affected child "has a two in three chance of being a carrier".
- Simulator question (1000 families of four, both parents Aa): one option listed is "About 42%, because each child is an independent trial".

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- "A dominant allele is always more common in a population than a recessive allele." (False) Page: dominance describes phenotype, not frequency.
- Do not say "25% of the children" as if the family outcome is locked in; say each child has a 25% probability.
- True/false (false): "Sex-linked traits can only be inherited from the mother."

Possible site errors: The hook says Garrod "had no knowledge of DNA, chromosomes, or molecular biology". No source is given. Chromosome-based theories of heredity were already being published in 1902 to 1903, so the claim needs checking before use.
