## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Sources, Sinks and Translocation: Phloem carries sugar from wherever it is made or stored to wherever it is being used, and these "from" and "to" points can change with the season.
2. The Pressure-Flow Mechanism: When sugar is actively pumped into the phloem at the source, water follows by osmosis, raising the pressure there and forcing the sap to flow toward the lower-pressure sink.
3. Why the Direction Can Reverse: Because translocation is driven by wherever sugar is loaded and unloaded, the direction of flow simply follows the changing positions of source and sink.
4. Why aphids tap into phloem, and how scientists use them: Aphids feed by pushing a needle-like mouthpart straight into a single phloem sieve tube, and the sap is under such high pressure that it flows into the insect on its own, the aphid barely has to suck.

## 4. Key terms

- Translocation: The movement of organic nutrients (mainly sucrose) through the phloem from a source to a sink.
- Source: A part of the plant that makes or releases sugar, e.g. a photosynthesising leaf or a storage organ in spring.
- Sink: A part of the plant that uses or stores sugar, e.g. a root, fruit or growing tip.
- Active loading: The active transport of sugar into the phloem at the source, using ATP from companion cells.
- Pressure-flow: The theory that a pressure difference between source and sink drives the bulk flow of phloem sap.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: Sugar always travels downwards in phloem, from leaves to roots, because of gravity. ✓ Right: Translocation moves sugar from source to sink in whichever direction the sink lies, upward to growing shoots and fruit, or downward to roots.
