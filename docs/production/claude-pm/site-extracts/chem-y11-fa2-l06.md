## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Equimolar Amounts, Different Masses: A mole is a counting unit, it always means $6.022\times10^{23}$ particles.
2. Calculating Masses for Equimolar Amounts: To weigh out a chosen number of moles you flip the mole formula.
3. Reacting in Simple Whole-Number Molar Ratios: The numbers in front of each formula in a balanced equation are the mole ratio, they tell you how many moles of one substance react with another, regardless of the masses involved.

## 4. Key terms

- Equimolar: Containing the same number of moles, and therefore the same number of particles, of two or more substances.
- Molar mass ($M$): The mass of one mole of a substance, in g mol -1, found by adding the relative atomic masses in its formula.
- Mole ratio: The ratio of moles of one species to another, given directly by the coefficients in a balanced equation.
- Stoichiometry: Using the mole ratio from a balanced equation to relate amounts of reactants and products.
