## 3. Key ideas in order

1. The Six Relationship Types: Every relationship between two organisms can be classified by its effect (+ benefit, − harm, 0 no effect) on each partner.
2. Reading a Predator–Prey Graph: Predator and prey numbers rise and fall in a linked cycle, with the predator's peak always coming after the prey's.
3. How Competition Affects Populations: Competition for a limited resource reduces the growth or survival of the competitors, capping population size and sometimes excluding the weaker species.
4. Why this matters: biological control gone wrong: Understanding these relationships is the basis of biological pest control, using a predator or parasite to suppress a pest.

## 4. Key terms

- Predation: One organism (predator) kills and eats another (prey): predator benefits (+), prey harmed (−).
- Competition: Two organisms use the same limited resource: both are harmed (−/−).
- Mutualism: Both organisms benefit from the relationship (+/+).
- Commensalism: One benefits, the other is neither helped nor harmed (+/0).
- Parasitism / Allelopathy: Parasitism: parasite benefits, host harmed (+/−). Allelopathy: a plant releases chemicals that harm other plants nearby.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: A predator and a parasite are basically the same thing because both harm another organism. ✓ Right: A predator kills its prey quickly and usually eats it whole or in part; a parasite lives on or in its host, feeding off it over time without (usually) killing it immediately, a dead host is no use to most parasites.
