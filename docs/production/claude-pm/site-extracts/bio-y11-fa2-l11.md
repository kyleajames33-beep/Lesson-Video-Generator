## 3. Key ideas in order

1. The Potometer Method: A potometer works because every drop of water the leaf loses must be replaced by water drawn in through the cut stem, so tracking the water pulled in tells us how fast it is leaving.
2. A Fair Test for One Factor: To demonstrate the effect of one environmental factor, we change only that factor and keep everything else the same, so any change in rate can only be due to it.
3. Validity, Reliability and Accuracy: A good practical is not just one that gives a result, it is one whose result we can defend as valid, reliable and accurate.
4. From a classroom potometer to whole-forest sensors: The same logic scales up enormously.

## 4. Key terms

- Potometer: An apparatus that measures the rate of water uptake by a leafy shoot, by timing how far an air bubble travels along a graduated tube.
- Independent variable: The one factor you deliberately change, here, the chosen environmental factor (e.g. wind, light or humidity).
- Dependent variable: What you measure in response, the rate of water uptake (used as a measure of transpiration).
- Controlled variable: A factor kept constant so it cannot affect the result, making the test fair (valid).
- Reliability: How consistent the result is when the measurement is repeated; improved by repeat trials and averaging.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: A potometer measures transpiration directly. ✓ Right: A potometer measures the rate of water uptake. We assume that almost all the water taken up is lost in transpiration, so uptake is a close proxy.
