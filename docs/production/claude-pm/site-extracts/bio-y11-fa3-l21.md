## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Abundance, Distribution & Diversity: When ecologists describe a community, they focus on three measures, how many, where, and how varied, and all three are shaped by abiotic and biotic factors.
2. How Abiotic Factors Shape Communities: Every species can survive only within a tolerance range of each abiotic factor, and the factor in shortest supply limits where and how many can live.
3. How Biotic Factors Shape Communities: Living organisms compete, eat one another and spread disease, and these interactions strongly shape who lives where, in what numbers, and how varied the community is.
4. Why this matters: managing land and pests: Knowing how abiotic and biotic factors shape a community is the basis of land and pest management.

## 4. Key terms

- Abundance: How many individuals of a species are present in an area.
- Distribution: Where in an ecosystem a species is found, its spatial pattern (e.g. clumped, even, zoned).
- Diversity: The variety of different species present in an ecosystem (often more = healthier).
- Limiting factor: A factor in shortest supply (or at an extreme) that restricts a population's size or distribution.
- Tolerance range: The range of an abiotic factor within which a species can survive; outside it, the species cannot persist.
