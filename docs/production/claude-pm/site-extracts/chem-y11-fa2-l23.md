## 3. Key ideas in order

1. The Five-Step Strategy: Every stoichiometry problem can be solved by the same five steps, because all of them route through moles: convert in, use the ratio, convert out.
2. A Worked Mixed Problem: Mass → Gas Volume: Watch how a given mass becomes a gas volume by passing through moles: convert the mass in, use the 1:1 ratio, then use the gas law to convert out.
3. Limiting Reagents and Common Pitfalls: When two reactant amounts are given, find which one is limiting by comparing their moles against the mole ratio; the limiting reagent sets how much product can form.

## 4. Key terms

- Mole ratio: The ratio of amounts (in mol) between species in a reaction, read directly from the coefficients of the balanced equation.
- Limiting reagent: The reactant that runs out first; it determines the maximum amount of product that can form.
- Significant figures: The number of meaningful digits in a result; the answer should not be more precise than the least precise data given.
- Molar volume ($V_m$): The volume occupied by one mole of any gas at a stated temperature and pressure; lets you swap between moles and gas volume.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: You can apply the mole ratio directly to masses or volumes given in the question. ✓ Right: The mole ratio only works between amounts in moles, so you must convert every quantity to moles first. ✗ Wrong: If two reactants are given, you can pick either one to base the calculation on. ✓ Right: You must identify the limiting reagent first; the reactant in excess is left over and does not set the amount of product.
