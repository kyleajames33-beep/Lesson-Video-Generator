## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Homogeneous and Heterogeneous Mixtures: A mixture is matter that contains more than one substance, combined physically rather than chemically, so each component keeps its own properties and can be separated again.
2. Physical Properties That Enable Separation: Every separation works by finding one physical property in which the components differ, then using a technique that responds to that difference.
3. Matching the Technique to the Property: Once you know which property differs, the right technique almost chooses itself.

## 4. Key terms

- Mixture: Two or more substances physically combined, not chemically bonded, so each keeps its own properties and can be separated by physical means.
- Homogeneous mixture: A mixture with a uniform composition throughout, with no visible boundaries between components (for example, salt water).
- Heterogeneous mixture: A mixture whose components are unevenly distributed and often visibly distinct (for example, sand in water).
- Physical property: A characteristic such as particle size, boiling point, density, solubility or magnetism that can be measured without changing the substance.
- Filtrate / residue: In filtration, the liquid that passes through the filter is the filtrate; the solid trapped on the paper is the residue.
