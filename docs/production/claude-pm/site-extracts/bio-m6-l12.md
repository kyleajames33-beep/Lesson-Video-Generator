## 3. Key ideas in order

1. A biotechnology evaluation identifies its benefit, risk, stakeholders and biodiversity effect before reaching a conditional conclusion.
2. Medical, agricultural and animal uses have different benefits and concerns, so one verdict does not fit them all.
3. A biodiversity question needs genetic, species or ecosystem effects, not productivity alone.
4. A synthesis response compares cases, weighs trade-offs and states when its conclusion holds.

## 4. Key terms

- Synthesis: “Combining multiple ideas and examples into one coherent judgement.”
- Evidence-based evaluation: “A judgement supported by relevant examples, conditions and limitations rather than absolute claims.”
- Trade-off: “A situation where a benefit is associated with a cost, risk or competing consequence.”
- Stakeholder impact: “How different groups are affected differently by the same biotechnology.”
- Qualified judgement: “A conclusion using conditional language such as ‘to a large extent’, ‘in some contexts’ or ‘provided that’.”

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- A strong answer lists all relevant facts. The page requires connected evidence and a judgement.
- Higher yield answers a biodiversity question. The page requires a named biodiversity level and effect.
- A gene drive always spreads completely and irreversibly. The page identifies resistance, fitness, design and population structure as limits.
