## 3. Key ideas in order

1. The Nucleotide Building Block: DNA is a polymer built from repeating units called nucleotides, each made of three parts: a phosphate group, a deoxyribose sugar, and a nitrogenous base.
2. Complementary Base Pairing: The two DNA strands are held together by base pairs that follow a strict rule, adenine always pairs with thymine, and cytosine always pairs with guanine.
3. The Double Helix and Hydrogen Bonds: The two strands twist into a double helix held by hydrogen bonds, individually weak, but numerous enough to be stable, while still easy to "unzip" when the DNA needs to be read or copied.
4. DNA in forensics: The complementary base-pairing rule you learned today is the engine behind DNA fingerprinting used in criminal investigations.

## 4. Key terms

- Nucleotide: The repeating unit of DNA, made of a phosphate group, a deoxyribose sugar and a nitrogenous base.
- Nitrogenous base: One of adenine (A), thymine (T), cytosine (C) or guanine (G); the "letters" of the genetic code.
- Complementary base pairing: The rule that A always pairs with T, and C always pairs with G.
- Hydrogen bond: A weak attraction that holds paired bases together; many together make the helix stable yet separable.
- Double helix: The twisted-ladder shape of two DNA strands wound around each other.
