## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Two Directions of Change: Evolution can make species more alike or more different, depending on whether their environments are similar or different, these are the two opposite patterns.
2. Analogous vs Homologous Structures: The internal structure of a body part reveals its history: shared underlying plans point to common ancestry (divergence), while similar function on different plans points to independent evolution (convergence).
3. Worked Examples of Each: Each pattern is best learned through a clear worked example that shows the selective pressure and the relatedness involved.
4. Why this matters: reading the past from bodies: Telling analogous from homologous structures is how scientists work out evolutionary relationships before they have any DNA, and it is essential for fossils, where DNA is usually gone.

## 4. Key terms

- Convergent evolution: Unrelated species independently evolving similar traits because they face similar selective pressures.
- Divergent evolution: Related species becoming increasingly different over time as they adapt to different environments.
- Analogous structures: Structures with similar function but different evolutionary origin (evidence of convergent evolution), e.g. bird and insect wings.
- Homologous structures: Structures with the same basic origin/plan but different functions (evidence of divergent evolution), e.g. the vertebrate forelimb.
- Common ancestor: A species from which two or more later species are both descended.
