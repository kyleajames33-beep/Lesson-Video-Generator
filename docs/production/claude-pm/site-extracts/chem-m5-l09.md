## 2. Syllabus content covered

“deduce the equilibrium expression (in terms of Keq) for homogeneous reactions occurring in solution (ACSCH079, ACSCH096)”

## 3. Key ideas in order

1. At equilibrium a ratio of product to reactant concentrations settles at a fixed Keq at a given temperature.
2. Products go on top, reactants below, each raised to its coefficient from the balanced equation as written.
3. Pure solids and pure liquids (including water as solvent) are excluded; gases, aqueous species and H₂O(g) are included.
4. Keq above 10³ means products are strongly favoured; below 10⁻³ reactants are; between them both are present.
5. Reversing an equation gives 1/Keq; multiplying all coefficients by n raises Keq to the nth power.
6. Only temperature changes Keq; concentration, pressure and catalysts shift the position only.

## 4. Key terms

- Equilibrium constant (Keq): “The ratio of product to reactant concentrations at equilibrium, each raised to stoichiometric power.”
- Homogeneous equilibrium: “Equilibrium where all species are in the same phase.”
- Heterogeneous equilibrium: “…pure solids and liquids omitted from Keq.”
- Stoichiometric coefficient: “The number in front of a species in a balanced equation; used as the exponent in the Keq expression.”
- Keq expression: “Written as Keq = [products]^p / [reactants]^r using molar concentrations in mol L⁻¹.”
- Inverse equilibrium: “If the reaction is reversed, the new Keq = 1/original Keq; if multiplied by n, Keq is raised to the nth power.”

## 5. Worked examples

- Worked Example 1: (a) 2NO(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g): Keq = [NO₂]² / ([NO]²[O₂]). (b) AgCl(s) ⇌ Ag⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq): Keq = [Ag⁺][Cl⁻]. (c) CO₂(g) + C(s) ⇌ 2CO(g): Keq = [CO]² / [CO₂]. (d) CH₃COOH(aq) ⇌ CH₃COO⁻(aq) + H⁺(aq): water is the solvent, excluded.
- Worked Example 2: N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), Keq = 977 at 300°C and 0.013 at 500°C. The reverse at 500°C: Keq = 1/0.013 = 76.9. The page rejects the claim that 0.013 means under 1.3% conversion, because conversion also depends on the initial conditions.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Including pure solids: “Never include a solid in a Keq expression.”
- Including H₂O in aqueous reactions: exclude H₂O(l) as solvent; include H₂O(g) in gas-phase reactions.
- Using the wrong equation version: “Always check which version of the equation is given.”

Possible site errors:
- Think First and Card 4 give Keq = 54 at 25°C for H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI; lesson 10 gives 54.3 at 430°C for the same equilibrium.
- Keq = 1 × 10¹² for 2NO + O₂ ⇌ 2NO₂ at 25°C has no basis stated; it is commonly quoted as a partial-pressure constant.
- Keq = 977 (300°C) and 0.013 (500°C) for the Haber equilibrium have no basis stated and do not match commonly tabulated Kp values (about 4.3 × 10⁻³ atm⁻² at 300°C; about 1.45 × 10⁻⁵ atm⁻² at 500°C).
- Guldberg and Waage are said to have measured K for N₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2NO in 1864; the 1 × 10⁻³⁰ value at 25°C is a later measurement.
