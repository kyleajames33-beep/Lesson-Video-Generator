## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Two Tempos of Evolution: Evolution by natural selection is not in dispute here, what these two models argue about is how fast change is spread out through time.
2. Reading the Two Models off a Tree: On a phylogenetic tree, time runs up the page and each branch is a lineage, so the slope and the timing of the splits reveal which tempo of evolution is being modelled.
3. What the Fossil Record Says: The fossil record does not crown one model the winner, different lineages show different tempos, so both gradualism and punctuated equilibrium can describe real evolutionary history.
4. Why tempo matters for predicting the future: The tempo of evolution is not just a fossil-hunter's puzzle.

## 4. Key terms

- Phylogenetic tree: A branching diagram showing the inferred evolutionary relationships among species; branch points are common ancestors, branch length can represent time or amount of change.
- Gradualism: The model that evolution proceeds by small, continuous changes accumulating steadily over long periods.
- Punctuated equilibrium: The model that species stay largely unchanged for long periods (stasis), interrupted by short bursts of rapid change, often at speciation.
- Stasis: A long period during which a species shows little or no morphological change.
- Speciation: The evolutionary process by which a new species forms from an existing one.
