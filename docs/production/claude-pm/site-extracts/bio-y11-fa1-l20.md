## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Unzipping at the Replication Fork: DNA replication begins when the double helix is unzipped at a replication fork, exposing each strand so it can act as a template for building a new complementary strand.
2. The Enzyme Team and the Two Strands: A team of enzymes copies the exposed strands: primase starts each new strand, DNA polymerase adds nucleotides, and ligase joins fragments, with one strand built continuously (leading) and one in pieces (lagging).
3. Assessing the Use of Models: Models make an invisible process easy to visualise and manipulate, but they simplify reality, assessing a model means weighing what it shows well against what it leaves out or distorts.
4. Copying DNA in a test tube: The natural replication you modelled today was harnessed by scientists to invent PCR (the polymerase chain reaction), a way to copy a chosen stretch of DNA millions of times in a tube.

## 4. Key terms

- Replication fork: The Y-shaped point where the double helix is unzipped and copying takes place.
- Helicase: The enzyme that unwinds and unzips the double helix by breaking the hydrogen bonds between bases.
- Primase: The enzyme that lays down a short RNA primer to give DNA polymerase a starting point.
- DNA polymerase: The enzyme that adds complementary nucleotides to build the new strand.
- Ligase: The enzyme that joins the fragments of the lagging strand into one continuous strand.
