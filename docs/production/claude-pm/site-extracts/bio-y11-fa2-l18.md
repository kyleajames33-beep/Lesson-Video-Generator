## 3. Key ideas in order

1. What Makes a Good Exchange Surface: Every efficient gas-exchange surface in biology shares the same four design features, and the lungs maximise all of them at once.
2. How Alveoli and Capillaries Are Organised: The genius of the lung is the arrangement: millions of tiny sacs each wrapped tightly in capillaries, putting air and blood almost in contact.
3. Models of Gas Exchange, Use and Limits: Models let us demonstrate why the lung's organisation is efficient, but, as always, a model trades accuracy for clarity.
4. Emphysema: when surface area is lost: In emphysema, often caused by smoking, the delicate walls between alveoli are destroyed, so many tiny sacs merge into fewer large ones.

## 4. Key terms

- Alveolus: A tiny air sac in the lung where gas exchange with the blood occurs; lungs contain millions of them.
- Gas exchange: The movement of oxygen into the blood and carbon dioxide out of the blood, by diffusion.
- Diffusion distance: The thickness a gas must cross; the shorter it is, the faster diffusion happens.
- Capillary network: The dense web of capillaries wrapping each alveolus, bringing blood right against the air.
- Concentration gradient: The difference in gas concentration between alveolar air and blood that drives diffusion.
