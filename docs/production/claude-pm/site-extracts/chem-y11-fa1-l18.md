## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Electronegativity Decides the Bond Type: Every bond is a tug-of-war for the bonding electrons.
2. Predicting Ion Charges from the Periodic Table: Atoms gain or lose electrons to reach the stable, full-shell configuration of the nearest noble gas.
3. The Ionic Lattice and Its Properties: Once cations and anions exist, each positive ion attracts every nearby negative ion and repels the like-charged ones, so the ions pack into a regular, repeating three-dimensional array in which oppositely charged ions alternate.

## 4. Key terms

- Electronegativity: A measure of how strongly an atom pulls on the shared electrons of a bond; it increases up and to the right of the periodic table.
- Ionic bond: The electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions formed by the transfer of electrons from a metal to a non-metal.
- Cation / anion: A positive ion formed when an atom loses electrons (cation); a negative ion formed when an atom gains electrons (anion).
- Ionic lattice: A regular, repeating three-dimensional arrangement of alternating cations and anions held together by electrostatic attraction.
