## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Calculating the Molar Mass M: Molar mass is just a sum: read the chemical formula, look up each element's relative atomic mass on the periodic table, multiply by how many of that atom appear, and add them all together.
2. Using n = m/M to Find Moles: If one mole weighs $M$ grams, then a sample of mass $m$ grams contains $m \div M$ moles, that is all the equation says.
3. Rearranging to Find Mass or Molar Mass: There is only one equation here.

## 4. Key terms

- Mole (mol): The SI unit for amount of substance, the number of particles equal to the Avogadro constant ($6.022\times10^{23}$).
- Amount of substance ($n$): How much of a substance you have, measured in moles, the quantity $n = \dfrac{m}{M}$ calculates.
- Molar mass ($M$): The mass of one mole of a substance, in g mol -1, found by summing the relative atomic masses in its formula.
- $n = \dfrac{m}{M}$: The NESA relationship linking amount of substance, mass and molar mass, the workhorse equation of quantitative chemistry.
