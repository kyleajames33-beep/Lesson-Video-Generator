## 3. Key ideas in order

1. The Sea-of-Electrons Model: In a metal, the loosely held valence electrons leave their atoms and pool together into a delocalised "sea", leaving behind a regular lattice of positive metal ions.
2. Explaining the Properties of Metals: Because the electron sea is mobile and the bond is non-directional, the same model explains why metals conduct electricity, can be reshaped, and look shiny.
3. Bond Strength, Melting Point and Conductivity: The strength of metallic bonding rises with the charge on the cation and the number of delocalised electrons, and that directly raises melting and boiling points.

## 4. Key terms

- Metallic bond: The electrostatic attraction between a lattice of positive metal ions and the surrounding sea of delocalised electrons.
- Delocalised electrons: Valence electrons that are no longer tied to one atom but are free to move throughout the whole metal.
- Cation lattice: The regular, repeating arrangement of positive metal ions left behind once valence electrons are delocalised.
- Malleability: The ability to be hammered or rolled into new shapes without breaking.
- Charge density: The amount of charge per ion size; higher charge and smaller ions give stronger metallic bonding.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: In a metal, the delocalised electrons belong to particular atoms and just jump between them. ✓ Right: The valence electrons are delocalised across the whole lattice and belong to no single ion, that is exactly why they conduct so freely. ✗ Wrong: Metals bend easily because the metallic bond is weak. ✓ Right: The bond is strong (hence high melting points), but it is non-directional, so layers of ions can slide over one another while staying bonded by the electron sea.
