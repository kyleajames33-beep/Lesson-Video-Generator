## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Two Strategies for Body Heat: Animals fall into two groups depending on whether their body heat comes from the environment or from their own metabolism.
2. Behavioural Adaptations in Australia: Because an ectotherm cannot generate much heat, it relies heavily on behaviour to gain or lose it; an endotherm uses behaviour too, but to support its internal heating and cooling.
3. Reading the Body-Temperature Graph: The relationship between an animal's body temperature and the air temperature is the clearest fingerprint of its thermoregulation strategy.
4. Why this matters: reptiles, roads and warming: Because ectotherms must bask to warm up, many Australian reptiles use sun-warmed roads in the morning, and are killed by traffic as a result.

## 4. Key terms

- Ectotherm: An animal that relies mainly on the external environment for body heat, so its body temperature varies with surroundings (e.g. reptiles, fish, insects).
- Endotherm: An animal that generates body heat internally through metabolism and keeps a stable body temperature (e.g. mammals, birds).
- Thermoregulation: Keeping body temperature within a range that allows enzymes and cells to function.
- Basking: A behavioural adaptation where an ectotherm positions itself in the sun to absorb heat.
- Torpor: A short-term, energy-saving drop in body temperature and activity used by some endotherms to survive cold or food shortage.
