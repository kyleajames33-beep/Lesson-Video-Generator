## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Preparing a Wet Mount Slide: A good wet mount keeps the specimen flat, hydrated and stained so its structures are clear and free of trapped air bubbles.
2. Measuring Cell Size with a Mini-Grid: A grid lying on the stage is magnified with the specimen, so reading a cell against it gives the real size straight away; the equation is what converts between a real size and a magnified image.
3. Specialised Cells Vary in Size: Cells are specialised for different jobs, and their sizes and shapes vary widely, measuring several reveals this range.
4. Cell size in the pathology lab: Measuring cells is not just a school exercise.

## 4. Key terms

- Wet mount: A slide where the specimen sits in a drop of liquid under a coverslip, keeping it hydrated and clear.
- Stain: A dye (e.g. iodine, methylene blue) that adds contrast so structures like the nucleus stand out.
- Magnification: How many times larger the image is than the real object (objective lens × eyepiece lens).
- Micrometre (µm): One-thousandth of a millimetre (1 mm = 1000 µm); the usual unit for cell size.
- Mini-grid: A grid of known square size placed on the stage beside the specimen.
