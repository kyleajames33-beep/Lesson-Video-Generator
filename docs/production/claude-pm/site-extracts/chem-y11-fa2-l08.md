## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Limiting vs Excess: What "Limiting" Means: Think of building bicycles: each needs 1 frame and 2 wheels.
2. Identifying the Limiting Reagent: You cannot judge the limiter by mass, you must compare how many "recipe portions" each reactant supplies.
3. Product Formed and Excess Left Over: Once the limiter is known, everything flows from its moles.

## 4. Key terms

- Limiting reagent: The reactant that runs out first; it is fully consumed and sets the maximum amount of product that can form.
- Excess reagent: The reactant present in more than enough quantity; some of it is left over after the reaction stops.
- Mole ratio: The ratio of coefficients in the balanced equation, used to convert moles of one substance into moles of another.
- Theoretical yield: The maximum amount of product calculated from the limiting reagent, assuming the reaction goes to completion.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: The reactant with the smaller mass is always the limiting reagent. ✓ Right: Mass alone tells you nothing. You must convert to moles and divide by each coefficient, the smallest result is the limiter.
