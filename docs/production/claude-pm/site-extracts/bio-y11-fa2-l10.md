## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Light and Temperature: Transpiration is fastest in bright, warm conditions because light opens the doorways out of the leaf and heat gives the water molecules more energy to escape.
2. Wind and Humidity: Whether water vapour keeps diffusing out depends on how dry the air just outside the leaf is, wind keeps it dry, humidity makes it moist.
3. Putting the Four Factors Together: All four environmental factors change the transpiration rate through just two mechanisms: how wide the stomata are open, and how steep the water-vapour gradient is out of the leaf.
4. Why farmers watch the weather to schedule irrigation: Australian farmers and horticulturalists use exactly these four factors to decide when and how much to water.

## 4. Key terms

- Transpiration: The evaporation of water from a plant, mainly as vapour diffusing out of the stomata in the leaf.
- Stomata: Pores on the leaf surface, opened and closed by guard cells, through which water vapour leaves and gases are exchanged.
- Water-vapour gradient: The difference in water-vapour concentration between the moist air inside the leaf and the drier air outside; a steeper gradient means faster diffusion out.
- Humidity: The amount of water vapour already in the surrounding air; high humidity reduces the gradient out of the leaf.
- Boundary layer: The thin layer of still, humid air clinging to the leaf surface that slows water loss until wind sweeps it away.
