## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Humans as a Selective Force: Humans change the environment so often and so strongly that our activities have become a major source of selective pressure, but the underlying mechanism is exactly the one from Lesson 1.
2. Chemicals: Antibiotic & Pesticide Resistance: A chemical such as an antibiotic does not target the susceptible cells' ability to mutate, it simply removes them, leaving the already-resistant minority to multiply.
3. Deliberate vs Accidental Selection: Human-induced pressures fall on a spectrum from deliberate selective breeding to entirely unintended consequences of other activities.
4. Why this matters: the antibiotic crisis: The World Health Organization lists antibiotic resistance among the greatest threats to global health.

## 4. Key terms

- Human-induced pressure: A selective pressure that results from human activity, e.g. pesticides, harvesting, pollution, habitat change.
- Artificial selection: Selective breeding, humans deliberately choosing which individuals reproduce, based on desired traits.
- Resistance: An inherited ability to survive exposure to a chemical (e.g. herbicide, antibiotic) that kills others of the species.
- Harvesting pressure: Selection caused by humans removing individuals with a particular trait, e.g. only catching the largest fish.
- Anthropogenic: Caused or produced by humans (from anthropos, human).
