## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Three Vessels, Three Jobs: Each vessel's wall is built for the pressure and the task it must handle: high-pressure delivery, low-pressure return, or close-up exchange.
2. Comparing the Three Vessels: Seeing the three cross-sections together makes the link between wall thickness, lumen width and the pressure each vessel handles obvious.
3. Using Models, Strengths and Limits: A model is a deliberate simplification that lets us see a relationship clearly, but every simplification leaves something out, so models must be used and judged with care.
4. Varicose veins and failing valves: When the valves in leg veins weaken, blood pools instead of being pushed back to the heart, stretching the vein into the bulging, twisted shape of a varicose vein.

## 4. Key terms

- Artery: A vessel carrying blood away from the heart, usually at high pressure; thick, muscular, elastic walls.
- Capillary: The smallest vessel, one cell thick, where exchange of materials with tissues occurs.
- Vein: A vessel returning blood to the heart at low pressure; thinner walls, wide lumen, with valves.
- Lumen: The hollow central space of a vessel through which blood flows.
- Valve: A flap that prevents the backflow of blood, keeping it moving in one direction (found in veins).
