## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Which Abiotic Factors, Where: Aquatic and terrestrial ecosystems share some abiotic factors but each also has factors that are specific to it.
2. Reading a Comparison Table: Laying the abiotic data out in a table makes the differences between ecosystems jump out, and lets you explain the life each supports.
3. Making the Comparison Fair & Meaningful: A meaningful comparison uses consistent methods and then explains the biological consequence of each abiotic difference.
4. Why this matters: warming seas and coral bleaching: Because reef corals live in a very stable temperature range, even a small rise of 1–2 °C above normal can trigger mass coral bleaching, as is now happening on the Great Barrier Reef.

## 4. Key terms

- Aquatic ecosystem: A water-based ecosystem, freshwater (rivers, lakes) or marine (reefs, estuaries, open ocean).
- Terrestrial ecosystem: A land-based ecosystem (e.g. forest, grassland, desert).
- Dissolved oxygen: The amount of oxygen gas dissolved in water, a key abiotic factor for aquatic life.
- Salinity: The concentration of dissolved salts in water (or soil); high in marine systems, low in fresh water.
- Turbidity: How cloudy water is from suspended particles; high turbidity reduces light penetration.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: The same abiotic factors matter equally in every ecosystem, so you measure the same things everywhere. ✓ Right: Some factors are shared (temperature, pH, light), but others are specific: dissolved oxygen, salinity and turbidity matter in aquatic systems, while soil type, wind and humidity matter more on land.
