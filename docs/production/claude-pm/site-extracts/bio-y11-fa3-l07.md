## 3. Key ideas in order

1. The Predation Model, Method: A simple, repeatable model of natural selection uses coloured paper dots as prey, a patterned background as the habitat, and you as the predator who removes the easiest-to-see prey each generation.
2. What the Results Show: As generations pass, the visible colour is removed faster than it reproduces, so the camouflaged colour comes to dominate, exactly mirroring how a selective pressure shifts a real population.
3. Discussing the Model's Validity: To judge a model's validity we weigh how faithfully it represents the real process, listing what it captures well and what it simplifies or ignores.
4. Comparing Various Models: A model is judged against the specific claim it is being used to support, so two models of the same process can both be valid while being valid for different things.
5. Why this matters: models run the modern world: Scientists model things they cannot watch directly all the time, climate decades ahead, the spread of a disease, the path of a spacecraft.

## 4. Key terms

- Model: A simplified representation of a system used to understand, demonstrate or predict how it behaves.
- Validity (of a model): How well a model represents the real process it stands for, whether its conclusions transfer to the real system.
- Generation: One round of survival and reproduction; in the model, one "hunt" followed by the survivors "breeding".
- Variable: A factor that can change; controlled variables are kept constant so the results are fair.
- Assumption: Something a model takes to be true to keep it simple; assumptions limit how far results can be trusted.
