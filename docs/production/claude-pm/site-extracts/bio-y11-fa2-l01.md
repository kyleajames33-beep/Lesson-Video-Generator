## 3. Key ideas in order

1. One Cell That Does Everything: A unicellular organism must carry out every function of life inside one cell, so that cell tends to be a "generalist" packed with all the structures needed for independent living.
2. Colonial Life, Together but Independent: A colony is a group of similar cells living together for a shared benefit, but with little specialisation, each cell remains structurally a generalist that could, in principle, survive alone.
3. Multicellular, Specialised and Dependent: Once cells specialise to do one job extremely well, they lose the ability to do the others, so they must depend on other specialised cells, and the organism becomes a single interdependent whole.
4. Why this matters for growing tissue in the lab: When scientists grow human cells in a dish to test drugs or build "lab-grown" skin, they relearn this lesson every day.

## 4. Key terms

- Unicellular: An organism whose entire body is a single cell that performs all life functions (e.g. Amoeba, yeast, bacteria).
- Colonial: A group of identical (or near-identical) cells that live together but where each cell could still survive on its own (e.g.
- Multicellular: An organism made of many specialised cells that depend on one another and cannot survive independently (e.g. animals, plants).
- Cell specialisation: The process by which cells develop different structures suited to a particular function (also called differentiation).
- Interdependence: A relationship in which cells rely on one another to survive, a hallmark of multicellular organisms.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: A colony of cells is the same thing as a multicellular organism, they are both "many cells stuck together". ✓ Right: In a colony the cells are largely identical and each can survive alone; in a multicellular organism the cells are specialised and interdependent, so a single cell cannot survive on its own.
