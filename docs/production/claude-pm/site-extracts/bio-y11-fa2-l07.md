## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Xylem, Water Going Up: Xylem is structured as a series of hollow, reinforced tubes precisely because its only job is to let water flow upward with as little resistance as possible.
2. Phloem, Sugar Going Anywhere: Phloem is built from living cells because transporting sugar against gradients and in changeable directions requires active, energy-using cells rather than empty pipes.
3. Structure Suits Function: Each structural feature of xylem and phloem exists because it makes that tissue better at its particular transport job.
4. Why ring-barking kills a tree: If you cut a complete ring of bark from around a tree trunk, "ring-barking", the tree dies, but slowly and from the bottom up.

## 4. Key terms

- Xylem: Vascular tissue made of dead, hollow vessels that transport water and dissolved minerals upwards from roots to leaves.
- Phloem: Vascular tissue made of living sieve tubes that transport sugars (sap) in any direction to where they are needed.
- Lignin: A strong, waterproof substance that thickens xylem walls, giving support and stopping the vessels collapsing.
- Sieve plate: A perforated end wall between phloem sieve-tube cells that lets sap flow from cell to cell.
- Companion cell: A living cell beside each sieve tube that provides the energy and metabolism the sieve tube lacks.
