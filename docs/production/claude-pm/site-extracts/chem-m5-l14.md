## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Ka is Keq for an acid dissociation, HA(aq) ⇌ H⁺(aq) + A⁻(aq), with water excluded as the solvent.
2. Kb is Keq for a base ionisation, with water excluded as a pure liquid.
3. A larger Ka means a stronger acid and more dissociation at equilibrium.
4. Ksp is Keq for an ionic solid dissolving, with the undissolved solid excluded.
5. Coefficients in a dissolution equation become powers in Ksp; a small Ksp means very little dissolves.
6. ΔG° = −RT ln Keq links the size of Keq to whether a reaction is spontaneous.

## 4. Key terms

- Acid dissociation constant (Ka): "The equilibrium constant for the partial ionisation of a weak acid: Ka = [H⁺][A⁻]/[HA]."
- Base dissociation constant (Kb): "The equilibrium constant for the partial ionisation of a weak base: Kb = [BH⁺][OH⁻]/[B]."
- Solubility product (Ksp): "The equilibrium constant for a sparingly soluble ionic solid dissolving; the solid itself is excluded."
- Gibbs free energy (ΔG°): "ΔG° = −RT ln Keq; negative ΔG° means spontaneous reaction and Keq > 1."

## 5. Worked examples

**Worked Example 1 (Apply/Analyse).** Write the Keq expression for (a) HF(aq) ⇌ H⁺(aq) + F⁻(aq), Ka = 6.8 × 10⁻⁴; (b) HNO₂(aq) ⇌ H⁺(aq) + NO₂⁻(aq), Ka = 4.5 × 10⁻⁴; (c) NH₃(aq) + H₂O(l) ⇌ NH₄⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq); (d) BaSO₄(s) ⇌ Ba²⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq), value about 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁰.
Answer: (a) Ka = [H⁺][F⁻]/[HF]. (b) Ka = [H⁺][NO₂⁻]/[HNO₂]; HF is the stronger weak acid. (c) Kb = [NH₄⁺][OH⁻]/[NH₃], water excluded. (d) Ksp = [Ba²⁺][SO₄²⁻], solid excluded; a value near 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁰ means very little dissolves.

**Worked Example 2 (Analyse/Evaluate).** H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g), Keq = 54.3 at 430°C: T = 703 K; ΔG° = −(8.314)(703)(3.994) = −23.3 kJ/mol, so the forward reaction is spontaneous. At 25°C (Keq = 794), ΔG° = −16.5 kJ/mol, less negative because the temperature factor is smaller.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- "A negative ΔG means a reaction will happen quickly." Page: it is thermodynamically spontaneous and "says nothing about reaction rate."
- "Ka is a different kind of constant from Keq." Page: "Ka is NOT a different type of constant."
- Writing water into the Ka expression, or the undissolved solid into Ksp.
- Using T = 25 instead of 298 K. Page: result "approximately 12× too small".
- Confusing Ka with pH or [H⁺]. Page: "Ka is not pH and not the concentration of H⁺."
