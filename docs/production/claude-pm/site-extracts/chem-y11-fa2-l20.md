## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Moles Are the Common Currency: A balanced equation only ever speaks in moles, so to use it you must first translate whatever you are given, a mass, a concentration, or a gas volume, into moles.
2. Worked Example: Mass of Metal to Volume of Gas: When zinc reacts with excess hydrochloric acid, the mass of zinc fixes the moles of hydrogen gas, and the ideal gas equation turns those moles into a volume at the stated pressure and temperature.
3. Starting from a Solution Instead of a Mass: The three-step pattern does not change when the known quantity is a solution, only the very first conversion does, using $n = cV$ to turn a concentration and volume into moles.

## 4. Key terms

- Mole ratio: The ratio of coefficients in a balanced equation, used to convert moles of one substance into moles of another.
- Ideal gas equation: $pV=nRT$, relating pressure, volume, moles and temperature of a gas, with $R = 8.314\ \text{J K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}$.
- Limiting reagent: The reactant that is fully used up first and therefore determines how much product can form; the other reactant is in excess.
- Consistent units: Using matching SI units (pressure in Pa, volume in m³, temperature in K) so the value of $R$ applies correctly.
