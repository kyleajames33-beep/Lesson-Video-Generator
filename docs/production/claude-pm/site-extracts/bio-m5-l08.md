## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Diploid cells contain homologous pairs; gametes must be haploid to prevent chromosome number doubling at fertilisation.
2. One DNA replication precedes two divisions: meiosis I separates homologues and halves chromosome number; meiosis II separates sister chromatids, producing four haploid cells.
3. Crossing over in prophase I and independent assortment at metaphase I generate new combinations of existing alleles.
4. Meiosis halves chromosome number and fertilisation restores it, maintaining the count across generations while allowing variation.
5. The Assess walkthrough weighs replication fidelity and variation against failures such as mutation, non-disjunction and uncontrolled mitosis.
6. The Explain walkthrough distinguishes recombination within chromosomes from random combinations of whole chromosomes and links both to random fertilisation.

## 4. Key terms

- Meiosis: Cell division that halves chromosome number to produce haploid gametes for sexual reproduction.
- Homologous chromosomes: A matching pair of chromosomes carrying the same genes in the same positions, one inherited from each parent.
- Diploid: Having two sets of chromosomes, one from each parent.
- Haploid: Having one set of chromosomes.
- Crossing over: Exchange of corresponding chromosome segments between homologous chromosomes during meiosis.
- Independent assortment: Random orientation of homologous chromosome pairs during meiosis, producing different chromosome combinations in gametes.

## 5. Worked examples

Activity 1: species body cells have 12 chromosomes. Answers: diploid body cell, 12; haploid gamete after meiosis, 6; zygote after fertilisation, 12. 

Activity 2 answers: homologues exchanging segments, crossing over; different maternal/paternal chromosome mixes, independent assortment; two haploid gametes forming a diploid zygote, fertilisation restoring chromosome number.

Explain walkthrough: 23 homologous pairs in humans give over eight million whole-chromosome combinations through independent assortment alone; crossing-over combinations multiply this variation. Assess walkthrough cites replication error control reducing errors to roughly one in a billion base pairs.

Assess (7 marks) answer: replication serves continuity by balancing fidelity and variation, despite mutation, non-disjunction and cancer; fidelity dominates somatic cells and variation the germ line. Explain (6 marks) answer: prophase-I crossing over recombines alleles within chromosomes; metaphase-I assortment shuffles whole chromosomes; their independent effects multiply.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Gametes should be made by mitosis: diploid gametes would double chromosome number at fertilisation.
- Meiosis produces four identical cells: its haploid products differ genetically.
- Crossing over is an error: regulated exchange between non-sister chromatids is normal and helps homologues align.
- Crossing over or assortment creates new alleles: they change combinations; mutation creates new alleles.
