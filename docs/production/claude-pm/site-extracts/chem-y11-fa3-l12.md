## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Collisions and Reaction: A chemical reaction cannot happen unless reactant particles actually meet, so the starting point of collision theory is simple: particles must collide before they can react.
2. Activation Energy ($E_a$): For a collision to react, the colliding particles must carry at least a certain minimum amount of energy, and that minimum is called the activation energy, $E_a$.
3. Orientation and Frequency Together: Even when colliding particles have energy $\ge E_a$, they will only react if they meet in the correct orientation, that is, the right geometry for the relevant bonds to break and form.

## 4. Key terms

- Collision theory: The model that reactions occur only when particles collide with sufficient energy and the correct orientation.
- Successful (effective) collision: A collision that has enough energy and the right orientation, so it actually produces products.
- Activation energy ($E_a$): The minimum energy that colliding particles must have for a reaction to occur.
- Collision frequency: How often particles collide per unit time; more frequent collisions give more chances for a successful one.
