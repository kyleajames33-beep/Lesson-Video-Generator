## 3. Key ideas in order

1. The Four Bonding Types and Their Fingerprints: Every bonding type leaves a recognisable "fingerprint" in four easily tested properties, relative melting point, solubility in water, electrical conductivity and hardness.
2. The Tests, and Why Conductivity Is Done Three Ways: The most powerful single test is conductivity, but only because it is done in three states, solid, molten and in solution, since each bonding type behaves differently across them.
3. Safety, Validity and Reliability: A classification is only as good as the fairness of the comparison, so the same quantity, same equipment and same conditions must be used for every unknown.

## 4. Key terms

- Electrical conductivity: Whether a substance lets charge flow, it needs free-moving charged particles (delocalised electrons, or mobile ions).
- Mobile charge carriers: Charged particles free to move: delocalised electrons in metals, or ions freed when an ionic solid melts or dissolves.
- Covalent network: A giant lattice of atoms joined by continuous covalent bonds (e.g. diamond, silicon dioxide), very hard, very high melting point.
- Validity: Whether the investigation tests what it claims to, achieved by controlling variables and using comparative, repeatable methods.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: If a solid does not conduct electricity, it cannot be ionic. ✓ Right: Ionic solids do not conduct when solid because the ions are locked in the lattice, they conduct only when molten or dissolved, once the ions are free to move. ✗ Wrong: Dissolving in water is proof a substance is ionic. ✓ Right: Many covalent molecular substances (e.g. sugar) also dissolve.
