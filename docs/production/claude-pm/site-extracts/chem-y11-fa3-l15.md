## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Exothermic Reactions, Heat Out: In an exothermic reaction the chemicals push heat out into everything around them, which is why the test tube, water or your hand gets warmer.
2. Endothermic Reactions, Heat In: In an endothermic reaction the chemicals draw heat in from everything around them, so the surroundings lose energy and feel cooler.
3. Energy, the Surroundings and the Sign of ΔH: Chemists split the world into the system (the reacting chemicals) and the surroundings (the solvent, container and air), and $\Delta H$ is measured from the system's point of view.

## 4. Key terms

- Exothermic reaction: A reaction that releases heat to the surroundings, so the surroundings warm up; the products store less energy than the reactants ($\Delta H < 0$).
- Endothermic reaction: A reaction that absorbs heat from the surroundings, so the surroundings cool down; the products store more energy than the reactants ($\Delta H > 0$).
- Enthalpy change ($\Delta H$): The heat energy released or absorbed by a reaction at constant pressure; its sign tells you which way heat flows.
- System and surroundings: The system is the reacting chemicals; the surroundings are everything else (solvent, container, air) that exchanges heat with them.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: "Endothermic" means the reaction feels hot because heat is going in. ✓ Right: An endothermic reaction absorbs heat from the surroundings, so the surroundings lose energy and feel cold. ✗ Wrong: Exothermic reactions release energy, so they don't need any energy to get started. ✓ Right: Even exothermic reactions need an input of activation energy to begin; they release more energy than they take in, giving a net release.
