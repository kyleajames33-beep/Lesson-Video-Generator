## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Gay-Lussac's Law and Particle Theory: At constant volume, heating a fixed amount of gas raises its pressure, because the particles move faster and strike the container walls harder and more often.
2. Calculating with Kelvin Temperatures: The proportion $p \propto T$ only works if $T$ is measured from absolute zero, that is, in kelvin, so $\degree$C must always be converted first.
3. Reading the Data: A Straight Line Through the Origin: If pressure is directly proportional to absolute temperature, then dividing each pressure by its kelvin temperature gives the same constant value, and a graph of $p$ against $T$(K) is a straight line through the origin.

## 4. Key terms

- Gay-Lussac's law: For a fixed amount of gas at constant volume, the pressure is directly proportional to the absolute (kelvin) temperature: $p \propto T$.
- Absolute temperature: Temperature measured in kelvin (K), starting from absolute zero.
- Constant volume: A closed, rigid container whose volume does not change, so any change in pressure is due only to temperature.
- Directly proportional: Two quantities that increase together at a constant ratio, so their graph is a straight line passing through the origin.
