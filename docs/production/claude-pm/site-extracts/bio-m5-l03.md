## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Flowering plants transfer pollen to the stigma, grow a pollen tube and fertilise an egg in the ovule, which becomes a seed.
2. Vegetative propagation produces plants from parent tissue without fertilisation.
3. Fungi use budding/spores and bacteria use binary fission.
4. Bacterial conjugation transfers DNA between existing cells and adds variation without producing cells.
5. Protists use binary fission or budding.
6. Compare mechanisms by speed, protection, dispersal and variation, then match them to environmental conditions.

## 4. Key terms

- Pollination: Transfer of pollen from anther to stigma in flowering plants.
- Fertilisation: Fusion of male and female gametes to form a zygote.
- Vegetative propagation: Asexual reproduction in plants using structures such as runners, bulbs, tubers or cuttings.
- Budding: Asexual reproduction in which a new organism grows from the body of the parent.
- Spore: A reproductive cell that can develop into a new organism under suitable conditions.
- Binary fission: Asexual reproduction in which one cell replicates its DNA and divides into two daughter cells.

The 5-mark Explain model states bacteria can divide in as little as twenty minutes; numerous replications generate mutations, horizontal transfer spreads alleles through pili/free DNA/bacteriophages, and antibiotics select already-resistant cells that divide, changing populations within days.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Pollination equals fertilisation: pollen transfer precedes sperm/egg fusion in the ovule.
- Self- and cross-pollination produce identical offspring: the page contrasts increased homozygosity with greater diversity from another plant.
- Only multicellular organisms reproduce: one cell dividing produces new individuals.
- Fast reproduction means high variation: copying and variation are different advantages.
- Conjugation is reproduction: it exchanges DNA without cell division.
- Antibiotics create resistance: they select resistance already present.

"Outline sexual reproduction in flowering plants from pollination to seed formation."

"Explain why bacterial populations can adapt rapidly despite reproducing asexually."

Answers: bee/seed sexual; tuber, yeast bud and binary fission asexual; mould spores usually asexual here. Stable pollinator-free paddock: runners; new crop disease: sexual reproduction; nutrient-rich broth: binary fission; distant fungal colonisation: spores. Asexual efficiency does not always provide long-term continuity in changing conditions.
