## 1. Title and main question

Ka, pKa and Acid Strength Rankings. How do Ka and pKa compare acid strength, conjugate bases and successive ionisations?

Source: `C:/Users/kygs/hscscience/subjects/chemistry/year12/module6/lesson12.sa.html`.

## 2. Syllabus content covered

“calculate and apply the dissociation constant (Ka) and pKa (pKa = -log10 (Ka)) to determine the difference between strong and weak acids (ACSCH098).”

## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Ka measures an acid's extent of ionisation at a given temperature; solution pH also depends on concentration.
2. Larger Ka means stronger acid; smaller pKa means stronger acid.
3. For a conjugate pair, Ka(HA) × Kb(A⁻) = Kw, so acid and conjugate-base strengths rank in opposite directions.
4. Polyprotic acids ionise in separate steps with Ka₁ much greater than later Ka values; the page relates this to removing H⁺ from increasingly negative ions.
5. The page applies Ka comparisons to cola and sparkling water, then to food, pharmaceutical and environmental analysis.

## 4. Key terms

- Ka: “Measures the extent of acid ionisation; larger Ka = stronger acid.”
- pKa: “pKa = −log₁₀(Ka); smaller pKa = larger Ka = stronger acid.”
- Polyprotic acid: more than one ionisable proton, each step with its own Ka.
- Levelling effect: water makes strong acids appear equally strong because they ionise fully.
- Oxoacid strength: the page links it to the number of oxygen atoms.

## 5. Worked examples

- For acids P, Q, R and S with Ka 1.3 × 10⁻², 8.4 × 10⁻⁵, 2.1 × 10⁻⁸ and 6.7 × 10⁻¹², the page ranks P > Q > R > S. Their pKa values are 1.89, 4.08, 7.68 and 11.17; conjugate-base Kb values are 7.7 × 10⁻¹³, 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁰, 4.8 × 10⁻⁷ and 1.5 × 10⁻³.
- For 0.0150 mol/L H₂CO₃ with Ka₁ 4.3 × 10⁻⁷ and Ka₂ 4.7 × 10⁻¹¹, the page gives pH 4.10, Ka₁/Ka₂ about 10⁴, Kb(CO₃²⁻) 2.1 × 10⁻⁴, and predicts basic Na₂CO₃ solution.
- A sports-drink example gives citric acid at 0.0200 mol/L (Ka₁ 7.4 × 10⁻⁴) and malic acid at 0.0150 mol/L (Ka 3.5 × 10⁻⁴), asking for separate pH contributions, a pKa ranking and dental-health assessment.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Larger pKa means stronger acid; the page reverses this ranking.
- Ka × Kb = Kw for unrelated acids and bases; it applies to a conjugate pair.
- One Ka expression removes all protons from a polyprotic acid; each ionisation has its own equilibrium.
- Equal carbonic acid content means cola and sparkling water affect enamel equally; the page points to cola's additional acids.

Tooth enamel hydroxyapatite, cola, sparkling water, vinegar, fruit juice, wine tartaric acid, dairy lactic acid, aspirin quality control, acid rain, wastewater pH probes and calibrated glass electrodes.
