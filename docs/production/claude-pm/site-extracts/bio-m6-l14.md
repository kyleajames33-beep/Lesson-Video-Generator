## 1. Title and main question

Reproductive Technologies, Artificial Insemination and Artificial Pollination. How do the two methods control fertilisation and inherited traits?

## 2. Syllabus content covered

“compare the processes and outcomes of reproductive technologies, including but not limited to:”

The page compares artificial insemination and artificial pollination.

## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Both methods choose which parents contribute gametes; neither directly edits a gene sequence.
2. Artificial insemination collects, prepares and introduces selected semen into a female at the appropriate time.
3. Artificial pollination transfers selected pollen to a chosen stigma while excluding unwanted pollen.
4. Both make desired trait combinations more likely, but meiosis and fertilisation still produce variation.

## 4. Key terms

- Artificial insemination: “Deliberate introduction of semen into the female reproductive tract without natural mating.”
- Artificial pollination: “Deliberate transfer of selected pollen to a stigma to control plant fertilisation.”
- Controlled breeding: “Managing which parents contribute gametes to offspring.”
- Trait control: “Increasing the likelihood that selected inherited characteristics appear in offspring.”

## 5. Worked examples

- A six-mark comparison contrasts semen collection, storage and timed introduction with pollen collection, stigma transfer, anther removal and bagging. It compares parental reach and diversity effects.
- A six-mark analysis considers 10,000 cows bred to five bulls to explain how heavy use of few sires can reduce effective population size and diversity.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- “Artificial” means DNA was edited. Both methods change the reproductive event.
- Selected parents guarantee the desired offspring. The methods increase probability, not certainty.
- Selective breeding produces only desired traits. The page notes increased homozygosity and harmful recessive traits.
