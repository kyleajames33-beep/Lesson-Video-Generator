## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Structural Adaptations, the Body's Build: A structural adaptation is a physical part of an organism's body, anything about its shape, size, covering or internal parts that you could point to in a diagram.
2. Physiological & Behavioural Adaptations: Not every survival feature is visible, some are internal chemical processes, and some are things the organism does rather than is.
3. Adaptations Are Shaped by Evolution: An adaptation is not a lucky accident an individual makes, it is the end result of natural selection acting on inherited variation over many generations.
4. Why this matters: designing for a changing climate: Understanding the three adaptation types helps ecologists predict which species will cope as Australia's climate warms and dries.

## 4. Key terms

- Adaptation: An inherited feature, shaped by natural selection, that increases an organism's chance of survival and reproduction in its environment.
- Structural adaptation: A physical feature of an organism's body, its shape, size, colour or parts (e.g. thick fur, long roots).
- Physiological adaptation: An internal, often chemical or metabolic process that helps survival (e.g. concentrated urine, venom, tolerance of salt).
- Behavioural adaptation: An inherited action or behaviour that aids survival (e.g. migrating, being active at night, basking in the sun).
- Selective pressure: An environmental factor (such as heat, drought or predators) that favours some variants over others, driving adaptation.
