## 3. Key ideas in order

1. The Summary Equation: A single summary equation captures what cellular respiration achieves: it takes glucose and oxygen and releases carbon dioxide, water and usable energy.
2. Reading the Equation Both Ways: The equation is a compact description, so understanding it means knowing what every reactant and product represents and remembering it stands for many smaller reactions.
3. When Oxygen Runs Out: Anaerobic Respiration: When oxygen is scarce, cells can still release some energy from glucose, but the pathway and its products change.
4. Bread, beer and the breath you exhale: Anaerobic respiration in yeast is one of the oldest biotechnologies on Earth.

## 4. Key terms

- Cellular respiration: The process in which cells break down glucose to release energy stored in its chemical bonds.
- Aerobic: Requiring oxygen, releases the most energy from glucose.
- Anaerobic: Without oxygen, releases far less energy and makes products such as lactic acid or ethanol.
- ATP: Adenosine triphosphate, the energy-carrying molecule the cell makes during respiration and spends on work.
- Summary equation: A single equation that shows the overall reactants and products of a multi-step process.
