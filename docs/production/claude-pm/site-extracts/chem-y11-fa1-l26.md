## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Diamond, a Tetrahedral Covalent Network: In diamond each carbon atom forms four strong covalent bonds to four neighbours in a rigid three-dimensional tetrahedral lattice, and that single structural fact explains all three of its key properties.
2. Graphite, Layers and Delocalised Electrons: In graphite each carbon bonds to only three neighbours in flat hexagonal layers, leaving one valence electron delocalised, and the layers are held to each other only weakly, which together explain every property.
3. Graphene, Fullerenes and Comparing the Data: Graphene and the fullerenes are newer carbon allotropes that follow exactly the same logic, their properties flow from how many bonds each carbon forms and how the structure is arranged.

## 4. Key terms

- Allotrope: One of two or more structural forms of the same element, differing in how its atoms are bonded and arranged.
- Covalent network: A giant lattice in which atoms are joined by continuous covalent bonds throughout the structure.
- Delocalised electron: A valence electron not fixed to one atom, free to move through the structure and carry charge.
- Secondary source: Information collected and reported by others (textbooks, databases, journals) rather than measured first-hand.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: Graphite is soft because the covalent bonds between its carbon atoms are weak. ✓ Right: The covalent bonds within each layer are strong; graphite is soft because the layers are held together only by weak dispersion forces, so they slide over each other. ✗ Wrong: Diamond conducts electricity because it is a network of carbon atoms. ✓ Right: In diamond every valence electron is locked into a covalent bond, so there are no free electrons, it does not conduct.
