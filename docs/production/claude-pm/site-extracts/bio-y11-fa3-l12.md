## 3. Key ideas in order

1. The Osmotic Problem: An aquatic animal's body fluids rarely match the salt concentration of the water around it, so osmosis constantly pushes water in or pulls it out.
2. Freshwater vs Saltwater Solutions: Each animal's adaptations exactly reverse the direction of its osmotic threat, gaining what it loses and shedding what floods in.
3. Why These Are Physiological Adaptations: Each adaptation is an internal process that the body carries out continuously, often using active transport, rather than a body part you draw or an action the animal chooses.
4. Why this matters: estuaries, fish farms and salinity: Osmoregulation explains which fish can live where, and that knowledge drives real decisions.

## 4. Key terms

- Osmoregulation: The control of water and salt (solute) balance in body fluids to keep them within a workable range.
- Hypertonic: A solution with a higher solute (salt) concentration than another; water tends to move toward it by osmosis.
- Hypotonic: A solution with a lower solute concentration than another; water tends to move away from it by osmosis.
- Active transport: Movement of substances against a concentration gradient, requiring energy (ATP); used by gills to pump salts in or out.
- Salt gland: A specialised gland (in marine birds and reptiles) that excretes excess salt taken in with seawater or food.
