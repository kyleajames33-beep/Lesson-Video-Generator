## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Somatic Cells, the Body Cells: Somatic cells are all the body cells of an organism except the sex cells; they are diploid, carrying two full sets of chromosomes.
2. Gametic Cells, the Sex Cells: Gametic cells (gametes) are the sex cells, sperm and egg, which are haploid, carrying only one set of chromosomes, and are specialised for fertilisation.
3. Comparing Structures and Roles: Somatic and gametic cells have complementary roles: somatic cells maintain the body's chromosome number through mitosis, while gametes halve it through meiosis so that fertilisation restores it exactly.
4. Why some mutations are inherited and others are not: The somatic–gametic distinction has real consequences in medicine.

## 4. Key terms

- Somatic cell: A body cell (e.g. skin, muscle, nerve) that is diploid and not involved in reproduction.
- Gametic cell (gamete): A sex cell (sperm or egg) that is haploid and fuses at fertilisation.
- Diploid (2n): Having two full sets of chromosomes (46 in humans).
- Haploid (n): Having one set of chromosomes (23 in humans).
- Fertilisation: The fusion of two haploid gametes to form a diploid zygote.
