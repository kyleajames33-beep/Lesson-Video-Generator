## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Abiotic and Biotic Features: An ecosystem is made of living organisms and the non-living environment together, and the two are linked, because conditions decide what can live there.
2. The Investigation, Collecting Data: Each abiotic factor has a measuring instrument, and biotic abundance is sampled with quadrats placed in a planned way.
3. Analysing & Discussing the Ecosystem: Analysis means organising the data, calculating averages, and explaining the living community in terms of the conditions you measured.
4. Why this matters: monitoring real ecosystems: Professional ecologists collect exactly these abiotic and biotic data to monitor the health of rivers, reefs and bushland.

## 4. Key terms

- Ecosystem: A community of living organisms together with the non-living environment they interact with in a particular area.
- Abiotic factor: A non-living physical or chemical feature of the environment (e.g. temperature, light, pH, moisture, salinity).
- Biotic factor: A living component of the environment (e.g. plants, animals, fungi and their interactions).
- Quadrat: A square frame of known area placed on the ground to sample the organisms inside it.
- Abundance: The number of individuals of a species in an area (or a measure such as percentage cover).
