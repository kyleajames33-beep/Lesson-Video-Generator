## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Molarity and Mass-per-Volume Measures: Every concentration measure answers the same question, how much solute is in a given amount of solution, but each chooses a different unit for "how much" and "how much solution".
2. Percentage Concentrations: A percentage concentration is "parts of solute per hundred parts of solution", the difference is only whether each part is measured by mass or by volume.
3. Parts per Million and Parts per Billion: For trace amounts, percentages become awkward, so chemists scale up to parts per million (ppm) and parts per billion (ppb), one part of solute in a million, or a billion, parts of solution.

## 4. Key terms

- Concentration: The amount of solute dissolved in a given amount of solution.
- Molarity (mol L -1 ): Moles of solute per litre of solution, the standard laboratory measure of concentration.
- % (w/v): Percentage weight in volume: grams of solute per 100 mL of solution, used for medical and aqueous solutions.
- ppm: Parts per million, one part of solute in $10^{6}$ parts of solution; equals mg L -1 for dilute aqueous solutions.
- ppb: Parts per billion, one part of solute in $10^{9}$ parts of solution, for even smaller traces than ppm.
