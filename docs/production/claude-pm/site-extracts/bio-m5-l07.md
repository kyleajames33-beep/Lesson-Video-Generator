## 3. Key ideas in order

1. DNA replicates during S phase of interphase, producing identical sister chromatids before mitosis begins.
2. Prophase condenses chromosomes, metaphase aligns them, anaphase separates sister chromatids, and telophase reforms nuclei before cytokinesis divides the cytoplasm.
3. Each daughter cell receives the parent's chromosome number, maintaining stability rather than reducing the count.
4. Mitosis supports growth, repair and some asexual reproduction; meiosis instead reduces chromosome number for gametes.
5. The Explain walkthrough links attached sister copies, opposite-pole spindle attachments and checkpoint control to accurate distribution.
6. The Compare walkthrough contrasts divisions, daughter-cell numbers, chromosome numbers, variation and functions while noting shared replication and spindle machinery.

## 4. Key terms

- Mitosis: Cell division that produces two daughter cells with the same chromosome number as the parent somatic cell.
- Somatic cell: A body cell that is not a gamete.
- Chromosome: A condensed DNA-protein structure carrying hereditary information.
- Chromatid: One of two identical copies of a replicated chromosome joined together before separation.
- Cytokinesis: Division of the cytoplasm to form separate daughter cells.
- Genetic stability: Maintenance of the correct chromosome number and hereditary information in daughter cells.

## 5. Worked examples

Activity 1 givens: A, sister chromatids move to opposite poles; B, DNA replicates before division; C, chromosomes line up at the equator; D, two daughter cells form after cytokinesis. Answer: B → C → A → D. Replication provides a full copy, alignment prepares even separation, anaphase separates chromatids, and cytokinesis forms two cells.

Activity 2 answers: skin repair replaces damaged somatic cells; growth increases body-cell number while maintaining chromosome number; asexual reproduction generates individuals through repeated stable division.

Compare walkthrough: mitosis has one division producing two diploid cells genetically identical to the parent; meiosis has two successive divisions producing four differing haploid cells.

Explain (6 marks) answer: replication makes attached sister copies, metaphase anchors them to opposite poles, and a checkpoint verifies attachment before anaphase separates them. Compare (5 marks) concludes that faithful copies support tissues while haploid, varied gametes support sexual reproduction.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Mitosis produces genetically different daughters: daughters are genetically identical, barring replication errors.
- Mitosis happens only in early growth: renewal continues in skin, gut lining, blood cells and wound repair.
- Mitosis halves chromosome number or mainly creates variation: its main role is stable copying and distribution.
- DNA replication occurs during mitosis: it occurs beforehand in S phase.
