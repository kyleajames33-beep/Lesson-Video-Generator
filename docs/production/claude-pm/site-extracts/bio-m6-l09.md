## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Ethical evaluation asks who benefits, who bears costs or risks, and which values affected groups hold.
2. Plant biotechnology may improve yield, pest resistance, drought tolerance or nutrition; seed ownership, crop diversity and environmental effects also matter.
3. Animal biotechnology may improve disease resistance or medical use; welfare, stress and suffering become central questions.
4. The page offers five questions covering the problem addressed, beneficiaries, risks, welfare or environmental harm, and equitable access.

## 4. Key terms

- Stakeholder: Person or group affected by a biotechnology.
- Ethical use: Use judged against fairness, welfare, harm, autonomy, environmental responsibility and justice.
- Food security: Reliable access to sufficient, safe and nutritious food.
- Equity: Fair access to benefits, risks and decision-making power.
- Ownership: Control of technologies, patents, products or biological material.
- Animal welfare: Consideration of animals’ suffering, stress and quality of life.

## 5. Worked examples

Golden Rice, reported in 2000, is described as engineered to supply beta-carotene to help address vitamin-A deficiency. The page maps the concerns of farmers, public-health workers, researchers, regulators and affected communities. A weak judgement, “This biotechnology is good because it increases production,” is rewritten to include stakeholders and harms as well as benefits.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Increased yield alone settles the ethics. The page asks about ownership, environmental effects and distribution.
- GM crop debates concern only biological performance. Seed access and local biodiversity also feature.
- Animal productivity is enough to justify a technology. Welfare remains a separate consideration.
- All cultures universally accept biotechnology. Different groups can weigh benefits and concerns differently.

## 7. Practical or real-world examples used

Golden Rice; GM crops; patented seeds; livestock disease resistance; animal biomedical research and therapeutic production.
