## 3. Key ideas in order

1. What gravimetric analysis is, and how it is done: Gravimetric analysis is a quantitative technique that determines the amount of an analyte (the substance being measured) by converting it into a pure, insoluble precipitate, then measuring the mass of that precipitate.
2. Why Must the Precipitate Be Insoluble?: If the precipitate dissolves even slightly, some of the analyte stays in solution and is never collected on the filter.
3. Why Add Excess Precipitating Reagent?: Adding a slight excess of the precipitating reagent (e.g. excess BaCl₂ when precipitating SO₄²⁻) drives the reaction to completion, ensuring all of the analyte has been converted to precipitate.
4. The Gravimetric Procedure, Step by Step: Gravimetric analysis determines analyte concentration by converting it to a weighed insoluble precipitate (BaSO₄, AgCl, CaCO₃).
5. Gravimetric vs Volumetric Analysis: Gravimetric analysis determines analyte concentration by converting it to a weighed insoluble precipitate (BaSO₄, AgCl, CaCO₃).
6. The three-step calculation chain: We just saw the gravimetric procedure, dissolve, precipitate, filter, dry, and weigh.
