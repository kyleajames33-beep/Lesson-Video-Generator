## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Knowledge of Adaptations on Country: To use an organism's adaptation, you first have to understand that adaptation in detail, what the feature does for the organism and how it works.
2. Using Plant Adaptations: Many plants produce defensive chemicals, a physiological adaptation against herbivores, and these same chemicals can serve human purposes such as fishing, medicine or food preparation.
3. Using Animal Adaptations & Behaviour: Knowing an animal's behavioural adaptation, its migration, breeding time or daily movement, tells you exactly when and where it can be reliably and sustainably harvested.
4. Why this matters: knowledge shaping modern science: First Nations knowledge of species' adaptations is increasingly recognised as a vital scientific resource.
5. Sources, and how to write about this knowledge: Each case study above names the People and the Country it belongs to, and cites a published source, because that is both respectful practice and checkable science.

## 4. Key terms

- Country / Place: The land, waters and skies to which a group of Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander People belong, and the knowledge, responsibilities and relationships connected to it.
- First Nations knowledge: The detailed, place-based knowledge of species and ecosystems developed and passed on by Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples over many thousands of years.
- Plant secondary compound: A chemical (such as a toxin or saponin) a plant produces as a defence, a physiological adaptation that can be put to human use.
- Seasonal indicator: A reliable natural sign (such as a plant flowering) that signals the timing of another event, used to know when a resource is available.
- Purpose: The specific human use to which an organism's adaptation is put, e.g. food, medicine, tools, fibre or fishing.
