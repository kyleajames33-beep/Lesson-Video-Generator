## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Calculating SA:V: As an object grows, its volume grows faster than its surface area, so the surface available for each unit of inside contents shrinks.
2. Why a High SA:V Means Fast Exchange: Materials enter and leave a cell only through its surface, so a cell with more surface per unit of volume can supply and clear its interior faster.
3. SA:V Sets the Limit on Cell Size: Because exchange cannot keep up once a cell grows too big, organisms either keep their cells small or adapt their shape to boost surface area.
4. Why small animals can't stop eating: A shrew, one of the smallest mammals, has such a high SA:V that it loses body heat across its surface incredibly fast, so fast that it must eat almost its own body weight in food every day just to stay warm, and will starve to death within hours if it stops.

## 4. Key terms

- Surface area: The total area of the outer boundary of a cell, the membrane through which materials must pass.
- Volume: The amount of space inside the cell, the metabolising contents that demand supply and produce waste.
- SA:V ratio: Surface area divided by volume; a measure of how much exchange surface there is per unit of cell contents.
- Rate of exchange: How quickly materials move across the membrane per unit of cell volume, higher when SA:V is high.
- Diffusion distance: How far a molecule must travel from the surface to the cell's centre, longer in larger cells.
