## 3. Key ideas in order

1. The Dilution Principle and c₁V₁ = c₂V₂: Diluting a solution means adding more solvent.
2. Solving Dilution Problems: Every dilution problem has the same four quantities, $c_1$, $V_1$, $c_2$, $V_2$.
3. Preparing a Primary Standard and Diluting It: A primary standard is a substance you can trust to give an accurately known concentration: it is pure, stable, non-hygroscopic, and has a known formula.

## 4. Key terms

- Dilution: Adding solvent (usually water) to a solution to lower its concentration; the amount of solute in moles stays the same.
- $c_1V_1 = c_2V_2$: The dilution equation, concentration × volume before equals concentration × volume after, because the moles of solute are unchanged.
- Primary standard: A pure, stable, non-hygroscopic substance of known formula and high purity that can be weighed accurately to make a solution of precisely known concentration.
- Volumetric flask: Glassware calibrated to contain one accurate volume up to a marked line, used to make a solution of known concentration.
- Stock solution: A more concentrated solution kept on hand, from which dilute working solutions are prepared.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: Diluting a solution changes the number of moles of solute in it. ✓ Right: The moles of solute stay the same, you only add solvent. The concentration drops because the same moles are now spread through a larger volume.
