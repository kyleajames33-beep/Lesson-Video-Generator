## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Three Kinds of Solution: Tonicity compares the solute concentration outside the cell with inside, and that comparison tells you which way water will move.
2. Animal Cells: No Wall, No Safety Net: An animal cell has only its membrane to contain it, so gaining or losing too much water has serious consequences.
3. Plant Cells: The Wall Changes Everything: A plant cell's rigid wall resists swelling, so the same osmosis produces very different, non-fatal outcomes.
4. Why IV fluids must be isotonic: When you receive fluids through a drip in hospital, the saline is carefully made isotonic (about 0.9% NaCl) with your blood.

## 4. Key terms

- Hypertonic: A solution MORE concentrated (more solute) than the cell, water leaves the cell by osmosis.
- Hypotonic: A solution LESS concentrated (less solute) than the cell, water enters the cell by osmosis.
- Isotonic: A solution with the SAME concentration as the cell, no net movement of water.
- Lysis / crenation: An animal cell bursting (lysis) in hypotonic, or shrivelling (crenation) in hypertonic solution.
- Turgid / plasmolysed: A plant cell firm and full (turgid) in hypotonic, or with membrane pulled from the wall (plasmolysed) in hypertonic.
