## 1. Title and main question

pH and pOH, Calculations for Strong Acids & Bases. How do concentration, dilution and mixing determine pH?

Source: `C:/Users/kygs/hscscience/subjects/chemistry/year12/module6/lesson08.sa.html`.

## 2. Syllabus content covered

“conduct a practical investigation to measure the pH of a range of acids and bases”; “calculate pH, pOH, hydrogen ion concentration ([H+]) and hydroxide ion concentration ([OH–]) for a range of solutions (ACSCH102)”.

## 3. Key ideas in order

1. pH = −log[H₃O⁺]; one pH unit is a tenfold concentration change.
2. Strong monoprotic acid gives [H₃O⁺] = c; the page uses 2c for dilute H₂SO₄ and notes its second ionisation is actually partial.
3. Strong base calculations find [OH⁻], then pOH = −log[OH⁻] and pH = 14 − pOH at 25°C.
4. Dilution conserves moles, lowers ion concentration and moves pH toward neutral.
5. For strong acid/base mixtures, subtract reacting moles and divide the excess by combined volume before finding pH.

## 4. Key terms

- pH: Negative base-10 logarithm of [H₃O⁺].
- pOH: Negative base-10 logarithm of [OH⁻].
- Excess reagent: Reactant remaining after neutralisation.
- Dilution: Adding water to lower concentration without changing acid strength.

## 5. Worked examples

- 0.025 mol/L HNO₃: pH 1.60; 0.0040 mol/L Ca(OH)₂: pH 11.90; 0.80 g NaOH in 500 mL: pH 12.60.
- 25.0 mL 0.200 mol/L HCl diluted to 500 mL: pH 2.00; diluted to 5000 mL: pH 3.00. At pH 3.40: [H₃O⁺] = 3.98 × 10⁻⁴ and [OH⁻] = 2.51 × 10⁻¹¹ mol/L.
- 40.0 mL 0.150 mol/L HCl + 60.0 mL 0.0800 mol/L Ba(OH)₂: 3.60 × 10⁻³ mol OH⁻ excess in 0.100 L; pH 12.56. Using only acid volume gives erroneous pH 12.95.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- A 0.3 pH change is negligible: it doubles or halves [H₃O⁺].
- H₂SO₄ gives one H⁺ per formula unit in the page's dilute-solution convention: it uses two.
- Negative pH is impossible: concentrated acid can have pH below zero.
- Use only acid volume after mixing: use total mixture volume.

## 7. Practical or real-world examples used

pH measurement of acids and bases; blood pH 7.40 to 7.10; HCl, HNO₃, NaOH, KOH, Ca(OH)₂ and Ba(OH)₂ solutions.

## 8. Activities and questions

Calculate pH from concentration or mass, reverse pH to ion concentrations, dilute, mix and correct student calculations. Representative: “40.0 mL of 0.150 mol/L HCl is mixed with 60.0 mL of 0.0800 mol/L Ba(OH)₂. (a) Calculate the pH of the resulting mixture at 25°C.”
