## 3. Key ideas in order

1. What Nardoo Is, and Why Processing Matters: Nardoo ( Marsilea drummondii ) is an aquatic fern found across inland Australia.
2. The Chemistry of Decomposition: A decomposition reaction is one in which a single compound is broken down into two or more simpler substances.
3. Connecting Traditional Practice to Reaction Classification: The detoxification of nardoo is classified as a decomposition reaction because a single, complex toxic substance (the thiaminase enzyme) is broken down into simpler products that can no longer harm thiamine.

## 4. Key terms

- Nardoo: An aquatic fern ( Marsilea drummondii ) whose spore cases (sporocarps) are ground into a flour and prepared as food by Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples.
- Thiaminase: An enzyme present in raw nardoo that destroys thiamine (vitamin B1); if eaten unprocessed it causes a severe B1-deficiency illness.
- Decomposition reaction: A reaction in which one compound is broken down into two or more simpler substances, general form AB → A + B.
- Leaching: Washing a ground material in water (often flowing water) so soluble or reactive components are broken down and carried away.
