## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Temperature is the only factor that changes Keq; concentration, pressure and catalysts move the position only.
2. For an exothermic forward reaction, raising T lowers Keq; for an endothermic one, raising T raises Keq.
3. A Keq-versus-temperature table answers three points: the direction of change, what it implies about ΔH, and the prediction outside the range.
4. Colourimetry works because FeSCN²⁺ is intensely red and absorbance is proportional to concentration (A = εlc).
5. A calibration curve turns absorbance into [FeSCN²⁺]eq; an ICE table then gives the other equilibrium concentrations, and Keq follows.
6. Dilution is applied to the initial concentrations before the ICE table.
7. ΔG° = −RT ln Keq links Keq to thermodynamics (extension).

## 4. Key terms

- Temperature effect on Keq: “Unlike LCP shifts, changing temperature changes the value of Keq itself.”
- Exothermic reaction and Keq: “Increasing temperature decreases Keq (less product favoured at higher T) for exothermic reactions.”
- Endothermic reaction and Keq: “Increasing temperature increases Keq (more product favoured at higher T) for endothermic reactions.”
- van 't Hoff relationship: “Links change in Keq with temperature change; higher T gives larger Keq for endothermic reactions.”
- Colourimetry: “A technique using absorbance of light to determine concentration of a coloured species in solution (Beer–Lambert law).”
- Absorbance: “A = εlc; proportional to concentration; used to track changes in coloured equilibrium systems like NO₂/N₂O₄.”

## 5. Worked examples

- Think First (Fe³⁺ + SCN⁻ ⇌ FeSCN²⁺, exothermic): Keq = 895 at 25°C, 580 at 35°C, 360 at 45°C.
- Worked Example 1: A(g) + B(g) ⇌ 2C(g): Keq = 0.25 at 300°C, 1.10 at 400°C, 4.80 at 500°C. Keq rises with T, so the forward reaction is endothermic; at 200°C Keq is below 0.25.
- Colourimetry Card 4: [Fe³⁺]₀ = [SCN⁻]₀ = 1.00 × 10⁻³; [FeSCN²⁺]eq = 1.50 × 10⁻⁴. Equilibrium [Fe³⁺] = [SCN⁻] = 8.50 × 10⁻⁴. Keq = 1.50 × 10⁻⁴ / (8.50 × 10⁻⁴)² = 207.6 ≈ 208.
- Investigation dilution: 5.00 mL of 0.00200 mol L⁻¹ Fe³⁺ in 10.00 mL gives [Fe³⁺]₀ = 0.00100 mol L⁻¹.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- “A large Keq means the reaction happens quickly.” Keq indicates the equilibrium position, not the rate.
- Warming FeSCN²⁺ “used up the FeSCN²⁺, so Keq must have gone down.” The page says the reasoning “runs backwards”: the temperature change lowered Keq, and the system shifted left.
- Using bottle concentrations in the ICE table: “Apply the dilution before the ICE table, every time.”

Possible site errors:
- Think First gives Keq = 895 at 25°C for Fe³⁺ + SCN⁻ ⇌ FeSCN²⁺. Card 4 and Worked Example 2 give Keq ≈ 208 for the same equilibrium. Worked Example 2 says only “at this temperature”; the investigation's inquiry question says “at room temperature”.
- Extended Response: “a higher absorbance at 35°C than at 25°C” means more FeSCN²⁺ at the higher temperature, so Keq rises with T. The page elsewhere states this forward reaction is exothermic, where Keq falls as T rises.
