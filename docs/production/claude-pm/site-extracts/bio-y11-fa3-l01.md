## 3. Key ideas in order

1. The Three Ingredients: Natural selection is not a single force but a result that emerges when three ordinary biological facts come together in a population.
2. From Variation to Evolution: Natural selection always runs through the same four steps: variation exists, a pressure acts, survivors reproduce, and the population shifts.
3. When Natural Selection Cannot Act: The three ingredients are each necessary: take any one away and the population cannot evolve by natural selection, no matter how harsh the environment.
4. Why this matters: antibiotic resistance: Natural selection is not just history, it is happening in hospitals right now.

## 4. Key terms

- Genetic variation: The differences in alleles (gene versions) between individuals in a population, arising from mutation and sexual reproduction.
- Inheritance: The passing of alleles from parents to offspring, so that heritable traits can be transmitted to the next generation.
- Selective pressure: An environmental factor (e.g. predators, climate, food supply) that affects an organism's chance of surviving and reproducing.
- Fitness: An organism's reproductive success, how many surviving offspring it leaves, relative to others in the population.
- Evolution: A change in the heritable characteristics (allele frequencies) of a population across successive generations.
