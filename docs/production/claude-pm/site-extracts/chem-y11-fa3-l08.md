## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Reactivity with Oxygen and Water: The first clue to a metal's reactivity is how eagerly it reacts with the two most common substances in the lab, the oxygen in air and water.
2. Reactivity with Dilute Acid: A metal that is more reactive than hydrogen will displace hydrogen gas from a dilute acid.
3. Metal-Ion Solutions and a Valid Method: The most direct comparison places one metal into a solution of another metal's ions.

## 4. Key terms

- Reactivity: How readily a metal loses electrons and takes part in a chemical reaction; a more reactive metal reacts faster and more vigorously.
- Tarnish: The slow, surface reaction of a metal with oxygen that dulls its shine, as opposed to burning brightly.
- Displacement reaction: A reaction in which a more reactive metal pushes a less reactive metal out of its compound or solution.
- Qualitative observation: A recorded description of what is seen, heard or felt (bubbles, heat, colour change) rather than a measured number.
- Fair test: An investigation in which only one variable is changed while all others (surface area, concentration, temperature) are kept the same.
