## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Large data sets improve the strength of inference and pattern detection, but "they never convert inference into absolute certainty".
2. Conservation genetics uses population data to detect reduced diversity, isolation or bottlenecks.
3. Diversity improves adaptive capacity, but "Do not say genetic diversity guarantees survival".
4. Disease inheritance studies show how disease-linked variants are distributed, supporting variant recognition and carrier frequency comparison.
5. Populations sharing more genetic markers likely share more recent common ancestry.

## 4. Key terms

- Genetic diversity: The variety of alleles present within a population.
- Disease inheritance study: A study of how variants linked to disorders are distributed within and between groups.

## 5. Worked examples

UK Biobank: "launched in 2006, enrolled 500,000 participants aged 40-69", with "800,000 variants per participant".

Sickle cell case (Analyse, 7 marks): frequency data showed the allele at high levels in parts of Africa, the Mediterranean and South Asia; pedigrees "established autosomal recessive inheritance"; molecular data "identified a single base substitution in the haemoglobin gene"; malaria distribution supplied the explanation of heterozygote advantage.

Explain card: "In malarial regions, individuals heterozygous for the sickle cell allele resist malaria better than unaffected homozygotes while avoiding sickle cell anaemia".


## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- "High genetic diversity guarantees that a population will survive." Page: "Diversity improves adaptive capacity, it does NOT guarantee survival."
- "The Human Genome Project sequenced the genomes of all humans." Page: it sequenced "a reference genome derived from a small number of donors", a starting map, not a universal template.
- "A harmful allele must disappear because selection acts against it." Page: most copies of a rare allele sit in unaffected heterozygotes, and heterozygote advantage or drift can keep it common.

Possible site errors: (a) "a single small study of 100 patients can only detect variants that cause 10% of a disease's genetic risk", "A hospital study of 200 patients produces results dominated by chance" and "variants that individually shift disease risk by less than 1%": no source is given and the figures look invented; check before use. (b) "20 terabytes of genomic data": unsourced; check what is counted. (c) "prohibits commercial exploitation without oversight": UK Biobank allows approved commercial users under its access terms, so "prohibits" overstates the position.
