## 3. Key ideas in order

1. DNA in Prokaryotic Cells: In a prokaryote, the DNA exists as a single circular chromosome lying free in the cytoplasm, often accompanied by small extra rings of DNA called plasmids.
2. DNA in Eukaryotic Cells: In a eukaryote, DNA exists as several linear chromosomes wound tightly around histone proteins and enclosed within the membrane-bound nucleus.
3. Comparing the Two Forms: The DNA molecule is identical in both cell types; only its shape, number, packaging and location differ.
4. Plasmids power genetic engineering: The humble bacterial plasmid you drew today is one of biotechnology's most important tools.

## 4. Key terms

- Chromosome: A single, long DNA molecule (with associated proteins in eukaryotes) carrying many genes.
- Plasmid: A small, extra circle of DNA in prokaryotes, separate from the main chromosome.
- Nucleoid: The region of a prokaryotic cell where the circular chromosome sits, not membrane-bound.
- Histone: A protein around which eukaryotic DNA is wound to package it tightly.
- Linear: Having two ends (a line), as opposed to circular (a closed loop).
