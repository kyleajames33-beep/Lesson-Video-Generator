## 3. Key ideas in order

1. How Dialysis Cleans the Blood: Dialysis is, at heart, controlled diffusion: blood flows past a membrane with a carefully designed fluid on the other side, so only the wastes leave.
2. Dialysis vs the Kidney: Dialysis copies the kidney's outcome (clean blood) but uses a simpler mechanism, which means it matches some functions and misses others.
3. Evaluating Your Sources: A secondary-source investigation is only as good as its sources, so judging their reliability is part of the science, not an optional extra.
4. Living on dialysis in Australia: Thousands of Australians rely on dialysis, typically spending around four hours, three times a week, hooked to a machine, a major life impact that a transplant can remove.

## 4. Key terms

- Renal dialysis: A treatment that removes wastes and excess water from the blood when the kidneys cannot, using a machine.
- Dialysate: The fluid on the other side of the dialysis membrane, matched to healthy blood so only wastes diffuse out.
- Selectively permeable membrane: A barrier that lets small molecules (urea, salts) pass but not large ones (cells, proteins).
- Secondary source: Information gathered by someone else (textbooks, websites, data), used and evaluated rather than measured first-hand.
- Reliability: Whether a source is trustworthy and consistent, e.g. peer-reviewed, recent, from a recognised body.
