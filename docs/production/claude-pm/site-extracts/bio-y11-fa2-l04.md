## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Gas Requirements: A plant runs two opposite gas-using processes, photosynthesis and respiration, so it requires both carbon dioxide and oxygen, just at different times and places.
2. Water Requirements: Water is needed for far more than photosynthesis: it carries dissolved substances, keeps cells firm, and is itself a reactant.
3. Mineral Nutrient Requirements: Glucose supplies carbon, hydrogen and oxygen, but a plant must build proteins, DNA and chlorophyll too, and these need extra elements that come from soil minerals.
4. Why fertiliser bags carry three numbers: Every bag of garden fertiliser is stamped with an "N-P-K" ratio, the proportions of nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium it supplies.

## 4. Key terms

- Autotroph: An organism that makes its own organic food (glucose) using an external energy source, plants use light in photosynthesis.
- Photosynthesis: The process that uses light energy, carbon dioxide and water to make glucose and oxygen.
- Respiration: The process in every living cell that uses glucose and oxygen to release energy, producing carbon dioxide and water.
- Mineral nutrient: An inorganic ion (e.g. nitrate, magnesium) absorbed from the soil and needed to build essential molecules.
- Stomata: Pores, mainly on the underside of leaves, through which gases enter and leave the plant.
