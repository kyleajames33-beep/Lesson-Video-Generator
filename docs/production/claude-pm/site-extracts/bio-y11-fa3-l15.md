## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Resistance Is Natural Selection in Fast-Forward: A resistant population is not produced by the drug; it is produced by selection acting on variation that already exists in the population.
2. Two Case Studies: Bacteria and Mosquitoes: Antibiotic resistance in bacteria and DDT resistance in mosquitoes are two instances of one process, only the organism and the human chemical differ.
3. Evaluating the Evidence, and the Cost: A secondary source is only as trustworthy as its methods, so analysing resistance means judging the data as well as reading the trend.
4. Why this is on every hospital wall: "Antimicrobial resistance" is named by the World Health Organization as one of the top global public-health threats.

## 4. Key terms

- Antibiotic resistance: The ability of a bacterial strain to survive and reproduce in the presence of an antibiotic that would normally kill it.
- DDT: Dichloro-Diphenyl-Trichloroethane, a synthetic insecticide once widely used against malaria-carrying mosquitoes.
- Human-induced selective pressure: A selective pressure created by human activity (e.g. using a drug or insecticide) that changes which individuals survive and reproduce.
- Resistant strain: A population descended from the rare survivors, in which the resistance trait is now common.
- Secondary source: Data or information collected and reported by someone else (e.g. published surveillance graphs), which you analyse rather than gather yourself.
