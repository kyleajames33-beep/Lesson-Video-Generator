## 3. Key ideas in order

1. The Calorimetry Method: Calorimetry works on one simple idea: the heat released by a reaction is absorbed by a known mass of water, so the water's temperature rise lets you measure the energy.
2. Calculating ΔH from Data: There are two steps: first find the heat the water gained with $q = mc\Delta T$, then divide by the moles of fuel ($n = \dfrac{m}{M}$) to get the energy per mole, attaching a negative sign because combustion is exothermic.
3. Accuracy and Secondary-Source Comparison: The data book is your secondary source: the accepted standard enthalpy of combustion of methanol is about ΔcH ≈ −726 kJ mol -1, and your experimental value is almost always smaller in magnitude than this.

## 4. Key terms

- Calorimetry: Measuring the heat released or absorbed by a reaction from the temperature change it causes in a known mass of water.
- Specific heat capacity ($c$): The energy needed to raise 1 g of a substance by 1 °C; for water $c = 4.18$ J g -1 °C -1.
- Molar enthalpy (ΔH): The heat energy released or absorbed per mole of reactant, in kJ mol -1; negative for exothermic, positive for endothermic.
- Secondary-source data: Accepted values published in a data book or reliable database, used to judge the accuracy of your own experimental result.
