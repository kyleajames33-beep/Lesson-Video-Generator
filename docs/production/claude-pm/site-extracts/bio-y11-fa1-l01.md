## 3. Key ideas in order

1. The Two Fundamental Cell Types: Every cell that has ever been found belongs to one of two groups, and the dividing line is simply whether the DNA is wrapped inside its own membrane.
2. Comparing the Architecture: The presence of a nucleus comes bundled with a whole set of other differences in size, DNA shape, and internal organisation.
3. How We Know, Using a Variety of Sources: What we can claim about a cell's structure is limited by the tool we use to look at it, better resolution revealed detail that earlier scientists simply could not see.
4. Why this difference matters in medicine: The structural gap between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells is exactly what makes antibiotics possible.

## 4. Key terms

- Prokaryote: A cell with no membrane-bound nucleus or organelles; DNA lies free in the cytoplasm (e.g. bacteria).
- Eukaryote: A cell with a membrane-bound nucleus and membrane-bound organelles (e.g. plant, animal, fungal cells).
- Organelle: A specialised structure within a cell that carries out a particular function.
- Nucleoid: The region of a prokaryotic cell where the single circular chromosome sits, not enclosed by a membrane.
- Resolution: The smallest distance between two points that a microscope can still show as separate.
