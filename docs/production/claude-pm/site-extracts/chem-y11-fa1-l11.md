## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Rows, Columns and the Configuration Behind Them: The periodic table is arranged in the same order that electrons fill energy levels, so the row and column an element sits in are really just labels for its electron configuration.
2. The s, p, d and f Blocks: The table splits into four broad regions, and the region an element belongs to is set entirely by the type of sublevel its final electron goes into.
3. Reading the Table Backwards, and Why Groups Match: Because the link is two-way, an element's position lets you reconstruct its valence shell, and that same valence shell is what makes a whole group behave alike.

## 4. Key terms

- Period: A horizontal row of the periodic table; its number equals the highest principal energy level (n) being filled.
- Group: A vertical column of the periodic table; main-group members share the same number of valence electrons.
- Valence electrons: The electrons in the highest occupied (outer) energy level, which largely determine chemical behaviour.
- Block: A region of the table (s, p, d or f) named for the sublevel that the last electron occupies.
