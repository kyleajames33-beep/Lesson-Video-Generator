## 3. Key ideas in order

1. The Data: Tabulating Melting Points: A secondary-source investigation begins by gathering reliable published data and laying it out in a clear table so that any patterns become visible.
2. The Within-Group Trend: Ionic Radius: The strength of the electrostatic attraction between two ions weakens as the distance between their centres increases.
3. The Between-Group Trend: Cation Charge: The electrostatic attraction between ions grows with the product of their charges.

## 4. Key terms

- Lattice energy: The energy released when gaseous ions come together to form one mole of an ionic lattice; the more energy involved, the stronger the lattice and the higher the melting point.
- Ionic radius: The effective size of an ion; cations get larger down a group as more shells are added.
- Charge density: The charge of an ion relative to its size; a higher charge packed into a smaller radius gives a stronger pull on neighbouring ions.
- Secondary-source investigation: An investigation that collects, tabulates and interprets data published by others, rather than measured in your own laboratory.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: A bigger, heavier metal ion makes a stronger, higher-melting lattice. ✓ Right: A larger ionic radius spreads the charge over more space, weakening the electrostatic attraction, so melting point tends to fall down a group despite the greater mass. ✗ Wrong: Only the cation's charge matters when comparing melting points. ✓ Right: Both charge and radius matter together.
