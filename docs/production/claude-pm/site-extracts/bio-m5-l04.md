## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Sperm flagellum, mitochondria and acrosome support egg access, while egg cytoplasm supports early development.
2. Oviduct fertilisation restores diploid chromosomes, then mitotic cleavage during transport produces a blastocyst that implants in the uterus.
3. The placenta exchanges oxygen/nutrients towards the fetus and wastes towards the mother without direct blood mixing.
4. Hormones maintain pregnancy, coordinate labour and support lactation.

## 4. Key terms

- Fertilisation: Fusion of sperm and egg to form a diploid zygote.
- Blastocyst: An early embryo stage formed after repeated cell division, ready for implantation.
- Implantation: Attachment and embedding of the blastocyst into the uterine lining.
- Placenta: An organ that enables exchange between maternal and fetal blood systems without direct mixing.
- Embryo / fetus: Developing stages of the offspring during pregnancy.
- Oxytocin: A hormone involved in uterine contractions and milk ejection.

## 5. Worked examples

No numerical calculation. The 7-mark Analyse model links fertilisation, implantation and hormones through timing: the endometrium is receptive for a few days; progesterone maintains it; implanted chorion produces hCG to sustain the corpus luteum; the placenta later supplies progesterone. Cervical stretching and oxytocin form positive feedback until delivery. Corpus luteum failure before placental takeover can end pregnancy.

The 5-mark Explain model contrasts progesterone inhibition of pituitary FSH/LH, preventing further ovulation during a nine-month gestation, with cervical pressure stimulating oxytocin and progressively stronger contractions until delivery removes the stimulus.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Fertilisation equals implantation: fertilisation occurs in the oviduct, blastocyst implantation later in uterine lining.
- The zygote implants immediately: cleavage and blastocyst formation come first.
- Maternal/fetal blood mix or placenta is entirely maternal: exchange crosses membranes; placenta includes maternal and embryonic tissues.
- Fertilisation always triggers immediate development: delayed implantation and dormant seeds can suspend development.
- Prolactin and oxytocin are interchangeable: prolactin produces milk; oxytocin ejects it and stimulates labour.
