## 3. Key ideas in order

1. What Specific Heat Capacity Means: Specific heat capacity is simply how "thirsty" a substance is for energy: how much heat 1 gram of it must absorb to get 1 °C hotter.
2. The Experiment: A Fair Comparison: The trick is to make the energy input identical for each substance, so any difference in temperature rise must come from the substance itself, not from how much heat it received.
3. Comparing and Interpreting the Results: The equation that ties everything together is $q = mc\Delta T$, where $q$ is the heat added, $m$ the mass, $c$ the specific heat capacity and ΔT the temperature change.

## 4. Key terms

- Specific heat capacity ($c$): The energy needed to raise the temperature of 1 g (or 1 kg) of a substance by 1 °C. Units: J g -1 °C -1 (or J kg -1 °C -1 ).
- Temperature change (ΔT): The final temperature minus the initial temperature, ΔT = T final − T initial, measured here with a thermometer.
- Heat (q): The thermal energy transferred to a substance, in joules (J). Linked to mass, $c$ and ΔT by $q = mc\Delta T$.
- Controlled variable: A quantity (such as mass, energy input or starting temperature) kept the same across each trial so the comparison is fair.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: A substance that reaches a higher temperature must have absorbed more energy. ✓ Right: With the same energy and mass, the substance that gets hotter has the lower heat capacity, it needs less energy per degree. ✗ Wrong: Heat and temperature are the same thing. ✓ Right: Heat ($q$) is energy transferred in joules; temperature is how hot something is.
