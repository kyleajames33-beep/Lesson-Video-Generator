## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Naming Binary Ionic Compounds: A binary ionic compound is named by writing the metal first, unchanged, then the non-metal with its ending changed to -ide, and you never write a number prefix.
2. Roman Numerals and Polyatomic Ions: Transition metals can form more than one ion, so a Roman numeral in the name states which charge is present, and many compounds contain polyatomic ions that keep their own name.
3. Naming Simple Covalent Compounds and Acids: Two non-metals can combine in several ratios, so covalent compounds use prefixes to count the atoms of each element explicitly.

## 4. Key terms

- IUPAC nomenclature: The international set of rules that gives every substance one unambiguous, systematic name.
- Binary compound: A compound made of exactly two elements, such as NaCl or CO 2.
- Polyatomic ion: A charged group of covalently bonded atoms that acts as a single unit, such as sulfate (SO 4 2− ).
- Roman numeral (oxidation state): A numeral such as (II) or (III) written after a metal name to show its charge when more than one is possible.
- Covalent prefix: A prefix (mono, di, tri, tetra…) showing how many of each atom are in a molecular compound.
