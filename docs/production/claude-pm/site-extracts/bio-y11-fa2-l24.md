## 3. Key ideas in order

1. The Blood-Sugar and Stress Hormones: Blood glucose is held steady by two opposing pancreatic hormones, while a separate pair of stress hormones can raise it quickly when the body needs energy fast.
2. Diabetes and the Loss of Control: Diabetes shows what the blood-glucose hormones do by revealing what happens when they cannot act: glucose control breaks down and homeostasis is lost.
3. Reading Hormone Data: When you graph a hormone level alongside the variable it controls, a regulating role shows up as the hormone rising whenever the variable strays from its set point, then falling as the variable returns.
4. Continuous glucose monitors: feedback you can watch: Many Australians with diabetes now wear a continuous glucose monitor, a small sensor that reads blood glucose every few minutes and streams the data to a phone.

## 4. Key terms

- Insulin: A pancreatic hormone that lowers blood glucose by promoting glucose uptake by cells and its storage as glycogen in the liver.
- Glucagon: A pancreatic hormone that raises blood glucose by stimulating the liver to break down glycogen and release glucose.
- Adrenaline: A fast-acting stress hormone from the adrenal glands that prepares the body for "fight or flight", including raising blood glucose.
- Cortisol: A slower stress hormone from the adrenal cortex that sustains raised blood glucose during prolonged stress.
- Diabetes mellitus: A condition in which blood glucose is not properly controlled, due to a lack of insulin (Type 1) or reduced response to insulin (Type 2).
