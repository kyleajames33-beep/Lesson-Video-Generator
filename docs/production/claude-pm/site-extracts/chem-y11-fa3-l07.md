## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Metal Displacement Is a Redox Reaction: When a more reactive metal meets a less reactive metal's ions, electrons flow from the metal atom to the ion.
2. Writing and Combining Half-Equations: A half-equation shows only one half of the story, either the loss of electrons or the gain, with the electrons written in.
3. Predicting Products Using Half-Equations: To predict the products, first decide which way the electrons flow.

## 4. Key terms

- Metal displacement: A reaction in which a more reactive metal pushes a less reactive metal out of its compound or solution.
- Half-equation: An equation showing just the oxidation or just the reduction, with electrons (e − ) written explicitly.
- Oxidation: Loss of electrons, shown by an increase in oxidation state (OIL: Oxidation Is Loss).
- Reduction: Gain of electrons, shown by a decrease in oxidation state (RIG: Reduction Is Gain).
- Spectator ion: An ion present in solution that does not change during the reaction and is left out of the ionic equation.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: When you combine the two half-equations you just add the number of atoms. ✓ Right: You first scale each half-equation so the electrons lost exactly equal the electrons gained, then add.
