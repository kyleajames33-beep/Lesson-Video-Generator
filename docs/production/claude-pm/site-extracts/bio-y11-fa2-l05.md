## 3. Key ideas in order

1. The Required Conditions: Photosynthesis can only proceed when light energy, raw materials, a working enzyme system and the light-absorbing pigment are all present together.
2. Limiting Factors: At any moment, photosynthesis runs only as fast as its most scarce condition allows, so increasing any other condition has no effect until the scarce one is topped up.
3. Temperature, A Condition with an Optimum: Because photosynthesis is run by enzymes, temperature speeds the reaction up to an optimum and then sharply slows it as the enzymes are destroyed.
4. Why greenhouses pump in carbon dioxide: Commercial greenhouse growers often burn gas or release bottled CO₂ to raise the carbon dioxide level to two or three times that of normal air.

## 4. Key terms

- Limiting factor: The single condition in shortest supply that is currently holding back the rate of a process.
- Light intensity: The amount of light energy reaching the leaf per unit time, the energy source for photosynthesis.
- Chlorophyll: The green pigment in chloroplasts that absorbs light energy for photosynthesis.
- Optimum temperature: The temperature at which the enzymes of photosynthesis work fastest; above it the rate falls as enzymes denature.
- Plateau: The flat part of a graph where increasing one condition no longer increases the rate, because another factor is now limiting.
