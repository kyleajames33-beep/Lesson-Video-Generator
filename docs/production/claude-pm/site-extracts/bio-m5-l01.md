## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Continuity requires transferred DNA and offspring that survive to reproduce.
2. Asexual reproduction copies one parent's genotype quickly without fertilisation, usually with low variation.
3. Sexual reproduction fuses gametes and generates new allele combinations, at greater time and energy cost.
4. Stable conditions favour efficient copying; changing conditions make variation valuable, so neither strategy is universally superior.

## 4. Key terms

- Reproduction: The biological process by which organisms produce offspring and transfer hereditary information to the next generation.
- Continuity of species: The ongoing existence of a species across generations through successful reproduction and inheritance of DNA.
- Sexual reproduction: Reproduction involving fusion of gametes, usually from two parents, producing genetically variable offspring.
- Asexual reproduction: Reproduction from one parent without gamete fusion, usually producing genetically identical offspring.
- Variation: Differences in characteristics between individuals in a population, often linked to differences in alleles.
- Gamete: A haploid sex cell, such as a sperm or egg, that fuses during fertilisation.

## 5. Worked examples

No numerical calculation. The 6-mark Explain model links DNA transfer, rapid asexual population establishment, sexual variation and environmental fit to continuity. The 5-mark Compare model uses variation, population growth and resources as criteria; both transfer complete genomes and undergo mutation, while crossing over, independent assortment and random fertilisation generate sexual variation.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Asexual reproduction means no variation: mutation can introduce differences.
- Sexual offspring are half-and-half copies: half the chromosomes come from each parent, but allele combinations are unique, rather than blended traits.
- Clones are identical in every way: somatic mutations, epigenetics and environment can differ.
- Sexual reproduction is always better or produces more offspring: speed and resilience suit different conditions.

Other examples: bacterial binary fission, yeast/hydra budding, strawberry runners, potato propagation, fragmentation, coral spawning. No apparatus-based practical.
