## 3. Key ideas in order

1. The Flame Test Method and Characteristic Colours: A flame test is one of the simplest ways to identify a metal ion: heat a tiny amount in a hot, colourless flame and many metals glow with a colour that is the same every time.
2. Explaining the Colour: Electron Energy: When a metal ion is heated, its electrons absorb energy and jump up to a higher energy level, an excited state.
3. From Flame Tests to Emission Spectroscopy: If the emitted light is passed through a prism or spectroscope, it spreads into a set of sharp, separate coloured lines rather than a continuous rainbow.

## 4. Key terms

- Flame test: Heating a metal ion in a flame to observe its characteristic colour, used for qualitative identification.
- Excited state: A higher-energy arrangement reached when an electron absorbs energy and jumps to a higher energy level.
- Photon: A packet of light energy emitted when an electron falls back to a lower energy level; its energy fixes its colour.
- Emission spectrum: The set of discrete coloured lines produced by an element, acting as a unique fingerprint.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: The flame colour comes from the heat of the flame itself burning the metal. ✓ Right: The colour is light emitted by excited electrons falling back to lower energy levels, the flame only supplies the energy to excite them. ✗ Wrong: Electrons can sit at any energy in the atom, so colours are random. ✓ Right: Energy levels are discrete, so the energy gaps are fixed.
