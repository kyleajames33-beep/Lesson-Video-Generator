## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Simple Diffusion and Osmosis: Particles in liquids and gases are always moving randomly, and this random motion alone spreads them out evenly over time.
2. Facilitated Diffusion: Charged or large polar molecules cannot cross the lipid core, so they pass through specific membrane proteins while still moving down their gradient.
3. Demonstrating Diffusion and Osmosis: Both diffusion and osmosis can be shown directly, and the observed change is the evidence that the process occurred.
4. Why a salty meal makes you thirsty: Eat a packet of salty chips and you soon feel thirsty, that is osmosis at work in your own body.

## 4. Key terms

- Concentration gradient: The difference in concentration of a substance between two regions; particles move "down" it (high → low).
- Simple diffusion: Net movement of particles from high to low concentration, directly across the membrane, until evenly spread.
- Osmosis: Net movement of water across a partially permeable membrane from a region of high to low water concentration.
- Facilitated diffusion: Passive diffusion of ions/large polar molecules through a membrane protein (channel or carrier).
- Passive: Requiring no energy (ATP) from the cell, driven only by the concentration gradient.
