## 1. Title and main question

Le Chatelier’s Principle: Concentration and Temperature. How do concentration and temperature changes shift equilibrium and affect Keq?

Source: `C:/Users/kygs/hscscience/subjects/chemistry/year12/module5/lesson05.sa.html`.

## 2. Syllabus content covered

2017: “investigate the effects of temperature, concentration, volume and/or pressure on a system at equilibrium and explain how Le Chatelier’s principle can be used to predict such effects, for example:”

Examples: “heating cobalt(II) chloride hydrate”; “iron(III) thiocyanate and varying concentration of ions (ACSCH095)”.

## 3. Key ideas in order

1. A disturbed equilibrium partially counteracts the disturbance, rather than eliminating it.
2. Adding reactant or removing product shifts right; adding product or removing reactant shifts left, without changing Keq.
3. Fe³⁺(aq) + SCN⁻(aq) ⇌ FeSCN²⁺(aq) demonstrates shifts through red colour intensity.
4. Reference and dilution-control tubes distinguish reaction changes from dilution.
5. Heating favours the endothermic direction and cooling the exothermic direction; temperature changes Keq.
6. CoCl₂·6H₂O(s) ⇌ CoCl₂(s) + 6H₂O(g) turns pink when humid and blue when dry or heated.

## 4. Key terms

- Le Chatelier’s Principle: If a system at equilibrium is disturbed, it shifts to partially counteract the disturbance.
- Exothermic reaction: A reaction that releases heat energy to the surroundings (ΔH < 0).
- Endothermic reaction: A reaction that absorbs heat energy from the surroundings (ΔH > 0).
- Position of equilibrium: A qualitative description of whether products or reactants are favoured at equilibrium.

## 5. Worked examples

- CH₃COOH(aq) + C₂H₅OH(aq) ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅(aq) + H₂O(l), initially at equilibrium: add acetic acid, shift right; distil off ethyl acetate, shift right; add water, shift left. The page treats water as a product, not the solvent.
- 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g), ΔH = −196 kJ/mol, at 450°C, Keq = 1.7 × 10⁵: heat to 600°C, shift left and Keq < 1.7 × 10⁵; cool to 300°C, shift right and Keq > 1.7 × 10⁵.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- The disturbance disappears completely: only some added reactant is consumed.
- Concentration changes Keq: only temperature does.
- Heating always produces more product: the endothermic direction is favoured.
- Cobalt paper is blue when wet: wet is pink, dry is blue.
- Paler colour proves a left shift: dilution also makes the tube paler.

## 7. Practical or real-world examples used

Fe(NO₃)₃/KSCN deepen red; AgNO₃ precipitates SCN⁻ as AgSCN; NaF complexes Fe³⁺; NaOH precipitates Fe(OH)₃, making red paler. Six tubes use 0.002 mol L⁻¹ Fe(NO₃)₃/KSCN, solids, 0.1 mol L⁻¹ NaOH, equal-drop water control, white tile, thermometer, approximately 60°C bath for three minutes and ice bath. Teacher supervision, risk assessment, glasses/gloves, no acid contact and collected chemical waste are specified. Other examples: humidity indicators, chromate/dichromate, AgCl and the Contact Process. Memory aid: “Pink in the rain, blue in the desert.”
