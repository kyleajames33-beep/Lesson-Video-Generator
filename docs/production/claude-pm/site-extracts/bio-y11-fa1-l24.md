## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Halving the Chromosome Number: Meiosis exists to make gametes, and to do that it must halve the chromosome number so fertilisation can restore the full set.
2. Three Sources of Variation: The genetic variety of gametes comes from three distinct processes that rearrange the chromosomes during meiosis.
3. Why It All Adds Up to Variation: The three processes do not act alone, they combine, so the number of genetically different gametes an individual can make is enormous.
4. Why no two people (except twins) are alike: From just 23 pairs of chromosomes, independent assortment alone can produce over 8 million different gametes, and crossing over multiplies that figure enormously.

## 4. Key terms

- Meiosis: A division producing four genetically different gametes, each with half the parent's chromosome number.
- Homologous chromosomes: A matching pair of chromosomes (one from each parent) carrying the same genes in the same order.
- Crossing over: The exchange of segments between homologous chromosomes in prophase I, creating new gene combinations.
- Independent assortment: Homologous pairs line up and separate randomly, so each gamete gets a random mix of maternal and paternal chromosomes.
- Random segregation: The random separation of homologous chromosomes (meiosis I) and chromatids (meiosis II) into the gametes.
