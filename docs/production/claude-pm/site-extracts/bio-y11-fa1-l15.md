## 3. Key ideas in order

1. What an Enzyme Is: An enzyme is a biological catalyst, a (usually protein) molecule that dramatically speeds up a specific chemical reaction in a cell without being used up by it.
2. Lowering Activation Energy: Every reaction needs a minimum "push" of energy to get started; enzymes work by lowering that barrier, so reactions proceed much faster at normal cell temperatures.
3. Why Cells Depend on Enzymes: Enzymes do more than speed reactions up, their specificity lets a cell control thousands of different reactions precisely and independently of one another.
4. Enzymes in your washing basket: Biological laundry detergents contain enzymes, proteases to break down protein stains like blood and grass, lipases for fats, and amylases for starch.

## 4. Key terms

- Enzyme: A biological catalyst, usually a protein, that speeds up a specific chemical reaction without being used up.
- Catalyst: A substance that increases the rate of a reaction and is unchanged at the end, so it can be reused.
- Substrate: The specific reactant molecule an enzyme acts on.
- Active site: The region of an enzyme, shaped to fit its substrate, where the reaction takes place.
- Activation energy: The minimum energy needed to start a reaction; enzymes lower it.
