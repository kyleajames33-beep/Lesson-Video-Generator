## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Particles in Constant Random Motion: A gas is a vast number of tiny particles spread far apart, moving in constant, rapid, random motion in all directions.
2. The Assumptions of an Ideal Gas: An ideal gas is a model: we assume the particles have negligible volume, exert negligible forces on one another, and lose no kinetic energy when they collide.
3. Pressure, Temperature and When the Model Breaks Down: Pressure is caused by particles colliding with the container walls, and absolute temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy of those particles.

## 4. Key terms

- Ideal gas: A model gas whose particles have negligible volume, exert negligible forces on one another, and collide elastically, used to predict gas behaviour.
- Kinetic particle model: The idea that matter is made of particles in constant motion; for a gas, the particles move rapidly and randomly with large spaces between them.
- Elastic collision: A collision in which no kinetic energy is lost overall, gas particles bounce off walls and each other without slowing down on average.
- Pressure: The force per unit area exerted on the container walls by particles colliding with them.
- Absolute temperature: Temperature measured in kelvin (K); it is directly proportional to the average kinetic energy of the gas particles.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: Gas pressure comes from the particles pushing on each other or from the gas "trying to escape". ✓ Right: Pressure comes from countless particles colliding with the container walls; in the ideal model the particles barely interact with each other at all. ✗ Wrong: Heating a gas gives the particles "more pressure" or makes them bigger. ✓ Right: Heating raises the average kinetic energy, so particles move faster and hit the walls harder and more often, that is what raises the pressure.
