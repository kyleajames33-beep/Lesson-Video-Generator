## 3. Key ideas in order

1. The Phospholipid Bilayer: The membrane self-assembles because each phospholipid has one end that loves water and one that avoids it.
2. Proteins in the Mosaic: Proteins scattered through the bilayer give the "mosaic" its variety and do the jobs the lipid alone cannot.
3. Cholesterol and the Working Whole: A membrane that is too fluid would leak and a too-rigid one would crack, so cholesterol tunes its fluidity.
4. Glycoproteins and blood groups: The reason you have a blood type, A, B, AB or O, comes down to glycoproteins on the surface of your red blood cells.

## 4. Key terms

- Phospholipid: A molecule with a hydrophilic (water-loving) phosphate head and two hydrophobic (water-fearing) fatty-acid tails.
- Bilayer: Two layers of phospholipids, tails pointing inward, heads facing the water on both sides.
- Integral protein: A protein embedded through the bilayer; often a channel or carrier for transport.
- Peripheral protein: A protein attached to the surface of the membrane (not spanning it); support and signalling roles.
- Glycoprotein: A protein with a carbohydrate chain attached; acts in cell recognition and signalling.
