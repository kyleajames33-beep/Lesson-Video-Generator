Collision Theory Applied to Equilibrium. How does collision theory explain equilibrium approach and disturbances?

## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Reactant consumption reduces effective forward collisions while product accumulation increases effective reverse collisions.
2. ΔH = Eₐ(forward) − Eₐ(reverse), so exothermic forward reactions have the smaller forward barrier.
3. Catalysts lower both barriers equally and accelerate equilibrium approach without changing position or K_eq.
4. Cooling or compressing 2NO₂(g) ⇌ N₂O₄(g) favours colourless N₂O₄.
5. Rate graphs show approach from reactants, approach from products, and re-equilibration after disturbances.

## 4. Key terms

- Collision theory: States that reactions occur when particles collide with sufficient energy and correct orientation.
- Activation energy (Ea): The minimum energy required for a collision to result in a chemical reaction.
- Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution: A graph showing the spread of particle kinetic energies in a gas or solution at a given temperature.
- Effective collision: A collision that results in bond breaking and formation, producing products.
- Frequency factor: The number of collisions per unit time between reactant particles.
- Temperature effect on rate: Increasing temperature increases the proportion of molecules with energy ≥ Ea, raising reaction rate.

## 5. Worked examples

- Example 1: pure PCl₅(g) in a sealed flask; PCl₅(g) ⇌ PCl₃(g) + Cl₂(g), ΔH = +88 kJ/mol. Forward rate begins maximum and falls; reverse begins zero and rises as products accumulate; equilibrium has equal non-zero rates. Eₐ(forward) > Eₐ(reverse), differing by 88 kJ/mol.
- Example 2: Contact process 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g), ΔH = −196 kJ/mol, initially at equilibrium. V₂O₅ lowers both barriers by the same value x: no shift or K_eq change. At 450°C it enables an economically viable rate. Cooling reduces reverse rate more because its barrier is higher: right shift, more SO₃.
- Q6: catalyst lowers both activation energies equally; both rates increase by the same factor; position and K_eq unchanged.
- Q7: sealed 2NO₂ ⇌ N₂O₄ at 25°C placed at 80°C: reverse rate rises more, mixture darkens and K_eq decreases.
- Q8: CO(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ CH₄(g) + H₂O(g), ΔH = −206 kJ/mol. Heating: left shift, K_eq decreases. Nickel catalyst: no shift, K_eq unchanged. Halving volume doubles concentrations: right shift from 4 mol gas toward 2; K_eq unchanged.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

“The reaction slows” ignores the increasing reverse rate. Exothermic reactions do not have equal forward/reverse barriers. Catalysts do not raise equilibrium yield. Cooling slows both directions, but not equally. Neither rate becomes zero at dynamic equilibrium.
