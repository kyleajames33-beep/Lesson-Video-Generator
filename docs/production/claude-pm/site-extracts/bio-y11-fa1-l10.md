## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Active Transport: Pumping Uphill: When a cell needs more of a substance than its surroundings hold, diffusion cannot help, so the cell pumps it in using energy.
2. Bulk Transport: Endocytosis and Exocytosis: Large particles cannot fit through any protein, so the membrane itself folds around them to carry them in or out.
3. Why It Is Worth the Energy: Energy buys the cell abilities diffusion cannot provide: concentrating scarce nutrients, expelling wastes, and handling bulky materials.
4. How your white blood cells eat bacteria: When a bacterium invades, certain white blood cells (phagocytes) destroy it by endocytosis, specifically phagocytosis.

## 4. Key terms

- Active transport: Movement of a substance against its concentration gradient using a carrier protein and energy (ATP).
- ATP: The cell's energy currency; its breakdown powers active transport and vesicle movement.
- Endocytosis: Bulk uptake of material by engulfing it in a vesicle formed from the membrane (phagocytosis/pinocytosis).
- Exocytosis: Bulk export: a vesicle fuses with the membrane to release its contents outside the cell.
- Vesicle: A small membrane-bound sac that carries material into, out of, or within the cell.
