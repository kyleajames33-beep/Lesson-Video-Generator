## 1. Title and main question

Titration Calculations, Standard Solutions, Concordant Titres & Errors. How is an unknown acid or base concentration measured accurately by titration?

Source: `C:/Users/kygs/hscscience/subjects/chemistry/year12/module6/lesson14.sa.html`.

## 2. Syllabus content covered

“conduct practical investigations to analyse the concentration of an unknown acid or base by titration.”

## 3. Key ideas in order

1. A standard solution has known concentration; a primary standard is pure, stable, non-hygroscopic, soluble and suited to accurate weighing.
2. NaOH absorbs moisture and CO₂, so the page standardises it against a primary standard.
3. Rinse the burette with titrant and the pipette with analyte; rinse the conical flask with distilled water.
4. Read the bottom of the burette meniscus at eye level, add titrant carefully near the endpoint and average concordant titres.
5. Find the unknown concentration by balancing the reaction, finding known moles, using the coefficient ratio and dividing unknown moles by the sample volume.
6. The page applies the method to vinegar, antacids, wine and household products, with error direction and reporting precision.

## 4. Key terms

- Standard solution: solution of precisely known concentration used as titration reference.
- Primary standard: material that can be accurately weighed and dissolved to establish concentration.
- Titrant: solution delivered from the burette.
- Analyte: solution of unknown concentration in the conical flask.
- Endpoint: indicator's observed colour change; equivalence point: stoichiometrically equal reacting amounts.
- Concordant titres: page uses readings within 0.10 mL of one another.

## 5. Worked examples

- 1.325 g Na₂CO₃ (M 106.0 g/mol) made to 250.0 mL gives 0.05000 mol/L. A 25.00 mL aliquot titrated with HCl at 22.45, 22.40 and 22.50 mL gives average 22.45 mL and c(HCl) 0.1114 mol/L using the 1:2 ratio.
- 10.00 mL vinegar titrated with 0.5000 mol/L NaOH: titres 18.60, 18.55, 18.55 and 18.90 mL. Excluding 18.90 gives average 18.57 mL, acetic acid 0.9285 mol/L and 5.55% by mass at density 1.005 g/mL.
- 0.6320 g oxalic acid dihydrate (M 126.1 g/mol) in 100.0 mL gives 0.05012 mol/L. A 20.00 mL aliquot titrated with NaOH gives concordant titres 19.85 and 19.80 mL; average 19.83 mL and c(NaOH) 0.1011 mol/L.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- NaOH can be weighed as a primary standard; it is hygroscopic and reacts with CO₂.
- Rinsing a flask with analyte is harmless; it adds unmeasured analyte and raises the titre.
- Equal titrant and analyte volumes imply equal concentrations; the mole ratio must come from the equation.
- All recorded titres should be averaged; exclude non-concordant results.

## 7. Practical or real-world examples used

Na₂CO₃ and oxalic acid dihydrate primary standards; burette, pipette, conical flask, volumetric flask, white tile and phenolphthalein; wine acidity, vinegar and antacid tablets.
