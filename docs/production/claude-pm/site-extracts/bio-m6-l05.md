## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Somatic mutations arise in body cells and can affect the individual, while germ-line mutations in gametes or their producing lineage can pass to offspring.
2. Coding-region mutations can change codons, amino acids and protein function, but can also be silent.
3. Non-coding regulatory changes can alter when, where or how strongly genes are expressed.
4. The page combines two dimensions: body location determines inheritance; genome location helps determine function.

## 4. Key terms

- Somatic mutation: A body-cell mutation that is not normally inherited by offspring.
- Germ-line mutation: A change in a gamete or gamete-producing lineage that can pass to offspring.
- Coding DNA: DNA contributing to a protein’s amino acid sequence.
- Non-coding DNA: DNA not translated into protein, often involved in regulation and genome function.
- Regulation: Control of when, where and how strongly a gene is expressed.
- Population relevance: Whether a mutation can enter the gene pool in future generations.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Any body-cell mutation passes to offspring. Somatic mutations generally do not.
- Every coding change is severe. Some are silent or have limited effects.
- Non-coding DNA is useless. Promoters, enhancers and other regions can regulate gene expression.
