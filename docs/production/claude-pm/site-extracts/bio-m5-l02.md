## 1. Title and main question

Reproduction in Animals. How do external and internal fertilisation support continuity under different environmental conditions?

## 2. Syllabus content covered

## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Haploid gametes fuse to form a diploid zygote, restoring chromosome number.
2. External fertilisation uses large synchronised gamete releases in water to compensate for environmental losses.
3. Internal fertilisation protects gametes in the female reproductive tract and supports reproduction on land.
4. Environment, gamete loss and parental investment shape strategy, while fertilisation location differs from development location.

## 4. Key terms

- Fertilisation: Fusion of haploid gametes to form a diploid zygote.
- External fertilisation: Fertilisation that occurs outside the body, usually in an aquatic environment.
- Internal fertilisation: Fertilisation that occurs inside the reproductive tract of the female.
- Gamete: A haploid reproductive cell such as a sperm or egg.
- Zygote: The diploid cell formed when two gametes fuse at fertilisation.
- Parental investment: The time, energy and protection invested by parents into the survival of offspring.

## 5. Worked examples

No numerical calculation. The 6-mark Explain model contrasts external numbers compensating for uncertain fertilisation and survival with internal protection and parental care. The 5-mark Analyse model relates offspring number inversely to care through finite parental resources: fish release thousands of eggs with little care, mammals have one or two young with gestation/feeding, and birds provide clutch care. Few-offspring species recover slowly when juvenile mortality rises.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Fertilisation produces gametes or meiosis: it fuses existing haploid gametes.
- External fertilisation is primitive/inferior: it suits aquatic environments.
- Egg-laying means external fertilisation: birds fertilise internally before laying.
- Internal fertilisation produces more offspring: it typically produces fewer with greater investment and survival.
- More gametes means greater success: environment and offspring survival matter.

## 7. Practical or real-world examples used

The page's 2022 NOAA Chinook salmon example: 90% return within 20 km of birthplace after 3–5 years at sea; detect pheromones at 1 part per 10 billion; females release 4,000–14,000 eggs and die (semelparity). Harbour seal: one egg, internal fertilisation, 11-month pregnancy. Coral spawning, frogs, bony fish, desert lizard, hen, reptiles and mammals illustrate habitat and development differences. No apparatus-based practical.

## 8. Activities and questions

Prediction, fill-in, MC, true/false, scenario classification, trade-off explanation, timed retrieval and short answers worth 3, 4 and 5 marks.

"Why do fish using external fertilisation usually release far more gametes than mammals using internal fertilisation?"

"Compare external and internal fertilisation in terms of environment, gamete number, protection and reproductive success."

Answers: marine fish external; desert lizard internal; pond-dependent frog suggests external; hen internal. External success needs water, synchronised timing and abundant gametes. Internal protection reduces drying and increases sperm access. Coral and mammal effectiveness depends on context.
