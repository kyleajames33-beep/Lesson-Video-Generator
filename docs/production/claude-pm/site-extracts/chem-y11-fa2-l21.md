## 3. Key ideas in order

1. What Molar Volume Is: Molar volume is the volume that exactly one mole of a gas occupies under stated conditions, and at a fixed temperature and pressure that volume is the same for every ideal gas, no matter how heavy its particles are.
2. Deriving Vm at STP: Setting $n = 1$ mol in $pV=nRT$ and making volume the subject gives $V_m = \dfrac{RT}{p}$, which we evaluate at the STP values of $T$ and $p$.
3. Using n = V/Vm for Gas Stoichiometry: Once you know $V_m$, the messy ideal gas law collapses to a simple conversion: the volume of a gas at STP is just the number of moles multiplied by the molar volume, so $n = \dfrac{V}{V_m}$ and $V = n \times V_m$.

## 4. Key terms

- Molar volume ($V_m$): The volume occupied by one mole of a gas at a stated temperature and pressure; about 22.71 L mol⁻¹ for any ideal gas at STP.
- STP: Standard temperature and pressure as defined by NESA: 100 kPa and 273.15 K (0 °C).
- Ideal gas: A model gas whose particles have negligible volume and no forces between them, so its behaviour follows $pV=nRT$ exactly.
- Gas constant ($R$): The universal constant linking $p$, $V$, $n$ and $T$, equal to 8.314 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: A heavier gas like CO 2 must take up more volume per mole than a light gas like H 2. ✓ Right: At the same temperature and pressure, one mole of any ideal gas fills the same volume, molar volume does not depend on the mass of the particles. ✗ Wrong: You can always use 22.71 L mol⁻¹ for any gas problem. ✓ Right: 22.71 L mol⁻¹ is the molar volume only at STP.
