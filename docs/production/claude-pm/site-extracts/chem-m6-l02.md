## 1. Title and main question

Nomenclature, Indicators & Predicting Acid Reactions. How do names, indicator colours and reactants help predict an acid-base reaction?

## 2. Syllabus content covered

“investigate the correct IUPAC nomenclature and properties of common inorganic acids and bases”; “conduct an investigation to demonstrate the preparation and use of indicators”; “predict the products of acid reactions and write balanced equations”.

## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Binary acids use hydro- and -ic; oxoacids use -ate to -ic and -ite to -ous.
2. Hydroxide bases use ionic names; NH₃ is basic by accepting H⁺ from water.
3. Indicator forms HIn and In⁻ have different colours; changing [H⁺] shifts HIn ⇌ H⁺ + In⁻.
4. Methyl orange, bromothymol blue and phenolphthalein have different transition ranges.
5. Acid with a hydroxide gives salt and water; with carbonate gives salt, water and CO₂; with a reactive metal gives salt and H₂.
6. Determine the salt from ion charges, then balance the equation.

## 4. Key terms

- Binary acid: Acid containing H and a non-metal but no oxygen.
- Oxoacid: Acid containing H, oxygen and another element.
- Indicator: Weak acid whose conjugate forms have different colours.
- Transition range: pH interval over which both indicator forms contribute visible colour.

## 5. Worked examples

- HNO₂: nitrous acid, weak; H₃PO₄: phosphoric acid, weak; Ba(OH)₂: barium hydroxide, strong; H₂S: hydrosulfuric acid, weak.
- H₂SO₄ + Mg(OH)₂ → MgSO₄ + 2H₂O; 2HCl + CaCO₃ → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂; 6HCl + 2Al → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂.
- Methyl orange at pH 4.0 is orange in its pH 3.1–4.4 range. Added HCl raises [H⁺], shifts HIn ⇌ H⁺ + In⁻ left and moves the colour toward red.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- H₂SO₄ is hydrosulfuric acid: that is H₂S; H₂SO₄ is sulfuric acid.
- HF must be strong like HCl: the page classifies HF as weak.
- Indicators give an exact pH: they show a transition range.
- Phenolphthalein is pink at pH 7: it is colourless below about pH 8.3.
- Acid and carbonate give only salt and water: CO₂ is also produced.

## 7. Practical or real-world examples used

Litmus, methyl orange, bromothymol blue, phenolphthalein, aspirin solution and ammonium sulfate fertiliser production.

## 8. Activities and questions

Naming and classifying formulas, balancing acid equations, correcting erroneous statements and explaining indicator colours. Representative: “Write balanced molecular equations for the following reactions. State the reaction type (Pattern 1, 2, or 3) for each.”
