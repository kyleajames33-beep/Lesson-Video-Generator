## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Proteins carry out enzyme, structural, transport, receptor and antibody roles.
2. Sequence determines shape, and shape determines function: a change in amino acid sequence can change a protein's job.
3. The pathway is genotype, then protein product, then biological effect, then phenotype; "Do not say genes directly become traits."
4. Phenotype reflects genotype and environment together: height is polygenic, and nutrition affects whether the genetic potential is reached.
5. Himalayan rabbit: the same dark-fur genotype is expressed only in cooler body parts, because the pigment enzyme works only at lower temperatures.
6. Page's assessment verdict: "the genotype determines what is possible and the environment determines what is realised".

## 4. Key terms

- Phenotype: The observable characteristics of an organism.
- Genotype: The genetic makeup or allele combination of an organism.
- Enzyme: A protein that speeds up a chemical reaction in living systems.
- Receptor protein: A protein that binds signalling molecules and helps cells respond.
- Polygenic trait: A characteristic influenced by many genes acting together, such as height or skin colour.
- Continuous variation: A smooth range of phenotypes (rather than a few distinct categories), typical of polygenic traits.

## 5. Worked examples

- Sickle cell (Ingram, 1956-57): at position 6 of the beta-globin chain, glutamate was replaced by valine. "One DNA base change, one amino acid substitution, one protein shape change, one whole-organism disease phenotype."
- Explain 5 marks model: hydrophobic residues fold inward, oppositely charged residues attract, cysteines form disulfide bridges; a substitution at a critical residue "alters the local interactions" and the active site no longer matches its substrate.
- Height activity answer: "Similar genetic potential does not guarantee identical adult height."

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- "Genotype directly determines phenotype with no other factors involved." Page: phenotype is the product of genotype, environment and their interaction.
- Do not say that genes alone fix phenotype completely, or that environment normally changes genotype during development.
- True/false (false): "All traits are determined solely by genes and cannot be influenced by environmental factors."

Possible site errors: The hook says Beadle and Tatum's 1941 work was "proving the one gene-one enzyme link". Experiments of that kind support the one gene-one enzyme hypothesis, which the page does not say was later refined.
