## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Reading an Energy Profile: An energy profile diagram is a side-on picture of a reaction's energy: the horizontal axis is the reaction progress (reactants on the left, products on the right) and the vertical axis is chemical potential energy.
2. Exothermic Profiles: In an exothermic reaction the products sit at a lower chemical potential energy than the reactants, so the curve finishes below where it began and energy is released to the surroundings.
3. Endothermic Profiles: In an endothermic reaction the products sit at a higher chemical potential energy than the reactants, so the curve finishes above where it began and energy is absorbed from the surroundings.

## 4. Key terms

- Chemical potential energy: The energy stored in the bonds and arrangement of the particles; it is plotted up the vertical axis of an energy profile.
- Activation energy ($E_a$): The minimum energy needed to start a reaction, the height of the hump from the reactants up to the peak.
- Transition state: The fleeting high-energy arrangement at the top of the curve, where old bonds are partly broken and new bonds are partly formed.
- Enthalpy change ($\Delta H$): The overall energy difference between products and reactants; negative when energy is released, positive when energy is absorbed.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: The activation-energy hump and $\Delta H$ are the same thing. ✓ Right: $E_a$ is the height from the reactants up to the peak; $\Delta H$ is the difference between the reactant and product levels.
