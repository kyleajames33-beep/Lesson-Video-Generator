## 1. Title and main question

Variation and Allele Frequency. How do mutation, gene flow, selection and drift change a population's gene pool?

## 2. Syllabus content covered

“evaluate the effect of mutation, gene flow and genetic drift on the gene pool of populations (ACSBL091, ACSBL092)”

## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Mutation creates new alleles; recombination reshuffles existing alleles; gene flow moves alleles between populations.
2. Allele frequency is an allele's share of all copies in the population's gene pool.
3. Selection favours alleles linked to survival or reproduction; drift changes frequencies by chance, especially in small populations.
4. Bottleneck and founder events can reduce variation and change allele frequencies.
5. Individuals do not evolve during one lifetime; population allele frequencies change over generations.

## 4. Key terms

- Allele frequency: “The proportion of a specific allele in a gene pool.”
- Gene pool: “The total set of alleles in a population.”
- Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium: “A theoretical state where allele frequencies remain constant.”
- Mutation: “A permanent change in the DNA sequence.”
- Genetic drift: “Random changes in allele frequencies in small populations.”
- Gene flow: “The transfer of alleles between populations by migration.”

## 5. Worked examples

- Four allele A copies among 100 allele copies give frequency 0.04 or 4%.
- A selection scenario begins with 4% carrying a resistant allele and says 90% of susceptible individuals die before reproducing. The resistant allele becomes more common in the next generation.
- A gene-pool simulator compares five replicates of N = 20 and N = 500, beginning at p(A) = 0.50 over 50 generations, then changes genotype fitness to contrast drift and selection.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Every allele-frequency change proves natural selection. Drift, mutation and gene flow can also change frequencies.
- Natural selection creates new alleles. Mutation creates them; selection acts on existing variation.
- An individual evolves after exposure to pressure. Evolution is a population change across generations.
