## 3. Key ideas in order

1. A describes equal non-zero rates and constant concentrations; B confuses rates/concentrations; C says reactions stop.
2. Escalator traffic models equal flows with unequal, constant populations.
3. Saturated NaCl demonstrates dissolution and recrystallisation continuing equally, supported by radioactive tracer evidence.
4. Concentration curves flatten at equilibrium; disturbances produce jumps and new plateaus.
5. Equal concentrations, stopped reactions, catalyst-increased yield and one-direction-only approach are four errors.

## 4. Key terms

- Dynamic equilibrium: Forward and reverse reaction rates are equal; concentrations remain constant.
- Le Chatelier's Principle: A system at equilibrium shifts to counteract any applied disturbance (stress).
- Equilibrium constant (K_eq): The ratio of product to reactant concentrations at equilibrium, raised to stoichiometric powers.
- Reaction quotient (Q): The same ratio as K_eq calculated at any point; compared to K_eq to predict shift direction.
- Entropy (S): A measure of disorder; drives many spontaneous reactions alongside enthalpy.
- Gibbs free energy (ΔG°): Predicts reaction spontaneity; ΔG° = −RT ln K_eq links thermodynamics to equilibrium.

## 5. Worked examples

- Example 1: N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), sealed flask; student claims equal concentrations and stopped reaction. Correction: concentrations each remain constant, not necessarily equal; both directions continue at equal non-zero rates.
- Example 2: 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g); SO₂/O₂ fall to plateaus, SO₃ rises; at t₁ both reactants spike then settle above their original values and SO₃ rises further. Page answer: SO₂ or O₂ added; more effective forward collisions; more total material raises all equilibrium concentrations; K_eq unchanged.
- Example 3 (7 marks): CO(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ CH₄(g) + H₂O(g), ΔH = −206 kJ/mol, sealed vessel. Heating: left, K_eq decreases. Nickel catalyst: no shift, K_eq unchanged. Volume halved: concentrations double, right shift, 4 mol gas to 2; K_eq unchanged.
- Activity answers: A + B ⇌ C, ΔH = −50 kJ/mol, heating gives less C, K_eq decreases; graph crossing is not the equilibrium test; forward rate zero is not dynamic equilibrium.
- Q6: equal NaCl dissolution/recrystallisation; tracer proves exchange; surface mechanism applies to solid-liquid equilibria.
- Q7: Fe³⁺(aq) + SCN⁻(aq) ⇌ FeSCN²⁺(aq), initially deep red; blood-red FeSCN²⁺, colourless with excess SCN⁻. Adding Fe(NO₃)₃ deepens red; FeSCN²⁺ rises gradually to a higher plateau; Fe³⁺ spikes then falls to a higher plateau.
- Q8 (8 marks): completed Mg combustion has zero rates; dynamic N₂O₄/NO₂ has equal non-zero plateaus and constant concentrations.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

Rates, not concentrations, are equal; reactions continue; catalysts change speed, not yield/K_eq; either starting direction reaches the same equilibrium with identical atomic composition. Concentration-graph equilibrium is flattening, not crossing.

## 7. Practical or real-world examples used

Van 't Hoff's 1888 statement; escalator floors hold 200/150 people with 50/min each way, then 100 added downstairs. Limits: fixed escalator speeds/capacity. NaCl(s) ⇌ Na⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq): ²⁴Na appears in solution while dissolved salt stays constant. Haber at 25°C: K_eq ≈ 6 × 10⁵.
