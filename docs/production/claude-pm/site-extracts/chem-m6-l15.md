## 3. Key ideas in order

1. An indicator is represented by HIn ⇌ H⁺ + In⁻; the two forms have different colours.
2. High [H⁺] favours HIn and low [H⁺] favours In⁻; a visible transition occurs across roughly pKIn ± 1.
3. The equivalence-point pH depends on the salt: strong acid/strong base about 7, weak acid/strong base above 7, strong acid/weak base below 7.
4. Choose an indicator whose colour transition occurs in the steep section of the titration curve.
5. Weak acid/weak base curves have no sharp jump, so the page prefers a pH probe or conductometric method.

## 4. Key terms

- Indicator: weak acid with differently coloured HIn and In⁻ forms.
- Transition range: pH span in which the observed colour changes, approximately pKIn ± 1.
- Equivalence point: stoichiometric amount of titrant has reacted.
- Endpoint: observed indicator colour change.
- Salt hydrolysis: reaction of a conjugate ion with water that changes equivalence-point pH.

## 5. Worked examples

- CH₃COOH with NaOH: the page gives equivalence pH about 8.7 and selects phenolphthalein (8.3–10.0). HCl with NH₃: pH about 5.3 and selects methyl orange (3.1–4.4). HCl with NaOH: pH 7 and lists methyl orange, bromothymol blue or phenolphthalein.
- For 25.00 mL of 0.0800 mol/L CH₃COOH (Ka 1.8 × 10⁻⁵) titrated with 0.0800 mol/L NaOH, [CH₃COO⁻] at equivalence is 0.0400 mol/L, Kb 5.56 × 10⁻¹⁰, [OH⁻] 4.71 × 10⁻⁶ mol/L and pH 8.67. The page chooses phenolphthalein.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- An indicator is a passive colour marker; its own equilibrium responds to pH.
- Every equivalence point has pH 7; salt hydrolysis can move it above or below 7.
- Choose the indicator whose range is closest to pH 7; the page says use the steep curve region.
- A weak acid/weak base titration always has a suitable visual indicator; the page says it often lacks a sharp endpoint.

## 7. Practical or real-world examples used

Methyl orange (3.1–4.4), bromothymol blue (6.0–7.6), phenolphthalein (8.3–10.0), acetic acid/NaOH, HCl/NH₃ and pharmaceutical quality control.
