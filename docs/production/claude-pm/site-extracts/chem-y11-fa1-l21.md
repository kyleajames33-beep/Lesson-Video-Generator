## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Electronegativity and Bond Polarity: A covalent bond is two atoms sharing a pair of electrons, but if one atom pulls harder, the sharing becomes lopsided.
2. VSEPR Theory and Molecular Shape: VSEPR theory says electron pairs around a central atom repel each other, so they arrange themselves as far apart as possible.
3. From Bond Dipoles to Molecular Polarity: A molecule is polar only if its bond dipoles do not cancel out.

## 4. Key terms

- Electronegativity: A measure of how strongly an atom attracts the shared electrons in a bond; it increases across a period and decreases down a group.
- Polar covalent bond: A bond where electrons are shared unequally, giving one atom a partial negative charge (δ−) and the other a partial positive charge (δ+).
- Bond dipole: The separation of charge in a polar bond, represented by an arrow pointing toward the more electronegative atom.
- VSEPR theory: Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion theory: electron pairs around a central atom arrange themselves as far apart as possible.
- Lone pair: A non-bonding pair of valence electrons that still repels other pairs and pushes the shape toward smaller bond angles.
