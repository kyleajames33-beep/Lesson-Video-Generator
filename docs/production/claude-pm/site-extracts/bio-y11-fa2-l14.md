## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Why Food Must Be Broken Down: Your cells run on small, soluble molecules, glucose, amino acids, fatty acids, but food arrives as huge insoluble structures, so the digestive system exists to convert one into the other.
2. The Journey: Mouth to Anus: The digestive tract is one long tube, and food is processed in a fixed order as it moves through, each region adding its own contribution before passing food on.
3. How Digestion Serves the Cell: Digestion only matters because of what happens next: the small molecules are absorbed and delivered to cells, where they fuel respiration and provide building blocks.
4. When absorption fails: coeliac disease: In coeliac disease, the immune system reacts to gluten and damages the villi of the small intestine, flattening them.

## 4. Key terms

- Mechanical digestion: Physical breakdown of food into smaller pieces by chewing and the churning of the stomach, increases surface area for enzymes.
- Chemical digestion: Breakdown of large molecules into small absorbable molecules by enzymes (e.g. starch → glucose).
- Absorption: The passage of digested nutrients from the small intestine into the blood or lymph.
- Elimination: The removal of undigested material (faeces) from the body via the anus, not the same as excretion.
- Villi: Tiny finger-like projections lining the small intestine that hugely increase surface area for absorption.
