## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Four sources of variation: crossing over, independent assortment, random fertilisation and mutation.
2. The first three create new combinations of existing alleles; mutation creates genuinely new alleles.
3. Crossing over in prophase I exchanges segments between homologous chromosomes.
4. Independent assortment at metaphase I gives 2^n gamete types; for humans (n = 23), "over 8 million" combinations.
5. Random fertilisation: any gamete from one parent may fuse with any gamete from the other.
6. Mutation changes the DNA sequence and is the only source of genuinely new alleles.
7. Siblings resemble each other but are usually not identical, because of these processes.

## 4. Key terms

- Genetic variation: Differences in genetic makeup within or between individuals of a population.
- Crossing over: Exchange of corresponding chromosome segments between homologous chromosomes during meiosis.
- Independent assortment: Random orientation and separation of homologous chromosomes in meiosis.
- Random fertilisation: The chance fusion of one gamete with another, creating many possible genotype combinations.
- Mutation: A change in DNA sequence that can create a new allele.
- Allele: An alternative version of a gene, occupying the same locus but differing in DNA sequence.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- "Genetic variation within a population is always visible in the phenotype." Page: much variation is cryptic; a heterozygous carrier does not show a recessive allele.
- "Crossing over creates new alleles." Page: it reshuffles existing alleles; mutation creates new ones.
- Do not call every variation source a "mutation".

Possible site errors: The hook says h^2 = 0.79 means "about 79% of the variation in beak depth among individuals was due to inherited genetic differences, and most of it came from reshuffling existing alleles, not from new mutations". A heritability value gives the share of variation due to genetic differences; it does not show how much came from reshuffling versus new mutation. The rapid review says the Grant study "demonstrates that most heritable variation in a population comes from three non-mutational processes"; the h^2 figure does not separate these sources.
