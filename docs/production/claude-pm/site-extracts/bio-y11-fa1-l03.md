## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Same Toolkit, Different Tools: Plant and animal cells are both eukaryotic, so they share the core organelles, the differences are a short, predictable list.
2. Why a Plant Cell Looks the Way It Does: A plant cell makes its own food and cannot move, so it needs to capture light, store the products, and hold itself rigid, and that explains its three extra structures.
3. Why an Animal Cell Travels Light: An animal is a heterotroph that moves and consumes food, so its cells are built for flexibility rather than rigidity.
4. Why a wilted plant droops: When you forget to water a plant, it wilts.

## 4. Key terms

- Autotroph: An organism (e.g. a plant) that makes its own food using light energy through photosynthesis.
- Heterotroph: An organism (e.g. an animal) that obtains food by consuming other organisms.
- Cellulose cell wall: The rigid outer layer of a plant cell that gives shape and support and resists turgor pressure.
- Central vacuole: The single large fluid-filled sac in a plant cell that stores cell sap and maintains turgor.
- Turgor pressure: The outward pressure of the vacuole's contents against the cell wall that keeps a plant firm.
