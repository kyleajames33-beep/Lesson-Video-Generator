## 3. Key ideas in order

1. A buffer contains a weak acid and its conjugate base in appreciable amounts.
2. Added H⁺ reacts with A⁻, while added OH⁻ reacts with HA, so pH changes only a little until a component is used up.
3. Henderson-Hasselbalch relates pH to pKa and the ratio [A⁻]/[HA]; equal amounts give pH = pKa.
4. A buffer can be made by mixing a weak acid and its salt or by partly neutralising a weak acid.
5. Finite moles of HA and A⁻ determine how much added base and acid the buffer can absorb.
6. The page applies buffer chemistry to blood, intracellular phosphate and ocean carbonate systems.

## 4. Key terms

- Buffer: a weak acid/conjugate-base solution that resists pH change when small amounts of strong acid or base are added.
- Henderson-Hasselbalch equation: pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA]).
- Buffer capacity: amount of acid or base absorbed before a buffer component is depleted.
- Half-equivalence point: equal HA and A⁻ amounts, giving pH = pKa.
- Acidosis: blood pH below the stated 7.35–7.45 normal range.

## 5. Worked examples

- Acetate buffer with 0.150 mol/L CH₃COOH, 0.200 mol/L CH₃COONa and Ka 1.8 × 10⁻⁵ gives pH 4.87. The ratio for pH 5.20 is [A⁻]/[HA] = 2.88. Adding 5.00 mL of 1.00 mol/L HCl to 500 mL of the original buffer gives pH 4.81.
- Adding 30.0 mL of 0.200 mol/L NaOH to 50.0 mL of 0.200 mol/L NaH₂PO₄ (Ka₂ 6.2 × 10⁻⁸) gives pH 7.39. The page gives additional NaOH capacity 4.00 × 10⁻³ mol and HCl capacity 6.00 × 10⁻³ mol.
- Diabetic ketoacidosis example: [HCO₃⁻] 6.0 mmol/L, [H₂CO₃] 0.60 mmol/L and pKa 6.10 give pH 7.10. Without respiratory compensation, [H₂CO₃] 1.20 mmol/L gives pH 6.80.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Buffers prevent all pH change; the page says they resist it.
- Buffers must have pH 7; acetate and blood buffers have different pH values.
- HA reacts with added H⁺; A⁻ consumes H⁺ and HA consumes OH⁻.
- Equal moles of weak acid and strong base produce a buffer; at equivalence no HA remains.
- pH = pKa for every buffer; that holds only when the acid and base forms are equal.
