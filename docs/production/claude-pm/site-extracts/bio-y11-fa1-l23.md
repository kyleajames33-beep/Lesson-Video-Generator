## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Interphase: G1, S and G2: Most of a cell's life is spent not dividing but preparing, growing and copying its DNA so that division can be safe and accurate.
2. Mitosis and Cytokinesis: Mitosis is the carefully ordered separation of the duplicated chromosomes, so that each new nucleus receives one complete, identical set.
3. Why the Order Matters, and Checkpoints: Accounting for the cell cycle means explaining not just what happens in each phase but why the order is essential for producing identical cells.
4. When the cycle loses control: cancer: Cancer is, at heart, a disease of the cell cycle.

## 4. Key terms

- Interphase: The G1, S and G2 phases together, the cell grows and copies its DNA before it divides.
- S phase (synthesis): The phase in which DNA is replicated, so each chromosome becomes two identical sister chromatids.
- Mitosis: Division of the nucleus producing two genetically identical nuclei; phases prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase.
- Sister chromatids: The two identical copies of a chromosome joined at the centromere after replication.
- Cytokinesis: Division of the cytoplasm after mitosis, producing two separate daughter cells.
