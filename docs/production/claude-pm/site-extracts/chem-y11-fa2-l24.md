## 3. Key ideas in order

1. The Mole as the Unifying Currency: Chemistry happens between particles you cannot count one by one, so every quantitative tool in this focus area exists to do the same job: turn a measurement you can make into a number of particles you cannot see.
2. One Mole Ratio, Three Kinds of Problem: The coefficients of a balanced equation give a fixed mole ratio, and that one ratio is what every stoichiometry problem turns on, no matter whether the data arrived as a mass, a solution, or a gas.
3. Choosing the Tool and Checking the Answer: The data itself tells you which doorway to use: the units in the question point straight at the correct formula, and the final units and magnitude tell you whether the answer can be believed.

## 4. Key terms

- Mole: The amount of substance containing $6.022\times10^{23}$ particles (the Avogadro constant); the universal counting unit that links mass, solution and gas measurements.
- Mole ratio: The ratio of coefficients in a balanced equation; the bridge from moles of one substance to moles of another.
- Limiting reagent: The reactant that runs out first and therefore sets the maximum amount of product that can form.
- Reasonableness check: A final sanity test: are the units, sign and order of magnitude of the answer sensible for the chemistry?
