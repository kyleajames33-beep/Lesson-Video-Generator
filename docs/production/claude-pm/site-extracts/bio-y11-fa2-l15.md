## 3. Key ideas in order

1. One Rule Explains Every Change: Every change in blood composition follows one simple logic: an organ removes from the blood whatever it consumes, and adds to the blood whatever it produces or absorbs.
2. Organ by Organ: Applying the rule to each organ in turn gives a complete map of how blood content rises and falls around the body.
3. The Liver: The Blood's Manager: After a meal, nutrient levels could swing wildly, the liver exists to buffer these swings so that the rest of the body receives a steady supply.
4. Why blood tests reveal organ problems: Because each organ changes specific substances in the blood, a simple blood test can flag where something is wrong.

## 4. Key terms

- Concentration gradient: A difference in concentration between two regions; substances diffuse from high to low concentration.
- Urea: A nitrogenous waste made by the liver from excess amino acids; removed from blood by the kidneys.
- Hepatic portal vein: The vessel carrying nutrient-rich blood directly from the small intestine to the liver.
- Deamination: The liver process that removes the amino group from excess amino acids, producing ammonia that becomes urea.
- Metabolic waste: A by-product of cell chemistry (e.g. CO₂, urea) that must be removed from the blood.
