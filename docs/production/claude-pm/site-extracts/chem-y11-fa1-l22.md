## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Dispersion (London) Forces: Even a non-polar molecule has electrons that are constantly moving, so for an instant they can bunch on one side, creating a temporary dipole.
2. Dipole–Dipole Forces: A polar molecule has a permanent dipole, a fixed δ+ end and δ− end.
3. Hydrogen Bonding and Ranking the Forces: A hydrogen bond is a particularly strong attraction that forms when a hydrogen atom bonded to a small, highly electronegative atom (N, O or F) is attracted to a lone pair on the N, O or F of a neighbouring molecule.

## 4. Key terms

- Intermolecular force: An attraction between separate molecules (not the covalent bond within a molecule); much weaker than a covalent bond.
- Dispersion (London) force: A weak attraction caused by temporary, fluctuating dipoles; present between all molecules and stronger with more electrons.
- Dipole–dipole force: An attraction between the permanent δ+ and δ− ends of neighbouring polar molecules.
- Hydrogen bond: The strongest intermolecular force, formed when H bonded to N, O or F is attracted to a lone pair on N, O or F of another molecule.
