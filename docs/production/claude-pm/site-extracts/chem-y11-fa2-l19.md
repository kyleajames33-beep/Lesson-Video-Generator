## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Charles's Law and Why Particles Push Out: For a fixed amount of gas at constant pressure, the volume grows in step with the absolute temperature: heat it and it expands, cool it and it contracts.
2. Calculating with V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂: Because $V/T$ is constant, the ratio at state 1 must equal the ratio at state 2, giving the working equation $\dfrac{V_1}{T_1} = \dfrac{V_2}{T_2}$.
3. Reading V versus T Data and Graphs: If a gas obeys Charles's law, then dividing each measured volume by its absolute temperature should give the same number every time, and a plot of V against T(K) should be a straight line passing through the origin.

## 4. Key terms

- Charles's law: For a fixed amount of gas at constant pressure, volume is directly proportional to absolute temperature: $V \propto T$.
- Absolute temperature: Temperature measured on the kelvin scale (K), which starts at absolute zero. Convert from Celsius using K = °C + 273.
- Absolute zero: 0 K (about −273 °C), the lowest possible temperature; the extrapolated point where the volume of an ideal gas would reach zero.
- Directly proportional: Two quantities rise together at a fixed ratio; here, $V/T$ is constant, so a graph of V against T(K) is a straight line through the origin.
