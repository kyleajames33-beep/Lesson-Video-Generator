## 3. Key ideas in order

1. What Counts as a Species: Before we can explain how diversity arises, we need a clear test for when two groups count as different species.
2. How One Species Becomes Two: Speciation usually begins when a population is split, after which different selection pressures push each fragment in a different evolutionary direction until they can no longer interbreed.
3. From Speciation to Biodiversity: Species diversity is the accumulated result of speciation happening over and over for billions of years, branching life into an ever-widening tree.
4. Why this matters: islands of life on land: Speciation explains why isolated places hold so many unique species.

## 4. Key terms

- Species: A group of organisms that can interbreed to produce fertile offspring (biological species concept).
- Speciation: The evolutionary process by which one species splits into two or more new species.
- Geographic isolation: The separation of populations by a physical barrier (river, ocean, mountain) so they cannot interbreed.
- Reproductive isolation: When populations can no longer interbreed to produce fertile offspring, even if brought back together.
- Adaptive radiation: Rapid diversification of one ancestral species into many, each adapted to a different niche.
