## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Filtration at the Glomerulus: The first step is deliberately un-selective: high pressure forces almost everything small out of the blood, leaving only large items behind.
2. Reabsorption and Secretion in the Tubules: As filtrate flows along the tubule, two opposite jobs happen: useful substances are reclaimed into the blood, and extra wastes are added in.
3. Why "Filter Then Reclaim" Works: The "filter everything, then take back what you need" design lets the kidney both remove wastes and precisely adjust the blood's water and salt levels.
4. Glucose in urine: a warning sign: Normally all filtered glucose is reabsorbed, so healthy urine contains none.

## 4. Key terms

- Nephron: The microscopic functional unit of the kidney that filters blood and forms urine; about a million per kidney.
- Glomerulus: A tight knot of capillaries where high pressure forces fluid out of the blood (filtration).
- Bowman's capsule: The cup-shaped start of the nephron that collects the fluid filtered from the glomerulus.
- Selective reabsorption: Reclaiming useful substances (glucose, most water and salts) from the filtrate back into the blood.
- Secretion: Adding extra wastes (e.g. some ions, drugs) directly from the blood into the tubule.
