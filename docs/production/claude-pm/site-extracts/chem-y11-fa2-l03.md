## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Open vs Closed Systems in Cooking: A system is just the part of the world we are studying, here, the food and the heat around it.
2. First Nations Pit-Oven Technology: Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples have, for many thousands of years, used earth ovens, known by names such as the kup-murri in some communities, and broadly as ground or pit ovens, as a deliberate, knowledge-rich cooking technology.
3. Modelling and Calculating Change of Mass: To model the law of conservation of mass, run the same gas-releasing reaction two ways and weigh the whole container before and after.

## 4. Key terms

- Pit (earth) oven: A cooking technology used by Aboriginal Peoples: a pit lined with heated stones or coals, food wrapped in leaves or bark, then covered with earth so heat and moisture are trapped.
- Open system: A system that exchanges both matter and energy with its surroundings, e.g. food over open coals loses water vapour and volatile compounds to the air.
- Closed system: A system that exchanges energy but not matter with its surroundings, e.g. a sealed pit oven traps the steam released by the food.
- Law of conservation of mass: In a chemical reaction, the total mass of the products equals the total mass of the reactants, matter is neither created nor destroyed.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: When food loses mass cooking over open coals, matter has been destroyed. ✓ Right: Mass is never destroyed. The "lost" mass leaves as water vapour and volatile gases that escape into the surroundings, an open system.
