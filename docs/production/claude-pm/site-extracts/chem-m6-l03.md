## 2. Syllabus content covered

“conduct a practical investigation to measure the enthalpy of neutralisation (ACSCH093)”.

## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Strong acid plus strong base has the net ionic reaction H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O and a reference ΔHn near −57 kJ/mol water.
2. The page says ionisation of a weak acid consumes some heat during neutralisation, making the measured ΔHn less negative.
3. A foam cup experiment measures initial and maximum temperatures after mixing known concentrations and volumes.
4. Calculate solution heat with q = mcΔT, find the limiting reagent and moles of water, then ΔHn = −q/n(H₂O).
5. Heat loss lowers the measured maximum temperature; a lid, insulation and temperature logging improve the method.

## 4. Key terms

- Enthalpy of neutralisation (ΔHn): Heat change per mole of water formed.
- Specific heat capacity: Heat needed to raise 1 g by 1°C.
- Limiting reagent: Reactant consumed first, setting n(H₂O).
- Exothermic: Releases heat; the solution temperature rises.

## 5. Worked examples

- 50.0 mL 1.00 mol/L HCl + 50.0 mL 1.00 mol/L NaOH; 21.5 to 28.0°C, density 1.00 g/mL, c = 4.18 J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹: q = 2717 J, n(H₂O) = 0.0500 mol, ΔHn = −54.3 kJ/mol.
- Replace HCl with 50.0 mL 1.00 mol/L CH₃COOH; 21.5 to 26.8°C: q = 2215.4 J, ΔHn = −44.3 kJ/mol.
- 30.0 mL 2.00 mol/L HNO₃ + 50.0 mL 1.00 mol/L NaOH; 20.0 to 26.4°C: NaOH limits at 0.0500 mol; q = 2140 J; ΔHn = −42.8 kJ/mol.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- All neutralisations have ΔHn = −57 kJ/mol: the page restricts this to strong acid plus strong base.
- Use only the acid mass in q = mcΔT: use the combined solution mass.
- Calculate ΔT as initial minus final: use final minus initial, then apply the negative sign in ΔHn.
- Say “human error”: name heat loss and a specific improvement.

## 7. Practical or real-world examples used

Foam cup calorimeter, probe/data logger, HCl, NaOH, acetic acid and HNO₃. Antacid examples include Gaviscon, Mylanta and Rennie.

## 8. Activities and questions

Predict measured versus theoretical ΔHn, calculate limiting reagent, evaluate error and improve a calorimetry method. Representative: “30.0 mL of 2.00 mol/L HNO₃ is mixed with 50.0 mL of 1.00 mol/L NaOH. T i = 20.0°C; T max = 26.4°C. (a) Identify the limiting reagent. (b) Calculate q.”
