## 3. Key ideas in order

1. The Three Subatomic Particles: Every atom is built from just three kinds of particle, and each has a fixed relative mass and charge that you can memorise.
2. Reading Z and A to Count Particles: Two numbers do all the work: the atomic number tells you the protons, and the mass number minus the atomic number tells you the neutrons.
3. Distribution of Mass and Charge: An atom's mass and positive charge are concentrated in a tiny central nucleus, while its negative charge spreads through the much larger surrounding space.

## 4. Key terms

- Proton: A positively charged subatomic particle in the nucleus; relative charge +1, relative mass 1.
- Neutron: An uncharged subatomic particle in the nucleus; relative charge 0, relative mass 1.
- Electron: A negatively charged subatomic particle outside the nucleus; relative charge −1, relative mass ≈ 1/1836 (negligible).
- Atomic number (Z): The number of protons in an atom; it defines the element and equals the number of electrons in a neutral atom.
- Mass number (A): The total number of protons plus neutrons in the nucleus.
