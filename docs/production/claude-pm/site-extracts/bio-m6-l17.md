## 1. Title and main question

Benefits of Genetic Technologies, Agricultural, Medical and Industrial Uses. How do benefits and trade-offs differ across these applications?

## 2. Syllabus content covered

“evaluate the benefits of using genetic technologies in agricultural, medical and industrial applications (ACSBL086)”

## 3. Key ideas in order

1. A claim of benefit needs a purpose, beneficiary and comparison with an alternative.
2. Agricultural applications include yield, resistance and nutrition, with possible biodiversity trade-offs.
3. Medical uses include production of proteins such as insulin and genetic testing to guide treatment.
4. Industrial uses include enzymes and biological manufacturing at scale.
5. The page ranks benefits by evidence, alternatives and who receives them.

## 4. Key terms

- Agricultural application: “Use of genetic technologies to influence crop or livestock performance and management.”
- Medical application: “Use of genetic technologies to produce medicines, support treatment or improve diagnosis.”
- Industrial application: “Use of biological systems or products in manufacturing, processing or large-scale production.”
- Nutritional modification: “Changing a food organism so its nutritional profile is improved.”
- Biological manufacturing: “Using cells or organisms to produce useful compounds at scale.”
- Biodiversity trade-off: “A situation where a gain in one area is linked to a possible cost in diversity or resilience.”

## 5. Worked examples

- A seven-mark evaluation compares modified microorganisms making industrial enzymes and chymosin, recombinant medicines, and insect-resistant and herbicide-tolerant crops.
- A five-mark justification compares recombinant human insulin with extraction from pigs and cattle; the page says porcine insulin differs by one amino acid and bovine insulin by three.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Higher output automatically means higher biodiversity. Crop genetic uniformity can reduce resilience.
- All modified crops have the same benefits and safety profile. The page says effects depend on the trait, assessment and setting.
- “Helps people” is a complete evaluation. The page asks who benefits and compared with what.

## 7. Practical or real-world examples used

Humulin, approved in 1982; the 2018 He Jiankui embryo-editing case; crop pest resistance and nutritional modification; modified microbes producing chymosin and other industrial enzymes.

## 8. Activities and questions

Benefit sorting, multiple choice, true or false, two guided extended answers and three short answers. Representative question: “Evaluate the benefits of using genetic technologies across agricultural, medical and industrial applications.”
