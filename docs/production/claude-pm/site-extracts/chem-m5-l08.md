## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Rank student responses by separate disturbance analysis, Keq reasoning and rate/yield distinction.
2. Analyse each disturbance's shift and Keq effect, then decide whether shifts reinforce or oppose.
3. Reinforcing shifts have a combined direction; opposing shifts cannot be resolved qualitatively, but the temperature effect on Keq remains clear.
4. Graph signatures distinguish species addition/removal, compression, temperature change and catalyst addition at equilibrium.
5. Diagnose immediate changes, affected species and later shifts, then check consistency and cite graph evidence.
6. Faster equilibrium attainment does not necessarily mean greater equilibrium yield.

## 4. Key terms

- Le Chatelier's Principle: A system at equilibrium shifts to partially counteract any applied disturbance.
- Concentration shift: Equilibrium shifts away from added species or towards removed species.
- Temperature shift: Increase in temperature favours the endothermic direction; ΔH sign determines Keq change.
- Pressure/volume shift: Equilibrium shifts towards fewer moles of gas when pressure is increased or volume decreased.
- Catalyst effect: Speeds up attainment of equilibrium without changing equilibrium position or Keq.

## 5. Worked examples

- CO(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ CH₄(g) + H₂O(g), ΔH = −206 kJ/mol: CO/H₂ jump at t₁, products initially unchanged, then reactants decrease and products increase. Answer: synthesis gas added; forward collisions increase, shift right until rates equalise; Keq unchanged.
- SO₂(g) + NO₂(g) ⇌ SO₃(g) + NO(g), ΔH = −41 kJ/mol: decrease temperature by 100°C and remove SO₃. Answer: both shifts right, reinforcing; overall right, SO₃/NO increase and SO₂/NO₂ decrease; Keq increases only because temperature decreased. Gas totals are 2 mol each.
- N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g), ΔH = +57 kJ/mol: sealed syringe halved in volume at t₁, then hot-water bath at t₂. Compression doubles concentrations immediately, darkening brown NO₂; shift left then pales it relative to the immediate colour, still darker than original; Keq unchanged. Heating shifts right, darkens gas and increases Keq because the higher forward activation barrier makes its rate increase proportionally more. Catalyst leaves equilibrium colour and Keq unchanged.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

All condition changes alter Keq: only temperature does. Temperature always dominates opposing shifts: their net direction needs quantitative information. Gradual re-equilibration alone identifies temperature: check absence of a sudden jump. Compression initially changes only one side: all gas concentrations jump. Catalyst raises yield: it changes rate.
