## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Bonds and Energy: A chemical bond is a region of low energy where atoms are held together, so pulling those atoms apart always needs an input of energy, and letting them snap back together always gives energy back out.
2. The Net Heat Change: A reaction's overall heat change is simply a balance sheet: add up the energy you spend breaking every reactant bond, subtract the energy you get back forming every product bond, and whatever is left over is $\Delta H$.
3. Conservation of Energy: The heat a reaction releases or absorbs is not created or destroyed, it is simply chemical potential energy stored in bonds being transferred between the reacting chemicals and their surroundings.

## 4. Key terms

- Bond energy: The energy (in kJ mol -1 ) needed to break one mole of a particular covalent bond in the gas phase, the same amount is released when that bond forms.
- Enthalpy change ($\Delta H$): The net heat energy absorbed or released by a reaction at constant pressure; negative for exothermic, positive for endothermic.
- Law of conservation of energy: Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred or changed in form; the total energy of the system plus surroundings stays constant.
- System and surroundings: The reacting chemicals are the system; everything else (the solution, flask and air) is the surroundings, which gain or lose the heat the system gives up or takes in.
