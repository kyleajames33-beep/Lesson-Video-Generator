## 1. Title and main question

Neutralisation in Everyday Life & Industry. How is acid-base neutralisation used in health, agriculture and pollution control?

Source: `C:/Users/kygs/hscscience/subjects/chemistry/year12/module6/lesson04.sa.html`.

## 2. Syllabus content covered

“investigate applications of neutralisation reactions in everyday life and industrial processes”; “model neutralisation of strong and weak acids and bases using a variety of media”.

## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Antacid hydroxides and carbonates neutralise stomach HCl; carbonate produces CO₂.
2. Lime raises acidic soil pH; toothpaste bases neutralise acids produced in the mouth.
3. Ammonia accepts protons from H₂SO₄ or HNO₃ to make ammonium fertiliser salts.
4. Slaked lime or limestone removes SO₂ from flue gas and reduces acid deposition.
5. Wastewater neutralisation adjusts discharge pH to protect aquatic ecosystems.
6. Particle diagrams and full/net ionic equations show which species react and which ions remain spectators.

## 4. Key terms

- Antacid: Substance that neutralises stomach acid.
- Flue gas desulfurisation: Removal of sulfur dioxide from combustion exhaust.
- Spectator ion: Ion present before and after a reaction without chemical change.
- Net ionic equation: Equation showing only reacting species.

## 5. Worked examples

- Industrial equations: NH₃ + HNO₃ → NH₄NO₃; Ca(OH)₂ + SO₂ → CaSO₃ + H₂O; 2HCl + CaCO₃ → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂.
- Antacid choice: the page compares CaCO₃, Mg(OH)₂ and Al(OH)₃ for a patient with kidney disease and recommends Al(OH)₃ under its stated chemical criteria, while adding a clinical caveat about aluminium.
- Environmental response: coal with 3% sulfur and flue gas near 2000 ppm SO₂; Ca(OH)₂ scrubbing gives CaSO₃, and the page cites 90–99% removal.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Mg(OH)₂ or Al(OH)₃ antacids produce CO₂: the page says hydroxides form salt and water, while carbonates release CO₂.
- One NH₃ fully neutralises H₂SO₄: two NH₃ molecules accept its two protons.
- SO₃ is the primary flue gas target: the page uses SO₂.
- Naming a wastewater neutraliser alone explains its value: give an equation and environmental pH consequence.

## 7. Practical or real-world examples used

Antacids, acidic soil, toothpaste, ammonium sulfate and nitrate fertilisers, coal station flue gas, acidic industrial effluent and freshwater lake acidification.

## 8. Activities and questions

Balance industrial equations, compare antacids for a patient, evaluate a scrubber and model neutralisation with particles and equations. Representative: “A patient with kidney disease cannot excrete large amounts of calcium or magnesium ions. They experience chronic acid reflux.” The task evaluates CaCO₃, Mg(OH)₂ and Al(OH)₃.
