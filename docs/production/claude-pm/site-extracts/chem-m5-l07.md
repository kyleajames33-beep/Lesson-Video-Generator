## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Disturbances change opposing rates unequally, and reactions shift until those rates re-equalise.
2. For exothermic forward reactions, heating increases the higher-activation-energy reverse rate proportionally more.
3. Haber temperature choices trade yield against rate, while higher pressure improves both.
4. Industry balances 400–450°C, 150–250 atm, iron catalyst and recycling against production costs.
5. Complete responses state direction, LCP reason, collision mechanism and the new equilibrium.
6. Concentration-time, yield-temperature and yield-pressure graphs reveal disturbance signatures and industrial choices.

## 4. Key terms

- Haber process: Industrial synthesis of ammonia: N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃; run at ~450°C, 150–250 atm, iron catalyst.
- Contact process: Industrial synthesis of sulfuric acid via SO₂ + ½O₂ ⇌ SO₃; uses vanadium(V) oxide catalyst.
- Industrial compromise: The optimum conditions that balance reaction rate and equilibrium yield in a cost-effective way.
- Heterogeneous catalyst: A catalyst in a different phase from the reactants (e.g., solid iron catalyst with gas-phase reactants).
- Yield: The amount of product obtained; in industrial processes a compromise between thermodynamic yield and reaction rate.
- Collision frequency: The number of effective collisions per unit time; increased by higher temperature, pressure, and concentration.

## 5. Worked examples

- 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g), ΔH = −196 kJ/mol; temperature rises from 450°C to 550°C. Answer: shift left to absorb heat; both rates increase, but reverse rate increases more because its activation energy is higher. [SO₃] decreases, [SO₂]/[O₂] increase, rates re-equalise and Keq = [SO₃]²/([SO₂]²[O₂]) decreases.
- N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), ΔH = −92 kJ/mol: 400–450°C balances acceptable rate with approximately 15–25% yield per pass; 150–250 atm improves rate and yield without the enormous cost of 1000 atm; iron catalyst permits rapid equilibrium at those temperatures without changing yield or Keq; condensation separates NH₃ and recycling gives over 95% overall conversion. Without catalyst, the page states approximately 600–700°C would be required for acceptable rates.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Faster particles alone explain shift direction: specify which rate increases more and the relative activation energies.
- Pressure improves yield but decreases rate: Haber pressure improves both.
- Catalyst improves equilibrium yield: it improves rate only.
- One dramatic graph change identifies the disturbance: examine all species and both immediate and later changes.
