## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Simple Mendelian inheritance: one allele masks the other in the heterozygote, often giving a 3:1 phenotype ratio.
2. Incomplete dominance: the heterozygote is intermediate (red x white gives pink), and the alleles still segregate normally, so they have not permanently blended.
3. Co-dominance: both allele products are expressed together in the heterozygote, as in blood group AB (I^A I^B).
4. Multiple alleles: a gene can exist in more than two allele forms in a population, although each individual carries only two.
5. Phenotype ratios point to the pattern: 3:1 suggests simple dominance, 1:2:1 suggests incomplete dominance or co-dominance.

## 4. Key terms

- Co-dominance: Both alleles are fully expressed in the heterozygous phenotype.
- Incomplete dominance: The heterozygous phenotype is intermediate between the two homozygous phenotypes.
- Multiple alleles: A gene exists in more than two allele forms within the population.
- ABO blood group: A human blood group system showing co-dominance and multiple alleles.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- "Co-dominance and incomplete dominance are the same thing." Page: in co-dominance both alleles are fully and independently expressed; in incomplete dominance the heterozygote is intermediate.
- "An intermediate phenotype means the alleles have blended." Page: the alleles still segregate normally; crossing two pink plants restores red and white phenotypes in a 1:2:1 ratio.
- "Every monohybrid cross gives a 3:1 phenotype ratio." Page: "Do not assume every monohybrid cross gives a 3:1 phenotype ratio."

Possible site errors: (a) "By 1902 he had identified groups A, B, O, and AB": Landsteiner described A, B and O in 1900-1901; AB is generally credited to Decastello and Sturli in 1902. (b) "approximately 50% of blood transfusions were fatal": no source is given and the figure looks too high to state without a citation.
