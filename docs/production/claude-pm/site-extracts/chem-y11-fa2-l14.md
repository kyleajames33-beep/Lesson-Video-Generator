## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Moles Are the Common Currency: A balanced equation only ever talks in moles, so every problem becomes the same three-step shape: convert what you are given into moles, use the mole ratio, then convert the moles you want into the quantity asked for.
2. Solid Reacting With a Solution: When a measured mass of solid reacts with a measured volume of solution, you convert each to moles by its own doorway, link them with the mole ratio, then convert the answer back to whatever the question wants.
3. Finding the Concentration of a Product: If you start from a mass, convert it to moles with $n=\dfrac{m}{M}$, use the mole ratio to find the moles of product, then divide by the final volume to get concentration with $c=\dfrac{n}{V}$.

## 4. Key terms

- Mole ($n$): The amount of substance; the common unit that links mass, concentration and the balanced equation.
- Molar mass ($M$): The mass of one mole of a substance, in g mol -1, found by adding the relative atomic masses in its formula.
- Concentration ($c$): The amount of solute per litre of solution, in mol L -1, given by $c=\dfrac{n}{V}$.
- Mole ratio: The whole-number ratio of moles of substances in a balanced equation, used to convert between reactants and products.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: You can compare masses or volumes of reactants directly to find how much reacts. ✓ Right: You must convert everything to moles first, the balanced equation gives a ratio of moles, not of grams or millilitres. ✗ Wrong: Volume in $c=\dfrac{n}{V}$ can be left in millilitres. ✓ Right: $V$ must be in litres.
