## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Concentration and Surface Area: Both concentration and surface area work the same way: they pack more reacting particles into the space where collisions can happen, so collisions become more frequent and the rate goes up.
2. Temperature: Raising the temperature gives particles more kinetic energy.
3. Catalysts: A catalyst speeds up a reaction by offering an alternative reaction pathway that has a lower activation energy, while it is not used up overall.

## 4. Key terms

- Successful collision: A collision with enough energy (≥ the activation energy) and the correct orientation to actually form products.
- Collision frequency: How often particles collide per second, the more frequent the collisions, the more chances there are for a successful one.
- Activation energy ($E_a$): The minimum energy colliding particles must have for a collision to lead to reaction.
- Catalyst: A substance that speeds up a reaction by providing an alternative pathway with a lower $E_a$, without being consumed.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: Heating works mainly because it makes particles collide more often. ✓ Right: Heating does increase collision frequency a little, but its main effect is giving far more particles energy ≥ $E_a$, so a much larger fraction of collisions succeed. ✗ Wrong: A catalyst speeds a reaction up by giving the particles more energy. ✓ Right: A catalyst does not add energy, it provides an alternative pathway with a lower $E_a$, so more existing collisions clear the (now lower) energy barrier.
