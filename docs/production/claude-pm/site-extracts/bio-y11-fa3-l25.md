## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Carrying Capacity & the Growth Curve: A population grows quickly while resources are plentiful, but as it becomes crowded, limited resources slow the growth until it levels off at the carrying capacity.
2. What Changes the Carrying Capacity: Carrying capacity is not fixed, it rises when limiting resources increase and falls when they decrease, whether the factor is living or non-living.
3. Evaluating Validity, Reliability & Accuracy: A conclusion about ecosystem dynamics is only as trustworthy as the data behind it, so you must judge whether the data is valid, reliable and accurate.
4. Why this matters: setting limits that hold up: Carrying capacity and data quality together underpin real management decisions, how many kangaroos a reserve can sustain, how big a fish stock is and therefore the safe catch, or whether a threatened population is truly recovering.

## 4. Key terms

- Carrying capacity (K): The maximum population size of a species that an ecosystem can support sustainably over time.
- Logistic (S-shaped) growth: Population growth that is rapid at first, then slows and levels off at carrying capacity.
- Validity: Whether the method actually measures what it claims to, testing the right thing, with controlled variables.
- Reliability: Whether repeating the measurement gives consistent results (improved by repeats and large samples).
- Accuracy: How close a measurement is to the true value.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: Carrying capacity is a fixed number for a species, and "reliable" data is the same thing as "accurate" data. ✓ Right: Carrying capacity changes whenever the limiting factors change (e.g. a drought lowers it; more rain raises it).
