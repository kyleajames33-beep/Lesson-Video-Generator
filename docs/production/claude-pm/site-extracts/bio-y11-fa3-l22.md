## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Three Sampling Techniques: Different sampling tools suit different questions: random quadrats estimate overall abundance, while transects reveal how a community changes across a gradient.
2. The Investigation & Calculating Abundance: You find the average number per quadrat, then scale it up to the whole area using the ratio of total area to quadrat area.
3. Comparing the Techniques & Reducing Error: No technique is best for everything: the right choice depends on whether you want overall abundance or a gradient, and reliability depends on random placement and enough samples.
4. Why this matters: surveying for conservation: Quadrats and transects are the workhorses of real ecological surveys.

## 4. Key terms

- Quadrat: A square frame of known area used to sample organisms within it (best for slow/non-moving organisms like plants).
- Line transect: A line laid across an area; organisms touching the line are recorded, used to show change along a gradient.
- Belt transect: A strip (often quadrats placed along a line) sampled across a gradient, gives abundance as well as distribution.
- Random sampling: Placing samples by chance (e.g. random coordinates) to avoid bias and get a representative estimate.
- Population estimate: Total ≈ mean number per quadrat × (total area ÷ quadrat area).
