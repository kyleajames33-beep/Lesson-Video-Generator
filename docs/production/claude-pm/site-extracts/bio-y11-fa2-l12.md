## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Preparing and Observing Plant Sections: To explain how a plant moves gases, water and nutrients, we first need to make the responsible structures visible by cutting thin sections and staining them.
2. Structure to Function, Leaf, Stem, Root: In every plant section, the shape of a structure is a clue to its job: pores for gas swap, hollow tubes for water, living tubes for sugar, and hairs for soaking up.
3. Reading the Slide as Evidence: In a good practical write-up, every claim about function is backed by something you observed, not just memorised.
4. Why plant pathologists slice stems too: The same sectioning skill is used to diagnose plant disease.

## 4. Key terms

- Stomata: Pores on the leaf surface (mainly underside) bounded by guard cells; they open for gas exchange and close to limit water loss.
- Mesophyll: The leaf's photosynthetic tissue; the spongy mesophyll has air spaces that allow gases to diffuse to and from cells.
- Xylem: Tissue of dead, hollow tubes that transport water and dissolved minerals upward from root to leaf.
- Phloem: Living tissue that transports dissolved sugars (nutrients) made in the leaf to the rest of the plant.
- Root hair cell: A root epidermal cell with a long extension that greatly increases surface area for absorbing water and minerals.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: Plants get all their gases through their roots, like drinking water. ✓ Right: Gas exchange happens mainly through the stomata of the leaf (and lenticels of the stem), where carbon dioxide diffuses in and oxygen out.
