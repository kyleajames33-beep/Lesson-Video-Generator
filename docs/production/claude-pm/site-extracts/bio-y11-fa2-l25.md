## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Optimal Range vs Tolerance Limit: A physiological variable has a narrow band where the body works best, a wider band where it merely copes, and a hard limit beyond which it can no longer survive.
2. Worked Example, Body Temperature: For human body temperature, the zones translate into definite numbers: about 37 °C is optimal, the body copes within a few degrees of this, but past roughly 42 °C (or below ~28 °C) survival is threatened.
3. Why the Limits Exist, and Generalising: The reason a critical limit exists is chemical: the enzymes that run every cell process only work within a narrow range and are permanently destroyed beyond it.
4. Heatwaves and the limits of the human body: Australia's increasingly severe heatwaves are a public-health emergency precisely because of tolerance limits.

## 4. Key terms

- Optimal range: The narrow range of a physiological variable in which the body (and its enzymes) function best.
- Tolerance range: The wider band a variable can move through while the body still copes, though not at peak performance.
- Critical tolerance limit: The point beyond which a variable causes system failure and survival is threatened, feedback can no longer cope.
- Physiological condition: An internal variable the body regulates, such as body temperature, blood glucose or blood pH.
- Denaturation: The permanent loss of an enzyme's shape (and function) when conditions move too far from optimal, a key reason limits exist.
