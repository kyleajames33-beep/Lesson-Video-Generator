## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Structural Adaptations for Water Balance: Australian plants face hot, dry conditions, so many have evolved physical leaf features that cut the rate of water loss while still allowing some gas exchange.
2. The Investigation, Method: A fair comparison weighs leaves of similar size at set intervals, so the only thing that differs is the leaf's structure.
3. Making It Reliable and Valid: A result only supports a conclusion about adaptation if every other influence on water loss has been kept the same and the test has been repeated.
4. Why this matters: water-wise gardens and crops: The same structural features you measured guide which plants survive in drought-prone gardens and farms.

## 4. Key terms

- Transpiration: The loss of water vapour from a plant, mainly through the stomata in its leaves.
- Stomata: Tiny pores, usually on the underside of a leaf, that open to let CO₂ in and water vapour out.
- Cuticle: A waxy, waterproof layer covering the leaf surface that reduces water loss.
- Sclerophyll: A plant (such as Eucalyptus) with hard, tough, often small leaves adapted to dry, low-nutrient Australian soils.
- Controlled variable: A factor kept the same across all samples so that only the variable being tested can affect the result.
