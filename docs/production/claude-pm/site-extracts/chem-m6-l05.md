## 2. Syllabus content covered

“conduct an investigation to demonstrate the use of pH to indicate the differences between the strength of acids and bases (ACSCH102)”; “construct models and/or animations to communicate the differences between strong, weak, concentrated and dilute acids and bases (ACSCH099)”.

## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Strength is extent of ionisation; concentration is amount dissolved per litre; both affect [H⁺].
2. The page lists HCl, HNO₃, H₂SO₄ first ionisation, HClO₄, HBr and HI as strong acids; HF is weak.
3. NaOH, KOH, Ca(OH)₂ and Ba(OH)₂ are the four listed strong bases; the latter two give two OH⁻ per dissolved formula unit.
4. Strong species use → and weak species ⇌ in the page's ionic equations.
5. Equal-concentration pH measurements compare the different amounts of ions formed by strong and weak acids or bases.

## 4. Key terms

- Acid/base strength: Extent of ionisation or dissociation in water.
- Concentration: Moles of solute per litre of solution.
- Strong acid/base: Essentially complete ionisation/dissociation.
- Weak acid/base: Partial ionisation or proton acceptance.
- Solubility: Amount that dissolves, separate from the extent of dissociation of dissolved material.

## 5. Worked examples

- Classifications and equations: HBr → H⁺ + Br⁻ (strong); HF ⇌ H⁺ + F⁻ (weak); Ba(OH)₂ → Ba²⁺ + 2OH⁻ (strong); NH₃ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻ (weak).
- Four 0.10 mol/L acids: HNO₃ and HCl both pH 1.0; HNO₂ pH 2.1; CH₃COOH pH 2.9. The page attributes the difference to ionisation, not a concentration difference.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- “Concentrated” means “strong”: concentration and degree of ionisation are separate.
- HF must be strong because HCl is: the page classifies HF as weak.
- Ca(OH)₂ is weak because little dissolves: its dissolved portion dissociates completely.
- Weak and strong equations can use the same arrow: the page assigns equilibrium arrows to weak species.

## 7. Practical or real-world examples used

Calibrated pH probe comparing 0.1 mol/L HCl (pH 1.0), CH₃COOH (pH about 2.9), NaOH (pH 13.0) and NH₃ (pH about 11.1); limewater and hydrofluoric acid.
