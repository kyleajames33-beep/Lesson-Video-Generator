## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Three Kinds of Mammal: Mammals fall into three groups based on how they reproduce, and Australia is unusual in being dominated by the first two.
2. The Role of Isolation: Australia broke away from the supercontinent Gondwana and drifted alone for tens of millions of years, cut off from the placental mammals that came to dominate elsewhere.
3. The Role of Adaptation: Isolation gave these mammals the opportunity, but it was adaptation by natural selection that actually fitted each species to a particular Australian environment.
4. Why this matters: when isolation ends: The protection of isolation lasted until people carried mammals across the barrier.

## 4. Key terms

- Monotreme: An egg-laying mammal; the only living examples are the platypus and the echidnas.
- Marsupial: A mammal that gives birth to very underdeveloped young that complete development in a pouch (e.g. kangaroo, koala).
- Placental mammal: A mammal whose young develop more fully inside the mother, nourished by a placenta (e.g. cat, human).
- Gondwana: The ancient southern supercontinent; Australia separated from it and drifted into isolation.
- Geographic isolation: Separation by a physical barrier (here, ocean) preventing gene flow with mammals elsewhere.
