## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Separation Methods: Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples developed and refined separation methods over tens of thousands of years, applying a deep, accurate understanding of the physical properties of plant and mineral materials.
2. Measuring Percentage Composition by Mass: Percentage composition by mass tells you what fraction of a mixture's total mass each component contributes.
3. Worked Example, Percentage Composition: With the total mass and the mass of each separated component, the calculation is just careful substitution into the formula.

## 4. Key terms

- Infusion / decoction: Steeping plant material in water (warm or cold) so a soluble component, such as a dye, dissolves out into the liquid.
- Dilly bag: A woven bag made by Aboriginal Peoples; the spacing of the weave controls which particle sizes pass through, acting as a filter or sieve.
- Percentage composition by mass: The mass of one component expressed as a percentage of the total mass of the mixture, % w/w.
- Constant mass: The point at which repeated drying and weighing give the same reading, showing all water has been removed.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: Traditional Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander separation methods are simple or pre-scientific. ✓ Right: They are sophisticated, knowledge-based applications of physical properties, solubility for dye extraction, particle size for woven filtration, refined over tens of thousands of years. ✗ Wrong: To find percentage composition you can weigh the mixture once and estimate the rest. ✓ Right: You must measure the mass of the component and the total mass directly, drying to constant mass where water is involved, then use mass of component ÷ total mass × 100.
