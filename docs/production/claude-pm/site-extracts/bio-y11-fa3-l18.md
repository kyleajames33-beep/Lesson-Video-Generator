## 3. Key ideas in order

1. The Four Lines of Evidence: Four independent fields each provide evidence that today's species descended, with modification, from common ancestors.
2. Strengths & Limitations of Each: Each line is powerful but has a weakness, which is exactly why no single line is relied on alone.
3. Reaching an Overall Judgement: The decisive point in an evaluation is that four lines using entirely different methods independently point to the same branching tree of life.
4. Why this matters: how science builds confidence: The way evolution is supported, by independent lines of evidence agreeing, is how science builds confidence in any big idea, from climate change to the safety of a vaccine.

## 4. Key terms

- Comparative anatomy: Comparing body structures across species; homologous structures point to common ancestry.
- Genetic phylogeny: Using DNA sequence comparisons to build evolutionary trees showing relatedness.
- Biogeography: The study of where species live; distribution patterns (e.g. on isolated continents) reflect evolutionary history.
- Biochemistry: Comparing shared molecules and pathways (e.g. DNA, ATP, common proteins) across all life.
- Evaluate: To judge the strength of something by weighing its strengths against its limitations and reaching a conclusion.
