## 3. Key ideas in order

1. The gene pool is all alleles in a population; genetic change includes altered allele presence or frequency.
2. Mutation adds new alleles, usually changing frequency slowly by itself.
3. Gene flow transfers alleles between populations when migrants breed.
4. Genetic drift changes allele frequencies by chance, with stronger effects in small populations and after founder or bottleneck events.
5. Relative effect depends on context: new allele creation, migration rate and population size.

## 4. Key terms

- Gene pool: “The total collection of alleles present in a population.”
- Allele frequency: How common an allele is relative to other alleles of the same gene.
- Mutation: Source of new alleles entering the gene pool.
- Gene flow: Movement of alleles between populations through migration and breeding.
- Genetic drift: Random change in allele frequency, especially influential in small populations.
- Founder effect and bottleneck: Drift caused by a small starting group or sharp population reduction.

## 5. Worked examples

The page describes separated Lake Victoria cichlid sub-populations and divergence in opsin genes. A few founders colonising an island illustrate chance sampling; a disease reducing a population illustrates a bottleneck. A scenario with two large populations exchanging many migrants is assigned to gene flow, while a population reduced to a few survivors is assigned to drift.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- A gene pool belongs to one individual. It is a population-level collection.
- Genetic drift selects the best allele. Its frequency change is random, not adaptive.
- Gene flow creates a new allele. It moves alleles between populations.
- Allele frequencies change only randomly. Mutation, selection, drift and gene flow have different effects.
