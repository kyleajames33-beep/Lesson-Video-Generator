## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Static equilibrium describes a completed irreversible reaction with both rates zero.
2. Dynamic equilibrium requires a reversible reaction in a closed system, with equal non-zero rates and constant concentrations.
3. Closed systems retain matter but can exchange energy; open systems exchange matter.
4. From pure reactants, forward rate falls and reverse rate rises until both meet at a non-zero plateau.
5. Particle diagrams show all reactants initially, a changing mixture, then a stable ratio that need not be equal.

## 4. Key terms

- Static equilibrium: A state where no net change occurs and no opposing process is running underneath.
- Dynamic equilibrium: A state where forward and reverse reaction rates are equal and concentrations remain constant.
- Open system: A system that can exchange both matter and energy with surroundings.
- Closed system: A system where matter cannot enter or leave, though energy may be exchanged.
- Macroscopic property: An observable bulk property (colour, pressure, concentration) that stays constant at equilibrium.
- Rate of reaction: The change in concentration of a reactant or product per unit time.

## 5. Worked examples

- Example 1: sealed H₂/I₂ flask, several hours at 450°C, colour unchanged: dynamic equilibrium, H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g). Cold ash after all campfire wood burns: static equilibrium, both rates zero. Water evaporating in an open warm-room beaker: neither.
- Example 2: Curve A falls from high to constant non-zero; B rises from zero to the same value. A is forward, B reverse; equilibrium begins where they meet and become horizontal. Adding reactant spikes A; B initially stays unchanged; both settle at a slightly higher equal rate.
- Q6 answer: open evaporating beaker versus sealed gas flask; retained matter allows constant concentrations and equal rates.
- Q7 answer: PCl₅(g) ⇌ PCl₃(g) + Cl₂(g) forward curve reaching zero is wrong; both curves must meet above zero.
- Q8 answer: opening CO₂(g) ⇌ CO₂(aq) lets gas escape; immediate resealing gives lower dissolved CO₂ because some gas has permanently escaped.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

Static is not slow dynamic equilibrium. Equal rates do not mean equal concentrations. Dynamic reactions do not stop. Closed does not mean isolated. Equilibrium rate curves do not reach zero. K_eq = 10⁶ means mostly products, not equal particle counts.
