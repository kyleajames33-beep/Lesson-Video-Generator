## 3. Key ideas in order

1. The Mass-to-Mass Roadmap: A balanced equation never compares substances by mass, it compares them by the number of particles, which we count in moles.
2. A Complete Worked Example, Burning Methane: Burning natural gas (mostly methane) in a heater is a real-world reaction whose CO 2 output we often want to know, and it is a clean test of the roadmap.
3. Strategy, Checking and Significant Figures: A mass-to-mass answer is only as trustworthy as its weakest step, so a short routine of checks protects you from the common slips: an unbalanced equation, an inverted ratio, or a rounded intermediate value.

## 4. Key terms

- Stoichiometry: The quantitative relationship between the amounts of reactants and products in a balanced chemical equation.
- Mole ratio: The ratio of the amounts (in moles) of any two substances in a reaction, read directly from the balanced equation's coefficients.
- Reacting mass: The mass of a substance that is consumed or produced in a reaction, linked to other substances through moles.
- Balanced equation: A chemical equation with equal numbers of each atom on both sides, so its coefficients give a valid mole ratio.
