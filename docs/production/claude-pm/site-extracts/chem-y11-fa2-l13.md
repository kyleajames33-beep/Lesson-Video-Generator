## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Infusion and Roasting: Infusion is dissolving: plant material is steeped in water so that the chemicals which are soluble pass out of the plant tissue and into the liquid, where they can be consumed or applied.
2. Pounding and Surface Area: Pounding grinds plant material into much smaller pieces, exposing far more of its surface to the water so dissolving happens much faster.
3. Leaching and Controlling the Rate of Delivery: Leaching soaks or washes pounded plant material in water, often over a long time or in flowing water, so soluble chemicals are gradually carried away.

## 4. Key terms

- Infusion: Soaking plant material in water (often warm) so soluble chemicals dissolve out into the liquid.
- Leaching: Washing plant material with water (sometimes flowing) over time to carry away soluble substances, including unwanted toxins.
- Pounding: Crushing or grinding plant material to break it into smaller pieces, greatly increasing the surface area exposed to the solvent.
- Rate of chemical delivery: How quickly a useful chemical is released from a plant and made available, controlled by the extraction method chosen.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: These methods are simple or "primitive" food preparation with no real chemistry behind them. ✓ Right: They are precise, knowledge-rich techniques that deliberately control surface area, temperature and solubility to manage what chemical is released and how fast. ✗ Wrong: Extraction only ever means getting more of a chemical out. ✓ Right: Leaching can also remove harmful chemicals (detoxification), and slowing delivery can be just as deliberate as speeding it up.
