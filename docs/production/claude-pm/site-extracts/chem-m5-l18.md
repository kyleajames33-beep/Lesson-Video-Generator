## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Qsp uses the same expression as Ksp, but with current (non-equilibrium) ion concentrations.
2. Qsp > Ksp means supersaturated and a precipitate forms; Qsp < Ksp means unsaturated and more solid can dissolve.
3. When two solutions are mixed, every ion concentration is first diluted: c_new = c_original × V_original / V_total.
4. The common ion effect lowers solubility: an added shared ion raises Qsp above Ksp and shifts the equilibrium left.
5. Ksp does not change with the common ion; only temperature changes Ksp.
6. For endothermic dissolution (most salts), higher temperature raises Ksp and solubility.
7. A kidney stone forms when Qsp = [Ca²⁺][C₂O₄²⁻] exceeds Ksp of CaC₂O₄; the net effect of dietary calcium depends on both concentrations.

## 4. Key terms

- Ion product (Qsp): "The product of ion concentrations raised to stoichiometric powers at any point; compared to Ksp to predict precipitation."
- Common ion effect: "The decrease in solubility of an ionic compound caused by the presence of a shared ion in solution."
- Precipitation condition: "If Qsp > Ksp, precipitation occurs until Qsp = Ksp; if Qsp < Ksp, the solution is unsaturated."
- Selective precipitation: "Using controlled ion addition to precipitate one ion from a mixture while leaving another in solution."

## 5. Worked examples

**Worked Example 1 (Analyse).** 25.0 mL of 4.0 × 10⁻³ mol/L Pb(NO₃)₂ is mixed with 75.0 mL of 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ mol/L Na₂SO₄. Ksp(PbSO₄) = 1.6 × 10⁻⁸. (a) Calculate [Pb²⁺] and [SO₄²⁻]. (b) Calculate Qsp. (c) Predict whether a precipitate forms.
Answer: (a) [Pb²⁺] = 1.0 × 10⁻³ mol/L; [SO₄²⁻] = 1.5 × 10⁻⁴ mol/L. (b) Qsp = 1.5 × 10⁻⁷. (c) Qsp > Ksp, so white PbSO₄ precipitates.

**Worked Example 2 (Analyse/Evaluate).** Molar solubility of CaF₂ (Ksp = 3.9 × 10⁻¹¹) in (a) pure water and (b) 0.10 mol/L NaF.
Answer: (a) s = 2.14 × 10⁻⁴ mol/L. (b) s = 3.9 × 10⁻⁹ mol/L, about 55,000 times less soluble; the common ion F⁻ shifts the equilibrium left and Ksp is unchanged.

**Worked Example 3 (Apply/Analyse).** 100.0 mL of 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ mol/L CaCl₂ is mixed with 100.0 mL of 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ mol/L Na₂SO₄. Ksp(CaSO₄) = 4.9 × 10⁻⁵. Will CaSO₄ precipitate?
Answer: After dilution, [Ca²⁺] = [SO₄²⁻] = 5.0 × 10⁻⁶ mol/L; Qsp = 2.5 × 10⁻¹¹ < Ksp, so no precipitate forms.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- "Qsp < Ksp means precipitation occurs." Page: it means unsaturated; only Qsp > Ksp triggers precipitation.
- "Adding a common ion increases Ksp." Page: Ksp is unchanged; the equilibrium position shifts.
- Using original concentrations without diluting. Page: the undiluted answer is "4× too large".
- Balancing equations by changing subscripts. Page: only coefficients may change.
