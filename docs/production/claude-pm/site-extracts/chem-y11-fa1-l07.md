## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Alpha, Beta and Gamma: Mass and Charge: The three types of radiation differ in what they are made of, which fixes their mass and charge, and that, in turn, decides how each one changes a decaying nucleus.
2. Balancing Nuclear Decay Equations: A nuclear equation is balanced when the total mass number and the total atomic number are equal on both sides, and that single rule lets you fill in any missing nuclide.
3. Finding a Nuclide and Balancing Fission: Whenever a nuclide is missing, you find it by making the mass numbers and atomic numbers add up, and the very same rule balances nuclear fission.

## 4. Key terms

- Alpha particle ($\alpha$): A helium-4 nucleus, $^{4}_{2}\text{He}$: mass number 4, charge +2.
- Beta particle ($\beta$): A fast electron emitted from the nucleus, $^{0}_{-1}e$: negligible mass, charge −1.
- Gamma ray ($\gamma$): A high-energy photon (electromagnetic radiation), $^{0}_{0}\gamma$: no mass, no charge.
- Daughter nuclide: The new nucleus produced after a parent nucleus decays.
- Nuclear fission: The splitting of a large nucleus into smaller nuclei, releasing neutrons and large amounts of energy.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: A beta particle is just an electron from outside the atom, so it has no effect on the nucleus. ✓ Right: In beta decay a neutron converts to a proton inside the nucleus, so the atomic number increases by 1 while the mass number stays the same. ✗ Wrong: In a nuclear equation you balance the number of each type of atom, like in a chemical equation. ✓ Right: You balance the totals of mass number ($A$) and atomic number ($Z$) on each side, the elements themselves can change.
