## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Biotechnology deliberately uses organisms, cells or biological processes for human purposes.
2. Traditional methods include fermentation, domestication and selective breeding without direct DNA editing.
3. Modern methods include recombinant DNA, cloning, DNA profiling, genetic markers and gene editing.
4. Agriculture, medicine and industry all use biotechnology; engineered insulin connects traditional medicine to modern microbial production.
5. The page traces past observation and selection to present genetic methods and possible future precision.

## 4. Key terms

- Biotechnology: Use of living organisms, cells or biological processes to make products or solve problems.
- Traditional biotechnology: Longstanding biological methods such as fermentation, domestication and selective breeding.
- Modern biotechnology: Genetic analysis or manipulation, including recombinant DNA, cloning and gene editing.
- Fermentation: Microorganisms used to produce bread, yoghurt, cheese or alcohol.
- Recombinant DNA: DNA formed by combining genetic material from different sources.
- Precision breeding: Genetic knowledge or tools used to guide inheritance more directly.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Biotechnology means only modern gene engineering. Fermentation and selective breeding also qualify.
- Modern methods replaced traditional methods. Both continue to be used.
- The field applies only to medicine. The page also gives agricultural and industrial uses.
