## 3. Key ideas in order

1. Transitional Fossils, Caught Mid-Change: If life forms have changed over time, we should expect to find fossils that show a blend of features from an ancestral group and its descendants, and we do.
2. Relative Dating and Superposition: In undisturbed sedimentary rock, sediment piles up over time, so the deeper you dig the further back in time you go, which lets us put fossils into an age order.
3. Fossil Assemblages and the Bigger Picture: Looking at all the fossils preserved together in a layer reveals not just single species but whole communities and environments of the past.
4. Fossils that find oil, and warn of mass extinction: Relative dating and index fossils are not just academic.

## 4. Key terms

- Fossil: The preserved remains, impression or trace of a once-living organism, usually found in sedimentary rock.

- Fossil assemblage: The collection of different fossils found together in the same rock layer, characteristic of a particular time and environment.
- Relative dating: Working out the age order of rocks and fossils relative to one another, without giving an exact numerical age.
- Superposition: The principle that in undisturbed sedimentary rock, lower layers are older than the layers above them.

## 6. Misconceptions the page addresses

- Misconceptions To Fix watch out ✗ Wrong: A transitional fossil is a "half-and-half" animal that was a direct ancestor of a living species, and gaps in the fossil record disprove evolution. ✓ Right: A transitional fossil shows a mix of ancestral and derived features and represents the kind of intermediate form predicted by evolution, not necessarily a direct ancestor.
