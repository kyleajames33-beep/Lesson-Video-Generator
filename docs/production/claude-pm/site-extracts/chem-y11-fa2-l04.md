## 3. Key ideas in order

1. The Mole as a Counting Unit: Just as a "dozen" is a name for 12 of anything, a mole is a name for a fixed number of particles, it just happens to be a colossal number, because atoms are unimaginably small.
2. Counting Particles with N = n × NA: Because each mole holds $N_A$ particles, the number of particles is simply the amount in moles multiplied by the Avogadro constant.
3. Relative Atomic Mass and the Carbon-12 Standard: Atoms are far too light to weigh in grams, so chemists compare each atom's mass against a single agreed reference: the carbon-12 atom.

## 4. Key terms

- Mole (mol): The SI unit for amount of substance: the amount that contains as many particles as there are atoms in 12 g of carbon-12.
- Avogadro constant ($N_A$): The number of particles in one mole, $6.022 \times 10^{23}$ mol -1.
- Relative atomic mass ($A_r$): The weighted average mass of an element's atoms compared with one-twelfth the mass of a carbon-12 atom; it has no units.
- Carbon-12 ( 12 C): The reference isotope assigned exactly 12 atomic mass units, anchoring the whole relative mass scale.
- Molar mass ($M$): The mass of one mole of a substance, in g mol -1, numerically equal to the relative atomic or molecular mass.
