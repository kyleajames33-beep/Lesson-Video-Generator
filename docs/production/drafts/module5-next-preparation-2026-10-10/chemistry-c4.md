## Part A proposed narration and staged board

**a-opening.** “Suppose a gas mixture is already at equilibrium. We squeeze it into a smaller space, keeping the temperature constant. Does it make more product? The answer depends on the reaction. Let's work out what the squeeze changes first.” Show only the sealed piston and `Temperature fixed`. Opening is rhetorical, not a claimed retrieval task.

**a-compress.** “Straight after the squeeze, the same gas particles occupy less space. They hit the walls more often, so the pressure rises. No new ammonia has appeared just because the piston moved. The reaction responds afterward.” Animate the compression first; retain the original piston-position marker. Hold before any composition change.

**a-reason.** “Our reaction is nitrogen plus hydrogen forming ammonia. The balanced equation says one mole of nitrogen reacts with three moles of hydrogen to form two moles of ammonia. All are gases: four moles become two. A net forward reaction therefore reduces the total amount of gas in the smaller container. This partly offsets the pressure rise. At constant temperature, compression favours that side with fewer gas moles. The pressure need not return to its original value.” Replace the gauge/trace with the separate equation `N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g)`, then reveal `1 + 3 = 4` and `2`. The counts describe the balanced reaction, not starting quantities. Show the particle transformation only when its meaning has been explained. Grouped particles are bookkeeping, not an actual ammonia mechanism.

**a-contrast.** “Expanding the container favours the side with more gas moles. But if the gas coefficients total the same on both sides, changing volume gives no equilibrium shift in this model. Count only gaseous species. A solid's coefficient does not add gas particles.” Clear ammonia working. Show `H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g)` and the `2 versus 2` comparison, without a second crowded piston.

**a-inert.** “Now imagine adding argon without changing the volume or temperature. Argon is inert here: it doesn't react. The total pressure rises, but each reacting gas still has the same amount in the same volume. Its own contribution to pressure, called its partial pressure, stays the same. So there is no shift. A higher total pressure alone doesn't tell us that the reacting gases were compressed.” Replace the earlier equation with a fixed-volume box. Reveal inert particles, then focus on unchanged reacting amount/volume. Do not imply that inert particles cannot collide with reacting ones.

**a-response.** “For two sulfur dioxide plus oxygen forming two sulfur trioxide, predict the shift when the volume increases at constant temperature. Explain your choice using the gas coefficients.” Show the full equation with `(g)` phases, volume increase, fixed temperature and the demand to explain. Hide all counts/answers through a provisional six-second quiet response, with optional pause. Measure the actual hold after recording.

**a-feedback.** “There are three gas moles on the left and two on the right in the balanced reaction. Expansion favours the side with more gas moles, so the equilibrium shifts left. Remember the equilibrium constant, K, from the temperature lesson? It describes the relationship between reactant and product concentrations at equilibrium for a particular reaction. Here it stays the same because the temperature stayed fixed.” Reveal count, direction, then K statement on their separate spoken cues. Clear inert-gas history.

**a-key-notes.** Say: “Here are the essentials for your book. Pause if you want longer to copy.” Stable optional notes after explanation: `At fixed T: compress → fewer gas moles; expand → more.` `Equal gas totals → no shift in the ideal-gas model.` `Inert gas at fixed V and T: reacting partial pressures unchanged.` `K unchanged when T is fixed.` Retain gas-only and constant-temperature conditions. Quiet copy hold, provisional eight seconds; no new reasoning or decorative motion. If this board crowds the intended small player, stage two note boards instead of shrinking text.

## Part B proposed narration and staged board

**b-opening.** “A catalyst can make product appear sooner. Does that mean there will be more product when equilibrium is reached? Those are two different questions: how fast we get there, and what mixture we finish with.” Show only `Sooner?` then `More at equilibrium?`.

**b-path.** “Activation energy is the energy hurdle for a reaction pathway. A catalyst provides another pathway with a lower hurdle. That reversible pathway helps the forward and reverse reactions. It does not change the energies of the starting substances and products. The equilibrium constant, K, describes the equilibrium concentration relationship for this reaction. As in the temperature lesson, its value stays the same when the temperature stays the same.” Use the corrected profile alone, explicitly labelled schematic, with identical pathway endpoints. Its accessibility description must describe the lower alternative pathway and unchanged endpoints without a universal same-factor or same-drop claim. Explain axes as energy versus progress along a pathway, not time. Reveal the alternative path on “another pathway”; keep endpoints stable. Do not display enhancement-factor bars.

**b-composition.** “If the mixture is still approaching equilibrium, the catalyst lets it approach faster. With the same starting amounts and composition, container volume and temperature, the final equilibrium composition is unchanged. If it is already at equilibrium, both directions continue with equal rates through the catalysed system. There is no net shift.” Clear the profile before a separately labelled schematic pair of concentration-time curves from the same product-forming initial composition, reacting amounts, fixed volume and temperature. Show a common asymptotic equilibrium level and faster approach, without claiming exact experimental times or a universal equal multiplier. Avoid a finite-time point labelled exact equilibrium.

**b-response.** “Two closed mixtures start with the same reacting amounts and composition, in the same container volume at the same temperature. Both are initially forming product as they approach equilibrium. Only one has a catalyst. Could it contain more product before both reach equilibrium? Could it contain more after both reach equilibrium? Explain the difference.” Retain every condition and both demands during a provisional six-second answer-free hold.

**b-feedback.** “It can contain more product earlier if the starting mixture is forming product as it approaches equilibrium. But after both reach equilibrium, their composition is the same. Faster production during a limited time is different from a larger equilibrium amount.” Reveal early-time comparison first, then the shared endpoint. This prevents the misleading blanket rule that catalysts never improve production.

**b-key-notes.** Say: “You can copy this short distinction for your book.” Show `Catalyst: alternative pathway with lower activation energy; assists both directions.` `Same initial composition, reacting amounts, volume and T: faster approach, same equilibrium composition and K.` Hold quietly, provisional six seconds, optional pause. Do not reintroduce the profile and graph alongside the notes.
