The separate legacy g01 companion explains why C3's route position differs from its core label. Follow learner order, independently of upload chronology. Applicable cohort: Chemistry Stage 6 (2017), Year 12 Module 5, Equilibrium and Acid Reactions, under the [current priority plan](../../year12-module5-teaching-priority-2026-10-10.md).

Primary learner action: distinguish the imposed change from the subsequent net reaction; predict its direction under stated conditions; explain why a concentration disturbance at fixed temperature retains K, while temperature can change K. Teaching structure: concept through contrast, supported by a declared kinetic model and a new two-part explanation task. The opening is a rhetorical question with immediate explanation. The transfer is the protected learner response.

Entry knowledge: C2's equal opposing rates, rates versus amounts, closed versus insulated systems and declared one-to-one A/B model. For temperature, recognise a stated forward reaction enthalpy: negative means heat released, positive means heat absorbed. The temperature scene supplies a short sign bridge rather than assuming that C2 taught temperature shifts. Entry check: “At equilibrium, are the concentrations equal, or are the opposing rates equal?” Expected answer: the rates are equal; concentrations can differ. If that distinction is unclear, revisit C2's matched rate and concentration boards. C2 is a prerequisite candidate, not a promised published link.

Start boundary: an established equilibrium is disturbed by a brief controlled addition or removal of one participating model species, or by a temperature change. For concentration examples, the volume and temperature remain fixed; after the brief transfer, the mixture is closed again. A and B are interconverting forms of one model substance, one-to-one, with positive fixed first-order coefficients, no side reactions and adequate mixing. No real reaction's rate law is inferred from its overall balanced equation. Temperature examples keep the written reaction, species and fixed volume explicit, with no material entering or leaving.

Stop boundary: the learner identifies which concentration jumps immediately after species transfer, which changes subsequently, the initial net direction and the final dynamic balance. They use the stated enthalpy to predict heating/cooling effects and qualitative K changes, and recognise that open-system water removal confounds a temperature-only explanation. This is qualitative disturbance reasoning. It does not teach pressure/compression, changing volume, inert gases, industrial operating compromises, equilibrium-expression construction, Q, numerical K or ICE tables.

Next core: `chem-m5-c04`, pressure/volume and catalyst distinctions, starting from a gaseous equilibrium with stated conditions and balanced gaseous coefficients. C5 later builds the equilibrium expression; C6 handles calculations. `chem-m5-c09` and its source L13 own temperature/K data interpretation and investigation. `chem-m5-p01` retains supervised practical investigation and the named examples required by the syllabus. This preparation supplies explanation and a prediction task; it does not certify practical conduct, full action coverage or completion of Module 5.

Likely misconceptions to diagnose, as teaching hypotheses: adding reactant instantly creates product; a rightward shift always leaves more product than before the disturbance; the system completely erases the imposed change; any heating makes more products; K changes because composition gradually changes; an open drying observation isolates temperature.

### c3-remove: a right shift does not guarantee more product than before

### c3-temperature-response: temperature changes the relationship before composition adjusts

## Model invariants and bounded implementation specification

The A/B concentration board may reuse C2's exact authored first-order relationship, scaled to relative concentration units for clear established equilibrium: forward coefficient 1, reverse coefficient 2, so equilibrium A:B is 2:1. These are model assumptions, not measured rates or a real reaction K. Production notes below verify the expected shapes; they are not an ICE calculation lesson and are not spoken.

- Initial reference A = 2, B = 1. Add A = 3: immediate A = 5, B = 1; later A tends to 4 and B to 2. Thus A falls from its jump but remains above original, and B rises without an instantaneous jump. After the addition, the new total is 6 and remains fixed.
- Reset to A = 2, B = 1. Remove B = 0.5: immediate A = 2, B = 0.5; later A tends to 5/3 and B to 5/6. B rises from its dip but remains below its original level. The new total is 2.5 and remains fixed. Do not use an addition final state for the removal scene.
- Transfer removal of A: reset to A = 2, B = 1 and remove A = 1; immediate A = 1, B = 1; the net reverse conversion then gives limiting A = 4/3, B = 2/3. A recovers only partly. The stimulus must hide these author answers during the attempt.
- A suitable first-order finite trace after the event has `A(t) = A_limit + (A_immediate - A_limit) exp(-3t)` and `B(t) = total - A(t)`. The pre-event state is separately fixed. A true event step changes only the transferred species. Keep limiting values in a separate exact reference; never equate a finite tolerance or rounded bars with exact equilibrium.
- Graphs must split the curve at the event and draw the vertical step explicitly. Constant-temperature K badges remain fixed. Both direction coefficients stay positive, and the opposite process continues during the net response. The equation means two forms in one mixture, not separate compartments or flight paths.
- The temperature board is a separate qualitative NO2/N2O4 example. Do not recycle one-to-one counts, kinetics or conservation equations for its two-to-one chemistry. Preserve nitrogen and oxygen atoms; never treat colour intensity or arbitrary particle count as measured concentration. No numeric K/T relationship is proposed.
- For an idealised temperature step at fixed volume, update the qualitative K appropriate to new T at that event. Concentrations are continuous, then relax. If implementation instead depicts a finite heating ramp, label it and let K follow T during the ramp, never composition progress. Any apparent flatness is finite approach, not an exact-equilibrium timestamp.
